<h2 id="understand">Separate monocultures, mixed cultures and explanatory models</h2>

Gause compared two Paramecium species alone and together, sharing a food source. The textbook notes that neither secreted a substance killing the other. These points come from the gauseR authors’ digitization of the1934 figures. The vertical axis is a **size-standardized volume index**, not individual cell counts. Monocultures provide reference growth; P.caudatum becomes strongly suppressed in the mixture.

The digitized series retains a positive P.caudatum value near34.58 at day24. Its endpoint must not be changed to zero to match a simplified diagram. The textbook summarizes eventual disappearance, while this record has a specific sampling window and digitization uncertainty. [Data compilation and methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7713957/), [author repository](https://github.com/adamtclark/gauseR)

The third view uses an independent illustrative competition model: dC/dt=0.65C[1−(C+1.8αA)/160] and dA/dt=0.65A[1−(A+0.5αC)/220], starting atC=A=2. These parameters express mutual limitation and are not fitted to the plotted observations. Setting resource overlapα to zero recovers independent logistic growth; increasing it changes predictions within the same model.

<h2 id="try">Look beyond long-term association for discriminating evidence</h2>

The hare–lynx view uses a supplementary1845–1935 historical compilation, **not an exact numerical transcription of the textbook graph**. Each series is divided by its own maximum. Equal height therefore means a similar relative position, not equal animal abundance. Geography, trapping and historical survey methods affect the records; this is not a consistent individual census. Shifting lynx records explores paired lag and correlation without proving a unique cause. [Compilation metadata](https://github.com/jarioksa/ecostudy/blob/master/man/lynxhare.Rd), [primary discussion of historical records and food webs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC24646/)

In the relationship diagram, a plus sign indicates a positive influence on growth and a minus sign indicates suppression. Hares supply food to lynx; lynx affect hare mortality. Consequences become new conditions through feedback, while other predators, food and physiological state remain relevant.

A supplementary Yukon experiment crossed food supplementation with reduction of mammalian predators. During peak and decline phases, mean density ratios were approximately1 for controls,2 for predator reduction,3 for food and11 for the combination. An additive increment calculation would give1+(2−1)+(3−1)=4. These are phase-average findings, not instant population changes caused by interface buttons. Hares could cross the fences, raptors were not excluded, and combined treatment did not completely prevent decline. [Krebs and colleagues’1995 paper](https://www.zoology.ubc.ca/~krebs/papers/122.pdf)

<h2 id="deeper">Space is a shared limiting resource</h2>

The sea-star experiment changed the predator while retaining the rock surface. Barnacles initially occupied more vacant space; mussels later expanded and displaced other occupants. The original study included a neighboring retained-star reference. Predation can indirectly shape communities by constraining dominant competitors, but the same outcome is not guaranteed for every predator and habitat. [Paine’s1966 study](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/282400)

The visual is a mechanism reconstruction. Its96 equal-area sites represent occupancy on a fixed surface rather than96 individual animals. Empty space, barnacles, mussels, limpets and chitons sum to a total area fraction of one. Barnacles recruit into vacant space, and mussels expand into vacant or occupied space. Old and incoming occupants are drawn separately. Percentages are **model values, not original field measurements or species richness**. The retained-star baseline is a held reference, not a prediction of all natural variability.
