<section id="understand">

Across period three, the textbook electronegativities increase. Plot first ionization energy instead, and the curve drops at magnesium to aluminium and phosphorus to sulfur. **Keep the evidence, then ask which electron is being removed.**

Magnesium loses a 3s electron; aluminium begins losing a 3p electron. Phosphorus has three singly occupied 3p orbitals. Sulfur adds a paired electron, whose removal leaves three singly occupied orbitals. Subshells and pairing provide a qualitative explanation; the numerical thresholds come from independent spectroscopy, not from counting drawn boxes.

Now give two separate gaseous ground-state atoms the same 6.5 eV. Aluminium needs about 5.986 eV, so the ionization channel is energetically allowed. Magnesium needs about 7.646 eV, so it is not. The departing electron and the positive ion retain a total charge of zero.

</section>

<section id="deeper">

<details>
<summary>Evidence, explanation and prediction</summary>

Electronegativities are copied from textbook figure 1-23 on page 25 of elective chemistry 2. The values from Na through Cl are 0.9, 1.2, 1.5, 1.8, 2.1, 2.5 and 3.0; Ar has none listed. The rounded K/Rb tie does not establish equality at higher precision. Electronegativity is a relative scale, distinct from gas-phase ionization energy and aqueous electrode potential.

NIST evaluated ionization energies are independent source records. The model converts units and compares a supplied energy; it does not label a generated curve a measurement. Occupancy diagrams identify the removed electron’s subshell. They neither solve the many-electron wavefunction nor calculate an energy difference from the words “half filled”.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Limits of the selected ionization event</summary>

The diagram selects an energetically allowed channel, neglects nuclear recoil, and assigns the energy above the first-ionization threshold to the outgoing electron. Sufficient energy is not a guarantee of ionization: no absorption cross section, probability or physical timescale is calculated. Being below threshold excludes this ionization channel, not all possible excitation.

The moving electron symbol is a persistent counting identity, not a spatial trajectory. Once in transit, it has been removed from the original occupancy and is counted alongside the residual positive charge. A gaseous atom-to-ground-state-ion comparison cannot be transferred directly to solution reactions.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Table layouts and filling rules</summary>

All 118 cells retain their identities. The 14-cell f-block insertion is a layout convention, not a ruling on group 3 membership. Helium has an s configuration but sits at the right edge with the noble gases. The d s label follows the textbook’s classification of the copper and zinc columns.

NIST gives [Ar]3d⁵4s¹ for Cr and [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹ for Cu. A mnemonic may preserve the electron total while missing the ground-state distribution. Arrows denote occupancy and spin, not orbits travelled by electrons.

</details>
