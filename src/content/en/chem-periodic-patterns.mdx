<section id="understand">

Alternative periodic tables can preserve the same element relationships. This film serves textbook research, theory-inquiry and data-analysis positions; it is a **model demonstration**, not a chemical-reaction experiment.

Follow the 18-column to 32-column rearrangement, examine valence filling across period three, then compare textbook electronegativities with first ionization energies. Missing entries, rounded equalities and real dips remain visible.

</section>
<section id="try">

Choose a table form and follow one element. In the filling view, step through period three. The data view independently selects period three, alkali metals or halogens and either electronegativity or first ionization energy.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>What the table and arrows represent</summary>

The118 coloured cells retain their identities throughout the rearrangement. A continuous 32-column layout illustrates a 14-cell f insertion without settling group-three placement conventions. Helium has an s configuration but sits beside the noble gases. The separate ds label follows the textbook’s classification of groups 11 and 12.

The occupancy boxes use the two-electron limit and a simplified Hund filling rule. Chromium and copper use observed NIST ground configurations instead of blindly extending a filling mnemonic. No many-electron Schrödinger equation is solved, and spin arrows are not electron trajectories.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Data and comparison limits</summary>

Electronegativities reproduce figure 1-23 on elective 2 page 25:0.9,1.2,1.5,1.8,2.1,2.5 and3.0 for Na through Cl, with Ar missing. Li through Cs use 1.0,0.9,0.8,0.8,0.7; F through I use 4.0,3.0,2.8,2.5. These rounded textbook values are not presented as a unique electronegativity scale.

First ionization energies use the NIST neutral gaseous ground-state table, converting eV to kJ/mol with 96.48533212 and rounding the display. Magnesium is about 738 and aluminium 578; phosphorus is about 1012 and sulfur 1000. The bars derive directly from these data. Electronegativity is neither the same quantity nor a replacement for aqueous electrode potentials.

</details>
