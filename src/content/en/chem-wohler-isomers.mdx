<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

A molecular formula does not specify molecular structure. This film follows the textbook from Wöhler’s historical preparation of urea through constitutional isomers to cis–trans alkene isomers, preserving the separate learning positions.

In 1828, Wöhler obtained urea from ammonium cyanate, providing important evidence that an organic substance could come from nonbiological starting material. Ammonium cyanate is an ionic salt; urea is O=C(NH₂)₂. Their overall composition is CH₄N₂O. The drawings compare structural endpoints, not a single elementary rearrangement. Only one resonance contributor of cyanate is shown; its charge and bonding are delocalized.

The three pentane skeletons, but-1-ene and but-2-ene, and ethanol and dimethyl ether illustrate skeletal, positional and functional-group isomerism. Implicit hydrogen counts follow from valence and bond order. These are connectivity diagrams rather than measured bond-angle or coplanarity models.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

Switch between the pentanes and count the carbon neighbors of the branching center. Compare what oxygen bonds to in ethanol and dimethyl ether.

Turn the hypothetical orbital angle toward 90° and watch sideways overlap disappear. The cos²θ indicator expresses an idealized geometric trend, not an energy barrier or reaction rate. Then rotate the entire cis or trans structure without twisting its double bond. Select the propene counterexample: two identical hydrogens at one end prevent a cis–trans pair.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

Cis-but-2-ene and trans-but-2-ene have the same connectivity but different spatial arrangements. An intact double bond cannot be treated as a freely rotating hinge. Isomerization under suitable light, heat or catalysis does not imply unrestricted rotation. More complex substituted alkenes require the appropriate E/Z rules.

<summary>Historical and model limits</summary>

Wöhler’s finding influenced a wider historical change; it did not instantly settle every form of vitalism. White crystals alone do not identify urea. Historical identification depended on comparison of properties. The displayed endpoint structures do not predict the microscopic reaction pathway, which involves proton transfer and the reaction medium.
