<section id="understand">
## Precipitation comes before oxidation

Fe³⁺ + 3OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₃(s), while Fe²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₂(s). Textbook experiment 3-1 compares both salts with base, then discusses the rapid grey-green change and later brown material in the iron(II) tube.

The textbook represents the later net change as 4Fe(OH)₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 4Fe(OH)₃. Iron atoms remain present while oxidation states change. An ideal oxygen-free comparison separates this step from precipitation.

</section>
<section id="try">
## Limit base and oxygen independently

Each tube begins with an illustrative 1 mmol of iron. Hydroxide limits precipitation in ratios of three per Fe(III) and two per Fe(II). Available oxygen then limits subsequent oxidation. Reaction extent is a completion fraction, not a real clock or rate constant.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Colour and phase limitations</summary>

Colours are qualitative textbook observations, not calculated spectra. Pure fresh Fe(OH)₂ may be very pale, while green or grey-green is common in air. [The RSC experiment](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/oxidation-states-of-iron/517.article) describes the usual green iron(II) and brown iron(III) precipitates. This reflects conditions and initial oxidation rather than contradicting the textbook sequence.

Actual oxidation may involve oxyhydroxides, green rusts or other phases. [Original research](https://doi.org/10.1039/DT9860001243) demonstrates condition-dependent products. This scene retains the textbook’s overall Fe(OH)₃ representation without predicting phase proportions, solubility equilibrium or oxygen mass transfer. Grey-green is not a unique phase identification. The close view shows stoichiometry rather than isolated hydroxide molecules or a crystal structure.

Textbook: Required Book 1, pages 71–72, PDF pages 76–77.

</details>
