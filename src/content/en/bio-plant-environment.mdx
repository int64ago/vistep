<h2 id="understand">The environment supplies both conditions and information</h2>

Light supports photosynthesis and acts as a signal. The selected phytochrome pathway begins with an intracellular receptor changing state, followed by nuclear expression and developmental responses. Phytochrome is not drawn as a generic membrane switch. Red, far-red and darkness lead to different modeled states; one pathway does not explain every light response in every plant.

Temperature also does more than change present growth rate. In a selected winter type, earlier cold exposure influences later flowering competence. Two plants receive matched later conditions but different cold histories. Annual xylem rings retain another seasonal signal: relatively large earlywood cells contrast with denser latewood. Water, species and other conditions also matter, so one ring is not a direct thermometer for a particular year.

<h2 id="try">Follow perception, transmission and response</h2>

Six persistent amyloplasts sediment within a reoriented statocyte. Sedimentation contributes to gravity perception, followed by changes in signaling and auxin distribution. Roots and shoots respond differently to the same directional asymmetry, producing opposite bends. Edge-length differences generate the curvature, and the cap remains connected to the computed root tip.

Removing the modeled directional gravity cue preserves elongation while removing that cue's directional bend. Lack of a gravity direction is not equated with growth arrest; real plants can still respond to other environmental information.

The root-cap design compares intact, decapped and sham-treated roots. Ask whether bending is reduced **while elongation continues**. If an intervention damages the growth zone and stops elongation, absence of bending cannot locate the sensor. The decapping model retains a small illustrative residual response. Real residual behavior depends on material and conditions; decapping does not universally remove every form of gravity perception.

<h2 id="deeper">Teaching parameters and independent checks</h2>

The active photoreceptor fraction approaches a light-dependent equilibrium exponentially. Active and inactive fractions sum to one, while downstream expression is delayed. The normalized cold-memory function, 1−exp(−3c), distinguishes past exposure from current conditions; it does not estimate a cultivar's required number of cold days.

Root and shoot models reverse the differential elongation. Centerline, boundaries and tip orientation derive from the same edge lengths. Tests independently integrate both edge lengths and distinguish reduced sensing from growth injury or removal of the directional cue. Every statolith remains inside the drawn cell with persistent identity and nonoverlapping geometry. The annual-ring view is an original reconstruction rather than measured microscopy.
