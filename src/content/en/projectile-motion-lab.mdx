<section id="understand">

PEP Required Book 2 §5.3 (pages 10–13) includes strobe/video records, simultaneous launch and drop, carbon-paper tracing, two-receiver ranging, and board/wall variants. Section5.4 then checks the parabola (page17) and measures launch speed with a ruler (page18). Separate chapter windows preserve this learning order.

Ideal horizontal launch follows x = v₀t and y = gt²/2, with the ball centre at launch as origin and right/down positive. Horizontal velocity changes range but not the fall time from a common height.

</section>
<section id="try">

Compare paired balls with the same starting height, release instant and zero vertical velocity. In tracing experiments, keep the ramp release point fixed and change only catcher height or wall distance, so all marks belong to one launch condition.

The two-receiver path locates the source from two one-way acoustic ranges. The parabola path calculates y/x² for nonzero points. Finally, tabletop height h and range l give v₀ = l√[g/(2h)]. Exploration recomputes trajectories and contact points.

</section>
<section id="deeper">

<details>

<summary>Common launch conditions and contact</summary>

A solid sphere rolls from rest at a fixed ramp point to a horizontal exit. Rotational kinetic energy is included; release potential energy is not all assigned to translation. The curved ramp joins the flight with continuous position and horizontal exit velocity.

The laboratory catcher presses carbon paper against a backing sheet. Home variants use a horizontal board or a vertical wall. Motion freezes at first contact; subsequent printing and rolling impacts are not dynamically simulated. Marks are corrected to centre coordinates, treating finite radius consistently. Values are ideal model examples without drag or instrument error, not newly measured evidence.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Two-range geometry</summary>

Receivers occupy known positions on a vertical baseline. Simultaneous infrared and ultrasound emission gives two one-way delays and ranges. Two circles generally have mirror intersections on opposite sides of the baseline. Knowing that the object lies to the right selects one; two ranges alone do not always give a unique unrestricted point.

Coordinates refer to emission time. Tiny infrared travel times are neglected; sound speed is343 m/s. Multiple echoes and missing pulses are omitted. Unlike reflected round-trip sensing, these one-way paths are not divided by two.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Parabola and launch-speed checks</summary>

For repeated identical launches, test whether y/x² is constant within uncertainty or plot y against x². Exclude0/0 at the origin. The ideal ratio is g/(2v₀²). Height and range infer actual flight speed independently of detailed ramp losses, but only if the exit is horizontal so initial vertical velocity is zero.

</details>

</section>
