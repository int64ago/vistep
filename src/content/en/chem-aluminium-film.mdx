<section id="understand">
## No bubbles does not mean no reaction

In textbook experiment 3-4 the surface oxide reacts as Al₂O₃ + 6H⁺ → 2Al³⁺ + 3H₂O, without hydrogen. Metallic aluminium subsequently reacts as 2Al + 6H⁺ → 2Al³⁺ + 3H₂.

Experiment 3-5 compares untreated and abraded aluminium in NaOH. Base can dissolve the film through Al₂O₃ + 2OH⁻ + 3H₂O → 2[Al(OH)₄]⁻. The metal reacts through 2Al + 2OH⁻ + 6H₂O → 2[Al(OH)₄]⁻ + 3H₂. The stripped comparison shows bubbles sooner.

</section>
<section id="try">
## Separate surface condition from reagent limitation

Initial metallic aluminium is 1 mmol. Change the illustrative oxide amount and available acid or base, then check whether reagent remains after oxide removal. If reagent is exhausted during film dissolution, the model cannot produce hydrogen. The uncoated comparison begins with the same metallic aluminium and reagent amounts.

The reaction coordinate allocates ideal conversion. It is not measured time or a rate, and equal coordinates are not an exact comparison at a particular experimental second.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Conservation benchmarks and limitations</summary>

Complete reaction of 1 mmol Al gives 1.5 mmol H₂. With an additional 0.1 mmol Al₂O₃, the acid path consumes 3.6 mmol H⁺ and the base path consumes 1.2 mmol OH⁻. Both produce 1.2 mmol dissolved Al. Aluminium is conserved among metal, remaining film and solution.

Only one magnified surface section is represented; oxide thickness is not a real nanoscale ratio. The sequential model omits defects, localized attack, repassivation, water activity and kinetics. Al³⁺ omits its hydration shell, and the [Al(OH)₄]⁻ diagram counts ligands rather than asserting planar geometry. [Original alumina-film research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7801786/) supports barrier behaviour and condition-dependent acid/base dissolution; its engineered-film rates are not adopted. [RSC’s teaching resource](https://edu.rsc.org/resources/reacting-aluminium-foil-with-acid/652.article) provides aluminium–acid experiment context.

Textbook: Required Book 1, page 82, PDF page 87, experiments 3-4 and 3-5.

</details>
