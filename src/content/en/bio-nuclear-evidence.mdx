<h2 id="understand">Change origin and compare outcomes</h2>

Nuclear transplantation separates nuclear from egg donors. The cow, axolotl and Xenopus examples examine trait origin, pigmentation and developmental potential of differentiated nuclei. They do not establish that all traits depend only on nuclear DNA: eggs supply cytoplasm and mitochondria, and gestational conditions matter.

Newt constriction and amoeba enucleation instead examine division and sustained cellular activity. Removing a nucleus does not instantly stop every reaction already under way. Nucleus and cytoplasm depend on one another. In Acetabularia, basal nuclear origin influences regenerated cap form while existing cytoplasmic products may have transient effects.

<h2 id="try">Change the nuclear condition within a case</h2>

- Disable nuclear transfer and compare division in the two newt-egg halves.
- Distinguish digestion of pre-existing food from sustained growth in amoeba fragments.
- Change algal nuclear origin while retaining the recipient stalk and inspect the new cap.
- Separate surrogate, pregnancy and infant counts in the macaque study.

Textbook: Required 1, Chapter 3, Section 3, pp. 54–58; Chapter 6 differentiation and developmental potential, p. 120. These are historical research reconstructions.

<h2 id="deeper">Keep conclusions within the evidence</h2>

<details>
<summary>Why not calculate a cloning success rate from the diagrams?</summary>

Most animations express structured textbook outcomes, not sample statistics. The macaque chart alone cites the original fetal-fibroblast group: 21 surrogates, 6 pregnancies and 2 healthy infants. Units differ, and these are not pooled results from all donor-cell groups. The paper also distinguishes nuclear and mitochondrial DNA origins.

Amoeba maintenance and digestion are qualitative states, and cleavage counts are illustrative. Acetabularia shows the eventual cap tendency without modeling transient cytoplasmic factors. Animal outlines and colors track sources; they neither guarantee successful transfer nor define a universal pigmentation rule.
