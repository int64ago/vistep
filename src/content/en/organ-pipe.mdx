## A pressure node does not pin the air

An open end has little acoustic pressure variation but can have large oscillatory particle displacement. A rigid stopped end prevents normal motion, giving displacement and velocity nodes but a pressure antinode. Pale particles move longitudinally, blue shows the same-state pressure profile, and gold arrows reveal velocity when needed.

An open cylinder contains roughly half a wavelength at its fundamental; a stopped cylinder contains a quarter. At the same length the ideal stopped fundamental is half the open frequency. Its next allowed mode is three times the fundamental, not twice. Allowed resonances do not mean a real sound contains only odd harmonics.

## Compare one air column

Change physical length, radius, boundary and mode, then inspect cycle phase. Displacement, velocity and pressure have different zeros. Radius changes the drawn bore. Without end correction, ideal frequency mainly follows length and boundaries; with correction, radius changes effective length too. This is the resonator segment, using an equivalent lower open boundary near the mouth rather than modelling flue self-excitation.

## Effective length selects the modes

<div className="formula">
  Open at both ends: fₘ = mc/(2Leffective)
  <br />
  Stopped at one end: fₘ = (2m−1)c/(4Leffective)
  <br />p ∝ sin[k(x+e)] cosφ
  <br />u ∝ −cos[k(x+e)] sinφ
</div>

Each open end adds about e=0.61r; a rigid closed end adds none. The effective pressure node lies slightly beyond the physical rim, made visible in a fixed-scale close-up. This is a low-frequency unflanged approximation, not an exact model of high-frequency mouth radiation.

The model uses a sound speed of 343 m/s and linear small-amplitude modes. Phase is slowed and displacement enlarged 20,000 times, while frequency readouts remain physical. Bore width is exaggerated for readability. Excitation transients, losses, wall motion, transverse modes, real timbre and audible audio are omitted. Pressure and longitudinal displacement share an independent wave model; the air is not treated as a sideways-moving string.
