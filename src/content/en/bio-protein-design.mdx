<h2 id="understand">Define what “better” means</h2>

Protein engineering begins with a functional objective, proposes structural and amino-acid changes, then tests products made through gene design and expression. This is backward reasoning in a design process. During cellular expression, information still proceeds from DNA through RNA to protein. An appealing structure or a similar sequence cannot replace functional evidence.

Under the standard genetic code, the textbook peptide Ala–Trp–Lys–Glu–Phe admits 32 illustrated coding-DNA combinations: 4×1×2×2×2. The displayed triplets are the **5′→3′ coding strand**, not the template strand. This counts only those fifteen bases, not all possible expression constructs.

The temperature comparison is a separate **synthetic enzyme assay**. Two variants have different initial activities and inactivation rates, so their ranking after incubation can change. Its values are neither measurements of an actual enzyme nor insulin data.

<h2 id="try">Track positions, then evaluate evidence</h2>

1. Change the coding choice: the DNA triplets change while the peptide remains the same.
2. Compare the synthetic assay at 20°C and 70°C. Change observation progress to distinguish initial activity from activity after incubation.
3. Rotate the insulin backbone to locate B28, B29 and the three disulfides. Switching structures selects independent measured coordinates.
4. Compare human, pig and bovine mature chains. Align A and B separately before aggregating their match counts.
5. Enter comparable protein regions from your own investigation and record the database, species, accession.version, region, retrieval date and original link. Blank records do not manufacture investigation results.

<h2 id="deeper">
  Structures, simplified equilibria and database records answer different questions
</h2>

Human insulin contains a 21-residue A chain and a 30-residue B chain. The view uses A/B-chain Cα and cysteine sulfur coordinates from [PDB 1TRZ](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1TRZ). Gold links mark the A6–A11, A7–B7 and A20–B19 disulfides. Dashed Cα-to-sulfur connections are geometric guides across omitted side chains, not a full atomic bond diagram.

Insulin lispro exchanges proline at B28 and lysine at B29. [PDB 1LPH](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1LPH) preserves structural evidence for this analogue, including its hexameric assembly. The two coordinate sets have not been superposed, so switching is not a molecular deformation trajectory. Reduced association does not mean that dimers or hexamers cannot form under any conditions.

The exploratory equilibrium retains only `2M ⇌ D`, with `D=K M²` and `C=M+2D`. Its arbitrary concentration units and illustrative constants 4 and 0.4 are not measured lispro parameters. Zinc, hexamers, administration and absorption are omitted. At zero total concentration, the monomer fraction is undefined. The model explains conservation and concentration dependence.

The fixed sequence records were retrieved on **2026-10-18**. Human [NP_000198.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/protein/NP_000198.1) and pig [NP_001103242.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/protein/NP_001103242.1) are RefSeq entries. Bovine [P01317.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/protein/P01317.2) originates in UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot and is also indexed by NCBI Protein. NCBI Protein aggregates several sources; its records should not all be described as belonging to the same source database.

The precursor lengths are 110, 108 and 105 residues. Extracting annotated mature chains gives 50/51 matching human–pig positions and 48/51 matching human–cow positions. These counts aggregate separately aligned A and B chains; they do not imply that mature insulin is one continuous concatenated polypeptide.

The first twenty human and bovine B-chain residues have 100% identity but cover only 20/30 of the chain. Extending to all thirty yields 29/30 identity. A perfect fragment match does not establish an identical full protein. The [NCBI glossary](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK62051/) helps distinguish identity, alignment scope and search statistics.

The personal sequence tool uses a teaching Needleman–Wunsch global alignment: match +2, mismatch −1 and gap −2, with up to 160 residues per input. It does not search a database, perform BLAST, return E-values or establish functional equivalence. Confirm that the input regions are comparable before interpreting the output.
