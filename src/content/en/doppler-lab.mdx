<h2 id="understand">Follow an emission to its arrival</h2>

The textbook places a battery-powered buzzer at the end of a bamboo rod, rotates it overhead and asks a listener several metres away to compare pitch. The apparatus retains the rod, battery, buzzer and fixed listener. Circles are horizontal slices of spherical sound fronts. Each front keeps its original emission centre rather than following the rotating rod.

The buzzer oscillates at 600 Hz while received frequency varies. Two tick rows represent the same consecutive crests. Their first ticks are aligned separately to compare intervals; this inset does not show total propagation delay.

<h2 id="try">Compare two distinct experiments</h2>

- For the buzzer, change rotation rate and radius, then scrub observation time to compare received frequency.
- Optionally start a synthetic pure-tone preview of the current reading. It is not a real buzzer recording.
- For the queue, stand still, walk against it or follow more slowly. Count people actually crossing the observer’s dashed position line.
- Queue members represent travelling crests, not material particles oscillating in place. The following observer always walks slower than the queue.

<h2 id="deeper">Circular motion requires propagation time</h2>

<div className="formula">t = τ + |O − S(τ)|/c; freceived = femitted / [1 − vsource(τ)·n(τ)/c]</div>

Here τ is emission time, t is arrival time and n points from the emission position toward the listener. The model solves the arrival equation before calculating frequency from radial velocity at emission. The ochre dot marks the source position responsible for the signal arriving now; the physical buzzer marks the current rod end.

The illustrative setup uses still air at 340 m/s and a listener 5 m from the pivot. Default radius is 1.2 m at two turns per second. Motion remains subsonic; wind, reflections, directivity and the real buzzer spectrum are omitted. Screen motion is slowed while readings use the labelled physical parameters. The source has been sounding before time zero, so earlier wavefronts are present.

Queue spacing is 2 m and speed is 1 m/s. Observer velocities 0, −0.5 and +0.5 m/s give 30, 45 and 15 encounters per minute. Counting starts after time zero, excluding the initially aligned person. The rate per minute and the actual elapsed-time count are distinct readings.

Textbook: Elective Volume 1, pages 79–80. See the [OpenStax Doppler effect](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/17-7-the-doppler-effect) for the basic relations; circular motion uses the retarded-time equation stated above.
