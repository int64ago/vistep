<h2 id="understand">Why voltage rises while current falls</h2>

Connect an empty capacitor to a 5 V supply through a resistor. Initially the capacitor voltage is zero, leaving the entire supply voltage across the resistor. With the default 1 kΩ resistor, the initial current is 5 mA, not infinity.

That current builds equal and opposite charges on the plates. As capacitor voltage rises, less voltage remains across the resistor and current decreases. **Current describes how quickly charge is changing; voltage describes the state already established.** They need not rise together.

The time constant is τ = RC. With the default 1 mF capacitor, it is 1 s. After one time constant, voltage has reached about 63.2% of its final value. After five, it reaches about 99.3%; the ideal exponential never snaps exactly to its limit.

For discharge, the supply is removed and the capacitor closes a loop through the resistor. Capacitor voltage stays continuous, while current reverses. Energy stored in the electric field becomes resistor heat.

<h2 id="try">Hold the observation time fixed</h2>

- Choose charging from empty and hold the sample time at 1 s. Increasing resistance lowers the voltage reached by that time and also lowers the initial current.
- Restore resistance and increase capacitance. The rise becomes slower, but the initial current still depends only on the supply and resistor.
- Move the time slider to zero and compare charging with discharging. Each choice defines a fresh initial condition, rather than continuing the previous experiment.
- Compare source energy, field energy and heat in the final chapter. In discharge exploration, the left-hand energy source becomes the capacitor's initial stored energy.

<h2 id="deeper">An exact solution with one continuous state</h2>

<details>
<summary>Voltage, current and charge</summary>

Let the initial capacitor voltage be V0 and the constant source voltage be Vs. Charging uses V0 = 0 and Vs = 5 V; discharging uses V0 = 5 V and Vs = 0.

<div className="formula">VC(t) = Vs + (V0 − Vs) exp(−t / RC)</div>

<div className="formula">I(t) = (Vs − VC(t)) / R; Q(t) = C VC(t)</div>

Positive current is the charging direction. A negative reading indicates reversal, not a negative resistance. Samples are evaluated from the exact solution, so seeking and continuous playback agree. Plate symbols encode stored charge; they do not depict particles crossing the insulating gap.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why not all source energy remains stored</summary>

Capacitor energy is C VC²/2, while the resistor dissipates power I²R. Charging an empty ideal capacitor through a positive resistor from a constant voltage source transfers CVs² from the source in the long-time limit. Half remains stored and half becomes heat. Resistance changes the timescale, but not that final ideal energy division.

The model omits leakage, equivalent series resistance, inductance, dielectric absorption, current limiting and breakdown. It rejects the singular R = 0 connection. This is a linear lumped-circuit explanation, not a component-rating guide.

</details>
