<h2 id="understand">Different evidence answers different questions</h2>

Aerobic bacteria indicate where oxygen accumulates; dispersed illumination links wavelength to oxygen production. The Hill reaction separates oxygen evolution from sugar synthesis. Oxygen isotopes trace evolved oxygen to water. Arnon’s work concerns photophosphorylation, while Calvin’s carbon tracing follows CO₂ into organic matter. These are distinct studies, not interchangeable classroom protocols.

Light reactions at thylakoid membranes supply ATP and NADPH to stromal carbon reactions. “Dark reactions” do not occur only in darkness. Stored carriers can support a brief continuation after darkening, but cannot sustain the process indefinitely.

<h2 id="try">Use controls to expose limits</h2>

- Move the beam, then switch to full illumination or darkness.
- Remove the suitable electron acceptor from the Hill model.
- Compare evolved oxygen after labeling water or CO₂.
- Switch to cyclic electron flow: ATP synthesis need not accompany oxygen evolution.
- Skip leaf infiltration or remove CO₂ and inspect the compromised comparison.

<h2 id="deeper">Keep historical and model qualifications</h2>

<summary>What is not an exact historical reconstruction?</summary>

The Engelmann scene follows the textbook’s Spirogyra version. Historical sources distinguish several algae in local-illumination work from Cladophora in spectral work. The textbook diagram is not the exact original preparation. Bacteria follow a qualitative oxygen-source distribution; densities, action response and timing are not measured data.

Hill and photophosphorylation views show relative progress. The textbook does not supply complete original apparatus or procedures, so none are invented here. Cyclic photophosphorylation can occur without water oxidation and oxygen evolution.

The carbon model conserves tracer across three illustrative pools; it is neither original chromatography nor the full branched carbon cycle. The coupling model consumes 9 ATP and 6 NADPH per net three-carbon product from finite stores, without modeling complete chloroplast regulation.

Matched disc thresholds support controlled comparison. Net gas accumulation combines a saturating light response with respiration; rates and buoyancy thresholds are illustrative. Floating counts do not directly measure gross oxygen production. Textbook discs are 0.6 cm across.
