<h2 id="understand">A changing flux induces current</h2>

A rod stationary in a magnetic field produces no current in its closed loop. Translating the entire rail assembly along the field preserves area and flux. Sliding only the rod along the rails changes loop area. Outward and return motions give opposite currents.

Nested coils provide another route. Coil A connects to a source through a switch and rheostat; coil B independently connects to a sensitive current meter. Switching changes A’s current, while a steady current gives no induction in B. Moving the rheostat slider changes current too. Moving A into and out of B at constant primary current changes their geometric coupling and therefore the flux linkage.

<summary>Model and limits</summary>

The rod is18 cm long in a uniform0.3 T field with5 Ω circuit resistance. Flux is Φ = Bℓx and emf is ε = −Bℓvₓ. Translation along B leaves x unchanged and produces no emf. Self-inductance, contact variation and field edges are omitted.

The100-turn inner coil has2 cm radius and samples the axial field of a300-turn outer air-core solenoid,10 cm long with5.5 cm radius. Nine axial samples average the inner coil’s4 cm length. The small-radius approximation neglects radial field variation. Rendered turns represent many real turns. Flux linkage is N₂Φ = M(x)I₁, giving ε = −d[M(x)I₁]/dt and I₂ = ε/R₂ for20 Ω secondary resistance.

Switching uses a prescribed smooth current window, slowed to distinguish before, during and after. It does not reconstruct contact arcing, real self-inductive transients or meter mechanics. The rheostat path uses a6 V supply with2 Ω fixed resistance. Secondary back-reaction, self-inductance and radiation are omitted; the needle directly indicates model current.
