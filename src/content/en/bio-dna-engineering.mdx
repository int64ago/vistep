<h2 id="understand">Understand it</h2>

Elective volume 3, pages 70–85, covers molecular tools, crude extraction, genetic engineering and PCR. Keep four distinctions clear: a white precipitate is not pure DNA; paired cohesive ends still need backbone ligation; cellular entry does not establish expression; and a matching gel band does not prove sequence identity.

Restriction enzymes cleave phosphodiester bonds at defined sites. EcoRI recognizes GAATTC and produces 5′ AATT overhangs. The antiparallel strands retain all their letters. Complementary ends can pair while backbone nicks remain. SmaI illustrates blunt ends from CCCGGG. Ligation assumes suitable T4 ligase conditions; it does not claim that all ligases have the same blunt-end efficiency.

Grinding and filtration produce a mixture. Equal-volume cold alcohol precipitates crude material that can retain contaminants. The diphenylamine comparison treats sample and reagent blank alike, except that only the sample receives the crude extract. In the textbook example both receive 5 mL of 2 mol/L NaCl and 4 mL reagent, followed by the specified heating and cooling. The animation compresses time and does not quantify DNA concentration or purity.

The vector map distinguishes a replication origin, marker and expression elements. Target selection requires functional evidence. PCR is one acquisition method, not a tool that invents a new gene. P and T denote a promoter and transcription terminator, both DNA elements rather than translation start or stop codons.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

- Inspect backbone nicks after cleavage, pairing and ligation instead of judging only whether fragments are close together.
- Remove the origin or functional promoter. Maintenance and expression are separate conditions; marker selection does not replace insert verification.
- Follow DNA symbols through the funnel neck and watch alcohol addition. Compare diphenylamine outcomes with absent DNA or missing heating without overinterpreting an inadequately controlled negative result.
- Track each primer's 3′ end. New strands extend in opposite directions. The textbook's 94, 55 and 72°C cycle is an example for specified conditions.
- Compare M ladder, positive, S sample and NTC lanes. Change sample bands, contamination or stain visibility and consider the resulting evidence.
- Keep actual conditions, gel concentration, fragment conformation, controls and observations in the initially blank journal.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

**Illustrative quantities.** Twenty persistent base markers represent ten base pairs. Filtration preserves two-dimensional liquid cross-sectional area between funnel and receiver, not full three-dimensional flow. The 20 + 20 mL precipitation example illustrates equal-volume addition, not a universal extract volume. Visible DNA lines are source markers, not claims that individual molecules are visible unaided.

**PCR yield.** N=N₀(1+E)ⁿ gives N₀2ⁿ at ideal efficiency E=1. Reagent depletion, inhibition, competition and plateau effects are omitted. Thus 2³⁰ is an ideal total from one starting double-stranded template. With an initial template longer than the primer-bounded region, not every early product has the exact target length. The ideal exact-length double-stranded count is 2ⁿ−2n for n≥1: the third cycle yields two exact products, not eight. This distinction is not used to claim experimental quantification.

Opposite template orientations require extension from the two primers in opposite directions. Heating disrupts pairing rather than cutting phosphodiester backbones. This PCR model starts with DNA; RNA requires an appropriate preceding reverse-transcription step.

**Expression evidence.** The abstract bacterial entry opening explains ordering, not the molecular mechanism of transformation. Hosts and delivery methods differ. In particular, Agrobacterium T-DNA transfer is not delivery of an entire plasmid ring into a plant chromosome. Entry, maintenance, transcription, translation and function need separate tests.

**Gel model.** For linear DNA in one illustrative gel, migration is d=100 log₁₀(3000/L), where L is 100–2000 bp and d is in drawing pixels. Ladder sizes are 2000, 1000, 500, 200 and 100 bp; positive and expected-size sample bands are 500 bp. An artificial alternative has 800 and 180 bp bands. These are not measured data. Plasmid conformations or changes in gel, buffer and field conditions require different treatment. The blue tracking-dye front is not DNA; invisible stain does not establish absent DNA. Length agreement leaves sequence, orientation and expression unverified.
