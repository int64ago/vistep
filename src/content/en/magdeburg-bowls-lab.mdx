<h2 id="understand">Outside air presses the cooled bowls together</h2>

The textbook heats enclosed air, seals two steel bowls and cools their exterior. If gas amount and volume remain fixed, inside pressure falls with Kelvin temperature. Higher atmospheric pressure outside produces the holding force.

Compare each side’s pull with pressure difference acting over the projected opening area. Five people per side pulling at 200 N each mean 1000 N on each side, not 2000 N added. Once a gap opens, air enters and the pressure difference disappears.

<h2 id="try">Change temperature and pull</h2>

- Cool from sealing temperature to 15 °C and observe internal pressure and holding force.
- Increase the force on each side to locate the separation condition.
- Compare the sealed state with equalized pressure after air entry.

<h2 id="deeper">The textbook’s idealization</h2>

<div className="formula">pᵢₙ = pₒᵤₜ × Tcool/Tseal; F = (pₒᵤₜ − pᵢₙ)πr²</div>

Defaults are external pressure 100 kPa, opening diameter 20 cm and sealing temperature 149.5 °C. Cooling to 15 °C gives about 1000 N holding force. Inverse temperature estimates must use Kelvin.

The quantitative model neglects leaks, deformation, combustion chemistry and condensation. Real burning and cooling are more complicated. Each exploration change reconstructs an independent sealed trial; pressure equalization after opening is approximated as rapid.
