## A larger angle, a narrower emerging beam

A Keplerian telescope uses two positive lenses. Parallel rays from each distant point form an intermediate real image at the objective focal plane. With that plane also at the front focal plane of the eyepiece, each bundle leaves parallel again. A relaxed eye sees a larger angle and a final virtual image at infinity. Negative angular magnification denotes inversion.

A second image has a different role: **the exit pupil images the objective aperture, not the intermediate image or the astronomical object.** Beam footprints from multiple fields share a centre in this plane. A shorter eyepiece increases angular magnification while shrinking the exit pupil.

## Compare the imaging path and the eye plane

Changing the eyepiece automatically maintains the lens separation at the sum of the focal lengths, separating magnification from defocus. Change the objective diameter and follow the pupil diameter. In pupil view, move the eye axially and laterally. Axial motion separates different field centres; lateral motion displaces the eye from the beams. Percentages are captured areas of uniform circular beams, not a complete prediction of astronomical surface brightness.

## Imaging and aperture relations

<div className="formula">
  Lens separation L = fO + fE
  <br />
  Angular magnification M = −fO / fE
  <br />
  Exit-pupil diameter = objective diameter / |M|
  <br />
  Exit-pupil distance after eyepiece zP = fE L / fO
</div>

The pupil distance follows from the eyepiece imaging the objective aperture: object distance L and image distance zP. Beam centres in the eye plane come from the same paraxial ray propagation. Their circular overlap with a fixed four-millimetre eye pupil gives the captured fraction. Colours identify three fields rather than wavelengths.

This model assumes an object at infinity, two ideal positive thin lenses and paraxial rays. It omits chromatic and spherical aberrations, diffraction, a finite eyepiece field stop and accommodation. The drawing uses schematic longitudinal and transverse scales. Intersections, parallel bundles, beam centres and pupil imaging remain linked, but the lens icons do not specify real surface curvature or instrument resolution.
