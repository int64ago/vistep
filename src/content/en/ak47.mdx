<h2 id="understand">How does a pulse of gas become a return journey?</h2>

The AK-47 is a **gas-operated rifle** from twentieth-century mechanical history. This explanation follows one broad relationship: **gas energy, linked motion, elastic storage, return**. The [Smithsonian National Museum of American History's collection record](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_439260) places the object in the development of postwar firearms. A moving mechanism can perform an automatic cycle; whether a weapon continues firing also involves other controls. Reciprocation alone does not establish that behavior.

The initial energy comes from chemical energy released by propellant. A portion of the expanding gas does work on a piston through a gas branch and moves the assembly. Neither the magazine nor the return spring supplies this energy independently. [Royal Museums Greenwich's related collection object](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-227949) identifies the gas-operated action. A highlight along the gas tube represents that energy transfer, without depicting real passages, pressures or gas proportions.

A defining relationship is **long-stroke gas operation**: the piston and carrier remain connected and move together. Follow the steel piston rod and carrier. Their separation does not change. That connection explains the term more meaningfully than the apparent length of a rod in a drawing. The [Historical Breechloading Smallarms Association's AKM classification](https://hbsa-uk.org/knowledge-and-research/hbsa-dvd-index-and-content/hbsa-historic-machine-guns-dvd/akm-7-62x39mm-automatic-rifle/) identifies this AK-family mechanism as long-stroke gas-operated.

Rearward movement compresses the return spring. Motion gives up energy as elastic energy accumulates. Near the turning point the assembly's speed approaches zero, but the spring still contains stored energy. It then does work on the linked assembly and moves it forward. **The spring returns energy that was put into it earlier.** Losses dissipate energy, so arriving at the same position does not automatically provide energy for another excursion.

<h2 id="try">Follow one linked assembly</h2>

- Watch the piston rod and carrier move together in the see-through view. Their connections come from one displacement, rather than three separately timed animations.
- Pause during storage. As the movement slows near reversal, the spring's share of energy increases.
- Watch the return chapter: the input has ended, yet the spring continues to move the assembly.
- In the comparison view, select the same displacement for the outward and returning states. Spring compression matches, but the movement arrows point in opposite directions. With losses, the motion energies need not match.
- Exploration sliders select a **teaching observation time** or a comparison position. They do not adjust real weapon parameters.

<h2 id="deeper">An energy explanation, not a firearm simulation</h2>

<details>
<summary>How do motion and energy stay consistent?</summary>

The energy calculation uses a generic mass–spring approximation with a single degree of freedom. With dimensionless position `x`, model time `τ` and a brief input `f(τ)`, it solves:

<div className="formula">x″ = f(τ) − x − c x′</div>

The same integration stages accumulate input work and damping losses. Throughout the teaching excursion:

<div className="formula">Input work = kinetic energy + elastic energy + dissipation</div>

Kinetic energy depends on velocity squared; spring energy depends on compression squared. Two states at the same position therefore have the same spring energy, while their motion can point in different directions. Every energy bar uses the final work of the same teaching pulse as its common scale. [OpenStax's treatment of spring energy](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/15-2-energy-in-simple-harmonic-motion) explains these energy relationships.

When the returning assembly reaches the front boundary, the model transfers its remaining kinetic energy into dissipation and stops. That ideal boundary closes one energy account. It does not predict a real firearm's impact dynamics, and it does not supply a new pulse.

<details>
<summary>What do the object and calculation each explain?</summary>

Wood grain, metal surfaces and connected major parts reconstruct the object visually. Transparent surfaces and close views reveal the piston rod, carrier and return spring. This remains an educational visual reconstruction, rather than exact part geometry for manufacture. It contains no real masses, stiffnesses, dimensions, times, operating pressures or flow rates. Its dimensionless settings cannot be converted into AK-47 performance.

The scene does not simulate feeding, firing, locking, ballistics or firing controls. It provides no use, disassembly, manufacturing or modification instructions. A single excursion refers only to the energy and motion being tracked; it is not a complete weapon cycle. Explaining this mechanical relationship does not make a judgment about the use of weapons or their historical consequences.
