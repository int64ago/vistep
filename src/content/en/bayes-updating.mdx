<h2 id="understand">
  80% of blue tickets have a star does not mean 80% of starred tickets are blue
</h2>

Imagine ten thousand tickets: one thousand in a blue group and nine thousand in another group. Give 80% of blue tickets a star, and also give 10% of non-blue tickets a star. Among tickets observed to have a star, what fraction are blue?

Count them: **800** blue tickets and **900** non-blue tickets have stars. Blue therefore accounts for `800 / 1700 ≈ 47.1%` of the starred group. The original 80% used only blue tickets as its denominator. The posterior uses all starred tickets. These are different conditional probabilities.

<h2 id="try">Change the base rate, keep the evidence rule</h2>

Reduce the blue base rate from 10% to 1%, preserving the 80% and 10% marking rules. There are now 80 blue starred tickets and 990 non-blue starred tickets. The posterior falls to `80 / 1070 ≈ 7.5%`. Evidence with the same likelihood ratio does not imply the same posterior.

Exploration changes all three proportions. Width represents a group's base rate; height represents the frequency of evidence within that group. Their product is a joint proportion. The lower strip renormalizes the selected counts. Counts and areas come from the same model. The background grid is an area reference, not one ticket per cell.

<h2 id="deeper">Update again only when the evidence really is new</h2>

<details>
<summary>Likelihood ratios and conditional independence</summary>

The default prior odds are `1:9`; the star's likelihood ratio is `0.8 / 0.1 = 8`. Posterior odds become `8:9`. If a second mark is conditionally independent of the first given the group, another factor of 8 gives `64:9`, or about 87.7%. Looking at the same mark again adds no evidence, leaving about 47.1%.

For two marks, the control explicitly distinguishes conditional independence from counting the same evidence twice. Independence is an assumption here; observing something twice does not establish it. Two marks also need not be independent in the mixed population.

The formula is `P(blue|star) = P(blue)P(star|blue) / [P(blue)P(star|blue) + P(non-blue)P(star|non-blue)]`. An impossible observation has no defined posterior; the model returns no value rather than dividing by zero. These ticket counts are constructed examples, not medical tests, real classifier performance or decision advice.

Selecting objects consistent with evidence and then changing the denominator can also help interpret [Bloom-filter false positives](/en/explore/bloom-filter/), though their mechanism is different.

</details>
