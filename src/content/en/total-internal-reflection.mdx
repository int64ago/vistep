## Total reflection has conditions

From higher to lower index, the transmitted ray bends away from the normal. At the critical incidence its direction reaches90°. Beyond it, no propagating beam carries average power away normally into the outer medium. A lossless ideal interface reflects100% of the incident normal power.

The result depends on direction, both indices and incidence. Raising the outer index at the same angle can restore transmission. Lower-to-higher index incidence has no corresponding critical angle.

## Change angle, then change only the medium

Exploration fixes the incident index at1.5 and varies incidence and outer index. Find the glass–air critical direction near41.8°, then hold incidence fixed while increasing the outer index. Equal indices remove the interface reflection and total reflection alike.

## No transmitted normal power does not mean zero outer field

<div className="formula">
  Critical angle = arcsin(n₂/n₁), only for n₁ greater than n₂
  <br />
  Field-amplitude decay length = λ / [2π √(n₁² sin²θ − n₂²)]
</div>

The model averages the two lossless isotropic Fresnel polarization power coefficients, with reflected plus transmitted normal average power equal to1. Angles are measured from the normal. The critical state is treated separately from a beam moving away from the interface.

Above critical incidence, the outer electric field decays exponentially with normal distance. Its drawn thickness is enlarged. The reported1/e length refers to field amplitude; intensity is its square and has half that1/e length. An evanescent field can contain local electromagnetic energy without carrying average power away normally.

Absorption, frustrated total reflection due to a nearby third medium, roughness and polarization-phase imaging are omitted. Their presence must be checked before applying an ideal100% result to a real surface.
