<h2 id="understand">Response, indirect evidence and direct detection</h2>

Weber bars illustrate an early detector concept and independent confirmation. Binary pulsars provide indirect evidence through orbital energy loss. LIGO measures differential arm changes. These are distinct evidential roles.

<h2 id="try">Compare resonance and common signals</h2>

Change drive frequency to inspect bar response. Removing the deliberately added common waveform leaves different background traces. This illustrates a method without estimating the significance of a historical event.

The pulsar example uses masses 1.4398 and 1.3886 solar masses, e=0.6171334, orbital period 27906.98 s and spin period 59.03 ms in an edge-on orbit. Leading-order radiation predicts dP/dt≈−2.40×10⁻¹². Accumulated periastron advance is approximately 0.5(dP/dt)t²/P. The orbital drawing omits slow size evolution and periastron precession. The illustrative pulse-period curve uses Pobs=P₀(1+vline-of-sight/c).

The interferometer has 4 km arms and λ=1064 nm. For selected plus polarization at normal incidence, ΔLx=+Lh/2 and ΔLy=−Lh/2, giving differential Lh and simple round-trip phase 4πLh/λ. The first two traces show opposite arm responses; the second segment shows delayed illustrative site strains.

The leading-order inspiral uses chirp mass 28 solar masses, time-to-coalescence 0.300 to 0.005 seconds, and amplitude proportional to f^(2/3). It omits merger, ringdown, antenna factors, detector noise and actual-event fitting.
