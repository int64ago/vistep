<h2 id="understand">Exchange, observation and regulation</h2>

A freely exchangeable solute starts in plasma and reaches interstitial fluid and lymph. Red cells remain inside the blood vessel. Brown dots represent relative tracer density, not individually tracked molecules. The lymph return reconnects with blood, while the total tracer amount stays constant.

Temperature investigation is an observation activity. Its data cannot be replaced by a theoretical curve. The textbook compares household members' axillary temperatures across a day and relates them to other observations and local air temperature. The three displayed series are **editable practice data**, not real survey results. They establish no age or sex differences. Bedtime is placed at 23:00 for plotting; a real investigation records each person's actual time.

<h2 id="try">Keep missing observations missing</h2>

Choose a person and time, replace a value, or mark it missing. The graph neither fills missing observations nor draws a line across them. Body temperatures share one scale; ambient temperature uses a separate scale. Actual measurements require a consistent method and contextual records such as activity and sleep.

The mechanism view separately changes heat input, water loss and sodium loss. Disable regulation to compare the resulting deviation. ADH retains water by reducing its loss; aldosterone retains sodium by reducing sodium loss. Neither creates matter. The brain-to-kidney path summarizes neuroendocrine communication: ADH is synthesized in the hypothalamus and released by the posterior pituitary.

<h2 id="deeper">Three levels of explanation</h2>

Tracer exchange uses well-mixed compartments with fixed volume ratios 3:12:1. Each outgoing tracer flux enters another compartment. This model omits pressure filtration, protein selectivity and calibrated lymph flow.

The temperature plot summarizes observations without imposing a physiological rhythm. The thermal mechanism instead balances normalized heat input and loss. Water and sodium each maintain an explicit input–output ledger. To isolate causal effects, the water model holds solute fixed and the sodium model holds water fixed. Real physiology couples them and includes additional regulatory signals.

Independent checks cover tracer conservation, missing-data behavior, observed ranges, the direction of feedback effects and closure of cumulative heat, water and sodium balances. These relative models are not health assessments.
