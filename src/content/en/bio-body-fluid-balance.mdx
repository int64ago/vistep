## A signal needs a working route

After water loss, more antidiuretic hormone is released. The hormone does not make new water. It changes renal pathways so that more water returns to the body instead of leaving in urine. Understanding homeostasis means following signals and accounting for material.

The film preserves three textbook learning positions: plasma, interstitial fluid and lymph; household temperature observations; then heat and water–salt regulation. The first two distinguish a compartment model from observation records. The final section connects feedback to membrane transport.

Red cells remain inside blood vessels while exchangeable solute redistributes. Brown dots show relative density, not individual molecular tracks. Three well-mixed compartments have fixed volumes and a conserved tracer total. Closing the lymph route changes distribution without deleting matter.

The temperature graph contains labeled practice data, not a real survey. Missing values remain empty, with no line drawn across the gap. Three people share a body-temperature scale; air temperature has a separate scale. These examples demonstrate comparison, not age or sex effects or a health assessment.

## Use a counterexample

- Close the lymph route and compare its local tracer amount with the conserved total.
- Remove a temperature observation, then restore the practice records. A missing point cannot establish that no peak occurred.
- Change heat input or disable the feedback response. After input stops, stored heat still takes time to dissipate.
- Follow the same AQP2 channel into the membrane, then remove the water-potential difference. Channels remain and two-way exchange is possible, but net flow is zero.
- Stop the sodium-potassium pump. Selected sodium ions can enter through channels but cannot complete sustained transepithelial recovery. In the balance view, compare hormone-responsive and unresponsive kidneys under the same external disturbance.

## Connect feedback to both membranes

ADH is synthesized in the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary. In collecting-duct principal cells, it promotes insertion of AQP2-containing membrane into the lumen-facing surface. We follow one added channel. The fusion geometry explains connectivity; it is not a molecular movie from an experiment. Water also crosses the other cell membrane. Net return to the renal interstitium, and subsequently circulation, requires an existing water-potential difference. Channels supply no driving energy. Two selected water markers move at fixed explanatory speeds in opposite directions. Their counts and speeds are not used to estimate net flux. Brown marks compare solute density in equal-area samples outside the two membranes; they do not inventory all cellular osmolytes.

[Nielsen and colleagues’ 1995 primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC42627/) compared water permeability and AQP2 distribution in isolated rat collecting ducts with a maintained osmotic difference. Vasopressin was added and withdrawn. Terminal electron micrographs came from separate treatment groups, not continuous tracking of one vesicle. The teaching balance model is not fitted to those measurements.

Aldosterone from the adrenal cortex acts on the aldosterone-sensitive terminal nephron. Apical ENaC admits sodium down its electrochemical driving force. The basolateral sodium-potassium pump exports sodium, with transport coupled to ATP hydrolysis. Following the [IUBMB reaction stoichiometry](https://iubmb.qmul.ac.uk/enzyme/EC7/2/2/13.html), one selected cycle exports 3 Na⁺ and imports 2 K⁺. Detailed conformations, electrical gradients and subsequent potassium secretion are omitted. This does not imply whole-body potassium retention, and one selected cycle is not a kidney-wide ion count.

[Wang and colleagues’ primary transport study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3904566/) supports the joint role of ENaC and the basolateral pump in transepithelial sodium transport. Its hormone-controlled experiments do not independently establish every aldosterone effect. [Endocrine regulation of kidney function](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-8-endocrine-regulation-of-kidney-function) also explains the roles of renin, angiotensin and blood potassium.

## Close both balances

<div className="formula">
  Terminal delivery = reabsorption + urinary output
  <br />
  Body stock = initial stock + intake − external loss − urinary loss
</div>

Tubular delivery is part of internal circulation. Reabsorption returns material; counting it again as external intake would double-count it. The water model holds solute fixed and the sodium model holds water fixed to isolate variables. Real physiology couples both.

<details>
<summary>Limits of the normalized model</summary>

Relative stocks, fluxes and teaching time are not liters, millimoles or minutes. Fixed terminal delivery is partitioned by a hormone response. Zero driving difference and pump inhibition are boundary counterexamples, not complete disease simulations. A sodium-deficit response stands for omitted regulation; it does not imply an adrenal sensor directly measures total body sodium. Volume, pressure, potassium, renin–angiotensin and other transport processes matter in reality.

The separate heat model closes input minus loss. It does not generate the temperature practice observations. A model curve alone cannot establish a survey finding or clinical conclusion.

</details>
