<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

Ethanol, CH₃CH₂OH, and dimethyl ether, CH₃OCH₃, share the formula C₂H₆O but differ in connectivity. The textbook uses proton NMR to distinguish their hydrogen environments and relative populations.

Under ordinary solution conditions, ethanol has methyl, methylene and hydroxyl environments with a 3:2:1 proton ratio. Dimethyl ether has equivalent methyl groups and one six-proton environment. Signal position is chemical shift δ, commonly referenced to TMS at 0 ppm. Values decrease from left to right.

An integral measures signal area. It is neither peak height nor the number of split lines. The illustrated methyl triplet and methylene quartet retain total areas corresponding to 3 H and 2 H.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

Select an environment to link its molecular hydrogens to the same color in the spectrum. Switch candidates and compare three signal groups with one.

In the coupling close-up, change the model J value. Line spacing changes while total area stays constant. At the fixed 400 MHz model frequency, spacing in ppm equals J / 400.

Move the model OH shift, then increase hydrogen–deuterium exchange. Only the hydroxyl contribution to ordinary ¹H NMR decreases; the five carbon-bound hydrogens remain. The exchange fraction describes an ensemble, not one hydrogen partly becoming deuterium. Deuterium is observed at a different frequency and does not contribute the original signal in this ¹H window.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

The instrument diagram contains a magnet, sample tube and sensitive probe region. Radiofrequency excitation is followed by a free-induction decay. Both the time signal and absorption spectrum use the same frequencies and exponential damping, an analytic exponential–Lorentzian Fourier pair. The displayed trace is the real component in a rotating reference frame, not a slow drawing of the original hundreds-of-megahertz carrier.

<summary>Conditions and simplifications</summary>

These are synthetic teaching spectra, not measurements or digitized textbook plots. Preset ethanol CH₃, CH₂ and OH shifts are 1.20, 3.65 and 2.20 ppm; dimethyl ether is set to 3.30 ppm. OH is adjustable. Shifts depend on solvent, concentration and temperature. Hydroxyl exchange, width and coupling depend on conditions; dry samples may show additional coupling. Quantitative integration also requires appropriate acquisition and relaxation settings.

The grouped view merges each environment’s lines. First-order close-ups use triplet weights 1:2:1 and quartet weights 1:3:3:1, default J = 7 Hz and rapid OH exchange. The n+1 shortcut does not apply universally to complex spectra. The integral is calculated analytically from the same Lorentzian lines. Small tails extend beyond the plotted window; labelled relative areas refer to complete model signals.
