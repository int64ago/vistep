## Heat and work are different energy channels

Adiabatic means zero heat transfer. Piston work can still add internal energy and raise gas temperature. Reversing a reversible adiabatic path lets the gas do work, lowering internal energy and temperature.

Isothermal compression instead removes the supplied work as heat. A final volume alone does not determine the final state; the thermal boundary and process conditions matter.

## Track volume and read the work area

Piston area is fixed at 0.01 m², so gas volume directly sets piston height. Pressure changes along the path; work accumulates pressure times each small volume change. The shaded pV area is the actual path integral, with 1 kPa·L equal to 1 J. Gold is adiabatic and dashed blue is the isothermal comparison from the same initial state.

Exploration changes volume ratio, heat-capacity ratio and thermal boundary. Work and heat into the gas are positive. Negative work means work by the gas; negative heat means energy removed thermally. The return-expansion chapter resets the ledger reference to its compressed initial state.

## Keep the conditions attached to the formula

<div className="formula">
  ΔU = Q_in + W_on
  <br />
  Quasistatic reversible adiabatic: pV^γ = constant, TV^(γ−1) = constant
  <br />
  C_V = R/(γ−1), U = nC_VT
</div>

The model assumes ideal gas, constant heat capacities and a frictionless quasistatic piston. The initial state is 300 K, 100 kPa and 1 L. With gamma 1.4, compression to 0.5 L gives about 395.9 K and 263.9 kPa. Isothermal compression to that volume gives 300 K and 200 kPa. Piston inertia, friction, sound waves and nonuniform transients are omitted; the curve is not an exact prediction of arbitrary rapid compression.

Adiabatic alone does not imply constant pV^γ. An ideal gas expanding freely into vacuum inside a rigid insulated vessel exchanges neither heat nor external boundary work and can retain the same temperature. Only its initial and final equilibrium states are shown. No uniform intermediate pressure or temperature is assigned to the irreversible transient.

Wall hatching denotes insulation. Conducting walls and the outward arrow denote the isothermal reservoir channel. Gas fill colour indicates thermal state, not visible glow. Gamma is a teaching constant; real heat capacities, phase changes and intermolecular interactions can alter these results.
