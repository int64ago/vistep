<section id="understand">

Experiment 7-1 on pages 64–65 of Compulsory Book 2 compares two fresh, identical gas mixtures. The illuminated mixture fades, oily droplets appear on its walls and the liquid level rises. Together these observations support chemical change; colour alone does not identify every product.

Each net substitution replaces one organic H by Cl, consumes one Cl₂ and forms one HCl. Methane can proceed through CH₃Cl, CH₂Cl₂, CHCl₃ and CCl₄. The animation tracks atoms rather than claiming a simultaneous elementary exchange. Initiation, propagation, termination and multiple substitution are described in [the author’s OpenStax textbook](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/10-2-preparing-alkyl-halides-from-alkanes-radical-halogenation).

Chloromethane is gaseous at room temperature: [NIST boiling-point data](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C74873&Type=TBOIL) place its normal boiling point near 249 K. Wall droplets must therefore not simply be labelled CH₃Cl. Higher chlorinated products contribute to droplets, while transfer of HCl into the aqueous phase helps explain gas removal and liquid rise.

</section>
<section id="try">

Choose a fresh illuminated or dark sample. This resets the starting condition rather than switching off an already initiated chain. Change progress and available chlorine to explore coexistence and repeated substitution. Atom totals stay conserved over the full control range.

This is a visual explanation, not a home gas-generation or irradiation procedure. The textbook explicitly excludes direct sunlight.

</section>
<section id="deeper">

<details>
<summary>Conservation without a fabricated yield prediction</summary>

Eight methane units follow a chosen illustrative substitution order, with no carbon receiving more than four chlorine atoms. The order shows how one carbon can undergo repeated substitution; it is neither a kinetic model nor a measured product distribution. Continuous progress represents bulk amounts and visual interpolation, not a molecule replacing half a hydrogen.

Each event removes one organic H, adds one organic Cl and one HCl, and consumes one Cl₂. Carbon, hydrogen and chlorine totals are exact. Colour follows relative remaining Cl₂ without a spectral calculation. Liquid rise is driven qualitatively by HCl and higher-product formation; vapour pressure, saltwater solubility, hydrostatic balance and transfer rates are not calculated. Droplets represent a mixture, without a conversion from drop count to yield.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Control and mechanism</summary>

A fresh dark mixture remaining visibly unchanged does not imply that an established chain stops instantaneously when illumination ceases. In propagation, a chlorine radical abstracts H to form HCl and an organic radical. That radical reacts with Cl₂, regenerating a chlorine radical. Radical encounters can terminate the chain. Chain length, quantum yield and explosions are outside this model.

The apparatus retains the inverted tube, submerged mouth, liquid reservoir and supporting clamp. Foil is partly cut away to show the control interior. Colours are adjusted for teaching legibility.

</details>
