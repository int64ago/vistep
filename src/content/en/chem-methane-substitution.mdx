<section id="understand">

Experiment 7-1 on pages 64–65 of Compulsory Book 2 compares identical methane–chlorine mixtures in light and darkness. The illuminated sample fades, forms wall droplets and draws liquid upward. The fresh dark sample does not show the same changes. These observations distinguish the conditions; colour alone does not identify all products.

Absorbed light can split Cl—Cl, leaving an unpaired electron on each chlorine. In propagation, one Cl· is consumed and another is produced. The carrier type is regenerated, rather than necessarily the same chlorine atom. [McMurry’s textbook](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/10-2-preparing-alkyl-halides-from-alkanes-radical-halogenation) distinguishes initiation, propagation and termination. [IUPAC’s chain-reaction definition](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/C00960) also uses methane chlorination as its example.

The film retains one C, four H and six Cl identities along a selected chain. After two substitutions, two chlorine radicals combine, transferring released energy to the surroundings. The final account contains CH₂Cl₂, two HCl and one remaining Cl₂. It does not require one new photon for every substitution.

Transfer of HCl into water helps explain gas removal. Further substitution can produce liquids, but chloromethane itself is gaseous at room temperature: its [NIST normal boiling-point values](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C74873&Type=TBOIL) are near 249 K. An oily drop is therefore not labelled CH₃Cl.

</section>

<section id="deeper">

<details>
<summary>A fixed sample and an independent expectation</summary>

A reproducible site ordering supplies one illustrative realization. Separately, exact finite combinatorics compute a mean. With N methane units there are 4N initial C—H sites. If m completed substitutions select distinct sites with equal likelihood, a given carbon has k selected sites with probability

`P(k) = C(4,k) C(4N−4,m−k) / C(4N,m)`.

The mean count is `N P(k)`. Summing counts gives N carbons; weighting by k gives m substitutions. Each net substitution consumes one Cl₂ and produces one HCl, preserving the full H and Cl inventory.

The equal-site assumption is a teaching counterfactual, not a fitted product yield. Real chlorinated species differ in reactivity. No rate constant, irradiance, transport parameter or guarantee of a single pure product is supplied.

</details>
<details>
<summary>What the microscopic scene does not measure</summary>

Atom paths interpolate the connectivity of selected reaction endpoints. They are not collision trajectories or transition-state calculations; fading bonds are presentation transitions rather than measured fractional bonds. Screen seconds do not represent reaction times, radical lifetimes, chain lengths or quantum yields.

The drawing describes connectivity rather than methane bond angles. The apparatus retains submerged tube mouths, reservoirs and clamps, with a foil cutaway. Colour, droplet count and liquid level show qualitative textbook observations. They do not determine composition or solve vapour–liquid equilibrium.

</details>
