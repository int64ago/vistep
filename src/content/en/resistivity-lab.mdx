<h2 id="understand">Attribute a resistance change to one condition</h2>

At approximately constant temperature, voltage is proportional to current for the same metallic conductor. The U–I slope is resistance. Changing the source obtains different points on one line; it does not mean resistance changes with current.

The textbook connects four wires in series so they carry equal current. Wire a is the reference. Only b's length is doubled; only c's cross-sectional area is doubled; only d's material changes. Voltage ratios therefore equal resistance ratios, separating the effects of length, area and material. A uniform wire follows R = ρl/S.

Temperature is another condition. Heat a filament connected in series with a small indicator lamp. The heated filament's resistance rises, current falls and the other lamp dims. Brightness gives qualitative evidence, not a direct resistivity measurement.

<h2 id="try">Move from circuit readings to a material property</h2>

- In voltage/current mode, keep the material fixed while changing source voltage, then change material to compare slopes.
- Compare a with b, c and d. Twice the cross-sectional area means only √2 times the diameter.
- To infer resistivity, obtain resistance from several U–I samples, then use the electrically active length and mean diameter measured at several positions: ρ = πd²R/(4l).
- Change entered dimensions without changing the wire. Diameter is squared, so a small reading bias can substantially affect inferred resistivity.
- Change ammeter placement and follow which readings include voltmeter current or ammeter voltage drop.

These follow pages 57–59 of chapter 11, section 2 and pages 65–67 of section 3 in PEP Required Book 3. The separate length-instruments scene explains how to read the dimensional tools.

<h2 id="deeper">Meters are part of the circuit</h2>

<details>
<summary>Controlled variables, connection error and temperature</summary>

The four-wire comparison uses ideal voltmeters to emphasize equal current. Resistivity measurement instead uses teaching values of 0.2 Ω ammeter resistance and 1 kΩ voltmeter resistance. External placement includes voltmeter current in the ammeter reading, making U/I too small. Internal placement includes ammeter voltage drop, making U/I too large. Changing topology does not eliminate every meter error.

Material resistivities use approximate textbook values at 20 °C; actual alloy composition and temperature matter. The heated filament uses tungsten's near-room-temperature linear coefficient as a qualitative model. Lamp intensity represents relative electrical power, not a full incandescent spectrum or thermal balance. Contact resistance and specimen self-heating are omitted. All plotted samples are model data.

</details>
