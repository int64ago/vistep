Screen distance is 1.2 m, slit separation 0.18 or 0.36 mm, wavelength 450–650 nm, and displayed positions ±20 mm. Ideal narrow coherent slits give normalized paraxial far-field I=cos²(πx/Δx), without a finite-slit envelope. Bench distances are compressed and the lens is not an imaging simulation.

The 160-element linear array spans 40 mm. Each 0.25 mm pixel analytically averages cos² intensity over its width. The readout highlight is not a moving probe. A downward beam follows the textbook apparatus. Noise, saturation, bandwidth and reduced coherence are omitted.

Lloyd’s mirror uses the exact real-source and virtual-source path difference plus phase π. Their separation is 2h and the reflection point is x=Lh/(h+y). Horizontal and vertical scales differ but each axis uses one consistent scale.
