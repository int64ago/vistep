<section id="understand">

## From dispersion to a hydrated gel

The textbook practice follows soaking, grinding, filtering, heating, coagulating and shaping. This film tracks the same batch through those stages, then opens a symbolic cutaway of the finished curd. Tofu contains a protein gel network together with water and other soymilk components.

Grinding releases dispersed material; filtration removes coarse okara. Heating changes protein state, and calcium or magnesium salts can promote association. Ingredients, thermal treatment and coagulation conditions influence structure and water retention, so a single “salt causes precipitation” rule is insufficient.

</section>

<section id="try">

## Separate network formation from pressing

Thermal preparation and ion-driven association jointly control the symbolic network. Pressing transfers some liquid from the gel to the expelled serum. Reduce association to inspect a weak network; then hold coagulation fixed and increase pressing to distinguish wet curd mass from its protein inventory.

The controls have no real temperature, time or dosage units and do not provide a recipe. The textbook separately asks students to research appropriate coagulants and operating conditions. The demonstration does not replace that practical investigation.

</section>

<section id="deeper">

<details>
<summary>Material accounts and limits</summary>

The illustrative starting batch contains 40 arbitrary mass units of protein, 60 of other solids and 900 of water. Filtration leaves 4, 16 and 100 in the okara, with 36, 44 and 800 in the milk. These chosen teaching numbers are neither measured soybean composition nor extraction yields.

Twenty symbolic nodes and fixed neighbourhood links represent the network. Smooth thresholds let thermal preparation and ion drive change association strength. The model does not predict binding sites, coagulation speed, rheology or optimal coagulant dosage. It does not imply that unheated proteins can never aggregate by any pathway.

Separate accounts track protein, other solids, water and added salt in curd, serum and okara. Pressing transfers water while preserving the protein inventory at fixed coagulation conditions. Evaporation, washing and processing losses are omitted.

Network lines summarize multiple interactions, and blue dots symbolize distributed water. No line is identified as a measured calcium bridge. Research supports the joint importance of coagulant, thermal history and protein state; the demonstration does not fit an experimental dataset.

</details>
