<section id="understand">
## Revisit the same evidence at different textbook positions

Experiment 4-1 uses tubes for Cl₂/Br⁻, Cl₂/I⁻ and Br₂/I⁻ displacement, comparing each with the added halogen water. Practical Activity 3 uses a white spotting tile and sodium salts. Changing the spectator cation leaves the net reaction unchanged. Its entry retains the three-well chlorine comparison followed by the two-well bromine comparison.

The period-three inquiry compares magnesium with cold and hot water, then prepares Al(OH)₃ and Mg(OH)₂ using aqueous ammonia and divides them for HCl and NaOH tests. The chapter activity revisits those designs. The three textbook entries preserve their chapter sequences and apparatus while sharing the same mechanisms.

</section>
<section id="try">
## Comparisons need explicit references and conditions

Select elemental halogen and halide to examine direction. Reverse combinations are additional mechanism exploration. Available elemental halogen is capped at 0.5 relative amount against one initial halide amount, avoiding a false simple-displacement account of subsequent oxidation by excess oxidant.

Cold water means no appreciable reaction on this demonstration timescale. The hot-water path follows Mg + 2H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ + H₂. Phenolphthalein qualitatively signals alkalinity, without a pH reading.

Hydroxides are prepared before separate portions are tested. Acid uses three proton amounts per Al(OH)₃ and two per Mg(OH)₂. Base uses one OH⁻ per Al(OH)₃ to form [Al(OH)₄]⁻; Mg(OH)₂ does not undergo the same dissolution in this comparison.

<summary>Models, sources and limitations</summary>

Three independent models conserve halogen atoms and electrons, magnesium/water stoichiometry, and hydroxide metal/reagent amounts. Colours are qualitative, not spectral mixtures. Aqueous iodine can include I₂/I₃⁻; the account tracks formed-I₂ equivalents, omitting hydrolysis, excess oxidation and complete equilibria. [RSC’s halogen experiment](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/halogens-in-aqueous-solution-and-their-displacement-reactions/733.article) supports aqueous colours, references and spectator ions. Its optional organic-solvent extension is not added here.

[RSC’s magnesium resource](https://edu.rsc.org/exhibition-chemistry/the-reaction-of-magnesium-with-steam/4012602.article) distinguishes slow room-temperature water reaction and heating effects; its steam apparatus does not replace the textbook hot-liquid-water apparatus. Hot/cold is qualitative, not a rate or temperature threshold. Precipitate preparation uses an ideal completion fraction, without treating ammonia as a strong base. NH₃/NH₄⁺ buffering, residual reagents, activities and equilibrium traces are omitted. [OpenStax on multiple equilibria](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry/pages/15-3-multiple-equilibria) provides real precipitation context.

Textbook: Required Book 1, pages 104, 109 and 122; PDF pages 109, 114 and 127. All three body passages were checked item by item.
