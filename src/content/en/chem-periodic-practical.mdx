<section id="understand">

## Keep the reference, then explain what is new

Chlorine water already has a colour. How can the orange mixture with bromide be attributed to a new substance rather than the original chlorine or dilution? The textbook keeps halogen water as a reference. The enlarged view then follows two halide identities and two electron credits, connecting colour to oxidation and reduction. The dots are net-accounting symbols, not free aqueous electron trajectories.

Chlorine oxidizing both bromide and iodide does not, by itself, order bromine and iodine. The bromine–iodide comparison supplies the missing evidence. The resulting order predicts no appreciable reverse displacement when iodine meets bromide under comparable aqueous conditions. Fixed textbook observations and the new prediction remain visibly separate.

Iodide-containing water also supports equilibria such as I₂ + I⁻ ⇌ I₃⁻. The film therefore counts oxidized iodine in I₂ equivalents; brown colour is not a counter of pure I₂ molecules.

## A quiet magnesium sample is not a proof of impossibility

The textbook cleans the old oxide coating from magnesium ribbon, observes it in cold water, then heats the same sample. Little visible bubbling during a short observation does not establish thermodynamic impossibility. Surface layers, their continuing evolution and reaction barriers affect the visible pace. This film does not calculate a real rate or declare that cold water must give zero reaction forever.

In the net reaction Mg + 2H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ + H₂, magnesium changes from oxidation state 0 to +2 while two hydrogens change from +1 to 0. All seven atoms retain their identities. The original oxygens and the other hydrogens remain in the solid’s hydroxide composition. A solid box is a net formula-unit account, not an isolated Mg(OH)₂ molecule. The paths do not claim an elementary surface mechanism. Phenolphthalein indicates alkalinity without measuring the yield.

## Similar white solids can have different destinations

Ammonia accepts a proton from water, yielding ammonium and hydroxide. Magnesium two-plus requires two hydroxides in the net neutral solid; aluminium three-plus requires three. The metals do not undergo redox in this step.

Acid consumes hydroxide, allowing further solid to dissolve and replenish it. In the net account, the original oxygens enter newly formed water while the metals remain in solution as hydrated ions. One unit of Mg(OH)₂ requires two net proton equivalents, whereas Al(OH)₃ requires three. Supply each unit of solid with only two: magnesium can dissolve completely while one third of the aluminium solid remains. A residue may indicate limited reagent, rather than no reaction.

Strong base on fresh portions reveals a different comparison. Aluminium hydroxide can dissolve through formation of soluble [Al(OH)₄]⁻; magnesium hydroxide remains conspicuous in the textbook conditions. The aluminium centre stays at +3 although the entire complex has charge −1. Complex formation couples to dissolution without reducing aluminium. The progression from strongly basic sodium hydroxide toward amphoteric aluminium hydroxide supports the textbook’s wider comparison of nuclear charge, radius and electron arrangement. These few trials cannot, alone, establish the entire periodic law.

<section id="try">

## Test the explanation with a new condition

Change the halogen and halide or limit the halogen amount. Distinguish a reverse pairing from insufficient reagent. The sample view shows bulk amounts; the net-event view enlarges one net reaction. Magnesium’s condition switch represents qualitative observations. Its atom paths can be paused, but are not a clock or thermometer.

The acid/base slider explicitly represents **net reagent after ammonia, ammonium and other background reactions**. It is not a laboratory drop count. The textbook splits the original ammonia-preparation mixture, so background chemistry affects the total dose. Half a net hydroxide equivalent can dissolve only half a unit of aluminium hydroxide in the bounded net account.

The textbook windows preserve their order and apparatus. Required Volume 1 P104 experiment 4-1 uses tubes and potassium salts. The P109 inquiry begins with magnesium and water, then compares hydroxides. P122 activity 3 uses a spot plate and sodium salts before the period comparison. Its chlorine comparison keeps three wells, and the bromine comparison keeps two. Changing spectator cations does not change the net displacement relation.

<summary>Sources and model boundaries</summary>

- The full textbook contexts at P102–104, P108–110 and P121–122 ground the order, conditions and property table. The 186/160/143 pm sodium/magnesium/aluminium radii are fixed textbook records.
- The [University of Washington chemistry demonstration](https://chem.washington.edu/lecture-demos/amphoteric-nature-aluminum-hydroxide) supports ammonia precipitation followed by separate acid, base and untreated portions. [OpenStax §15.3](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/15-3-coupled-equilibria) explains dissolution coupled to acid consumption or aluminium complex formation. Its constants are not imported as an exact model of the textbook mixtures.
- [Taub et al. (2002), author-institution abstract](https://scholarworks.brandeis.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Mechanism-of-Dihydrogen-Formation-in-the/9923989475601921), describes the favoured but normally sluggish magnesium–water reaction and surface oxide/hydroxide constraints. The paper’s Mg/Fe/NaCl conditions differ from the textbook. Its catalytic mechanism, electron intermediates and rate data are not transferred here. The actual review scope was the institutional abstract.
- [OpenStax’s iodine/triiodide example](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-atoms-first-2e/pages/13-4-equilibrium-calculations) distinguishes net iodine equivalents from complete aqueous speciation.

The model omits complete halogen hydrolysis, excess oxidation, activities, solid polymorphs, trace solubility and full complex speciation. Composition drawings are not crystal coordination geometries; hydration shells are omitted. Conserved atom and electron accounts constrain an explanation but do not prove an elementary mechanism. Periodic position supports conditional predictions that still require evidence.
