<h2 id="understand">The same point O establishes equivalence</h2>

Fix one end of an elastic strip and attach a small ring to the other. Pull with two force meters in different directions. At equilibrium, record point O, both force directions and both readings. Replace the pair with one meter and bring the ring back to the same O.

Neither the fixed end nor O has moved, so strip extension and direction are unchanged. Its force on the ring is therefore the same. One force reproduces the effect of the original two. Merely looking similar, or comparing readings without restoring the point, would not establish that condition.

Draw the two force vectors from O with one scale and complete their parallelogram. Its diagonal matches the equivalent single force. The resultant is a replacement in the analysis, not an additional third physical pull.

<h2 id="try">Keep the effect and change the directions</h2>

- Switch between two and one forces, checking that O and the strip remain unchanged.
- Change the left and right directions. Individual readings change while the required equivalent force stays fixed at the same O.
- Change strip length. Now O, elastic force and the equivalent pull change together.
- Reverse the procedure: establish an effect with one force, then reproduce it with two. This investigates force resolution. Without prescribed directions, a resultant generally does not determine a unique pair.

<h2 id="deeper">Separate apparatus from vector records</h2>

<details>

<summary>Computing the meter readings</summary>

The strip has natural length 0.15 m and stiffness 20 N/m. O lies directly above its anchor, at length L, requiring upward resultant R = 20(L − 0.15). Left and right forces deviate from upward by α and β:

<div className="formula">F₁ = R sinβ / sin(α + β); F₂ = R sinα / sin(α + β)</div>

Their horizontal components cancel and their vertical components add to R. Arrow endpoints, parallelogram and diagonal are derived from these components. Adding force magnitudes instead would generally overestimate the resultant.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Experimental assumptions</summary>

Strings and ring are massless and every state is an equilibrium. Meters read along their strings and should be zeroed first. The strip uses a linear elastic approximation here. A real band need not obey Hooke's law exactly; restoring the same stable deformation within a comparison still supplies the equivalence condition.

Meter housings, springs and rods stay connected as readings change. The vector-record view distinguishes the mathematical diagonal from physical apparatus, avoiding the false impression that three upward pulls act together.

</details>
