<section id="understand">

## Reduction is shared; routes depend on the system

Metallurgy reduces metals in compounds to their elemental state. The textbook groups routes into thermal decomposition, chemical reduction and electrolysis. Choice also depends on the feed, temperature, thermodynamics, product removal and cost; reactivity order is not an exception-free operating chart.

The film compares mercury-oxide decomposition, carbon-monoxide and aluminium reduction of iron oxide, and electrolysis of sodium-chloride, magnesium-chloride and alumina-based melts. Each route keeps both element and electron accounts.

</section>

<section id="try">

## Change a condition and inspect the account

Select a route and change progress to follow atom identities. Removing power or the molten condition stops the selected ideal electrolytic route. A thermal-condition switch stops the selected thermal route. These switches express required conditions; they do not predict threshold temperatures, reaction rates or electrode overpotentials.

Aqueous sodium chloride cannot replace the melt to obtain sodium by this route. The phase and electrode drawings explain interfaces, not a buildable plant. The film does not provide mercury or thermite operating procedures.

</section>

<section id="deeper">

<details>
<summary>Conservation, oxidation states and industrial limits</summary>

Thermal decomposition: `2HgO → 2Hg + O₂`. Two Hg(+II) centres accept four electrons while two O(−II) atoms release four.

Carbon-monoxide reduction: `Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂`. Two Fe(+III) centres accept six electrons as three C(+II) atoms become C(+IV). This net equation omits intermediate iron oxides, coke reactions and slag.

Aluminium reduction: `Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃`, with six electrons on each side of the transfer account. Oxide circle clusters are formula units, not a claim that the solid consists of separate little molecules. Animated paths track identities rather than measured reaction trajectories.

Electrolysis: `2NaCl(l) → 2Na(l) + Cl₂(g)` and `MgCl₂(l) → Mg(l) + Cl₂(g)` each transfer two electrons, accepted by two sodium ions or one magnesium ion. Ions move within the melt; electrodes and the external circuit exchange electrons. Displayed ion and bubble counts are schematic, not concentrations.

For aluminium production with carbon anodes, a common net equation is `2Al₂O₃ + 3C → 4Al + 3CO₂`, transferring twelve electrons. Cryolite belongs to the electrolyte system and is not a net reactant here. Complex aluminium-fluoride speciation, carbon monoxide and other operating effects are omitted. Oxygen alone is not presented as the industrial anode product.

</details>
