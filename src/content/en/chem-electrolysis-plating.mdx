<h2 id="understand">What determines where the products appear?</h2>

In copper chloride electrolysis, copper deposits on the carbon electrode connected to the negative supply terminal, while gas forms at the positive electrode. Textbook experiment 4-2 uses **25% copper chloride solution** and moist potassium iodide–starch paper near the anode. Blue paper is consistent with chlorine oxidizing iodide under the known conditions. The test is not absolutely specific to chlorine; a dry paper failing to change color does not establish that no gas formed.

For these conditions, the cathode reaction is Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu and the anode reaction is 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻. Electrons travel in the external conductors. Ions carry current through the solution. Electrons should not be depicted swimming through the liquid from one carbon rod to the other.

Product selection is conditional. Electrode material, electrolyte concentration and working potential can alter competing reactions. Results for concentrated copper chloride with carbon electrodes do not apply to every aqueous electrolyte.

<h2 id="try">Prepare the surface before connecting iron to the negative terminal</h2>

Experimental activity 4 begins by abrading the iron, degreasing it in 1 mol/L sodium hydroxide and rinsing with distilled water. It then uses 20% hydrochloric acid for rust removal, followed by another rinse. The animation follows one iron piece and lifts it clear of the rim before moving between separate containers.

For plating, connect iron to the negative terminal and copper to the positive. Keep the parallel electrodes about 5 cm apart, use a 2–3 V DC supply and inspect after 5–10 minutes. The film chooses 2.5 V, but **mean current is a separate observed or assumed input**. It is not inferred from voltage alone: electrode area, spacing, composition, contact and overpotential also matter.

Copper dissolves at the anode and deposits on iron at the cathode. In the ideal charge model, the transferred amounts match and total bath copper stays constant. Copper sulfate is the main plating reagent. The textbook also mentions an ammonia-containing alternative; ammonia changes copper speciation and electrode behavior, and that complexation is outside this model.

Pause at an equal treatment time and compare:

- **Reversed connections:** iron becomes the dissolving anode, while copper deposits on the copper electrode. This does not produce the intended controlled coating on iron.
- **Open external circuit:** external charge is zero, but local displacement between iron and copper ions can still deposit copper. Copper-colored material alone does not prove electroplating.
- **A partly masked surface:** with equal total charge and assumed current efficiency, the deposited copper is distributed across less active area. The ideal coating becomes uneven. Actual adhesion, grain structure and roughness require experimental evaluation.

The observation field starts blank. Record your actual bath, preparation, polarity, mean current, time and observations; do not report illustrative animation results as measurements.

<h2 id="deeper">Check charge and display thickness at an honest scale</h2>

At 100% cathodic current efficiency, n(Cu) = It/(2F), with F = 96485.33212 C/mol. A current of 0.20 A for 600 seconds transfers 120 C and theoretically deposits about **39.52 mg** of copper. The plating model additionally assumes 6.7 cm² of active area and copper density 8.96 g/cm³, giving an average coating thickness near **6.58 μm**. The apparatus uses surface color to indicate deposition; quantitative thickness appears in a magnified section.

The chloride calculation additionally assumes 20 g of 25% solution and accounts for total copper and chlorine using the molar mass of CuCl₂. The immersed carbon surface is a cylinder of radius 0.25 cm and length 3 cm, including its bottom face. Plating additionally assumes 50 mL with total copper concentration 0.2 mol/L. Copper loss, deposition and bath composition share one conservation calculation. These added numerical conditions are not all measurements supplied by the textbook.
