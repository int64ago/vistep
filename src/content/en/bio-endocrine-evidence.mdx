<h2 id="understand">Remove a signal, then restore it</h2>

The insulin experiment has separate donor and recipient roles. Following duct ligation, the donor's exocrine acini regress while islets remain. A different animal loses its pancreas and develops high blood glucose. Extract from the donor lowers glucose in the recipient. The illustration keeps the extract source separate from the recipient's glucose markers. The curves reconstruct the comparison; **they are not historical measurements**. Later extraction methods also used normal pancreas, so ligation does not create insulin.

The testis-removal and graft comparison follows the same causal structure. Comb size is the selected observable, and restoration takes time. The presentation compares historical evidence without demonstrating animal procedures.

The rabbit table distinguishes the textbook's reported external genital outcomes from its requested **prediction** after androgen supplementation. XX and XY remain unchanged. This limited comparison does not reduce all sexual differentiation to a single hormone; internal reproductive ducts involve additional signals such as anti-Müllerian hormone.

<h2 id="try">Compare controlled interventions</h2>

Compare intact pancreas, removal, and extract rescue. In the comb sequence, inspect decline before the graft and recovery afterward. In the rabbit table, vary gonadal presence and androgen supplementation to identify the conditions behind each prediction.

The glucose mechanism starts separate fed and fasting comparisons. Feeding supplies an extra input; fasting increases basal use in this teaching model. Insulin increases utilization, while glucagon increases hepatic output. Hormone readouts show the response above baseline; zero does not mean the hormone is absent. Disabling feedback retains every source and sink in the mass ledger.

The thyroid sequence compares pituitary absence, TSH supplementation, TRH supplementation and additional thyroid hormone. The historical pituitary extract is explicitly represented by an effective TSH input. Readouts are relative signals, not clinical concentrations.

<h2 id="deeper">Model limits and independent checks</h2>

Historical phenotypes use declared decay and recovery functions with no calibrated day scale. Glucose follows a source–sink balance checked against independently accumulated absorption, hepatic production and tissue utilization.

The thyroid model begins at H = S = T = 1. Its baseline equations are dH/dt = 2/(1+T) − H, dS/dt = 2H/(1+T) − S and dT/dt = S − T. Interventions change individual terms. The normal state is an exact equilibrium; after pituitary removal, TSH has an independently checkable exponential decay. Rates are teaching choices, not fitted physiological constants.
