<section id="understand">
## A new solid does not empty the solution

Barium chloride and sodium sulfate form white barium sulfate, while sodium and chloride remain dissolved. The net ionic equation retains the barium and sulfate that enter the solid. A sparingly soluble substance still dissolves slightly; the usual near-complete-reaction approximation does not mean every relevant dissolved ion becomes exactly zero.

Solids can also dissolve or change composition. Acid removes hydroxide and drives magnesium hydroxide dissolution. Silver can pass from chloride to iodide and then sulfide. Brown iron hydroxide requires an additional distinction: hydroxide may come from the original magnesium solid or already be free in solution.

</section>
<section id="try">
## Keep totals while changing reagent

Alter barium addition and inspect the spectator-ion ledger. Compare magnesium hydroxide with the retained water control as acid addition increases.

Follow the silver reagent order and remember the two silver atoms in each Ag₂S formula unit. In the hydroxide comparison, add initial free OH⁻. The same brown solid can form directly from it, so colour alone cannot prove complete conversion of the original magnesium solid.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Calculation and qualifications</summary>

The barium sulfate calculation uses Ksp=1.1×10⁻¹⁰ at 25°C, a fixed final volume of 0.1 L, material balances and the ion product. Equimolar residual ion concentrations equal the square root of Ksp. The magnesium model uses Ksp=1.5×10⁻¹¹, water autoionization, total magnesium and charge balance. Concentrations approximate activities; mixing rates are not predicted.

Silver exchange is a near-complete stoichiometric approximation without sulfide hydrolysis, complexation or conversion kinetics. The hydroxide model first consumes free OH⁻ to form Fe(OH)₃, then applies the 3:2 magnesium-solid/iron stoichiometry to the remaining conversion. Model amounts are not exact conversions of textbook drops. Real constants depend on temperature, concentration and solid form.
