<h2 id="understand">Make each control answer a question</h2>

Spallanzani’s cage excludes direct grinding as the sole explanation. Buchner’s extract shows fermentation without intact cells. Sumner separately demonstrated catalytic activity and protein properties of urease crystals. These are historical reconstructions.

The four peroxide tubes compare decomposition rates with matched starting substrate. The textbook uses 2 mL of 3% peroxide, a heated group near 90 °C, and equal drop counts of iron chloride or liver homogenate. Catalysts alter rates, not the theoretical oxygen yield from fixed substrate. Bubbles are not precipitate.

<h2 id="try">Change one condition at a time</h2>

- Move through time and compare early and late oxygen production.
- Compare reducing products after the same amylase acts on starch or sucrose.
- Use iodine for the temperature comparison and oxygen release for the catalase pH comparison.
- In recovery mode, every assay remains at 25 °C while the middle pretreatment changes.

Required 1, Chapter 5, Section 1, pp. 76–83. The p. 81 amylase material is incubated near 60 °C before the Fehling assay. That does not establish a universal enzyme optimum.

<h2 id="deeper">Distinguish the model from measurement</h2>

<details>
<summary>Where do the colors and percentages come from?</summary>

A first-order peroxide model respects 2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂. Rate constants are illustrative, not textbook measurements. Temperature acceleration, heat damage and pH are separate factors; heat damage retains pretreatment history. Extreme pH is not claimed to be universally reversible.

Iodine color represents starch in equal-time samples, not continuous live imaging. Real work requires matched assay conditions, blanks and repeats. The protein assay is a conceptual reconstruction rather than every analytical step performed by Sumner. Some RNA molecules also catalyze reactions.
