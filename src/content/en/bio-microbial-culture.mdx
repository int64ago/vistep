<h2 id="understand">Understand it</h2>

Culture results depend on the sample, its distribution and growth conditions. The first yeast streak receives inoculum from a culture tube. For each later region, the treated and cooled loop contacts the preceding streak before carrying fewer organisms onward. The model makes five regions progressively sparser without reconnecting the last to the initial dense region. Growing colonies keep their deposited positions.

PEP Biology Elective 3 pages12–13 cover medium preparation, sterilization, pouring, streaking, incubation and an uninoculated control. The animation explains the process; real work follows the school laboratory’s procedures. It is not a substitute for supervised culture of unknown samples. Growth on an uninoculated plate calls for investigating medium, equipment or handling contamination. Isolated colonies support purification, but similar appearance does not establish organism identity, and a cell cluster may initiate one colony.

Page15 describes high-throughput screening: culture many candidates in parallel, screen with a defined metric, then retest. A compact24-well teaching layout demonstrates how location preserves identity; it does not claim actual high-throughput work is limited to24 candidates. B3 leads initially but falls back in three repeats, while A4 retains higher output. All readings are synthetic examples, not evidence for a real high-yield strain.

Pages18–19 use urea as the sole nitrogen source for preliminary selection of soil organisms. General-nitrogen, no-nitrogen and uninoculated controls answer different questions. Growth under selected conditions does not establish species identity. Nutrient carryover, cross-feeding and contamination can complicate interpretation; further biochemical or other appropriate identification evidence is needed.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

- Scrub streaking through treatment, cooling, pickup and movement into a new region. Without treatment between streaks, subsequent deposition does not automatically become sparse.
- Compare an uninoculated control and then introduce simulated contamination to reconsider the interpretation.
- Rank the microplate by total product or product per biomass. These use different denominators. Preserve well identity and raw readings before inspecting independent retests.
- Check source, pipette and receiver during dilution. Own replicate counts start blank; enter three records while retaining dilution, the0.1 mL plated volume and culture conditions.
- Select air, water, milk or soil in the investigation view. Compare active air sampling with settling; units and conversion conditions change with the method.

Page20 lists four optional site investigations: airborne microbes, water bacteria, milk bacteria, and soil fungi or actinomycetes. Record your own source, date, method, conditions and findings; the animation does not complete the investigation.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

**Streaking model.** The five regions use64,24,9,4 and2 deposited origins to illustrate decreasing density, not measured loop-transfer counts. Later paths begin at the previous endpoint, with cooling before pickup. The first streak instead comes from the culture tube at bottom right. Every deposited point stays within agar. Cell and colony views use different scales; changing circle radius is not a real magnification measurement.

**CFU accounting.** The textbook’s nominal dilution chain starts with10 g soil plus90 mL diluent as the first1:10 suspension, followed by successive tenfold steps. “g/mL” denotes original soil equivalent rather than a precision claim about solid and liquid volume addition. At10⁻⁵ g/mL and0.1 mL plated, synthetic replicate counts96,103 and101 average100. Thus100/(0.1×10⁻⁵)=10⁸ CFU/g. The initial suspension is already included in the exponent and must not be omitted or counted twice.

This textbook exercise uses30–300 colonies and at least three replicates at one dilution. Actual methods may specify different ranges, weighting and reporting rules. Blank inputs are not treated as zero; out-of-range counts prompt review. A CFU may originate from one cell or an attached cluster, while organisms unable to form colonies under the selected conditions remain uncounted.

**Screening.** Well identities, readings, biomass and repeat measurements remain fixed. Total output and output/biomass answer different questions. Well colour retains total output; the control changes ranking only. Miniature culture requires attention to volume, evaporation, edge effects, oxygen, blanks and instrument response. Confirmation in a plate does not establish large-tank performance.

**Sampling units.** An illustrative active sample of100 L yielding12 CFU gives120 CFU/m³ when volume and recovery assumptions are known; real samplers may require corrections. Settling does not measure an air volume, so retain plate area and exposure time instead. Water and milk report relative to original liquid volume, while soil uses sample mass with an explicit dry- or wet-mass basis. Culture counts do not identify pathogens or establish overall quality.
