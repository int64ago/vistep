<h2 id="understand">Identify the question each kind of evidence answers</h2>

Muller’s irradiation research detected changes through descendants. The textbook reports increased mutation detection without a complete dose or frequency dataset, so none is invented. The recessive-lethal X family extends the detection logic; it is not the complete historical strain and screening protocol. Treatment does not guarantee a particular mutation.

The P84 textbook case-control example covers 4 hospitals in 2006–2013: 1,303 cases and 1,303 healthy controls, including 823 and 509 smokers. The 63.16% and 39.06% figures are smoking proportions within sampled groups, not cancer risks among smokers. The crude odds ratio is about 2.67, not directly a risk ratio. This table supports association; causal inference also considers confounding, bias, temporal order and other evidence. Extensive evidence establishes that smoking causes cancer; the limitations of this one table do not negate that conclusion.

<h2 id="deeper">Counting rules and boundaries</h2>

<details>
<summary>Why DNA replication alone does not double ploidy</summary>

A replicated chromosome has two sister chromatids but counts as one while they share a centromere. The simplified mechanism starts with 2n = 4 replicated chromosomes. Normal segregation produces two nuclei with 4 each; the disrupted example separates sisters without completed poleward segregation or cytokinesis, producing a restitution nucleus with 8. It illustrates one route, not the inevitable outcome for every treated cell. The actual-material counting panel uses normal 16 and doubled 32 in subsequent clear division figures. These are diagrams, not micrographs or an estimate of treatment success.

The textbook sequence is 4 °C pretreatment for one week; rooting near 25 °C to about 1 cm; induction at 4 °C for 48–72 h; then fixation, dissociation, rinsing, staining and mounting. Process icons orient the learner; actual practical work follows the complete classroom protocol and supervision.

The separate set model uses 3 chromosomes per set to distinguish individual changes from complete sets. Haploidy also depends on gamete origin: a tetraploid’s gamete-derived individual can contain two sets. Structural letters denote large segments, not single bases or necessarily individual genes. Inversion arrows rotate with fragments; translocation colors preserve nonhomologous origins. Paths explain structural relationships without simulating every break, repair or selection step or assigning a universal beneficial/harmful effect.
