<h2 id="understand">Observe before choosing an invariant</h2>

Equal hanging balls in an ideal elastic collision exchange velocity: the incident ball stops while the other rises to the original height. A heavier incident ball of the same size can send the lighter ball higher, so velocity sums are not generally conserved. This follows PEP Elective Book1 page2.

Pages3–4 give three measured sticking-cart trials. Original masses and speeds are preserved. The first trial changes total momentum from0.325932 to0.318666 kg·m/s, a residual of about−2.23%, while kinetic energy falls by roughly half. This supports approximate momentum conservation without erasing measurement or external-force effects.

<h2 id="try">Trace each actual collision path</h2>

Air-track modes use elastic frames, a pin and putty, or a compressed spring released between two gliders. At natural length the spring detaches and drops onto the track; it does not keep stretching between separating gliders. Air cushioning reduces friction, and leveling is needed to make horizontal external impulse negligible.

Photogates record the full transit of a20 mm flag. Width divided by transit time gives speed; visible motion supplies its sign. A rebounding left glider must have negative velocity. The paper-tape extension uses stable regions before and after collision, taking every fifth50 Hz dot for0.10 s intervals and avoiding the collision-spanning region.

Finally, as on page21, audit momentum and kinetic energy separately. Ideal elastic collisions conserve both. Sticking conserves system momentum while some kinetic energy becomes deformation and other internal energy. Spring separation draws kinetic energy from stored elastic potential energy.

<h2 id="deeper">Time boundaries and data provenance</h2>

<details>
<summary>Ideal models versus printed measurements</summary>

The track uses signed momentum and restitution1 or0. Collision is instantaneous, without an invented force waveform; external impulse during impact is omitted. Rendered attachment contact planes agree with model contact positions.

Hanging balls follow the nonlinear pendulum equation, with one-dimensional elastic impact at the bottom. Length0.5 m and initial angle0.5 rad are teaching parameters. Equal-size balls may have different density; rendered radius does not calibrate material density. String forces have horizontal components during swinging, so horizontal momentum is compared only immediately around impact.

Spring separation uses0.4 and0.8 kg gliders, stiffness4 N/m and12 cm compression. Reduced-mass motion conserves elastic plus kinetic energy. Gliders coast after natural-length detachment. Paper dots and photogate timings come from the same trajectories.

Printed data are from page3, air-track and tape methods from pages18–20, and energy comparisons from page21. Nonzero residuals in the three printed trials remain distinct from the ideal models; neither is misrepresented as measured validation of the other.
