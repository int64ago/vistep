<h2 id="understand">How can a field change motion without contact?</h2>

A disturbed meter pointer oscillates. Shorting its terminals makes it settle sooner, without adding a power supply: the original mechanical disturbance supplied the energy. A disc slows near magnetic poles, and a magnet falls slowly inside an intact aluminium tube. Both suggest the same account: motion supplies energy, and induced currents turn some of it into heat.

Follow one circuit. Having magnetic flux is not sufficient for sustained induction; the flux must change with time. A closed conducting path is also required for the emf to drive current in this resistive approximation. An open circuit can still have induced emf without the additional electromagnetic damping.

<div className="formula">ε = −dΦ/dt, I = ε/R</div>

The disc close-up follows two field edges on the same moving surface. Flux increases at entry and decreases at exit. Opposite local currents together produce braking torque. Reversing the field also reverses the currents, so braking does not become acceleration. As the disc slows, flux changes more slowly, reducing current and braking torque.

<h2 id="try">Predict a changed condition</h2>

- **Reverse only the disc's field.** Predict the torque direction, then inspect the current and torque signs.
- **Cut a longitudinal slit in the pipe.** Complete circulating paths break, so the magnet falls faster. Local return paths can remain beside the slit; not every eddy disappears.
- **Hold the magnet inside an intact pipe.** External support balances gravity. Flux remains but stops changing. After neglected transients, emf, sustained current and Joule-heating power are zero.
- **Open only the spring apparatus's coil.** Keep the spring, magnet and initial displacement fixed, then compare additional damping with background losses.

The aluminium frame raises a different question: how can a rotating magnet drive it without contact? Track both angles. Their relative rotation changes flux and produces current and torque. Driving the magnet continually requires external power, divided between mechanical transfer to the frame and slip-related heating. Exact synchronism is an instantaneous counterfactual: no slip means no sustained induced drive. It does not imply permanent synchronous rotation against friction.

<h2 id="deeper">One calculation links current, force and energy</h2>

Use position or angle q as a coordinate, and write the local flux gradient as K = dΦ/dq. The same coupling calculates emf, current and generalized force; the opposing arrow is not assigned independently.

<div className="formula">ε = −Kq̇, I = ε/R, F = KI, Fq̇ = −I²R</div>

For rotation, F represents torque. Two local equivalent disc loops reproduce the disc's low-speed braking coefficient; they are not a solved eddy-current field mesh. The meter uses an effective linear coupling for a moving coil in a radial field; the spring coil is linearized over a limited displacement range. The frame's single-loop flux is instantaneous, whereas its motion and power use cycle averages. They must not be read as the same instantaneous torque.

The intact pipe uses a point-dipole approximation inside a long, thin conducting tube. Drawn rings are fixed samples; A identifies the same ring as the adjacent current readout. These rings are not the integration mesh. The magnet symbol is enlarged for visibility, and its drawn dimensions are not numerical inputs. Integrating ring resistance and flux gradients gives the effective overall drag. Smaller effective damping represents residual local eddies in the slit pipe. Energy lost when the magnet reaches the catch is recorded separately from electrical heating.
