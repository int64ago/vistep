## One beam measures one direction

Pulsed lidar measures emission-to-reception delay. Range is half light speed times that round-trip time. Combining range with the known emission angle locates a point. This film presents a horizontal room slice; walls, a partition and a cylinder all use the same analytic ray intersections.

A scan builds a point cloud, with each point on the first visible surface. Occluded walls receive no invented points. Angular spacing, nanosecond timing error and a detection threshold separately expose sparse sampling, uncertain position and missing returns.

## Select a ray and find its first hit

Change beam angle to inspect the nearest cylinder, partition or wall intersection. Smaller angle steps produce more points. Timing deviation spreads points along their original rays; a higher normalized threshold removes weak returns. A fixed seed retains measurement identities across seeking and comparisons.

## Time, direction and coordinates

<div className="formula">
  r = cΔt / 2<br />x = r cos θ<br />y = r sin θ<br />
  σr = cσt / 2
</div>

Here c=299792458 m/s. A 1 ns round-trip timing standard deviation corresponds to roughly 0.15 m range deviation. Analytic line-segment and circle intersections select the nearest positive hit. The phone composition rotates the world projection without altering physical coordinates.

For a clear missing-return comparison, normalized teaching strength is reflectivity × incidence cosine / range² and is compared against a threshold. This is not a complete lidar equation, calibrated reflectivity or probabilistic detector. Seeded independent radial noise excludes systematic bias.

The model is a two-dimensional first return from opaque surfaces. It omits beam footprint, mechanical origin offsets, 3D channels, glass, multiple reflection, fog, motion distortion and mapping. An empty part of a point cloud does not prove empty space.
