<h2 id="understand">No instrument filmed the nucleus. What did it reveal?</h2>

A scintillation screen registers arrivals. Cloud chambers and emulsions record charged tracks. Chemical separation identifies products. A reaction model must connect these different observations while respecting momentum, charge and nucleon accounting.

Rutherford first found long-range particles after nitrogen bombardment. Blackett's later cloud-chamber work added a discriminating observation: eight anomalous forks each had two outgoing tracks. An alpha particle leaving unchanged would require a third outgoing track. The observed topology supported alpha capture with an oxygen-17 nucleus and a proton emerging. [Blackett's lecture, printed pages 100–103](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/blackett-lecture.pdf)

<h2 id="try">Ask the model to predict something</h2>

- Change proton emission angle. Oxygen momentum must change with it; the products cannot move independently.
- Compare a candidate mass with fixed hydrogen and nitrogen recoil records, then predict a helium target. Controls never rewrite the records.
- Leave one fission product unknown. The ledgers 236=144+A+3 and 92=56+Z require A=89 and Z=36: krypton-89.
- Move one fine track to another depth. Its projection still crosses the others, but focusing distinguishes the origins.
- Follow the same three neutron IDs while changing abstract loss fractions. Predict generation averages from their possible outcomes.

<summary>Two recoil sources, not one interchangeable data table</summary>

The PEP elective 1 exercise on page 24 supplies 3.3×10⁷ m/s for hydrogen and 4.4×10⁶ m/s for nitrogen. For elastic head-on impacts from the same incident speed limit, with projectile mass m in hydrogen-mass units:

<div className="formula">U_H/U_N = (m+14)/(m+1)</div>

Those rounded textbook inputs yield m=1. Chadwick's 1935 lecture instead reports approximate range-derived speeds of 3.7×10⁷ and 4.7×10⁶ m/s, giving about 0.89. This supported a mass near the proton's; it was not a modern precision neutron-mass measurement. <details>
<summary>How did the product become identifiable as barium?</summary>

With barium salts as carrier, the artificial product could not be enriched away from barium. Known natural radium tracers at similarly weak activity did enrich in early fractions. Mixed-tracer tests also distinguished the two. Daughter-product tests and a barium chemical cycle supplied further checks.

Our marks encode published qualitative outcomes, not invented fraction counts or separation yields. Not all indicator tests were complete at the first report of 6 January 1939; this view summarizes subsequent evidence. [Hahn's lecture, printed pages 58–59](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/hahn-lecture.pdf)

<summary>Teaching kinematics, tracks and generation averages</summary>

Nitrogen kinematics uses a 7.7 MeV alpha, Q=−1.191 MeV and integer masses in u. Momentum arrows share one scale. Energy includes Q; precise nuclear masses and excited states are omitted. Nuclear bodies do not locate individual nucleons or represent microscopic dimensions.

The fission ledger follows the textbook's 144Ba+89Kr+3n example, not a unique yield. Multi-fragment tracks are constructed examples, not photographs, calibrated ranges or occurrence probabilities. A closed momentum polygon alone cannot identify nuclides. [Original 1947 paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/159773a0)

The chain model assigns abstract escape, non-fission absorption and subsequent-fission fractions. Under the teaching assumption of three neutrons per fission, k=3(1−escape−absorption) and the expectation is N_g=32k^g. Three selected paths are illustrative, not a random historical sample. A fixed log₁₀(1+N) vertical scale accommodates every control setting. No material dimensions, control-rod positions, delayed-neutron kinetics or reactor engineering are calculated. [Official archive of the 1942 notebook](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/manhattan-project-notebook)
