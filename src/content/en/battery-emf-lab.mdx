<h2 id="understand">Terminal voltage changes with load</h2>

Within a specified operating range, a source can be represented by EMF E and internal resistance r. More current increases internal drop Ir, reducing terminal voltage U = E − Ir. Adding parallel lamps to one battery can therefore reduce the voltage across each lamp. An ideal zero-resistance source is the counterexample: this dimming would not occur.

The dry-cell circuit measures U and I directly. The pear cell uses a voltmeter and known resistance box to infer I = U/R. The orange-juice cell instead uses a microammeter and box, calculating box voltage U = IR. That last fit includes the meter’s 100 Ω resistance in the slope; subtract it to infer the cell alone.

<h2 id="try">Follow the distinct apparatus</h2>

- Parallel lamps: compare zero and nonzero source resistance.
- Dry cell: move the rheostat contact, collect several points and magnify voltage changes. A truncated axis still requires the actual coordinates when calculating slope.
- Fruit cells: change electrode immersion and spacing to observe a qualitative output trend. Pear and orange juice retain different voltmeter and microammeter connections.
- Power bank: compare measured load ranges, charge states and current limiting. A regulated output behaves differently from a bare lithium cell.

Power-bank gold points at 100%, 80% and 60% charge are transcribed from textbook pages 132–133. Optional curves at 40%, 20% and 5% are reconstructed from its parameter tables and labeled accordingly. The six-state comparison uses textbook table 3. These findings describe this example over its studied range, not every bank at every load.

<h2 id="deeper">Models, loading and evidence limits</h2>

<details>
<summary>Which readings are modeled and which are textbook data?</summary>

The dry-cell model has E = 1.5 V and r = 1 Ω, with a 10 kΩ voltmeter and 0.1 Ω ammeter. Finite voltmeter loading slightly reduces the fitted intercept and slope when load current is used: both are divided by 1 + r/Rᵥ. Lamps are fixed 6 Ω resistances; brightness represents relative electrical power.

The pear-cell value E = 0.95 V is illustrative. Spacing divided by immersion represents only a resistance trend; electrochemistry, polarization and time drift are omitted. Its voltmeter is 10 MΩ. Orange-cell gold points come from textbook page 92; green points use approximate E = 0.95 V and r = 1200 Ω. The textbook fit must still have the 100 Ω microammeter contribution removed.

A power-bank output line is an operating-range equivalent, not a direct measurement of chemical EMF. The separate 2 A current-limit demonstration is a model counterexample, not a specification of the textbook device or extra measured data. Displayed external resistance includes the protection resistor.
