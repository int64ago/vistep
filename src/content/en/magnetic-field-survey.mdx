<h2 id="understand">An alignment axis is not a field direction</h2>

Oersted’s arrangement places a north–south wire above a compass. With current, the needle follows the combined wire and Earth fields; without it, the needle returns north–south. Reversing current changes the deflection. The effect is not contact or airflow from the wire.

Magnetized filings align with local field axes, revealing the overall pattern without identifying which direction along each axis is north. A compass north pole provides that directional information. Reversing a straight-wire current reverses circulation while preserving filing axes.

<h2 id="try">Compare five sources using the same probes</h2>

Inspect bar and horseshoe magnets, a straight wire, one current loop and a solenoid. Move the compass and compare it with nearby filings. Change current to contrast an oriented compass with unoriented alignment.

The straight wire passes perpendicularly through the observation plane. Loop and solenoid views instead contain the source axis; dots indicate current out of that section, crosses into it. The solenoid is drawn as a continuous helix while seven circular turns approximate its field, omitting pitch and external supply-wire contributions.

Oersted mode varies current and wire height, with height shown in a small side view. Zero current opens the switch; reversed current reverses source polarity. Textbook coverage is required volume 3, pages 105–107; the horseshoe is an additional comparison source.

<h2 id="deeper">Calculate the response from its source</h2>

<details>
<summary>Which physical relationships are retained?</summary>

The long straight wire uses B = μ₀I/(2πr). Oersted mode adds a 25 µT horizontal Earth field and uses compass equilibrium, without mechanical damped oscillation. Pattern views omit Earth’s field to separate source patterns from the background.

Circular loops use midpoint Biot–Savart integration, checked on the axis against μ₀IR²/[2(R²+x²)³ᐟ²]. Seven coaxial loops approximate the finite solenoid. Permanent magnets use dipoles distributed along magnetization to illustrate the overall external field, not calibrate real strength. No filings or probes are placed inside magnetic material.

Filings use a damped unoriented alignment model, so field reversal preserves axes; a compass has north and south poles. At zero source field, its needle fades and the remaining appearance does not establish direction. A finite core regularizes the immediate wire region; source singularities are not probed.

</details>
