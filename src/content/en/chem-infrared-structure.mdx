<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

The two instrumental-evidence positions come from PEP Selective Compulsory 2 pages 41–42 and Selective Compulsory 3 pages 18–19. The first relates infrared measurement to chemical bonds; the second combines O–H, C–H and C–O evidence with C₂H₆O to support ethanol rather than dimethyl ether. These are measurement and reasoning models, not acquired data.

Transmittance is T=I/I₀; absorbance is A=−log₁₀(T). The displayed field amplitude scales as √T and attenuates continuously through the cell. Absorption therefore appears downward on a transmittance plot. Wavenumber decreases from 4000 cm⁻¹ to 500 cm⁻¹. The optical path represents conceptual transmission measurement, not a particular FTIR interferometer or reconstruction algorithm.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

The oscillator uses reduced mass μ=m₁m₂/(m₁+m₂) and wavenumber ν̃=(1/2πc)√(k/μ). O–H and O–D share an illustrative 700 N/m force constant; ideal N₂ uses 2294 N/m. Mass-weighted displacement preserves the center of mass. Isotope mass changes the computed frequency instead of directly prescribing it. This is an approximate model rather than a complete quantum calculation.

Frequency matching is insufficient for infrared activity: vibration must change the dipole. Ideal isolated N₂ symmetric stretching is the counterexample. It can still vibrate; the model only removes infrared dipole coupling. A small background displacement represents unspecified thermal/initial motion independent of the driving light. Collision-induced absorption and other weak real-gas effects are omitted.

Both synthetic organic spectra contain C–H and C–O bands, but only pure ethanol has an intrinsic O–H band. Relative concentration times path length changes absorbance and transmittance without changing functional groups. An independent water-background absorbance is added to both curves to illustrate a false positive. This is not a claim that arbitrary real mixed samples preserve phase, hydrogen bonding or linearly additive band shapes.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

Two separate models serve different purposes. A damped diatomic oscillator explains reduced mass, resonance and dipole selection. Synthetic Gaussian bands teach spectral reading: ethanol O–H near 3350 cm⁻¹ and C–O near 1050 cm⁻¹; the ether model has its C–O region near 1100 cm⁻¹. Positions, widths and strengths are illustrative assumptions, not fits to raw spectra. They are not forced to equal the isolated O–H oscillator frequency.

Hydrogen bonding, phase, temperature and coupled molecular motion affect actual spectra, whose fingerprint region is much richer. Time is slowed greatly, displacement is magnified, and planar structures locate functional groups. The abbreviated ethanol O–H motion is not a calculation of all normal modes.

An O–H band alone does not uniquely identify ethanol. The inference here depends on the finite formula-based candidates, purity, background correction and complementary evidence. The textbook next uses proton NMR to check hydrogen environments; complex unknowns require additional methods.
