<section id="understand">
## Do not collapse two pictures into one

For sodium and chlorine, electron loss and gain explain the rise and fall of oxidation states. Electrons lost equal electrons gained, so total charge is conserved. The displayed local ion pair is only part of the extended ionic lattice.

In a covalent HCl molecule, the bonding pair is shared but favours chlorine. Formal oxidation states of +1 and −1 do not turn gaseous HCl into fully separated H⁺ and Cl⁻ ions.

</section>
<section id="try">
## Track the electron, then compare the accounting

Follow one sodium valence electron and pause at its final allocation. Switch to hydrogen–chlorine bonding and observe the shared pair with a chlorine-biased cloud. Progress represents the explanation, not a classical trajectory or measured reaction time.

Dots represent electron counting, the envelope represents polarity, and oxidation states represent a formal allocation rule. These three representations must not be treated as interchangeable measurements.

</section>

<summary>Formal states and actual electron distribution</summary>

IUPAC bases oxidation states on an ionic approximation to heteronuclear bonds, while homonuclear bonds are shared equally. This useful accounting convention is not generally the exact measured charge on an atom in a molecule. Partial-charge values additionally depend on definitions and calculation methods.

The diagram preserves eight tracked valence electrons and total charge. It does not solve a wavefunction. The cloud is an illustrative polarity envelope, with no numerical partial charge.
