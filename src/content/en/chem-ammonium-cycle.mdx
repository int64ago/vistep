<section id="understand">

Experiment5-7 compares NH₄Cl, NH₄NO₃ and (NH₄)₂SO₄ with aqueous NaOH and warming, testing gas at the mouth with damp red litmus. The shared net relation is NH₄⁺+OH⁻→NH₃+H₂O. Warming helps ammonia escape; strong base can still react with ammonium at room temperature.

Trace the cause of the colour. Ammonia gives an alkaline aqueous layer on damp paper. Direct alkali-liquid contact also turns paper blue but is not independent evidence that ammonia was evolved. Ideally dry paper cannot be treated as equivalent to damp paper.

</section>
<section id="try">

The test separates reaction extent from the fraction of formed ammonia released. Change OH⁻ amount to explore the limiting reactant. One ammonium-sulfate formula unit supplies two ammonium ions; the other salts supply one. The textbook does not prescribe equal molar quantities in all tubes, so model units are not experimental reagent amounts.

Collection begins with air in the vessel and shows ammonia introduced high replacing the original gas. Paper at the mouth provides qualitative evidence, not a purity measurement. The separate absorption discussion compares shallow and deeper funnel immersion to see whether an air gap appears before water reaches the narrow stem.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Reaction inventory and collection boundaries</summary>

The model separately tracks ammonium amount, residual OH⁻, gaseous ammonia and formed ammonia retained in solution. Nitrogen in nitrate remains in the spectator anion, outside the ammonium-derived nitrogen inventory. Preparation uses 2NH₄Cl+Ca(OH)₂→CaCl₂+2NH₃+2H₂O, checking chlorine, calcium and ammonium-derived nitrogen.

Twenty vertical cells use a one-dimensional upwind transport scheme, with the delivery tube feeding the closed upper end and displaced gas leaving below. Introduced gas equals retained plus escaped gas, and local ammonia plus air fractions sum to one. Numerical diffusion represents limited mixing for teaching, not measured density layering, turbulence or purity curves. Marker colours do not represent real gas colours.

Figure5-13 is a simple apparatus without a separate drying tube, so the film does not claim dry ammonia is collected. The generating tube slopes toward its mouth and a continuous delivery tube leads to the inverted collector. Cotton is drawn loosely rather than as a sealed plug; it does not imply complete tail-gas treatment.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Filling checks and absorption limits</summary>

The textbook asks how to check filling. The film preserves that question and its limitation: paper at the mouth indicates ammonia locally. A [more recent RSC explanation](https://edu.rsc.org/states-of-matter/demonstrate-changes-of-state-using-volume-differences/4017698.article) cites updated guidance that a damp indicator at the mouth does not effectively establish complete flask filling. The colour response is not a 100% purity detector.

The funnel is an additional response to the textbook’s excess-ammonia absorption question, not a claim that it appears in Figure5-13. The geometric model compares cone capacity πr²h/3 with reservoir cross-section times rim immersion depth. If the latter is smaller, an air gap can appear before water fills the cone. A teaching reference uses radius25mm, height30mm and reservoir area100cm². At 0.5mm immersion, 5mL uptake exposes the rim before the approximately19.6mL cone fills. At5mm immersion that order changes. These values construct a geometric counterexample, not recommended apparatus dimensions.

Actual absorption also depends on pressure, airflow, mass transfer and transient liquid motion. Those processes and removal efficiency are not solved here. Both water levels follow cone volume and reservoir area rather than decorative position shifts.

</details>
