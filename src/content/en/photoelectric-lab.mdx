<h2 id="understand">Control intensity, frequency and voltage separately</h2>

The zinc electroscope makes electron loss visible. A photocell collects electrons into a measurable current. The zinc example uses a 4.3 eV work function, while the photocell uses 2.1 eV: they are different materials with different thresholds.

Above threshold, increasing frequency raises the maximum electron energy. At fixed frequency, brighter light mainly releases more electrons per second. A retarding voltage supplies an energy barrier; the stopping voltage identifies the maximum kinetic energy.

<h2 id="try">Make three controlled comparisons</h2>

- Increase intensity at fixed frequency and compare current with stopping voltage.
- Move below threshold and check whether brighter light produces current.
- Keep the material fixed and plot stopping voltage against frequency.
