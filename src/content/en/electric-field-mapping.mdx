<h2 id="understand">Fragment orientation makes an invisible pattern observable</h2>

The textbook places small hair fragments in castor oil and applies different electrode arrangements. Polarization torque gradually aligns each fragment with its local electric field. The demonstration keeps this observation first, then adds computed field lines.

A single positive charge produces a radial pattern. Opposite charges bend orientations from one electrode toward the other; like charges bend the pattern outward. Near the middle of parallel plates, fragments are approximately parallel, indicating a more uniform region.

A short fragment reveals an axis, not an arrow. Electric-field direction is defined by the force on a **positive test charge**. Added arrows are mathematical annotations: no continuous physical field lines exist in the oil.

<h2 id="try">Compare the pattern, direction and potential</h2>

- Hide the calculated lines and compare fragments for all four electrode arrangements.
- Reset the orientation process, then advance it. Local field strength and the damping model determine the alignment rate.
- Show field lines and tap the tray to place a virtual probe. Its field and potential readings come from the same calculation.
- Enable equipotentials. At the midpoint between opposite charges, potential can be zero while the field is nonzero. For identical like charges, the converse occurs.

This follows “Model electric field lines” on pages 15–16, chapter 9, section 3 of PEP Required Book 3. The virtual probe and equipotentials are additional computational aids, not measurements made by the textbook oil-tray experiment.

<h2 id="deeper">Rotation and potential reference</h2>

<details>
<summary>How do the fragments turn?</summary>

Each fragment is approximated as a rod that polarizes more easily along its length. An induced dipole experiences electric torque while viscous drag suppresses oscillation. An overdamped rotation equation reduces angular misalignment. Translation, chaining, collisions and interactions among fragments are omitted. A zero-field location is not assigned an artificial direction.

Point-charge potentials add as scalars and fields as vectors. Singular regions are masked. Parallel plates show only the central region, using an ideal uniform field and omitting fringing. Point-charge potential is referenced to infinity; the plate potential is referenced to the midpoint. Changing the reference changes neither potential differences nor the electric field.

</details>
