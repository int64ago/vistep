## Distribution, density and draining are separate questions

Liquid–liquid extraction uses different solute distributions in two immiscible phases. Layer separation then relocates those phases. The organic layer can be above or below water depending on density, and favourable organic distribution does not guarantee that no solute remains in water.

The same textbook passage introduces solid–liquid extraction: a solvent removes a component from a solid while insoluble or unextracted matrix remains. The film uses an unspecified schematic solute rather than inventing a named experiment absent from the source.

</section>

<summary>Model, conservation and limits</summary>

With D = corganic/cwater, a nonreacting, nonionizing solute and constant distribution ratio, the organic amount is M·DVo/(Vw + DVo); the rest remains aqueous. Chosen teaching values are 100 mL water, 50 mL organic solvent and D = 4, with total solute normalized to 1. These are not measurements of the textbook photograph or any named chemical. Transfer progress is an explanatory interpolation rather than kinetics.

For n fresh portions sharing the same total organic volume, the retained fraction is [Vw/(Vw + DVo/n)]ⁿ. Mutual solubility, emulsions, pH-dependent speciation and handling losses can change real outcomes. The equation does not apply to a single homogeneous solvent phase.

Funnel walls and liquid levels use the same revolved pear profile, calibrated to 220 mL capacity. Initial liquid occupies 150 mL, leaving 70 mL headspace. The sealed-head comparison balances isothermal Boyle pressure with hydrostatic pressure from both layers. Water density is 1000 kg/m³ and organic density is chosen as 800 or 1300 kg/m³. Gas leakage, solvent vapour, capillarity and stopcock resistance are omitted; flow rate is not predicted. Draining stops at the lower-layer boundary.

Solvent volume and solute are retained across both funnel layers and the receiver. Dots identify material schematically; exact values come from the model account. The solid view chooses a maximum extracted fraction of 75% with inert matrix unchanged, not a measured solubility or extraction yield.

</details>
