<h2 id="understand">Distinguish two kinds of separation</h2>

DNA replicates once before meiosis. Division I separates homologs while sisters remain associated. Division II separates sisters without another replication. The mechanism uses 2n = 4; each final spermatid receives two chromosomes. Cell outlines illustrate stages rather than simulate complete cell mechanics.

A tetrad contains two chromosomes and four chromatids. Corresponding segments exchange between non-sisters while retaining visible identity. The close-up explains segment exchange without modeling every recombination enzyme or repair step.

<h2 id="deeper">Separate model counts, real karyotypes and historical inference</h2>

<summary>Why is the fixed field explicitly schematic?</summary>

The immutable teaching field uses 2n = 4 to distinguish pairing, homolog segregation and sister segregation. It is not a micrograph, measured dataset or specific grasshopper karyotype. Textbook p. 29 uses 24 → 12 → 24 to illustrate reduction and restoration; this must not be generalized to every grasshopper species and sex. Real counts require an actual specimen and species information.

C denotes relative DNA content: the original cell goes from 2 C to 4 C during replication. The entire lineage remains at 4 C afterward, distributed into four final cells of 1 C each. Each daughter does not contain 4 C.

Fertilization starts a separate complement diagram: two colors distinguish egg and sperm sets, not female and male products of the preceding spermatocyte. It does not describe pronuclear-envelope behavior in every species. Sutton’s parallels supported a chromosome hypothesis; assigning a particular gene to a chromosome required further evidence.
