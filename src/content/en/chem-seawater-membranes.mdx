## Separate water and salt, and keep track of what remains

Distillation uses differences in volatility and collects condensed water vapour. Reverse osmosis uses pressure-driven selective transport and produces both permeate and concentrate. Salt remains in the material account. Seawater also supports salt production and recovery of magnesium, bromine and other resources.

A later textbook position compares microfiltration, ultrafiltration, nanofiltration and reverse osmosis. Selectivity determines what stays. The desired product may be the permeate or the retained stream.

</section>

<section id="try">

## Change pressure, then salinity

Increase salinity to inspect the osmotic-pressure estimate and water production. Hold salinity fixed and increase pressure. Extend the model duration to see the remaining liquid concentrate and subsequent production slow. The membrane-class view changes the selected membrane and water recovery to account for salt transferred into permeate.

The concentration view retains its solute markers as water is removed. Juice processing, dialysis and seawater reverse osmosis are not presented as the same process.

</section>

<section id="deeper">

<details>
<summary>Model and limitations</summary>

The osmotic estimate uses ideal NaCl-equivalent π = 2RTc, with R = 0.08314 L·bar·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ and T = 298 K. Real seawater is a nonideal multicomponent solution. Natural osmosis is an instantaneous direction diagram, without integration of the subsequent head difference.

The chosen teaching law is Jw = A(ΔP − π), with A = 1.2 L·m⁻²·h⁻¹·bar⁻¹. Only positive production is integrated in the RO batch account, using 1 m² and 100 L initial feed. Low-salinity permeate is approximated as having negligible osmotic pressure. Combining Js = B(Cf − Cp) = JwCp gives Cp = BCf/(Jw + B), with B = 0.12 L·m⁻²·h⁻¹. These are chosen teaching coefficients, not membrane specifications. Near the production threshold, the dilute-permeate approximation deteriorates; the salt concentration there is not a quantitative prediction. Concentration polarization, fouling, pressure losses, activity and energy recovery are omitted.

Remaining and permeated water and salt are accounted for separately. Markers identify species rather than molecular proportions. Distillation assumes nonvolatile salt and no entrainment or volatile impurities. Evaporated water is not counted as collected when cooling is absent.

Membrane-class passage fractions are illustrative conserved amounts, not a pore-size or rejection table. Nanofiltration selectivity depends on ion charge and other properties. A dense RO selective layer is not a rigid sieve. The concentration view ideally retains its chosen solute, without implying complete retention of all solutes by all membranes.

The textbook also names electrodialysis. Its ion-selective membranes and electrical drive are not conflated with reverse osmosis here. Resource arrows represent feed relationships, not a process sequence, yield or economic recovery claim.

</details>
