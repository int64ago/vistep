## Specify which quantity is reflected

Pressure and particle velocity are related differently for opposite travelling directions. For right-going plane waves p=Zu; for left-going waves p=−Zu, where Z=ρc is characteristic specific impedance. The model joins two lossless fluids at equal cross-section. This is not the frequency-dependent complex input impedance of an instrument mouth.

A higher second impedance gives upright reflected pressure; a lower one reverses its sign. Transmitted pressure amplitude can exceed one without exceeding incident energy. Pressure and velocity together determine the complete-pulse energy shares.

## Compare media with the same pulse

Change impedance ratio and switch between pressure and normalized particle velocity. Stop before, during or after the boundary encounter. The pale left-side pressure is the sum of incident and reflected components, rather than either component alone.

Set equal impedances and vary speed. Density changes correspondingly, maintaining the match and zero reflection. The energy bar represents final full-pulse fractions, not energy already accumulated inside the displayed field. Vertical curves represent physical profiles, not sideways motion of pipe walls.

## Two continuity conditions determine the result

<div className="formula">
  pIncident + pReflected = pTransmitted
  <br />
  (pIncident − pReflected)/Z1 = pTransmitted/Z2
  <br />
  rP = (Z2−Z1)/(Z2+Z1); tP = 2Z2/(Z2+Z1)
  <br />R = rP²; T = tP² Z1/Z2; R+T=1
</div>

The incident peak is 1 Pa and a smooth finite-width pulse has balanced positive and negative area. At a 4:1 impedance ratio, rP=0.6, tP=1.6, R=0.36 and T=0.64. Velocity is normalized by the first-medium incident reference to compare signs on a common fixed scale.

This is one normal-incidence interface between lossless, nondispersive fluids of equal cross-section. The first speed is 343 m/s and displayed time is slowed. Oblique mode conversion, finite wall thickness, absorption, complex pipe radiation and repeated echoes are omitted. No test tone is played.
