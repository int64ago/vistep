<h2 id="understand">Observe a signal, then test an explanation</h2>

Becquerel’s comparison removes sunlight; Curie combines chemical fractionation with ionization measurements; magnetic deflection tests charge and momentum; an expansion chamber makes droplets along ionization paths; X-rays produce transmission contrast. Each technique observes a different signal.

<h2 id="try">Change one condition and keep the comparison clear</h2>

Toggle the sample and sunlight, compare illustrative enrichment, reverse the magnetic field, expand the chamber or change example tube voltage. Historical experiments are teaching reconstructions without original measurement data.

<summary>Calculated quantities and illustrative signals</summary>

<div className="formula">r = p/(|q|B); T₂/T₁ = (V₁/V₂)^(γ−1); λmin = hc/(eU)</div>

Deflection uses 5 MeV alpha particles and 2 MeV beta-minus electrons with relativistic momentum, neglecting material energy losses. Gamma remains straight. Dots or crosses indicate field direction. Paths stop at the displayed boundary; colors are not visible radiation colors.

Plate darkness uses 1−exp(−At), with a relative blank background of 0.02. Enrichment and meter signals are illustrative rather than chemical yields or elemental analysis. The ionization apparatus is a functional electrical schematic.

The chamber starts at 293.15 K and 0.9 relative humidity. Adiabatic expansion uses γ=1.4 and the Magnus approximation to saturation vapor pressure. Droplets appear after supersaturation, without a nucleation or stopping-power calculation.

Illustrative X-ray attenuation is exp[−μ(20/E)³], with μ=1 or 4 and E in keV. These dimensionless coefficients do not specify tissue thickness. Tube voltage sets an energy ceiling; real bremsstrahlung has a continuous spectrum.
