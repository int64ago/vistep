<h2 id="understand">Transfer fluid and watch a different heart</h2>

In the textbook comparison, heart A retains the nerve being stimulated; heart B has no such connection. Stimulation slows A. Transferring some of A's fluid then slows B. This supports a transferable chemical signal, but one transfer experiment alone cannot identify its molecule. Subsequent research supplies additional evidence about identity.

The film keeps both liquid volumes and the marked signal consistent. Transfer does not create extra signal. B's own fluid dilutes the recipient concentration, and clearance reduces the signal over time. Each heart's relative frequency is driven by its local concentration. These values are teaching-model outputs, not historical measurements.

The secretin sequence compares acid in the intestine, acid directly in blood, a denervated intestine with intact circulation, and mucosal extract introduced into blood. Together, the controls support a chemical messenger originating in the intestine and carried by circulation. They do not exclude additional neural regulation of digestion.

<h2 id="try">Remove one link at a time</h2>

In the heart view, separately disable stimulation or fluid transfer. A can slow without B changing if no fluid moves; unstimulated fluid also lacks the released signal represented here. Changing transfer fraction updates the two liquid levels, recipient concentration and relative rate together.

In the secretin view, compare all four conditions while following the intestine, blood and pancreas routes. The collection vessel indicates an increase in secretion. Its level cannot be read as an original experimental volume.

<h2 id="deeper">Conserved compartments and an evidence model</h2>

Each heart compartment initially contains one normalized volume unit. Tubing reconstructs a traceable transfer route rather than reproducing historical apparatus. If qₒ has left A and qᵢ has reached B, VA = 1−qₒ, VB = 1+qᵢ and the transit volume is qₒ−qᵢ. A common exponential clearance leaves amount M, distributed as M(1−qₒ), Mqᵢ and M(qₒ−qᵢ). Their sum remains M. A declared transit time of 0.06 normalized units prevents B from responding before arrival. Fixed markers show the continuous route; the ledger supplies exact quantities. Concentration is amount divided by volume. The relative beat rate is:

<div className="formula">r = 1/(1 + 2C)</div>

This selected relation illustrates increasing inhibition with concentration; it is not fitted to an original frog-heart dose response. Integrating changing frequency gives continuous beat phase while fluid moves. Tests check total volume, transferred plus cleared amount, recipient dilution, absent stimulation, absent transfer and zero transfer fraction.

Secretin uses a declared intervention–outcome representation. Chemical availability, the blood route and a delay determine normalized secretion. It does not solve endocrine kinetics or demonstrate surgery or experimental animal procedures.
