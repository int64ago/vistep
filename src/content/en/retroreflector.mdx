## One face reverses one component

A flat mirror reverses the component of direction normal to its surface. Two perpendicular mirrors reverse two components, leaving the third unchanged for a general three-dimensional ray. After actual encounters with all three perpendicular faces, all components change sign and the outgoing ray is antiparallel to the incoming ray.

Antiparallel does not mean the same line. Moving the entry can leave a lateral separation between input and return. The gold incident ray, pale internal segments, blue outgoing ray and moving point all follow one three-dimensional trace. Numbered encounters correspond to the mirror sequence below.

## Change direction and entry separately

Turn the incident direction about z and follow the encounter points. Then keep the direction fixed and change entry x; another coordinate changes to preserve the triangular entrance plane. Three actual encounters produce zero ideal return error. The final automatic chapter supplies a counterexample: a finite edge ray leaves after only two reflections.

## Direction is conditional on the encounters

<div className="formula">
  Reflection at x=0: (dx, dy, dz) → (−dx, dy, dz)
  <br />
  After all three faces: dOut = −dIn
  <br />
  Lateral line separation = |(pOut − pIn) × dIn|
</div>

Direction vectors are normalized and separation is expressed in entrance units L. The ideal mirrors occupy the coordinate planes x=0, y=0 and z=0, truncated by x+y+z=L. Each step compares the next mirror intersection with the next entrance intersection and takes the earliest event. It never inserts a third bounce merely to force a return.

The view is a fixed orthographic projection. Screen angles are not three-dimensional angles. This is a hollow ideal-mirror model; it omits entrance refraction, total-internal-reflection limits, coating losses, dihedral errors, polarization and diffraction. Solid glass corner cubes can use total internal reflection, but their acceptance and phase response also depend on material and interfaces.
