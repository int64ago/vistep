<h2 id="understand">Voltage depends on both temperature endpoints</h2>

Two homogeneous alloys meet at the measuring junction; their other ends connect to a high-impedance voltmeter. K+ and K− label the type K wires. Copper leads are marked Cu. Both copper contacts sit in an isothermal block at Tc, while the measuring junction is at Th.

An isothermal circuit gives zero thermoelectric voltage even when its temperature is above zero Celsius. Heating the measuring junction changes voltage, but so does heating only the reference block. **One voltage reading cannot determine the measuring junction’s absolute temperature.**

<h2 id="try">Change the reference end and check compensation</h2>

- Hold the measuring junction at 400°C and warm the reference block from 25°C to 100°C. Raw voltage falls; correctly compensated temperature stays unchanged.
- Compare the assumed-zero reference reading with the compensated result. Compensation operates on voltage before the nonlinear conversion back to temperature.
- Add a reference-sensor error. The compensated answer shifts too, so the reference measurement matters.
- Insert metal M at the hot junction. Its net contribution is zero when both new contacts have the same temperature.
- Concentrate the thermal gradient near the hot end. For homogeneous wires, unchanged endpoint temperatures give unchanged integrated voltage.

<h2 id="deeper">Returning to the zero-Celsius reference</h2>

<details>
<summary>The type K reference and order of compensation</summary>

NIST's E_K(T) gives type K thermoelectric EMF relative to a 0°C reference. It is nonlinear; the positive-temperature reference uses a polynomial plus an exponential term.

<div className="formula">Vmeasured = E_K(Th) − E_K(Tc)</div>
<div className="formula">E_K(Th) = Vmeasured + E_K(Tc measured)</div>
<div className="formula">Th indicated = E_K⁻¹[Vmeasured + E_K(Tc measured)]</div>

The historical term “cold junction” means the reference end; it need not be below room temperature. Voltage does not originate exclusively at the weld. The difference between the two materials' contributions along temperature gradients determines the open-circuit reading. For homogeneous wires, its integral depends only on endpoint temperatures.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Connectors, temperature profiles and model boundaries</summary>

A third metal inserted at the hot junction contributes zero net EMF if both new contacts are at Th: its thermoelectric integral runs from Th back to Th. This requires equal contact temperatures and does not justify arbitrary extension wires across a temperature gradient.

The model uses the NIST ITS-90 reference and a high-impedance voltmeter with negligible current. Copper contact temperatures are equal. Temperature profiles are prescribed steady states, without heat-flow transients, contact thermal resistance, thermal inertia, aging or material inhomogeneity. Real readings also depend on reference-sensor errors, terminal gradients, interference and calibration.

</details>
