<h2 id="understand">Count, sequence, connectivity and conformation</h2>

A linear chain of n residues contains n−1 peptide bonds. Bovine insulin has two chains of 21 and 30 residues, giving 49 peptide bonds. Disulfides are distinct connections: A6–A11 is within one chain, while A7–B7 and A20–B19 join the chains.

Chinese researchers completed the synthesis of biologically active crystalline bovine insulin in 1965. Correct sequence and connections are necessary, but activity requires an independent test rather than a precipitate or recognizable shape. This conformation comes from a later structure determination, not footage of that synthesis.

<h2 id="try">Rotate and inspect the connections</h2>

- Toggle disulfides to distinguish them from the peptide backbone.
- Rotate the structure: a crossing in a projection need not be a chemical bond.
- Unfold the chains while tracking gold cysteine positions, then return to the measured conformation.

<summary>How are experimental coordinates used?</summary>

Static coordinates come from the A and B chain Cα atoms of RCSB PDB 2A3G, totaling 51 residues. Thick lines trace the backbone; gold lines indicate residue connectivity, not exact sulfur-atom positions. Rotation preserves three-dimensional distances. Unfolding uses geometric interpolation, not molecular dynamics or an activity calculation.

The condensation diagram represents net composition changes while omitting some hydrogen and group details. Ribosomal synthesis involves activation and energy coupling; the net-change drawing is not a procedure for spontaneous condensation of free amino acids.
