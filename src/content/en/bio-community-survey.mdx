<h2 id="understand">Describe a niche through several kinds of observation</h2>

The wetland view uses the four wintering-waterbird rows from textbook page 28. Bands represent salt marsh and mudflat, sedge marsh, and ponds with reedbeds, **not a scale map**. Percentages describe habitat occurrence rather than habitat area, food availability or directly measured preference. Observation totals of 2120, 1513, 1678 and 1517 remain in the model data; multiplying them by rounded occurrence percentages would not establish exact habitat counts.

Teal and spotted redshank have similar habitat occurrence patterns but different stomach contents. The original categories retain nutlets, stems, plant fragments, snails, shells and grit, crustaceans and other material. Greenshank’s three values of 33.3% and residual 0.1% are preserved. Grit is not nutritional food, and detected material does not automatically show active preference. Timing, other resources and interactions also contribute to a niche. The original shorebird study combined distribution surveys with benthic sampling rather than inferring habitat use from a spatial sketch alone. [Chongming Dongtan foraging-strategy research](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11284-006-0302-7)

Personal field notes start blank and retain place, time, habitat, behavior and interaction evidence. The automatic journal is explicitly a practice example. Nearby foraging suggests possible resource sharing; competition also requires considering resource limitation. Non-disturbing observations do not require reproducing stomach-content sampling.

<h2 id="try">Trace collected animals through the apparatus into records</h2>

A soil core with a 5 cm diameter and 5 cm depth has a cylindrical volume of about 98.2 mL, rounded to approximately 100 mL in the textbook. The removed core corresponds to the remaining hole. Comparisons require consistent sample size, depth, timing, extraction duration and replication, alongside environmental records and labels.

The extraction sample rests on a mesh with airflow space around it. Heat and drying encourage responsive animals to move through mesh openings, funnel and neck into a collector. Eighteen illustrative individuals retain their identities throughout. Different responses produce detection bias. Switching the lamp off stops this model’s driving input; **it does not assert that real animals never move in darkness**. [Harvard Arnold Arboretum fieldwork guide, Berlese-funnel section](https://arboretum.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Fieldwork-Techniques.pdf)

The aspirator intake enters the collection vial. A separate screened outlet connects to a hand pump. Blue markers represent air; the specimen remains inside the vial and does not pass through the screen into the pump. This reconstructs the apparatus principle, while appropriate collection methods still depend on organisms and site conditions.

The practice sample contains eight mites, six springtails and four unresolved specimens: eighteen individuals in three recorded groups. **Broad groups are not identified species**, so this must not be reported as three confirmed species. Retain unresolved labels and features; later identification may merge or split records. Personal counts remain blank until entered.

<h2 id="deeper">Succession changes conditions and dominance</h2>

Primary succession begins without developed soil. Secondary succession can retain soil, seeds or underground propagules. Both cases use the same model and process scale, with different initial conditions. Substrate formation, colonization, shading and feedback alter opportunities for later arrivals, while earlier groups may persist in different layers. [National Park Service explanation of starting conditions](https://www.nps.gov/kefj/learn/nature/plant-succession.htm)

Soil thickness, plant height and roots follow the same calculated state. Low moisture suitability prevents a tree layer in this model, avoiding an inevitable forest endpoint. Time is a compressed teaching progression, not a prediction of years at a real site. The plants represent functional layers rather than a universal sequence of named species. [Glacier Bay’s long-term succession research](https://home.nps.gov/glba/learn/nature/succession.htm)
