<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

Textbook experiment 2-1 compares benzene with bromine water and acidified permanganate; experiment 2-2 adds toluene to the comparisons. The central distinction is redistribution of a colored substance versus its chemical consumption.

Benzene and toluene are immiscible with water and form the upper layer. Under the uncatalyzed conditions represented here, without initiating side-chain halogenation, both can extract bromine. The aqueous layer becomes paler while the organic layer gains color. Total bromine is conserved; aqueous fading is not proof of addition.

The aromatic ring resists the textbook permanganate comparison, while toluene’s benzylic side chain can be oxidized. Exhaustive side-chain oxidation can produce benzoic acid with the ring retained. The displayed percentages belong to a conditioned teaching model, not a prediction that a room-temperature mixture reaches a given conversion in a few seconds.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

Change contact progress, model K or phase volume. The model defines K as organic concentration divided by aqueous concentration and solves the partition balance. Default K = 8 is illustrative. Both solvents share it for comparison; their real measured coefficients are not asserted equal.

Track an intact Br₂ marker through the interface. Its position is illustrative, not a concentration measurement from a few drawn particles. Layer heights include the rounded-bottom area; color follows a monotonic concentration–absorption model.

In the oxidation model, initial relative amounts are 1 for the arene, 0.6 for MnO₄⁻ and 3 for H⁺. Stoichiometry limits consumption. Benzene remains unchanged; toluene consumes oxidant. The extension counterexample, tert-butylbenzene, lacks benzylic hydrogens and does not undergo this modeled side-chain oxidation.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

An acidic net equation for exhaustive oxidation is:

5 C₆H₅CH₃ + 6 MnO₄⁻ + 18 H⁺ → 5 C₆H₅COOH + 6 Mn²⁺ + 14 H₂O.

Each benzylic carbon loses six electrons, matching the reduction of six permanganate ions by five organic molecules. Different acidity can produce different manganese species; brown MnO₂ is not inserted into this sufficiently acidic model.

<details>
<summary>Limits of the evidence</summary>

The partition model treats extractable molecular Br₂ and omits bromine-water hydrolysis and nonideal activities.

Color observations alone do not establish benzene’s complete structure. Equal carbon–carbon bonds and delocalization have additional structural support. Resistance under these conditions does not imply resistance to every reaction; catalyzed substitution and hydrogenation are separate topics.

Actual oxidation rates depend on conditions. Exhaustive side-chain oxidation commonly requires strong conditions, and the compressed teaching progress is not a kinetic experiment. A benzoic-acid endpoint diagram is a net-product explanation, not a claim that the color test alone identified the product. tert-Butylbenzene is labelled as an extension counterexample.
