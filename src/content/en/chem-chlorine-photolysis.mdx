<section id="understand">
## Follow the probes before interpreting their readings

After illumination, the textbook sample shows falling pH, rising chloride concentration and a rising oxygen fraction above the liquid. Two probes are in the liquid and one is in the gas. They measure different quantities. The film first preserves these observed directions, then enters a molecule to look for a connection. Its calculated examples do not impersonate measured textbook curves.

An absorbed ultraviolet photon can open an initial channel in hypochlorous acid: the oxygen–chlorine bond splits homolytically, with one selected bonding electron remaining on each fragment. Hydrogen stays joined to oxygen, producing a neutral hydroxyl radical, HO·, and a neutral chlorine radical, Cl·. That Cl· is not yet the chloride ion, Cl⁻, measured by the probe.

**This initial cleavage is not the complete route to oxygen.** The film explicitly changes to a net account that combines subsequent reactions. Two HOCl units are consumed; two H⁺ and two Cl⁻ are added to the liquid, and the two oxygen atoms form one O₂ molecule. The same six atom identities persist. Oxygen does not enter the headspace account before crossing the liquid surface.

</section>
<section id="try">
## Does a lower fraction mean less oxygen was made?

Two separate samples have equal liquid volumes and equal net HOCl decomposition. Only B has a larger initial headspace. Predict before revealing the readings: both make the same amount of O₂, but B starts with more gas, so its final oxygen fraction is smaller. Equal liquid volumes and equal H⁺ and Cl⁻ production leave pH and chloride concentration equal.

Explore the bond event and the net destinations, seeking backward and forward to follow the same atoms. Select an unabsorbed photon event and the oxygen–chlorine bond stays intact. The measurement view compares the illuminated net model with an ideal dark control and lets you change net decomposition extent. Its horizontal axis is normalized reaction amount, **not the textbook’s 0–150 second time axis**. Changing B’s headspace creates a separate sample; it does not stretch one sealed bottle.

<summary>Light, radicals and the net reaction: what is omitted?</summary>

The microscopic view selects one absorbed 254 nm ultraviolet photon that leads to dissociation. This is not the textbook light source’s full spectrum or a claim that all incident photons have the same dissociation probability. Ultraviolet is invisible; the grey-blue symbol is a tracking marker. Electron dots represent only the two selected O–Cl bonding electrons. The later net view does not expand the subsequent electron-transfer network. Slowed atom paths are teaching allocations, not measured molecular trajectories or reaction times.

[Watts and Linden’s original research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.watres.2007.03.032) examines aqueous chlorine photolysis and hydroxyl-radical production. [Yin and colleagues’ original research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.watres.2018.06.018) shows that wavelength and pH affect photolysis and subsequent reactive species. Their ultraviolet rates, yields and pH conditions have not been transferred to the textbook experiment.

[Equation 8 in the JACS Au paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10207101/) gives the illuminated net account HClO → ½O₂ + H⁺ + Cl⁻. The original equation image was checked for this revision. It is not a single elementary step, and atom conservation alone does not establish a mechanism. Numerical data from supplementary Figure S24 were not used.

<details>
<summary>Example conditions, conservation and limits</summary>

The example uses 100 mL of liquid, initially 0.018 mol/L HOCl and 0.008 mol/L each of H⁺ and Cl⁻. Initial gas is at 298.15 K and 1 atm, with an oxygen fraction of 18%. These are teaching assumptions, not reconstructed textbook measurements. Every mole of net HOCl decomposition adds one mole each of H⁺ and Cl⁻ and half a mole of O₂, preserving total chlorine and charge.

pH uses the concentration approximation −log₁₀[H⁺]; chloride mass concentration uses 35.45 g/mol. New O₂ enters both the numerator and denominator of the oxygen fraction. The endpoint assumes all generated oxygen has entered the headspace. Dissolution, activities, chlorine-water equilibria, competing reactions and mass transfer are omitted. H⁺ denotes hydrated acidic hydrogen; its halo is not a detailed hydration structure. The ideal dark control omits slow dark chemistry.

Textbook source: PEP Senior High Chemistry, Compulsory Book 1, printed pages 46–48 (PDF pages 51–53), especially the digital experiment and Figure 2-16 on page 48. The complete relevant text and the three plotted directions were checked.
