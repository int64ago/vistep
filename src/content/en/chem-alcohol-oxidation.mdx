One copper coil blackens and then regains its copper colour. A separate orange-to-green observation reveals a different oxidant changing oxidation state.

## Follow the copper through the cycle

The required textbook’s experiment7-5 alternates heating a copper coil in air with contact with ethanol. Copper oxide forms and is then reduced:

`2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO`

`CH₃CH₂OH + CuO → CH₃CHO + Cu + H₂O`

Adding the steps cancels the cycling copper species. The net reaction oxidises ethanol to ethanal using oxygen. One coil remains identifiable throughout the film; its colour, oxide fraction and accumulated products share the same conservation account. The returning copper colour supports oxide reduction, but alone is not a full identification of the organic product.

## The oxidising conditions matter

Elective3 experiment3-3 uses acidified potassium dichromate. The following textbook page describes ethanol becoming ethanal and then ethanoic acid. Orange-to-green change supports reduction of Cr(VI) to Cr(III); it does not by itself identify a pure organic product or establish complete conversion.

The model accounts for the endpoint in which ethanol reaches ethanoic acid:

`3C₂H₅OH + 2Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 3CH₃COOH + 4Cr³⁺ + 11H₂O`

## Pause on the same coil

Pause at the black surface, then advance to ethanol contact. Exploration provides four copper cycles and an option to remove the air oxygen supply, restricting this model’s Cu/CuO loop.

In the acid oxidation view, change the ethanol and dichromate model units independently. Advance to the endpoint and observe excess chromium(VI) or limited acid product. Initial hydrogen-ion units stay fixed at16, so adding more of the other two reactants cannot produce unlimited product. Exploration does not alter the film.

<details>
<summary>Model limits and textbook positions</summary>

One model unit of copper surface switches between Cu and CuO. Surface area, temperature gradients, transport, evaporation and competing reactions are omitted. Timing directs observation rather than predicting kinetics, and the product count is not a measured yield. Removing air disables this particular oxide-based oxygen cycle; it does not rule out every possible copper-catalysed transformation under oxygen-free conditions.

The atom view is net accounting, not a microscopic surface mechanism: ethanol retains its oxygen in the carbonyl while oxide oxygen is counted in water. The dichromate model uses limiting reagents and net electrons for an acid endpoint without explicitly modelling the accumulation of ethanal. The RGB colour blend is not a spectrum or calibrated concentration.
