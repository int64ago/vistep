<section id="understand">

Copper blackens, then its original colour returns. The coil appears to be back where it started, but the ethanol has changed. Following the same copper distinguishes what regenerates from what is consumed overall.

## Colour starts the explanation

Experiment7-5 on P78 of Compulsory Book2 alternates heating a copper coil in air with contact with ethanol. Loss of the black layer and returning copper colour support reduction of CuO. The textbook also includes an odour observation. Neither colour nor smell alone replaces identification of the organic product. Air supplies oxygen and the flame supplies heat; forming ethanal differs from burning ethanol completely to carbon dioxide.

Write the two material accounts together:

`Cu + ½O₂ → CuO`

`CH₃CH₂OH + CuO → CH₃CHO + Cu + H₂O`

Cancel copper and copper oxide from both sides. What remains is `CH₃CH₂OH + ½O₂ → CH₃CHO + H₂O`, the net catalytic oxidation discussed on P79. Copper regenerates; ethanol and oxygen are still consumed. Surface colour, oxide fraction and accumulated product follow one shared inventory in the scene.

## Balance electrons and atoms in the same reaction

The carbon bearing the hydroxyl group rises from−I in ethanol to+I in ethanal, supplying two electron equivalents. Copper falls from+II in CuO to0 and accepts two. Oxidation numbers are a formal assignment: the electron markers are an accounting device, not observed electron trajectories.

Now follow the atom correspondence. Ethanol’s original oxygen remains in the carbonyl. One hydroxyl hydrogen and one hydrogen on the adjacent carbon are accounted for in water with oxygen from CuO. The remaining carbon–hydrogen framework stays intact. Colour evidence alone does not directly measure these atom origins. This is a balanced endpoint mapping for the representative material route shown, not isotope-tracing data, a simultaneous bond-change mechanism or a claim about a unique surface pathway.

</section>

<section id="try">

## Predict what happens when air is cut off

Start with one CuO unit and excess ethanol, then supply no new oxygen. The displayed Cu/CuO oxygen route can still form one ethanal unit because the oxide already contains oxygen. Once copper returns, repeated contact cannot draw more oxygen from an exhausted inventory.

In exploration, change the initial oxide, fresh air or available ethanol, then test your prediction. Initial oxide oxygen plus incoming oxygen always equals oxygen left in the oxide plus oxygen accounted for in water. Total copper also remains fixed. This limitation does not rule out oxygen-free copper-catalysed alcohol dehydrogenation under other conditions.

</section>

<section id="deeper">

## A different colour and a further endpoint

Experiment3-3 on P62 of Selective Compulsory Book3 uses acidified dichromate. P63 continues with ethanol→ethanal→ethanoic acid. Chromium is reduced from+VI to+III, with a qualitative orange-to-green colour shift. Follow the organic carbon from−I to+I to+III: each stage supplies two electron equivalents, or four in total. One dichromate contains two chromium atoms and accepts six electrons overall. Three ethanol units therefore match two dichromate units.

For the assumed complete ethanoic-acid endpoint, acid and water must also balance:

`3C₂H₅OH + 2Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 3CH₃COOH + 4Cr³⁺ + 11H₂O`

Keep two dichromate units and sixteen H⁺ units fixed, and double ethanol from three to six. This model still produces at most three acid units. Too little ethanol leaves some Cr(VI) oxidant; insufficient acid also limits this net reaction. These endpoint amounts come from a specified conservation model. Colour cannot establish that a real mixture contains pure ethanoic acid at a particular instant.
