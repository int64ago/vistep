<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

This mechanism film follows pages 78–83 of PEP Selective Compulsory 2, Chapter 3, Section 2. Trace solid covalent bonds before interpreting dashed intermolecular connections. Iodine contains identifiable I₂ units and ice contains H₂O units. In ideal diamond and quartz, covalent connectivity extends through the crystal. Short boundary stubs indicate continuation beyond the selected view. See [OpenStax on bonding in solids](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/10-5-the-solid-state-of-matter).

The iodine comparison preserves nine molecular identities while moving the marked pair without changing its internal distance. Its dashed connections to neighboring units fade. **The arrangement is not an experimentally refined iodine crystal, and the path is not molecular dynamics.** The approximately 2.666 Å bond scale comes from the [NIST ground-state gas-phase molecular compilation](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?Mask=1000&Source=1973TEL849). It is not a precision solid-state bond measurement or a claim that molecules never dissociate under any conditions.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

Select a structure, rotate the view and pause while connections appear or separate. The five-water cluster reuses this site’s ice geometry: an ideal hexagonal oxygen framework with O···O separation 2.76 Å, O—H distance 0.96 Å and H—O—H angle 104.5°, with one ice-rule proton arrangement. The central molecule donates two hydrogen bonds and accepts two. Different ice phases are not represented.

Diamond uses an FCC lattice with a quarter-body-diagonal offset. Its nearest-neighbor distance is the textbook’s 154 pm; coordinates yield a tetrahedral angle of about 109.47°. The view contains three connected shells around one carbon. Incomplete coordination at the selected boundary is a viewing cut. Revealing surrounding bonds does not simulate a crystal being synthesized bond by bond.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

Quartz uses the 298 K parameter table in [Sutter and colleagues’ α-quartz study](https://journals.iucr.org/j/issues/2022/04/00/te5094/index.html): a=b=4.9137 Å, c=5.4047 Å and γ=120°. Prototype positions and symmetry operations produce 3 Si and 6 O per unit cell. Replicated cells supply Si—O neighbors around 1.6 Å. A finite connected neighborhood is displayed, so its boundary counts must not replace the complete-cell ratio. Interior Si has 4 O neighbors and bridging O has 2 Si neighbors: 4N(Si)=2N(O). No optical-rotation sign or experimental handedness identification is inferred here.

Cleavage selects a plane normal to (1,1,1), removes crossing bonds and then translates the two sides rigidly. Uncut bond lengths remain fixed and the displayed count comes from the same bond table. No stress, reconstruction, crack velocity or fracture energy is computed. This staged graph operation is not physical fracture timing. Hardness concerns scratching; fracture resistance is a different property. Diamond can cleave, as discussed by [GIA](https://www.gia.edu/gia-news-research/how-protect-diamond-chipping). High hardness does not imply immunity to breakage, and a melting temperature measured at one pressure is not universal.
