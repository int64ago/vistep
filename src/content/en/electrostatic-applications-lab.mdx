<h2 id="understand">Two distinct apparatus in one chapter</h2>

The shielding experiment brings a charged sphere near an electroscope, then adds a metal mesh. Free charges redistribute so that an ideal closed conducting shell cancels the applied field inside at electrostatic equilibrium. The external field remains; charge has not disappeared. An insulating mesh of the same shape does not provide the same free-charge redistribution.

Precipitation uses different apparatus: a bottomless bottle, negative serrated electrode, positive collector and smoke source. Stronger fields near tips support the discharge that charges particles. Negative particles feel force opposite the electric field and migrate to the positive collector.

<h2 id="try">Observe first, then reveal field arrows</h2>

- Shielding: keep the sphere and detector unchanged while comparing no cage, metal and insulating meshes. Reversing source charge changes induced signs while preserving ideal shielding.
- Collection: compare before power, charging and collection, then switch-off. A collected particle retains its location instead of being hidden to mimic disappearance.
- Disable particle charging as a model counterexample. The displayed field alone cannot cause this model’s neutral particles to drift electrically. Other effects, such as dielectrophoresis, are not modeled.

These activities correspond to required volume 3, pages 20 and 22. Dots are enlarged representative particles; counts are model samples, not measured particle numbers or actual efficiency.

<h2 id="deeper">Ideal conductors and representative particles</h2>

<details>
<summary>How does the model connect images with conclusions?</summary>

The mesh is approximated by an isolated neutral closed spherical shell of radius 0.15 m. The external small sphere is a point-charge approximation. An image-charge solution determines exterior field and surface charge: the surface is equipotential, integrated induced charge is zero, and the applied field inside is zero. Leakage near real mesh holes is omitted. The insulating mesh is a no-free-charge-redistribution control, without dielectric polarization.

The detector uses a potential difference between two positions to determine charge separation, followed by electrostatic/gravitational leaf equilibrium. It is a relative indicator, not an instrument calibration.

Collection uses continuous smoke supply, constant upward gas flow and representative particles. Charge approaches saturation while energized; electrical migration balances qE against 6πηav. Birth, collection and departure from the observation region are retained, and deposits remain after switch-off. Tip ionization is represented only through charging. Ionization avalanches, space charge, size distributions and back corona are omitted, preventing predictions of actual efficiency.

</details>
