<h2 id="understand">The needle carries thread; the hook makes the interlock possible</h2>

The eye near the needle tip carries the upper thread through the fabric. A second thread comes from the bobbin below. As the needle rises from its lowest position, a loop becomes available behind it. The rotating hook catches that loop and carries it around the bobbin case, enclosing the lower thread.

The take-up lever then removes the extra upper-thread length, drawing the interlock into the fabric. The feed dogs advance the cloth and leave the stitch behind. Copper always identifies the same upper-thread route; teal identifies the separate lower-thread route. Both continue into their supplies and the existing seam.

<h2 id="try">Pause at the three easily missed moments</h2>

In exploration, move the sewing-cycle slider slowly. First inspect the needle's initial rise after bottom dead centre. Then follow the loop in front of and behind the case. Finally, watch take-up and feeding. Seeking reconstructs all of these parts together at the selected phase.

Changing feed distance reconstructs a different sample at the same cycle position. It changes the feed stroke and stitch spacing, while needle length, bobbin dimensions and hook phase remain fixed. The film's final comparison uses two such samples; it does not stretch an already sewn piece of cloth.

<h2 id="deeper">One phase coordinates the mechanism</h2>

<div className="formula">θ = 2πp · Hook angle = 2θ + fixed phase offset</div>

`p` runs from 0 to 1 over one needle cycle. A slider-crank calculation keeps the needle connecting rod at constant length. The illustrated rotary hook turns twice per cycle. Pickup occurs on the needle's upward stroke, and feeding occurs only after the tip has cleared the cloth. This teaching sequence separates tightening and feeding; actual machines can overlap parts of those actions.

<summary>What does the flexible-thread model calculate?</summary>

Needle, crank, hook, take-up lever and feed-dog positions share one phase. A continuous spatial envelope represents the upper loop from the needle eye, around the case, off the hook and into the finished interlock. Targeted checks cover case-envelope clearance, separation of the two routed threads, the pickup connection and continuity at phase boundaries.

This is not flexible-thread dynamics. It does not solve tension, friction, stretch, fabric compression or complete collision response, and its routing envelopes are not measured trajectories from a particular machine. The illustration places the interlock inside the fabric; real tension settings and material properties affect its depth.
