<h2 id="understand">Understand it</h2>

Tissue culture retains a continuous material origin: one explant produces callus, followed by shoots and roots under suitable conditions, then acclimatization, transplanting and survival observations. Stems, leaves and roots grow from existing tissue rather than being replaced by unrelated plant icons. The stage transition shows a plantlet entering another environment, not glass transforming into a pot.

PEP Biology Elective3 pages35–37 use young chrysanthemum stems, emphasizing orientation, contamination, growth, differentiation and post-transplant survival. Callus is not a perfectly uniform collection of interchangeable “universal cells.” Regeneration depends on material, genotype, hormones, nutrition and environment. Compressed animation time does not guarantee success at a fixed number of seconds or days.

Plant somatic hybridization begins by removing cell walls while retaining intact plasma membranes. Suitable induction brings protoplasts into contact and fusion, followed by further nuclear changes, wall regeneration, culture and selection. This is one successful pathway; real preparations also contain unfused cells, same-parent fusions and cells unable to regenerate.

Two colours and four nuclear shapes only mark origin. They are not real fluorescent dyes and do not mean tomato and potato each have two chromosomes. Organelles initially share the cytoplasm, but regenerated plants need not retain both parental organelle genomes indefinitely.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

- Track the inserted explant, callus, shoots and connected roots. Introduce the contaminated-observation variant and consider why that vessel cannot establish a valid regeneration result.
- Pause before and after protoplast contact: two intact boundaries precede a continuous common boundary. Follow the original nuclear labels inside it.
- Compare no added hormones with supplemented conditions, then change relative proportions. Finally hold the ratio at one while changing both concentrations.
- Enter actual explant, shoot and root counts into the initially blank records. One explant can have both shoots and roots, so the two fractions need not sum to100%. Match conditions and sample definitions.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

**Ratio is not the only variable.** Skoog and Miller’s classic1957 work included tobacco culture and is not a quantitative recipe for every chrysanthemum material. Relatively more auxin favouring roots and more cytokinin favouring shoots is a useful teaching framework, but hormone identity, absolute dose, endogenous levels, tissue state and genotype also matter. Zero divided by zero is undefined: no added hormone is a separate control, not a ratio of one. Explants can contain endogenous hormones.

The model uses relative concentrations a and c, a smooth bias based on log((a+0.08)/(c+0.08)), and an illustrative response strength dependent on total dose. Very low totals give weak responses; an assumed inhibitory term reduces the response at high totals. No parameters were fitted to chrysanthemum data. Organ sizes indicate model tendencies, not actual rooting percentages or a culture recipe. Real outcomes require replicates, consistent reading times, contamination records and analysis.

**Membrane geometry.** Two separate circles precede contact. After contact, the connected outer boundary follows the union of two circles. As separation decreases, radius is solved so the illustrated area remains the original combined area. This2-D constraint avoids arbitrary loss of depicted material; it does not measure biological3-D volume, membrane area or osmosis. Narrow screens arrange cells vertically and wide screens horizontally, with labels and boundaries sharing the transformation.

Nuclear fusion is shown inside the already connected cytoplasm while four origin labels keep their identities. Actual nuclear and chromosome behaviour and selection can differ from this successful route. Regenerated wall appears outside the plasma membrane, avoiding the misconception that original cell walls directly fuse.

**Historical hybrid evidence.** Melchers, Sacristán and Holder’s1978 study used particular tomato material and a dihaploid potato line. Nuclear-encoded Rubisco small-subunit products in regenerated leaves helped demonstrate both parental origins, rather than plant appearance alone. The abstract reports differing chloroplast origins and limitations in chromosome estimates. The shapes do not replace chromosome analysis, and the colours are not presented as the original experimental labels.

Textbook pages36 and38 distinguish obtaining a hybrid from achieving the imagined combination of tomatoes above and potatoes below ground. Evidence of both nuclear parents does not guarantee every desired agricultural trait. Grafting is another process and must not be confused with somatic fusion.
