<h2 id="understand">Less litter does not mean matter disappeared</h2>

Some soil microbes break large organic molecules down outside their cells before taking up suitable small products. Carbon can subsequently enter cell material, metabolites and carbon dioxide. **A shrinking leaf neither destroys carbon nor proves that all its carbon immediately became carbon dioxide.**

C1–C6 are teaching markers for portions of the same organic carbon batch, not six free carbon atoms or fully specified molecules. Broken connections represent outside cleavage. Subsequent enzyme reactions, transport and cell metabolism are grouped. This explains the order of a pathway without simulating complete respiration or assigning each marker to a particular final molecule. Litter is not all starch: cellulose and starch have different linkages and require different enzymes. [IUBMB cellulase definition](https://iubmb.qmul.ac.uk/enzyme/EC3/2/1/4.html), [alpha-amylase definition](https://iubmb.qmul.ac.uk/enzyme/EC3/2/1/1.html).

The textbook compares untreated soil with treated soil. Its example of 60 °C for 1 h aims to reduce microbial effects; it **does not guarantee sterility or unchanged soil chemistry**. Residual activity, extracellular enzymes and treatment effects can influence the result. Match litter source, soil depth, moisture, temperature, observation times and replication. Holes illustrate appearance; they do not measure mass. Dry-mass comparisons require consistent cleaning and drying.

The moisture function represents one aerobic process limited by dryness or oxygen availability. Its zero-rate endpoints are model boundaries, not a claim that no decomposition occurs in dry or waterlogged soil. Model masses remain examples. Personal observations begin empty, with a genuine zero distinguished from missing data.

<h2 id="try">Does a negative sugar test mean no breakdown?</h2>

Soil extract can contain microbes, extracellular enzymes, reducing substances and pigments. Gauze holds back coarse particles; it is not a sterile filter. Starch may form soluble products that are subsequently used. Thus, the amount remaining at the end differs from the amount formed over the whole process.

Compare two pathways for the same carbon batch. Stop later use and the small products can remain. Stop outside cleavage and intact material cannot enter through this pathway. **These are ideal controls for testing causality, not a claim that heating selectively switches off one process in real soil.** Original research with isolated amylase and cellulase added to pretreated soil and minerals demonstrated activity without living cells producing new enzyme. Persistence depended on enzyme and mineral type. [Olagoke et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms8111796).

Use independent aliquots. Iodine tests remaining stainable starch; fresh heated Fehling reagent tests reducing products. Adding both reagents to one aliquot would not provide two independent observations. Fehling reagent is not glucose-specific, and iodine color depends on chain structure and background. The animation's intensities and thresholds are illustrative, not a concentration calibration. [University of Maryland starch-hydrolysis exercise](https://user.eng.umd.edu/~nsw/ench485/lab5.htm).

Increase product use and compare early sampling with day seven. A late sample can contain little reducing product even after extensive starch breakdown. Initial samples, extract blanks without starch, time series and replicates help distinguish explanations. Preserve real observations when they differ from the animation.

<h2 id="deeper">Three carbon pools, not three pure substances</h2>

S, R and U represent starch carbon, soluble-product carbon and other carbon pools. Assume a well-mixed culture, constant coefficients and no new carbon input:

<div className="formula">dS/dt = −kS; dR/dt = kS − uR; dU/dt = uR; S + R + U = 1</div>

U groups cell material, metabolites and mineralized carbon; it is not all carbon dioxide. Conservation refers to carbon equivalents, not equal masses of starch and hydrated hydrolysis products. Percentages come from the equations. The 24 persistent markers are a finite sample governed by the same rates, not exact percentages. Each marker's center crosses a pool boundary at its committed transition time. Smooth motion adds no extra waiting interval.

Uptake is also distinct from mineralization. Hill, Farrar and Jones measured depletion of labeled glucose from agricultural-soil solution separately from labeled carbon dioxide production and reported a delay between the processes. This scene uses that qualitative distinction without importing rates or claiming to reconstruct their experiment. The publisher and institutional abstracts, not the full paper, were checked for this reference. [The 2008 study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.soilbio.2007.09.008).
