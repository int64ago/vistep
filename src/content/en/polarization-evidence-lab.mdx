<h2 id="understand">Direction selection is characteristic of transverse waves</h2>

Transverse rope motion separates into directional components. Longitudinal spring motion follows propagation and lacks the same transverse selection. Real optical polarizers select electromagnetic components through their material response, not macroscopic wooden slits.

<h2 id="try">Rotate the board, then the analyzer</h2>

Compare rope and continuous helical spring waves. Choose window light, reflected glass light, a glossy table or an LCD. Rotate the analyzer and vary incidence for the reflecting surfaces.

<summary>Separate mechanical and optical models</summary>

For a unit vertical incident string wave and allowed unit vector u, P=uuᵀ transmits the projected field. The reflected field is −(I−P) applied to the backward traveling wave. Displacement is continuous at the constraint; ideal channel powers cos²θ and sin²θ sum to one. Friction and losses are omitted. The longitudinal spring ignores end reflections and retains positive coil spacing under axial displacement.

Glass index is 1.5 with exact Fresnel Rs and Rp. Readouts normalize to reflected light incident on the analyzer, not the original source. The illustrative table uses index 1.45 plus 12% unpolarized diffuse light. Window light is fully unpolarized; the LCD is 98% linear at 25 degrees plus 2% unpolarized. These are teaching parameters, not device measurements. Display tone mapping is nonlinear. Glass ray geometry follows incidence; absorption and multiple reflections are excluded. Real windows, skies and screens may differ.
