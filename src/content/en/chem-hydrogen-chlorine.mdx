<section id="understand">
## Flame and mist convey different information

Hydrogen can burn in chlorine with a pale flame: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl. The textbook uses this observation to broaden the concept of combustion beyond reactions involving O₂.

HCl gas is colourless. In moist air, fine hydrochloric-acid droplets can scatter light and become visible as white mist. This is a liquid aerosol, not white HCl molecules or smoke made of solid powder.

</section>
<section id="try">
## Separate reaction from visibility

In the mist view, compare moist air with an ideal dry environment. HCl production is unchanged while the visible mist differs. In the molecular view, count both atom types: one amount each of H₂ and Cl₂ gives two of HCl.

Progress is the conversion of a tracked portion, not a flow rate or real time. Flame, droplets and atom paths are teaching diagrams. No procedure for handling hydrogen–chlorine mixtures is provided.

</section>

<details>
<summary>Thermochemistry and limitations</summary>

[NIST lists](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C7647010&Mask=1) a gas-phase standard formation enthalpy of −92.31 kJ/mol for HCl. Forming 2 mol from standard-state H₂ and Cl₂ therefore gives ΔH°=−184.62 kJ. This is a thermochemical account near 298.15 K; flame temperature also depends on heat capacities, dissociation, heat transfer and reaction conditions.

The model conserves hydrogen and chlorine atoms and qualitatively distinguishes gas from moisture-dependent mist. It does not solve radical-chain kinetics, aerosol nucleation or particle scattering. Droplet symbols distinguish hydrated ions without representing detailed solvation structures. The textbook observation is cross-checked against the [Wisconsin demonstration](https://www2.chem.wisc.edu/deptfiles/genchem/demonstrations/Inorganic/pages/Group67/chlorine_and_hydrogen.htm). Textbook position: Required Book 1, page 45 (PDF page 50), Experiment 2-7.

</details>
