<section id="understand">

Breaking bonds requires energy; forming bonds releases it. The overall heat effect compares every term. Required 2 uses H₂(g)+Cl₂(g)→2HCl(g): input 436+243=679 kJ, output 2×431=862 kJ, giving an approximate net enthalpy change of −183 kJ.

This hypothetical bookkeeping route is neither the elementary mechanism of hydrogen reacting with chlorine nor an activation-energy curve. Atom markers preserve the identities of two hydrogen and two chlorine atoms; they do not claim that all real molecules first break apart simultaneously.

</section>
<section id="try">

Change account progress and reaction extent in the bond view. Doubling extent doubles every energy term. Zero extent represents no actual batch. Continuous broken/formed fractions direct the visual interpolation; they do not imply that a single molecule has a partially broken bond by that percentage.

The Hess view splits graphite oxidation into two reactions. Reverse or multiply them: species coefficients and enthalpies change together. CO cancels when equations are added, while carbon and oxygen remain conserved.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Textbook approximations and standard thermochemistry</summary>

The film retains textbook bond energies 436,243 and 431 kJ/mol rather than treating them as high-precision values. NIST gives a standard gas-phase formation enthalpy near −92.31 kJ/mol for HCl. Two moles would therefore give approximately −184.62 kJ using those standard formation data. This differs slightly from the textbook’s approximate −183 kJ bond account. Mixing the datasets and claiming exact agreement would be incorrect.

The energy account first considers separated gas-phase atoms, then products. That hypothetical route uses endpoint differences rather than claiming an observable collective intermediate. Activation energies, rates, light-initiated radical chains and molecular quantum dynamics are outside this model.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hess addition and consistent states</summary>

Elective 1 uses C(s)+O₂(g)→CO₂(g),ΔH₁=−393.5 kJ/mol, and CO(g)+½O₂(g)→CO₂(g),ΔH₂=−283.0 kJ/mol. Subtraction gives C(s)+½O₂(g)→CO(g),ΔH₃=−110.5 kJ/mol. Carbon here is the graphite reference state; another allotrope changes the initial state and its enthalpy.

The independent model adds signed stoichiometric vectors over C, O₂, CO and CO₂ alongside their enthalpies. Produced CO at+1 cancels consumed CO at −1. Each equation conserves carbon and oxygen. Reversal multiplies coefficients and enthalpy by −1, while scaling multiplies both by the same factor.

Vertical positions come from state enthalpies, not spatial elevation or reaction barriers. Hess comparisons require consistent temperature, phases, substance states and endpoint composition. Replacing liquid water with gaseous water changes the endpoint and needs a phase-change term. Heat is related to enthalpy under appropriate constant-pressure and work conditions.

</details>
