## Equilibrium still contains emission

The sphere emits radiation and simultaneously absorbs radiation from the enclosure. Net heat flow is their difference. At equal temperatures the powers balance, while radiation continues in both directions. A vacuum lacks a material conduction or convection path, but electromagnetic radiation can cross it.

Heating the sphere increases emission; heating the surroundings increases absorption. A cooler sphere still emits even when net energy enters it. The film keeps these contributions separate so that “radiating” is not confused with “cooling overall.”

## Hold temperatures, change the surface

For a grey surface, the same emissivity multiplies both emitted and absorbed power. Reducing it slows exchange without reversing the direction set by temperature. Real material properties can depend strongly on wavelength. Visible colour alone cannot establish thermal-infrared emissivity.

Exploration also changes area, with the sphere radius calculated from A = 4πr². Doubling area doubles both powers. Temperatures are externally maintained; no warming or cooling history is simulated.

## Power balance and spectrum answer different questions

<div className="formula">
  P_emit = εσAT⁴
  <br />
  P_absorb = εσAT_env⁴
  <br />
  P_net,out = εσA(T⁴ − T_env⁴)
</div>

Temperatures use kelvins. The small sphere sees a much larger isothermal black enclosure with view factor one. Its opaque, diffuse-grey surface has wavelength-independent emissivity equal to absorptivity. Unabsorbed incident power is reflected: (1−ε)σAT_env⁴. Blue arrows represent absorbed radiation only.

Planck’s law gives the spectral distribution, with λ_peak T ≈ 2.898×10⁻³ m·K. Each plotted spectrum is normalized to its own peak; its drawn area is not emitted power. From 300 to 600 K, the peak remains in the infrared. The sphere material drawing does not predict visible incandescence.

Direction lines and moving marks do not depict physical light speed, wavelength, individual photons or power. Numbers and bars share a fixed power scale. Conduction, convection, wavelength selectivity, finite-enclosure reflections and complex view factors are omitted. This model cannot be directly applied to Earth under sunlight or arbitrary facing plates.
