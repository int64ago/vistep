<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

This scene covers PEP Selective Compulsory 2 page 42 and Selective Compulsory 3 pages 17–18: molecular-mass evidence and the combination of empirical formula with a molecular ion. These are instrumental models, not student-acquired spectra.

Net electron ionization is M + e⁻ → M⁺· + 2e⁻. Fragmentation then gives additional peaks. The textbook uses toluene at 92, a hydrogen-loss ion at 91 and a smaller fragment at 65. The drawing follows the net 92 → 91⁺ + 1 mass relationship while retaining the visible carbon skeleton, without solving collisions, excitation distributions or rearrangements. Actual C₇H₇⁺ gas-phase structure cannot be fixed by one benzyl drawing.

The magnetic sector uses qV=mv²/2 and qvB=mv²/r, giving r=√(2mV/q)/B. Positive ions enter upward into a field toward the viewer and bend right. Equal m/z gives equal radius at the same voltage and field, even for separately different masses and charges. The spectrum's m/z is a mass-number value divided by charge number, not mass expressed in kilograms.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

Voltage, field, mass and assumed charge recompute the trajectory. Radius acceptance is ±0.25 mm; the drawn slit is widened for visibility while the offset gauge uses the actual tolerance. A fixed collector requires different analysis settings for different m/z values. Oversized trajectories terminate at the displayed chamber wall instead of leaving the image.

The film compares 65, 91 and 92 at 1000 V and 0.300 T with a collector matched to 92. The tuning button solves the field for the current mass, charge and voltage. The z=2 extension explains m/z; it does not claim a doubly charged species has high abundance in the textbook experiment.

The toluene teaching spectrum has base peak 91 and molecular ion 92. Ethanol's prominent 31 fragment is not its 46 molecular ion. Heights are illustrative values preserving the textbook relationships, not a complete database spectrum or direct molecule counts. C₂H₆O has empirical mass near 46; a confirmed singly charged molecular ion at 46 supports n=1. Fragment, isotope or charge misassignments trigger inconsistent results. Ordinary neutral C/H/O valence bounds also constrain candidate multiples.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

The isotope extension uses representative carbon composition p(¹³C)=0.0107. For N carbons, M+1/M=Np/(1−p) and M+2/M=N(N−1)/2×[p/(1−p)]². Toluene uses N=7. Its M peak is drawn at 60% of the base peak, and isotope heights use that same vertical scale. Deuterium and isotope series of all fragments are omitted. Natural compositions vary; this extension is not the textbook's original diagram.

Neither the tallest nor the rightmost peak is a universal molecular-mass rule. Molecular ions can be weak or absent, and different ionization methods may produce adducts. This scene concerns the textbook electron-ionization examples. Integer nominal mass differs from high-resolution exact mass. Vacuum, energy and angular distributions, calibration, response and resolution are not fully modeled.

Even a secure C₂H₆O formula leaves ethanol and dimethyl ether as different connectivities with the same approximate mass. Infrared, proton NMR or other evidence remains necessary. Likewise, a molecular ion at 92 alone does not uniquely identify toluene; toluene is the textbook's named example.
