<h2 id="understand">Two incline studies answer different questions</h2>

A **timed shallow incline** slows the motion. A ball rolls from rest while collected water provides a time comparison, allowing distance and time to be related. Equal-time records here show distance proportional to time squared. The flow must be stable for water mass to represent elapsed time.

The **ideal double incline** preserves the release height while changing the return slope. Without dissipation the ball regains the same height; a shallower slope requires a longer path. A horizontal continuation cannot regain height, so the ideal ball continues at constant speed. This combines observation with an ideal limiting argument, rather than claiming a real infinite track without resistance.

<h2 id="deeper">Rolling is not free fall</h2>

<details>

<summary>Why this does not directly measure g</summary>

A uniform solid sphere rolling without slipping has I = 2mr²/5 and straight-incline acceleration a = (5/7)g sinθ. It still follows s = ½at², but rotational dynamics alter the coefficient. Extending that rolling expression to a vertical angle does not produce the free-fall value g.

The historical argument combines experiments, mathematics and reasoned extrapolation. This reconstruction preserves the method while distinguishing rolling constraints from free fall. Water flow is set to 15 g/s and ramp length to 1.6 m; proportional water mass and time require the constant-flow approximation.
