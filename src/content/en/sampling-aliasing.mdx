## The same dots, different waves

A sampler records values at fixed instants. Every gold dot is calculated from the input. Blue shows that input; coral is another continuous explanation. A 6 Hz cosine sampled at 8 Hz produces the same record as a signed −2 Hz cosine with the same phase. Interpolation after acquisition cannot recover information that never distinguished them.

The film builds a record, crosses half the sampling rate, compares two matching explanations, raises the sample clock, examines phase at exact Nyquist equality, and removes a fast component before sampling.

## Why does frequency fold?

<div className="formula">
  x(t) = A cos(2πft + φ)
  <br />
  x[n] = x(n/fₛ)
  <br />
  fₐ = f − round(f/fₛ) fₛ
</div>

Subtracting an integer multiple of the sampling rate changes phase at each measured instant by an integer number of turns. The samples therefore agree. The calculation retains signed fₐ and the original phase, while the readout reports its magnitude. For arbitrary baseband signals, sample faster than twice the highest input frequency and allow for a real filter transition band.

This model assumes instantaneous, noiseless samples without quantization or jitter. The filter chapter directly attenuates a 7 Hz component while leaving a 1 Hz component unchanged; it illustrates an ideal low pass rather than implementing a physical filter.
