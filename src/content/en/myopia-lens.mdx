<h2 id="understand">Clear sight and slower progression are separate questions</h2>

Nearly parallel rays from one distant point focus in front of the retina in a myopic eye. They spread again before reaching the retina, leaving a blur instead of a small sharp point. This film uses an equivalent eye with fixed optical power: increasing its refractive mismatch moves the receiving plane farther behind its focus. Real eyes have a cornea, crystalline lens, curved retina and aberrations; myopia can involve eye shape and optical power. The [National Eye Institute's explanation](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/nearsightedness-myopia) describes this focusing relationship.

A negative-power spectacle lens makes incoming rays slightly diverge before the eye focuses them onto the retina. **Correcting the present blur does not, by itself, predict subsequent eye growth.** Myopia-control spectacle designs add another optical signal. Their structures and evidence differ. This lesson uses DIMS lenslets as one researched example, rather than treating every product as the same mechanism.

The front view contains a 9 mm central clear zone and an annular lenslet region extending to 33 mm in diameter. The studied lenslets were 1.03 mm across, with +3.50 D added to the distance correction. These dimensions are retained, but the displayed positions are an original sampled layout, not a manufacturing map. The distance correction is part of the lens base, including beneath the added lenslets.

For one off-axis point, the blue correcting component converges on the retinal plane while an admitted amber lenslet component focuses in front of it. At straight-ahead incidence, the illustrated bundle from the selected annular lenslet is blocked by the pupil. Changing the incident angle brings it into the eye. Pupil size, viewing direction, vertex distance and optical design affect the admitted light; rays from every lenslet cannot simply be drawn through the iris.

<h2 id="try">Follow the rays, then inspect the evidence</h2>

- Pause at “One point, two components” and trace both bundles through the same components. Replay the lens-surface chapter to identify the selected lenslet.
- In exploration, switch among no correction, single vision and the DIMS illustration. Increase the equivalent myopic mismatch; the receiving plane moves and the model recomputes its correcting spectacle power.
- Set incidence to 0°. The chosen annular bundle stops at the pupil. Slowly change the angle until it is admitted.
- Adjust the added power and open the close view. With 0 D addition the extra focus coincides with the clear focus; positive addition moves it forward. These inputs are teaching parameters, not a prescription.

<h2 id="deeper">An optical model is not a forecast of eye growth</h2>

<details>
<summary>How are the rays and focal positions calculated?</summary>

The section contains the optical axis and selected lenslet centre; radial height determines circular-pupil admission. Each ray carries height h and reduced angle u = nθ, using metres. Translation by d gives h′ = h + d·u/n; a thin lens of power P gives u′ = u − P·h. The equivalent eye has power E = 60 D, interior index n = 1.336 and spectacle distance v = 0.012 m. This is a paraxial, flat-image-plane teaching model. The 18° off-axis view omits the aberrations and curved refracting surfaces of a real eye.

For mismatch M, the receiving plane is x = n/(E−M). Vertex-corrected spectacle power is P = −M/(1−vM). A lenslet centred at h₀ changes reduced angle by an additional −A(h−h₀). The focus, ray intersection, pupil admission and close-view separation follow these same equations. [OpenStax's eye-optics chapter](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/2-5-the-eye) provides the equivalent-eye and refractive-correction foundations. The equivalent receiving distance shown here is not a clinical measurement of axial length.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What do the clinical bars measure?</summary>

The [original two-year randomized trial by Lam and colleagues](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/82284/1/Lam_DIMS_Spectacle_Lenses.pdf) enrolled 183 Hong Kong Chinese children aged 8–13, with myopia from −1 to −5 D. This film consistently uses **follow-up completers** in Table 2: 79 DIMS wearers and 81 single-vision wearers. Mean two-year axial elongation was 0.21 ± 0.02 mm and 0.53 ± 0.03 mm respectively. Error bars are standard errors of the means, not ranges for individual children. Mean refractive progression was −0.38 ± 0.06 D and −0.93 ± 0.06 D. Both groups still had axial growth.

These are group results for a particular design, population and follow-up, not an individual forecast. The separation between simulated foci cannot be converted into a child's future growth. Other designs, ages, refractive ranges and wearing patterns do not automatically inherit the same result. Slower progression does not mean an already elongated eye returns to its previous length. Actual selection, fitting and follow-up require an eye-care professional's assessment.

</details>
