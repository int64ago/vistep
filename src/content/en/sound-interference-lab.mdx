<h2 id="understand">The same sounds travel different paths</h2>

Two identical speakers driven by one signal source emit at the same frequency with a fixed phase relationship. Different travel distances change their relative arrival phase, creating stable regions of reinforcement and weakening.

The textbook divides students into two groups. Each moves within a small neighbourhood: white flags mark louder positions and dark flags mark quieter ones. The displayed flag positions are found from the actual calculated intensity field.

<h2 id="try">Move the receiver, then change wavelength</h2>

- Move horizontally or vertically and compare path difference with relative intensity.
- Show the two flag groups and inspect local reinforcement and weakening.
- Change the shared speaker frequency and watch the spatial pattern change.
- Explicitly start the optional receiver preview to hear its calculated amplitude. The visual does not require audio.

<h2 id="deeper">Quiet does not always mean silent</h2>

<div className="formula">Phase difference = 2π(r₂−r₁)/λ; I ∝ A₁² + A₂² + 2A₁A₂cosΔφ</div>

Ideal point sources are 4 m apart with sound speed 340 m/s. Each amplitude decreases as 1/r and phase follows propagation distance. Opposite phases cancel completely only when local amplitudes are equal. Relative intensity is normalized to the constructive midpoint; it is neither decibels nor a calibrated sound-meter reading.

The model omits room reflections, background noise and speaker directivity.
