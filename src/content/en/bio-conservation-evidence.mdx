<h2 id="understand">Survey records must remain traceable to conditions</h2>

A local survey can address water quality, waste handling or air quality. Specify sites, timing, responsibilities, methods and replication, then retain original observations, monitoring sources and interviews. The illustrated lake and dissolved-oxygen values of 7.8, 5.4 and 6.9 mg/L are practice examples. Personal inputs remain blank; these are not measurements of the reader’s locality.

Temperature, pH, transparency, oxygen, phosphorus and nitrogen answer different questions. The textbook cites GB 3838–2002. Assessment requires the applicable waterbody, protection objective, methods and sufficient indicators. A single oxygen reading does not establish an overall class or identify a pollution source merely through proximity. [Official Ministry of Ecology and Environment standard](https://www.mee.gov.cn/ywgz/fgbz/bz/bzwb/shjbh/shjzlbz/200206/t20020601_66497.shtml)

A proposal should identify evidence, unresolved questions, actions, responsibilities and follow-up indicators. Keep the survey plan separate from observations, preserving gaps rather than filling them with simulated measurements.

<h2 id="try">Separate movement, identity and reproductive evidence</h2>

Wildlife crossings can improve connectivity between habitats divided by roads. The animation retains one animal’s identity as it traverses the bridge; removing the connection keeps it on its original side. This is a spatial diagram, not actual Banff GPS data.

Sawaya and colleagues combined crossing records, genetic identification, parentage and population genetics to study migration, reproduction and admixture. A crossing photograph alone establishes facility use, not gene flow. The example illustrates levels of evidence, not a universal prediction for all species. [Original 2014 study](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.1705)

Local cases need sources, dates, methods, protected organisms and limitations. In-situ protection retains habitat and interactions, while ex-situ conservation and germplasm storage can complement it. One captive individual or one facility cannot replace long-term habitat protection. Interviews and historical recollections should retain their identity as such evidence.

<h2 id="deeper">Engineering and carbon inventories both need complete accounts</h2>

The nutrient view preserves twenty-four nitrogen labels: fourteen return to soil, six leave in harvest and four enter a loss pool. Without reuse, processed nutrients remain in a separate stored pool. These proportions are illustrative, not a farm’s measured nitrogen-use efficiency. Nutrient reuse does not establish energy cycling or eliminate external inputs. Self-organization depends on biological renewal and interactions, while coordination requires environmental fit and capacity. Functional plant types and the ten-input, six-used, four-excess example test constraints rather than prescribe species or fertilizer rates.

The courtyard section connects feed input, an anaerobic vessel, digestate overflow and a separate gas line. Green markers track selected nitrogen retained in slurry; gold markers represent biogas, mainly methane and carbon dioxide. Nitrogen labels are not converted into methane. Ammonia volatilization, other losses and real treatment kinetics are omitted. Digestate use requires consideration of quality, sanitation, environmental conditions and management; this is not a construction design. [EPA anaerobic-digestion guidance](https://www.epa.gov/anaerobic-digestion/basic-information-about-anaerobic-digestion)

A visit should record observed structures and connections separately from interviews about quantities, maintenance and costs. Performance requires evidence, and local designs must consider climate, economics and management capacity.

The plot example converts vegetation dry mass and carbon fractions into density; soil carbon uses bulk density, depth, carbon fraction and coarse fragments. Defaults are 2000 kg aboveground biomass, 500 kg roots, 80 kg litter and a 400 m² plot, with carbon fractions of 0.47 and 0.45 for litter. Soil bulk density is 1.2 g/cm³, depth 30 cm, carbon fraction 0.02 and coarse-fragment volume fraction 0.1. Soil carbon is 64.8 Mg C/ha and the combined estimate is about 95.1 Mg C/ha. These are teaching inputs, not original national-survey samples. Real inventories require representative sampling, strata and uncertainty analysis.

Regional totals sum density times area across ecosystem types. Twenty hectares of forest, fifty of grassland and thirty of cropland at 100, 60 and 40 Mg C/ha give a regional mean of 62 Mg C/ha. Additional forest plots do not enlarge forest area. Naïve plot-count averaging confounds sampling allocation with landscape composition.

The historical project sampled about 17,090 plots; Tang and colleagues’ stock paper used a 14,371-plot subset from 2011–2015. Fang and colleagues’ 2018 synthesis estimated an annual sink of 201.1 Tg C during 2001–2010, rounded in the textbook to about 201 million tonnes of carbon. Keep sample scope, stock and annual change distinct. Converting carbon to CO₂ mass requires multiplication by 44/12, not relabeling the unit. These are dated research findings, not current-year statistics. [Tang et al.: carbon stocks](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1700291115) [Fang et al.: carbon-budget synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1700304115)
