<section id="understand">
## Give reactants and evidence distinct locations

Damp cotton supplies steam, hot iron forms the reaction region, and soap solution makes departing gas visible as bubbles. The textbook net reaction is 3Fe + 4H₂O(g) → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂. Oxygen from water enters the black solid, while hydrogen enters the gas.

Bubbles alone show gas passage. Combustibility and the chemical account together support H₂ production. A black solid is likewise not a unique identification of magnetite without other evidence. The scene preserves this chain of reasoning.

</section>
<section id="try">
## Change conditions separately from the limiting reactant

Compare red-hot and unheated states, then vary available water in the illustrative calculation. With 1 g of iron, the model uses 3:4:1:4 stoichiometry. Limited water restricts both iron conversion and hydrogen yield. Follow the oxygen responsible for solid mass gain and check the total mass of all products and remaining reactants.

The heat selector represents a demonstration condition, not an exact temperature. Progress is conversion rather than real time. Apparatus and flame diagrams do not replace supervised laboratory procedures.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Conservation benchmarks and limitations</summary>

The model uses molar masses based on Fe=55.845, H=1.008 and O=15.999. Complete conversion of 1 g of iron can ideally give about 1.382 g Fe₃O₄ and 23.88 mmol H₂. These are stoichiometric limits, not textbook measurements. Remaining iron and water plus oxide and hydrogen conserve total mass.

Reaction kinetics, phase equilibria, oxide layers, gas mixing and steam condensation are omitted, as are initial air displacement and gas purity. Actual iron–oxide behaviour depends on temperature, atmosphere and material. [KIT describes the same reaction cycle](https://vbt.ebi.kit.edu/english/28_1127.php); an [ETH Zürich research study](https://doi.org/10.1039/D3SE01228J) provides experimental engineering context, without supplying operating parameters for this scene. Textbook: Required Book 1, page 70 (PDF page 75). Figure 3-5 was rendered to verify cotton, iron, both heat sources and soap solution.
