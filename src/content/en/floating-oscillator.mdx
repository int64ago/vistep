---
title: A Floating Chopstick Oscillator
description: Calculate buoyancy from immersion and identify the boundary of simple harmonic motion.
---

<h2 id="understand">The waterline creates a restoring force</h2>

A uniform wooden chopstick with iron wire at its lower end floats upright. Lifting and releasing it reduces displaced volume, making buoyancy smaller than weight. Below equilibrium, displaced volume increases and buoyancy exceeds weight.

Let upward displacement be x. While the waterline cuts the same cross-sectional area A, immersion decreases by x and buoyancy changes by −ρwatergAx. Equilibrium buoyancy already balances weight, so this change is the hydrostatic restoring force.

<h2 id="try">Test small oscillations and their boundary</h2>

Compare position with the buoyancy and weight arrows, then introduce linear damping and observe decay. The ΣF readout includes drag; the Fᵦ − mg graph shows only hydrostatic restoring force.

Increase the initial lift. During its downward motion the whole rod can become submerged. Displacement no longer increases its displaced volume, producing a plateau in the force graph. The motion may still repeat without remaining a linear harmonic oscillator throughout.

<h2 id="deeper">Retained physics and simplifications</h2>

The model retains finite rod length, uniform cross section, wood mass, iron mass and displaced iron volume. Small oscillations have effective stiffness ρwatergA and period 2π√(m/ρwatergA), with wood and iron included in the moving mass.

Motion is constrained to be vertical, assuming the ballast keeps the apparatus upright. The model omits tipping, fluid added mass, ripples and complex drag. The iron stays submerged throughout the offered controls and the rod never touches the glass bottom. Rod and wire thickness are visually enlarged for visibility.
