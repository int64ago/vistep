<h2 id="understand">Add first, then examine the distribution</h2>

Each trial is zero or one, with fixed success probability p. The sum S of N independent trials has an exact binomial distribution. Among four equally likely outcomes of two fair trials, both `01` and `10` give S = 1. The middle probability is one-half, rather than all three bars being equal.

The central limit theorem concerns a centered and scaled sum. Under independent, identically distributed trials with finite nonzero variance, the distribution of `(S−Np)/√(Np(1−p))` approaches standard normal as N increases. Original zero-one observations do not become normal, and finite sums remain discrete.

<h2 id="try">The sum spreads while the mean concentrates</h2>

Switch among the success count, standardized sum and sample mean S/N. From 8 to 32 fair trials, sum variance increases from 2 to 8, while sample-mean variance decreases from 0.03125 to 0.0078125 and its standard deviation halves. These are different horizontal coordinates.

Bars come from exact probability convolutions. Their heights change with horizontal bin width so bar area represents probability. The brown line is a normal density with matching mean and variance, rather than the probability at any individual point.

<h2 id="deeper">More is not automatically enough</h2>

<details>
<summary>Rare events, dependent copies and zero variance</summary>

With p = 0.05 and N = 8, zero successes has probability about 66.3%, leaving visible skew. Standardization changes location and scale, without instantly removing skew. Approximation quality depends on the original distribution, sample size and event of interest; one universal sample count does not guarantee accuracy.

Instead, draw one zero-one result and copy it N times. The sum can only be zero or N. Perfect dependence violates the independence condition; the sample mean remains that single result, with variance p(1−p) instead of p(1−p)/N. Additional copies do not provide independent information.

Explore changes N, p, horizontal coordinates and the dependence mode. At p = 0 or 1, variance is zero. Standardization is undefined, so the display explicitly falls back to the original sum coordinate. This is not a theorem about every dependent or heavy-tailed distribution, and no normal random draw substitutes for the actual sum distribution.

</details>
