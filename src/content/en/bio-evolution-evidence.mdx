<h2 id="understand">Why does a whale flipper contain the bones of an arm?</h2>

Hide the inside of a whale flipper. If it shares a history with an arm and a bat wing, which connections would you predict? The reveal is an independent textbook observation. It is not an answer generated from the hypothesis. Fossils, anatomy, embryonic regions and molecular records contribute different evidence.

A single fossil does not identify a direct ancestor. Human embryonic pharyngeal structures are not breathing fish gills; embryos are neither identical nor a replay of modern adult animals. Ten cytochrome c difference counts cannot by themselves supply substitution positions, a complete tree or exact dates. Evolution branches; it is not a ladder toward a highest form.

<h2 id="try">Change an assumption and test its consequence</h2>

- Reveal the concealed forelimb and compare corresponding bone connections. The shapes are schematic, not measured three-dimensional specimens.
- Keep the same SS, Ss and ss representatives while changing their background. Their genotype and color do not change.
- Begin with 10, 20 and 70. Under the textbook's annual +10%, +10% and −10% assumptions, year two has 11, 22 and 63. Count S copies: (2 × 11 + 22) / (2 × 96) = 44 / 192 ≈ 22.92%. The dark fraction is a different quantity.
- Remove the preset R-3 clone. Selection in this model cannot create its higher threshold. Remove the drug source and no inhibition halo remains.
- Change migration between A and B. Allele divergence changes, but the model does not turn a numerical distance into a species boundary. Test offspring fertility separately.

These windows follow textbook volume 2, chapter 6, P101–104, P112–115 and P116–118.

<h2 id="deeper">What the models can establish</h2>

<details>
<summary>Observations, hypotheses and numerical limits</summary>

The ten protein difference counts are fixed textbook records. Forelimb relationships are separately transcribed from the textbook figure. The conceal/reveal is a classroom prediction exercise, not a claim that the original researcher used a held-out sample. Bone proportions and joint details are schematic; they do not support measurements or species identification. Embryos highlight homologous regions without claiming identical age or precise staging.

Moth class counts directly receive the assumed yearly multipliers, without adding Hardy–Weinberg mating. Later class weights may be fractional expected counts. Drift, mutation, density regulation and mating are not simulated. Zero growth differences preserve the initial allele frequency within this model.

A normalized disk-concentration snapshot is q exp(−r² / (2σ²)), with σ = 18 model units. A threshold intersection sets the inhibition boundary, with a disk radius of 8. A zero source leaves only the disk, not a halo. This is not a time-dependent diffusion solution or clinical susceptibility result. Interacting disks, real doses and growth kinetics are omitted. Small parallel threshold factors are illustrative, not measured variation.

R-3 and its descendants preserve lineage and threshold; the animation clock is not culture time. Actual disk observations alone do not establish when variation arose. This lesson contains no protocol for culturing or enriching resistant microorganisms.

A and B are persistent population labels, not mice living for fifteen generations. F1 fertility and an illustrative F2 test distinguish two separate outcomes; they do not represent every possible reproductive barrier. Fertility is not computed from allele distance. The biological species concept also has limits for asexual organisms and fossils.

</details>

<summary>Primary evidence and reading scope</summary>

[Hu et al. (2009)](https://www.ivpp.ac.cn/sourcedb/zw/rck/yjy/200908/P020140313545720736756.pdf) document skeletal and feather evidence in specimen LPM-B00169. Our drawing locates evidence rather than tracing measured fossil coordinates, resolving its exact clade or establishing a direct ancestor.

[Cook et al. (2012)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3391436/) report selective bird predation on peppered moths under naturalistic conditions. Their daily survival estimates are separate from the textbook's hypothetical annual ten-percent changes. We do not substitute one for the other.

[Lederberg and Lederberg (1952)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC169282/) provide replica-plating evidence for clones already present before selection. The reading here specifically includes the original scanned summary on printed P405. R-3 remains a preset teaching clone, not a digitized experimental colony.

[Richardson et al. (1997), primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9278154/), warn against erasing real embryonic variation with excessively standardized drawings. Our different outlines emphasize limited homologous regions.
