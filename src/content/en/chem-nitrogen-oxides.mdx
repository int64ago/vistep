<section id="understand">

Colourless NO has low water solubility and forms red-brown NO₂ on contact with O₂. Reaction of NO₂ with water does not transfer all nitrogen to acid: 3NO₂+H₂O→2HNO₃+NO. Much colourless NO can therefore remain after the brown colour disappears.

The syringe in Experiment 5-5 and the copper/nitric-acid comparison in Experiment 5-8 retain separate entries. One follows oxidation and absorption; the other compares the textbook’s main gas products and uses subsequent NO oxidation to explain similar final colours.

</section>
<section id="try">

The syringe retains the textbook’s 20 mL NO and 5 mL water, with a default 10 mL air addition. Adjust oxidation and water-reaction extents and watch the piston and liquid level. Removing water or oxygen produces different boundaries. Values are ideal equal-temperature/pressure gas equivalents, not measurements extracted from a video.

The oxygen budget compares two explicit cases: 10 mL air supplies approximately 2.1 mL O₂, whereas the net reaction needs 15 mL pure-oxygen equivalent to convert 20 mL NO completely under ideal assumptions. This is a stoichiometric comparison, not a procedure for repeatedly overfilling a 50 mL syringe.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Closing the two-step inventory</summary>

First, 2NO+O₂→2NO₂. Combining 20 mL NO with 10 mL air, treated as 21% oxygen and 79% inert gas, initially yields 4.2 mL NO₂ alongside 15.8 mL NO and 7.9 mL inert gas: 27.9 mL total gas.

Next, 3NO₂+H₂O→2HNO₃+NO. The 4.2 mL NO₂ transfers 2.8 mL equivalent of nitrogen to solution and regenerates 1.4 mL NO. Final NO is 17.2 mL and total gas 25.1 mL. Liquid volume is held near 5 mL, giving about 30.1 mL total occupied volume. Reaction gas-volume equivalents encode molar ratios; they are not literal liquid-acid or liquid-water volumes to add.

Repeated oxidation and absorption sum to 4NO+3O₂+2H₂O→4HNO₃. NO, NO₂, O₂, inert gas, aqueous nitrogen and consumed water are tracked, with nitrogen and oxygen balance checks. Water is sufficient, temperature and pressure are fixed and the piston is free to move. NO₂/N₂O₄ dimerization, vapour pressure, piston friction and dissolution kinetics are omitted. Liquid depth in the horizontal cylinder is obtained by inverting circular-segment area rather than treating depth directly as volume fraction.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Acid conditions and product limits</summary>

The textbook cases are Cu+4HNO₃(concentrated)→Cu(NO₃)₂+2NO₂+2H₂O and 3Cu+8HNO₃(dilute)→3Cu(NO₃)₂+2NO+4H₂O. Copper, acid nitrogen and electrons balance: each Cu loses two electrons; nitrogen going from +5 to +4 takes one, and from +5 to +2 takes three.

Real gas mixtures can contain NO₂, N₂O₄, NO and minor components. The two teaching equations are not precision product distributions for every condition. The schematic retains the textbook NaOH tail-gas treatment but does not calculate removal efficiency. NaOH alone does not efficiently remove all NO; prior oxidation or mixed-nitrogen-oxide conditions matter.

Textbook: required 2, pages 13 and 15 (PDF 18 and 20). The [University of Wisconsin demonstration](https://www2.chem.wisc.edu/deptfiles/genchem/demonstrations/Gen_Chem_Pages/04chemrxnpage/nitric_acid_acts_on_copper.htm) supports brown gas and dissolved copper in the concentrated-acid case. A [2018 infrared gas-analysis study](https://doi.org/10.1333/s00897182790a) reports the main products under both conditions and more complex mixture components.

</details>
