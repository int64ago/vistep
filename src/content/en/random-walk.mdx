<h2 id="understand">Accumulate actual one-step moves</h2>

A one-dimensional lattice walker starts at zero and independently chooses `+1` or `−1` each step. A trajectory accumulates these moves. Its position is not the population mean. Fixed pseudorandom seeds replay examples; the exact endpoint distribution comes from probability transitions rather than estimates from three paths.

For a fair unbounded walk after N steps, mean position is zero and variance is N. Root-mean-square displacement is `RMS=√E[x²]=√N`. Zero mean balances left and right outcomes in expectation; it does not require each walker to return to zero or remain there.

<h2 id="try">Separate random spread from average drift</h2>

Increasing from 16 to 64 steps doubles fair-walk RMS from 4 to 8. This square-root scale is neither mean absolute displacement nor a bound on every individual path.

With right-step probability p = 0.7 and N = 32, mean position is 12.8 and standard deviation around that mean is about 5.185. For an unbounded walk, `E[x]=N(2p−1)` and `Var(x)=4Np(1−p)`. RMS also includes drift and therefore differs from standard deviation when mean position is nonzero.

<h2 id="deeper">Lattice parity and boundaries change reachable outcomes</h2>

<details>
<summary>Parity, absorption and scope</summary>

Each step changes parity. Starting at zero, a walker cannot be at zero after 31 steps but can after 32. Alternating empty bins are a reachability constraint rather than missing graphics. This does not say the path never returned earlier: the displayed return probability only concerns its position at the current step.

At absorbing boundaries ±4, a walker remains where it first reaches a boundary. The endpoint mass stays in the distribution and contributes to the absorbed fraction. Walkers are not deleted, and surviving mass is not renormalized. Unbounded-walk variance and square-root formulas do not automatically apply after adding these boundaries.

Explore changes step count, directional bias, example seed and absorption. Exact transitions update probabilities and moments immediately. This is a discrete one-dimensional teaching model, not continuous Brownian motion, a forecast of people or a model of every diffusion process. At zero steps, position, variance, RMS and absorbed fraction are exactly zero.

</details>
