<h2 id="understand">Without filming the whole journey, where does the route come from?</h2>

The textbook presents three localization points: rough ER at 3 minutes, Golgi at 17 minutes, and near-surface vesicles plus extracellular secretion at 117 minutes. These are diagrams of samples taken at different times. They are not a continuous movie of one protein, and the illustrated marks do not provide measured percentages.

A brief labeling pulse distinguishes a newly made protein cohort from later material. Unlabeled material follows during the chase, so later Golgi labeling cannot be explained by a fresh application of label there. The ordered observations support transport from ER through Golgi and onward. Fractionation and morphology provide additional evidence; three localization points alone have not filmed every membrane handoff.

<h2 id="try">What should happen when only one exit is blocked?</h2>

The explanatory model blocks ER export while leaving other processes functional. Starting from the same state, the control continues transport, while the blocked condition retains more label in ER and delivers less new cargo downstream. This is a fixed cohort: greater retention does not mean unlimited accumulation.

Apply the block later, and cargo already exported can continue. Closing the upstream exit does not send it backward. Restoring export lets the retained cohort proceed. Early block, late block and restoration are conditional model predictions, not interventions measured in the three historical diagrams. No specific drug is claimed to affect only this process.

<h2 id="deeper">Why is contact different from continuity?</h2>

During ER budding, a closing neck packages cargo into an independent carrier. Fusion with Golgi connects the enclosed spaces so cargo can enter. The compartments are not joined by a permanently open pipe, and cargo does not pass through an intact lipid bilayer. The scene omits several details of intra-Golgi traffic and does not claim that every protein follows this secretory route.

At the cell surface, docking alone cannot release cargo. A fusion opening must connect the vesicle lumen with the exterior, while vesicle membrane joins the cell membrane. A model can be tested with one cargo: intact boundaries must retain it, and an appropriate opening must permit passage. Rotating the view must preserve the same spatial relationship.

<summary>Textbook positions, historical clocks and model limits</summary>

The source positions are P51–52 of PEP Required Biology 1 for secretory transport and P57 for constructing a three-dimensional eukaryotic cell model. The film preserves the textbook's 3, 17 and 117 minute localization points.

Caro and Palade's 1964 abstract describes major locations approximately 5 minutes, 20 minutes and more than 1 hour after injection. Jamieson and Palade's 1967 slice studies distinguish a 3 minute pulse from subsequent chases at +7, +17, +37, +57 and +117 minutes. Different time origins must not be combined into a fabricated quantitative timeline. This film extracts no quantitative curve from those papers.

Explanatory model progress is not measured in minutes. The 24 teaching labels preserve identity and inventory; organelle dimensions, rates and routes are illustrative. The block comparison's distribution grid represents compartment counts, not real spatial particle coordinates. Local budding and fusion views demonstrate connectivity in a section without simulating molecular membrane rearrangements.
