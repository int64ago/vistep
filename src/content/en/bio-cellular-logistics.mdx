<h2 id="understand">Movement of a label connects a route</h2>

A short radioactive-leucine pulse marks newly synthesized secretory proteins. Textbook observations at 3, 17 and 117 minutes locate label in rough ER, Golgi, and later secretory vesicles and extracellular material. Temporal ordering together with cellular structure supports the route; one photograph alone does not establish the whole process.

Cargo travels within membrane compartments. Budding, transfer and fusion connect ER, Golgi and secretory vesicles, followed by exocytosis. Ribosomes are not membrane enclosed, and the nucleus is not the starting compartment for the secreted protein.

<h2 id="deeper">How is tracing reconstructed?</h2>

<details>
<summary>Teaching paths versus experimental records</summary>

The 24 markers are an identity-preserving illustration, not counts from the paper. Continuous paths explain relationships between the textbook observation times; they do not determine molecular speeds. Real pulse-chase studies combine methods such as autoradiography, fractionation and differently timed samples, rather than filming a single protein continuously.

Three-dimensional coordinates are projected into a rotatable cutaway. Organelle sizes and teaching colors are schematic. The model preserves membranes, compartments, connections and cargo locations, without suggesting that every cellular protein follows the secretory route.
