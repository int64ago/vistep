## Buoyancy is not exclusive to floating objects

Liquid pressure is greater on the lower face than the upper face, giving an upward hydrostatic resultant. Side forces cancel. A sinking object still has buoyancy; it is simply insufficient to balance weight.

For partial immersion, more submerged volume increases buoyancy. After full immersion, moving a fixed-volume object deeper in a uniform incompressible liquid raises both face pressures without increasing their difference.

## Separate holding, motion and equilibrium

The positioning rod can push or pull and permits unequal buoyancy and weight. After release, net force determines acceleration, with reproducible integration. Loading and fluid-density chapters compare new static equilibria rather than loading transients.

Floating equilibrium displaces liquid equal to the total weight. More load requires greater draft. A denser liquid supports the same object with less displacement, so it floats higher. Equilibrium buoyancy still equals weight rather than continually exceeding it.
