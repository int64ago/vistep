## An inferior-mirage ray above a hot road

Air close to a hot road is warmer, less dense and lower in refractive index. Light arriving from a skyward direction can turn continuously within this near-ground layer before reaching the eye. A straight extension of the arrival tangent points lower, producing a sky-like patch that can make a dry road appear wet.

The complete view preserves hundreds of metres of travel; only the close-up enlarges the lowest two centimetres. Horizontal and vertical units differ, with exaggerated height. Screen angles are not physical elevations. The blue minimum lies in air, without a collision at a mirror surface. This scene explains arrival direction, **not complete inverted images, multiple images or every kind of mirage**.

## Test a ray that does not return

Look more steeply downward and the ray can reach the road, where the trace stops. Change the near-ground temperature to test uniform-air straight propagation and reversed bending with colder air below. Eye height moves the thin-layer encounter and the straight viewing extension. Extremely shallow turns can lie beyond the main field; the close-up follows the relevant ray.

## An analytic continuous-refraction model

<div className="formula">
  Fixed-pressure approximation: n − 1 = C / T<br />
  Within the layer n²(y) = n0² + a y<br />
  Horizontal optical invariant: p = n cosθ
  <br />
  Within-layer path y(u) = H + u tanθ + a u²/(4p²)
</div>

Upper air is fixed at 300 K and the layer is 2 cm thick. C = 0.079386 K approximates dry air at 633 nm and 1010 hPa, rather than arbitrary pressure or humidity. Index squared varies linearly inside the layer and is constant above, with continuous index at the join. Warmer lower air gives a lower bottom index and upward ray curvature.

The analytic segments preserve position, tangent and n cosθ. Independent numerical integration checks the curved segment. The displayed construction traces outward from the eye; actual light travels in reverse. Real temperature profiles, turbulence, humidity variations, dispersion, road relief and Earth curvature are omitted. This is not a complete local mirage or weather prediction.
