<h2 id="understand">Control the conditions before interpreting a reading</h2>

Mixing calorimetry first equilibrates a metal sample in boiling water, then transfers it into a known mass of cool water. Their common temperature is not a simple average: metal, water and cup have different heat capacities. With negligible environmental losses, energy leaving the metal equals energy gained by water and cup.

A sealed syringe contains a fixed amount of gas. Slowly move its plunger and record total volume and absolute pressure. Under isothermal conditions, pV stays constant and p is proportional to 1/V. The model includes a 1 mL connection volume. Rapid compression heats the gas, providing a counterexample when temperature control fails.

A freely moving water plug keeps pressure in a flask close to atmospheric pressure. Hand warming expands the gas and moves the plug outward; cooling returns it. The volume ratio follows the Kelvin-temperature ratio.

<h2 id="try">Change one condition at a time</h2>

- Compare equilibrium temperatures with cup heat capacities of zero and 60 J/K.
- Move the syringe slowly and check absolute pressure times total volume.
- Compare isothermal and adiabatic temperature and pressure at the same volume.
- Warm the flask from 20 to 30 °C, then return to its original temperature.
