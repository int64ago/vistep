<h2 id="understand">Understand it</h2>

Brine submerges the vegetables. A separate outer trough surrounds the lid skirt with water and limits air entry. Those are different liquid regions. Lactic bacteria vary in oxygen tolerance; the animation selects a homolactic pathway rather than describing every organism and product in a natural mixed community. Its progress does not predict when food is edible.

Pages 6–8 of PEP Biology Elective 3 group pickles and fruit wine/vinegar under one inquiry activity. Nitrite tracking is further experimental design, and industrial-process comparison is a source investigation. Pages 22–23 introduce fermenter structure and control. All five learning positions retain their own sequence here.

Ideal alcoholic fermentation converts one mole of glucose to two moles of ethanol and two moles of carbon dioxide. Gas follows the neck and water bend to the outlet, never crossing the bottle wall. This is an airlock reconstruction; the textbook apparatus uses scheduled cap loosening. Yeast is facultatively anaerobic, and oxygen also affects membrane synthesis and growth in real winemaking. Alcoholic fermentation does not imply oxygen is irrelevant at every stage.

Vinegar production changes to a gas-permeable covering. Oxygen participates in converting ethanol into acetic acid and water. The model assumes the appropriate bacteria and available ethanol; it does not claim any exposed wine will reliably become vinegar. Microbial community, contamination, temperature and initial composition matter. The textbook's example conditions are not a universal finishing schedule.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

- Compare a filled and dry sealing trough. Identify the separate food brine and external seal. In the conversion view, increase the illustrative salt concentration and observe the slower rate; this is not a recipe or safety assessment.
- Scrub the nitrite sampling step. The pipette first withdraws liquid, rises above both vessel rims, travels horizontally, descends and delivers. Source, pipette and receiver retain a total of 50 mL.
- Remove oxygen in the vinegar view. Unreacted ethanol remains in the ideal model instead of disappearing into another product.
- Enlarge a similarly shaped vessel and compare its volume and area. Then independently change reactor aeration, mixing and cooling to recalculate oxygen and temperature.

Own records start blank. Retain sample dates, batch or location, materials, method, temperature and source. An industrial investigation can compare natural communities with selected strains, medium and equipment sterilization, seed culture, monitoring, recovery, quality checks and waste treatment. The animation cannot substitute for an actual visit or source review.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

**Nitrite quantities.** This synthetic calibration expresses every concentration as nitrite ion, NO₂⁻. For concentration C in mg/L, absorbance A = 0.020 + 0.040 C; 0.020 is the matched blank. A 10 g homogenized sample is extracted to 50 mL, followed by an additional twofold dilution of the extract. A = 0.068 gives 1.2 mg/L in the measurement solution, and 1.2 × 50 / 10 × 2 = 12 mg/kg of food. The calibration range is 0–10 mg/L; values outside it or below the blank do not produce a confident-looking result.

Real extraction, clarification, matrix effects, recovery, replicates and detection limits require validation. This is not a complete food-analysis method. The EPA aqueous method supports general principles of blanks, calibration and colorimetry; it is not a pickle preparation protocol. EPA results are often expressed as nitrogen, whereas this exercise uses NO₂⁻, so they must not be combined without conversion. A textbook example time series does not establish a universal peak day. Taste, smell and colour cannot replace nitrite measurement, and one result does not establish overall food safety.

**Reaction accounting.** Let alcoholic conversion be x and subsequent ethanol oxidation be y, with 0 ≤ y ≤ x ≤ 1. Glucose is 1−x mol, ethanol 2(x−y), carbon dioxide 2x, and both acetic acid and newly formed water 2y. External oxygen consumption is 2y mol. Carbon totals six moles of atoms, while integer teaching molar masses give equal input and output mass of 180+64y grams. Biomass, side products, evaporation and volatilization are omitted. Bubbles indicate paths, not a count-to-moles calibration.

**Reactor model.** Dissolved oxygen changes by transfer kLa(C*−C) minus microbial use 0.65C/(0.5+C). Here C*=8 mg/L and kLa changes with aeration and mixing; these are illustrative parameters. Temperature follows continuous heat production minus jacket heat removal proportional to the broth–coolant temperature difference. The model assumes mixing and omits actual impeller flow fields, biomass dynamics, pH, foam, vessel pressure and advanced controllers.
