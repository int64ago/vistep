<h2 id="understand">Understand it</h2>

Brine submerges the vegetables. A separate outer trough surrounds the lid skirt with water and limits air entry. Those are different liquid regions. Lactic bacteria vary in oxygen tolerance; the animation selects a homolactic pathway rather than describing every organism and product in a natural mixed community. Its progress does not predict when food is edible.

Pages 6–8 of PEP Biology Elective 3 group pickles and fruit wine/vinegar under one inquiry activity. Nitrite tracking is further experimental design, and industrial-process comparison is a source investigation. Pages 22–23 introduce fermenter structure and control. All five learning positions retain their own sequence here.

Ideal alcoholic fermentation converts one mole of glucose to two moles of ethanol and two moles of carbon dioxide. Gas follows the neck and water bend to the outlet, never crossing the bottle wall. This is an airlock reconstruction; the textbook apparatus uses scheduled cap loosening. Yeast is facultatively anaerobic, and oxygen also affects membrane synthesis and growth in real winemaking. Alcoholic fermentation does not imply oxygen is irrelevant at every stage.

Vinegar production changes to a gas-permeable covering. Oxygen participates in converting ethanol into acetic acid and water. The model assumes the appropriate bacteria and available ethanol; it does not claim any exposed wine will reliably become vinegar. Microbial community, contamination, temperature and initial composition matter. The textbook's example conditions are not a universal finishing schedule.

The nitrite window begins with separate observations in the textbook exercise on page 8: days 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13, read approximately as 5, 22, 16, 8, 5 and 4 mg/kg. Each point represents a sampling occasion, not one tube changing repeatedly. Reading uncertainty is about ±1 mg/kg; these are not measurements made by this app. Some fermentation microbes can reduce nitrate, while subsequent biological or chemical processes can remove nitrite. A rise-and-fall curve alone cannot identify the responsible organism or pathway.

The next view is a **nitrogen-portion account**. Nitrate supplies the nitrite pool, which supplies other nitrogen-containing products. Three numbered markers illustrate selected portions; the percentages come from a continuous model, not those three markers. Faster removal with unchanged formation predicts less nitrite at the same model time. The film pauses for that prediction before starting a clearly labelled new run. This does not calculate a safe fermentation day.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

- Compare a filled and dry sealing trough. Identify the separate food brine and external seal. In the conversion view, increase the illustrative salt concentration and observe the slower rate; this is not a recipe or safety assessment.
- Compare the independent nitrite records, then vary the later removal rate. At zero removal, the downstream pool no longer grows. Scrub the separate sampling view. The pipette first withdraws liquid, rises above both vessel rims, travels horizontally, descends and delivers. Source, pipette and receiver retain a total of 50 mL.
- Remove oxygen in the vinegar view. Unreacted ethanol remains in the ideal model instead of disappearing into another product.
- Enlarge a similarly shaped vessel and compare its volume and area. Then change aeration, mixing and cooling. A switch changes future rates from the current time; it preserves existing oxygen, heat and previously formed acid. Rewind to revisit history; an intervention in the past starts a new scenario.

Own records start blank. Every nitrite record retains its own dilution factor. An absent reading is missing; a valid reading equal to the blank yields zero in this exercise. Changing the next dilution does not rewrite earlier results. Retain sample dates, batch or location, materials, method, temperature and source. An industrial investigation can compare natural communities with selected strains, medium and equipment sterilization, seed culture, monitoring, recovery, quality checks and waste treatment. The animation cannot substitute for an actual visit or source review.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

**Nitrite quantities.** This synthetic calibration expresses every concentration as nitrite ion, NO₂⁻. For concentration C in mg/L, absorbance A = 0.020 + 0.040 C; 0.020 is the matched blank. A 10 g homogenized sample is extracted to 50 mL, followed by an additional twofold dilution of the extract. A = 0.068 gives 1.2 mg/L in the measurement solution, and 1.2 × 50 / 10 × 2 = 12 mg/kg of food. The calibration range is 0–10 mg/L; values outside it or below the blank do not produce a confident-looking result.

Real extraction, clarification, matrix effects, recovery, replicates and detection limits require validation. This is not a complete food-analysis method. The EPA aqueous method supports general principles of blanks, calibration and colorimetry; it is not a pickle preparation protocol. EPA results are often expressed as nitrogen, whereas this exercise uses NO₂⁻, so they must not be combined without conversion. A textbook example time series does not establish a universal peak day. Taste, smell and colour cannot replace nitrite measurement, and one result does not establish overall food safety.

**Nitrogen-model boundaries.** For normalized pools A, B and C, A′=−aA, B′=aA−bB and C′=bB, so A+B+C=1. Integer labels use largest remainders to retain a displayed total of 100%. The first-order parameters are per unit of model time, not fermentation days, and are not fitted to the textbook points. C combines other nitrogen destinations rather than identifying one compound. Oxygen, hydrogen, electron balances and microbial-community dynamics are not solved. Selected markers use waiting-time quantiles from the same rates; a finite sample need not match analytical proportions at every frame.

[Liu et al.'s 2014 primary study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0093308) compared nitrite changes in a strain system and detected a nitrogen-containing gas. Only that qualitative evidence is used here, not numerical values with inconsistent units in the paper, and one strain is not generalized to all pickles. The [primary abstract of Oh et al. (2004)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15117551/) supports strain and temperature dependence. We do not claim access to its full raw data.

**Reaction accounting.** Let alcoholic conversion be x and subsequent ethanol oxidation be y, with 0 ≤ y ≤ x ≤ 1. Glucose is 1−x mol, ethanol 2(x−y), carbon dioxide 2x, and both acetic acid and newly formed water 2y. External oxygen consumption is 2y mol. Carbon totals six moles of atoms, while integer teaching molar masses give equal input and output mass of 180+64y grams. The numbered carbons follow the net destinations of one selected sugar backbone; interpolated positions are not chemical intermediates. Salt-dependent bulk conversion is calculated separately, not inferred from the six selected carbons. Biomass, side products, evaporation and volatilization are omitted. Bubbles indicate paths, not a count-to-moles calibration.

**Reactor model.** Dissolved oxygen changes by transfer kLa(C*−C) minus microbial use 0.65C/(0.5+C). Here C*=8 mg/L and kLa changes with aeration and mixing; these are illustrative parameters. Temperature follows continuous heat production minus jacket heat removal proportional to the broth–coolant temperature difference. Piecewise integration preserves state before each air, cooling or mixing event; a stopped shaft retains its current phase. The model assumes mixing and omits actual impeller flow fields, biomass dynamics, pH, foam, vessel pressure and advanced controllers. A narrow screen retains a dedicated section view and readable type.
