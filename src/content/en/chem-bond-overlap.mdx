<section id="understand">

Lewis diagrams first account for electrons; spatial models then explain a different aspect of bonding. Ideal NaCl accounting conserves total electrons and net charge. The solid extends through a three-dimensional ionic neighborhood rather than separate NaCl molecules.

Cl₂ shares without a directional bias; HCl shares unevenly toward chlorine. Partial delta charges differ from integer ion charges. These textbook positions are structure/theory demonstrations, not student reaction experiments.

</section>
<section id="try">

Unfold a Lewis diagram, rotate the six-neighbor Na⁺ environment, or choose s–s, s–p and p–p slices. Change separation to change overlap. In the pi view, turn only one p direction and inspect the orthogonal 90° boundary.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Distinguishing sigma and pi</summary>

End-on s–s, s–p and p–p overlap can have sigma symmetry. A pi distribution has a nodal plane containing the internuclear axis; its two regions do not count as two pi bonds. See the [IUPAC glossary](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/gtpoc/SaSp.html) and [valence-bond discussion](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/8-1-valence-bond-theory).

</details>
<details>
<summary>Reproducible functions and limitations</summary>

The normalized Gaussian s basis is N exp(−αr²); a p basis multiplies it by 2√α(u·r), with N=(2α/π) to the 3/4 power and α=0.8. They retain spherical symmetry, p nodes and directionality, without fitting atom-specific radial distributions. In particular, they omit chlorine 3p radial structure.

The sum of two bases is normalized by 2(1+S) before squaring, where S is the analytic three-dimensional overlap integral. For parallel pi-type bases S=exp(−αR²/2); turning one direction in the slice multiplies it by cosθ, giving zero at 90°. Independent volume integration checks both normalization and overlap. This single-orbital shape model is not a quantitative many-electron density, bond order, energy or reaction-rate calculation for H₂, HCl or Cl₂.

Shading maps slice density without showing wavefunction phase signs. Separation uses model units. The Lewis dots unfold an accounting scheme, not particle trajectories. The six chloride neighbors have equal three-dimensional distances and share one projection; sphere radii are illustrative.

</details>
