<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

PEP Selective Compulsory 3 experiments 3-7 and 3-8, printed pages 69–70, use two mild oxidants to test ethanal. Their reduced products differ: silver ions give metallic silver, while copper(II) gives copper(I) in Cu₂O.

During reagent preparation, an initial silver-oxide precipitate dissolves through ammonia complexation, producing clear diamminesilver solution. No mirror has formed yet. After ethanal is added and the tube is warmed in a water bath, silver can coat the clean inner wall. The alkaline net equation is RCHO + 2 [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + 3 OH⁻ → RCOO⁻ + 2 Ag + 4 NH₃ + 2 H₂O.

The copper experiment uses fresh Cu(OH)₂ in excess base, not the different formulation of Fehling’s reagent. CH₃CHO + 2 Cu(OH)₂ + OH⁻ → CH₃COO⁻ + Cu₂O↓ + 3 H₂O. Blue suspension forms a brick-red solid. Copper changes from +2 to +1, not to metallic copper.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

Switch to acetone: it does not give the same reduction under the represented mild test conditions. Reduce the initial metal amount to expose the limiting reagent. One oxidized ethanal supplies two electrons, giving two Ag atoms or one Cu₂O formula unit.

Markers represent selected metal atoms, not measured concentrations. Silver markers reach the actual inner-wall geometry; pairs of copper markers approach the same formula-unit position. Functional-group drawings show net changes rather than elementary trajectories. The alkaline organic product is a carboxylate.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

Ethanal’s carbonyl carbon changes from oxidation state +1 to +3. Its two-electron oxidation explains both metal balances. Mirror quality also depends on clean glass, nucleation and deposition; a dark silver deposit is possible in practice. The model does not promise a perfect mirror under arbitrary conditions.

<details>
<summary>Conditions and limits</summary>

The automatic demonstration assumes the textbook heating conditions. The unmet-condition control represents not reaching that observation endpoint, rather than a claim that reaction can never occur at room temperature. Prolonged heating and Cu(OH)₂ decomposition to CuO are outside the model.

Formaldehyde, reducing sugars and certain tautomerizable compounds have additional behavior. The ethanal/acetone comparison does not uniquely identify every unknown positive sample. Silver–ammonia reagent and residues require fresh use and appropriate laboratory disposal, not storage; this visualization is not a home preparation guide.
