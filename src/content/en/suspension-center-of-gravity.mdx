<h2 id="understand">The same material point survives both suspensions</h2>

Hang an irregular sheet from one small hole and let it settle. Its center of gravity lies below the support, but one plumb line only identifies a line. Draw that line on the sheet, hang it from a different hole, and draw again. Their intersection locates the center of gravity. When the sheet turns, the recorded first line turns with it.

For a uniform triangular sheet, the medians provide a prediction that suspension can check. Uniform thickness and surface density matter. Adding a weight on one side shifts the center of gravity even though the outline remains unchanged. The suspension method still works, while the intersection of the geometric medians no longer locates the combined center of gravity.

<h2 id="try">Keep the first mark when changing the support</h2>

- Follow the brown dashed record. After changing holes, it rotates with the sheet; it must not stay vertical on the screen.
- Explore both shapes and change the suspension hole. The center stays at the same point on the sheet while the equilibrium orientation changes.
- Add a weight and compare the plumb-line intersection with the geometric centroid. Changing the mass distribution moves the center relative to the sheet.

<h2 id="deeper">Equilibrium supplies a geometric test</h2>

<details>
<summary>Why the intersection works</summary>

Across a small object near Earth, gravitational forces are approximately parallel. At rest, the support acts through the suspension point. If gravity acted along a different vertical line, it would still turn the sheet. In stable equilibrium the center of gravity is directly below the support, so every valid recorded plumb line passes through it. Two distinct directions locate their common point; coincident records require a different hole.

The string is treated as massless, the sheet is rigid and planar, and the pivot allows rotation. Wait for equilibrium before recording a line; the claim does not apply to a swinging snapshot.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Computation and approximations</summary>

Polygon area and centroid are calculated from signed area integrals. For a uniform sheet, the area centroid is its center of mass. The added green weight is represented by a point mass; the combined center is mass weighted. Hole locations, traces, orientation and the center marker share the same geometry.

The introductory settling uses a damped physical pendulum in the small-angle approximation, with rotational inertia calculated from the sheet. The recording steps use equilibrium. Airflow and dry pivot friction are not resolved, and the animation duration is not presented as an experimental measurement.

</details>
