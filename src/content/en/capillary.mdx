<h2 id="understand">How does liquid climb without a pump?</h2>

Wetting sets the contact angle between liquid and tube wall. A wetting liquid forms a concave meniscus. Surface tension around the contact line has an upward component capable of supporting liquid above the reservoir surface.

Narrowing the tube reduces contact-line circumference, but reduces column area even faster. The same liquid and contact angle therefore support a taller column in a narrower tube. The explanation is a ratio of circumference to area.

<h2 id="try">Cross ninety degrees</h2>

Vary radius, contact angle, surface tension and elapsed time. Halve the radius and compare final heights. Then pass through a 90° contact angle. The vertical tension component vanishes and reverses, changing a rise into a depression.

The main diagram widens tube cross sections for visibility, so its apparent contact angle is not a measurement. The contact-line close-up uses equal horizontal and vertical scales and derives tension direction from the same circular geometry. Heights are measured from the reservoir surface; depression is negative.

<h2 id="deeper">Circumference divided by area</h2>

The vertical surface-tension force is 2πrγ cosθ and the column's relative weight is ρgπr²h. Equating them gives Jurin's law, h = 2γ cosθ/(ρgr). The contact angle θ is measured through the liquid.

Defaults are γ = 0.072 N/m, ρ = 1000 kg/m³ and viscosity 0.02 Pa·s: a deliberately viscous teaching liquid, not a claim about the viscosity of water. Time evolution uses a quasi-steady viscous Washburn model without inertia, integrated in squared height to avoid the initial singularity. Its mirrored depression dynamics are also a teaching approximation. Low-viscosity liquids can have a distinct initial inertial regime.

The meniscus is approximated by a spherical section. Its volume, dynamic contact angles, hysteresis, evaporation, contamination and changes in reservoir level are excluded. Jurin's relation is appropriate for sufficiently narrow tubes with small gravity-induced meniscus deformation.
