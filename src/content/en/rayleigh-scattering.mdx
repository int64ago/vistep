## Blue redirected light and warm direct light share one budget

The Rayleigh limit applies to structures much smaller than the wavelength. Away from resonances and neglecting weak material dispersion, scattering cross-section varies approximately as the inverse fourth power of wavelength. Short wavelengths leave the direct beam more readily, giving redirected light a short-wave preference and warming the direct spectrum after a long path.

The main plot accounts for each wavelength separately. Warm direct energy and blue redirected energy add to the input outline. The blue area does not all enter one eye: it is shared over solid angle. The angular view assumes unpolarized input. Ninety-degree scattering is nonzero; forward and backward densities are equal and larger.

## Separate spectral selection from direction

Relative path length changes spectral attenuation. Observation angle changes the first-scattered angular share. Supply only650-nanometre light: the scattered light remains red. Elastic scattering changes direction without converting wavelength. The450/650-nanometre cross-section comparison assumes equal input energy, rather than predicting the brightness ratio of two actual sky colours.

## From spectra to displayed hue

<div className="formula">
  τ(λ) = 0.12 m (550nm / λ)⁴
  <br />
  Direct spectrum = input spectrum exp(−τ)
  <br />
  Removed spectrum = input spectrum − direct spectrum
  <br />
  Angular density P(θ) = 3(1 + cos²θ) / (16π)
</div>

P integrates to one over solid angle. The first-event angular spectrum is the removed spectrum times P, without subsequent attenuation, absorption or further scattering. The direct exponential attenuation and energy budget can illustrate long paths, but the first-scattered swatch does not predict radiance finally reaching an observer through a complex atmosphere.

Input is a5778K blackbody approximation normalized at550nanometres. Wyman, Sloan and Shirley’s analytic CIE1931 colour-matching fits integrate the spectrum into XYZ and then display RGB. These are not three individual cone-sensitivity curves. Each swatch is separately normalized and gamut-clipped to compare hue, not brightness. Actual sky colour also depends on the solar spectrum, molecular depolarization, aerosols, clouds, ozone, multiple scattering, surfaces and viewing geometry.
