## A short neck, a whole cavity

In a rigid cavity much smaller than the acoustic wavelength, pressure variation can be approximately uniform in phase. Neck air provides inertia; compression of cavity air provides a restoring force. This forms a Helmholtz oscillator, distinct from an organ pipe with a distributed pressure pattern along its length.

Outward neck displacement slightly expands the cavity air and lowers pressure, pulling the neck air back. Speed peaks at equilibrium. Inward overshoot raises pressure and reverses the motion. Pressure and energy in the film refer to the same state of this cycle.

## Change one geometric quantity

Raise the stationary water level to reduce air volume and strengthen the pressure restoring effect. Lengthen the neck to add moving mass and lower frequency. Widening the neck changes mass, swept volume and force area together: “heavier air” alone cannot predict the result.

In exploration, hold phase fixed while changing geometry. The gold outline includes effective moving air near both neck ends. It represents air, not a solid piston, and does not imply that surrounding air is perfectly still.

## Effective length and limits

<div className="formula">
  S = πr², L_eff = L + 1.6r
  <br />m = ρSL_eff, k = γP₀S² / V<br />f = c / (2π) × √[S / (VL_eff)]
</div>

The model uses sound speed 343 m/s, adiabatic index 1.4 and ambient pressure 101325 Pa, with a consistent density from c² = γP₀/ρ. Positive displacement is outward, giving p = −γP₀Sx/V. Neck kinetic energy and cavity compression energy exchange while their sum remains constant in this lossless model.

Approximate end corrections are 0.6 radii outside and one radius inside. Opening geometry, flanges and nearby objects can change them. The illustrated cylindrical cavity has fixed radius 5.5 cm and maximum volume 1.5 L. Stationary water only sets the remaining air volume; water and wall vibrations are omitted. Small rounded drawing corners are not part of the volume calculation.

Displacement is enlarged 80 times and phase is slowed; frequency readouts remain physical. Viscous, turbulent and radiation losses and blowing excitation are omitted. Large oscillations, flexible walls, multiple ports and significant standing waves inside the cavity require a fuller model. One formula cannot describe every bottle tone.
