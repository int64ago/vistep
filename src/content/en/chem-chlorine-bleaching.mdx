name: Dry and damp controls in chlorine bleaching
title: Why do two papers respond differently to the same chlorine?
---

<section id="understand">
## The key difference is water in the specimen

The two gas jars contain the same dry chlorine, but one coloured paper is dry and the other damp. Under the textbook conditions, dry paper retains colour while damp paper fades. A fresh flower can also change because its tissue contains water even when the surrounding gas is dry.

Chlorine partly reacts reversibly with water: Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HOCl. The textbook links bleaching to the oxidizing action of HOCl and changes to colour-bearing structures. Dry gas does not imply dry specimens.

</section>
<section id="try">

In exploration, switch the second paper between dry and damp and advance the reaction. The first dry paper remains the reference. The flower view separately represents water within fresh tissue. <section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>What the evidence supports and what the model omits</summary>

Water availability and reaction extent control the displayed colour fraction. The model does not solve chlorine dissolution, HOCl concentration, dye kinetics or actual product structures. Changed connections only indicate an oxidative structural change; the paper and flower are not assumed to contain the same pigment. Materials and humidity affect real observations. The dry-paper control does not imply that chlorine is unreactive with every dry organic substance.

The [RSC teaching experiment](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/reactions-of-chlorine-with-water-or-halide-ions/530.article) explains the acidic, bleaching system formed with water. A [JACS Au research paper](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/jacsau.3c00066) reports HOCl bleaching of actual dyes; its production method and doses are not used here. Textbook: Required Book 1, pages 46–47 (PDF pages 51–52). The digital-sensor photolysis experiment has a separate scene.

Removing chlorine provides an independent control: moisture alone does not bleach the specimen in this model. The same 24 schematic sites retain their identities as their connections and colours change continuously. They are not a count of real pigment molecules or a list of oxidation products.

</details>
