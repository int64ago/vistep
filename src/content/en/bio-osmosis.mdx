<h2 id="understand">Ask what can cross, then what drives it</h2>

Ink diffusion evens out a solute distribution. Osmosis concerns water moving through a semipermeable membrane that retains other substances. A rising funnel column builds hydrostatic pressure, so it cannot rise indefinitely. Equilibrium means zero net flow, not motionless molecules.

Red cells have no cell wall and can rupture after excessive swelling. A plant wall limits expansion; water loss makes the protoplast retract more than the wall, producing plasmolysis. Viable cells can recover when the external solution is replaced with fresh water.

<h2 id="try">Change one condition within a comparison</h2>

- Replace the funnel membrane with sucrose-permeable gauze and test for sustained column rise.
- Match the initial inner and outer concentrations and inspect the osmotic drive.
- Dehydrate a plant cell, then observe recovery against the fixed wall.
- Remove ATP from the active-transport model and inspect concentration and energy use.

Textbook: Required 1, Chapter 4, pp. 62–68, plus passive and active transport mechanisms. The aquaporin shot distinguishes early tracer evidence, protein isolation in 1988 and subsequent functional expression experiments.

<h2 id="deeper">Conservation, boundaries and simplifications</h2>

<details>
<summary>How is the model kept consistent?</summary>

Ink follows one-dimensional discrete diffusion with reflecting boundaries and conserved total solute. Cells retain a fixed amount of impermeant solute, making concentration inversely proportional to volume. Osmotic differences and plant-wall pressure determine water flow. Red cells have an illustrative rupture threshold, beyond which normal osmotic equilibrium calculations stop.

Concentration, time and pressure use relative units. Diagrams carry no invented microscopy scale. Effective funnel volume determines column height. The gauze control represents the lack of a sustained osmotic difference after free solute exchange; it omits the initial mixing transient. Aquaporins change permeability, not equilibrium conditions.

Carrier transport saturates, while the active pump couples to ATP supply. Membrane voltage, leakage and complex regulation are omitted, so the model cannot calculate an actual cell’s electrochemical equilibrium.

</details>
