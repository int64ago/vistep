<h2 id="understand">When salt makes a cell shrink, what leaves?</h2>

Watch the highlighted dye particle. It sometimes moves away from the original drop and sometimes doubles back. The whole distribution still spreads. **An individual direction and a population's net change are different things.** A molecule does not sense where the concentrated solution is and choose to swim there.

Now place a selectively permeable membrane between two solutions. Water crosses both ways while sucrose remains behind during the observation. With equal initial pressure, the more concentrated sucrose solution gains water overall. The funnel column rises, diluting its contents and building opposing hydrostatic pressure. Eventually the two flow arrows become equally thick. The level barely changes, but the magnified particles keep moving.

At osmotic equilibrium, **net transfer stops; molecular motion does not**. Solute concentrations need not become equal because pressure also contributes. Water potential combines these effects as the pressure-equivalent difference in water’s chemical potential from a pure-water reference. At equal temperature and pressure, mixing with solute lowers the solvent’s chemical potential. In the ideal-solution approximation, this contribution follows water activity. The diagram retains the mixing baseline and adds the pressure contribution; the difference between the current levels determines net transfer from higher to lower water potential. [IUPAC definition of activity and chemical potential](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/A00115)

<h2 id="try">Carry the argument from a funnel to a cell</h2>

Identical red cells change differently in three baths. An isotonic bath preserves average volume. A hypertonic bath causes net water loss, concentrating the material retained inside an intact cell. A hypotonic bath causes net uptake and may rupture a cell that has no supporting wall. This comparison concerns effective osmotic concentration: equal mass percentages of salt and sucrose do not establish equal osmotic conditions.

A plant cell adds a mechanical constraint. Concentrated sucrose solution draws water out of purple onion cells; the vacuole and protoplast shrink while the less extensible wall remains outside. External solution fills the gap. Plasmolysis does not create a vacuum or separate two plasma membranes.

Follow the same viable cell as the bath becomes hypotonic. Water re-enters and the vacuole expands. Once the protoplast presses against the wall, turgor increases and opposes further uptake. These observations support predictions you can check in exploration:

- With matching initial concentrations, should the funnel still build a sustained column?
- If gauze allows sucrose to exchange, can the same osmotic difference persist after mixing?
- Will aquaporin resist water loss in a hypertonic bath, or accelerate it?
- Why can a plant wall oppose swelling while a red cell has no equivalent support?

The aquaporin comparison also illustrates how to test a proposed mechanism. Preston and colleagues injected CHIP28 RNA into _Xenopus_ oocytes and found increased osmotic water permeability. The first comparison reconstructs this functional evidence. The later reversed-bath shot is a **prediction from passive transport**, not a claimed replication of a treatment reported in that paper. [Original 1992 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1373524/)

<h2 id="deeper">What connects the observations to a theory?</h2>

<details>
<summary>How did the textbook evidence for water channels develop?</summary>

Required 1, page 68, recounts a 1950 hydrogen-isotope tracer observation: water crossed cell membranes faster than artificial membranes. This supported a possible special pathway, but the rate difference alone could not identify what formed it.

In 1988, Denker, Agre and colleagues isolated and purified a membrane protein of about 28 kDa from erythrocyte and kidney membranes. Obtaining a candidate and demonstrating water transport are distinct evidential steps. That paper still proposed a possible role in membrane-skeleton linkage. [Original 1988 study](https://jscholarship.library.jhu.edu/items/e0d285cd-8397-4a46-b4da-2a849c98efa7)

In 1992, Preston and colleagues tested function through controlled expression: oocytes expressing CHIP28 acquired greater water permeability. The sequence from an unusual rate, to protein isolation, to a functional change after expression shows how an observation leads to a hypothesis and a test of its mechanism. [Original 1992 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1373524/)

</details>

<details>
<summary>Net flow, pressure and water potential</summary>

The relative-unit model uses net uptake = permeability × (internal minus external osmotic concentration − internal minus external pressure). This linear relation assumes isothermal, approximately ideal conditions. Concentrated or non-ideal mixtures require a more complete treatment. The opposing arrows are a phenomenological decomposition whose difference equals the computed net flow; they are not measured molecular crossing counts. [Original theoretical study by Cardoso and Cartwright](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.8077)

The blue particles and gold trace follow reproducible unbiased random walks without reading the concentration gradient. Their paths obey the same membrane geometry that is rendered. Brown squares represent solute retained over the observation interval. A small set of trajectories illustrates thermal motion, not the full molecular population, water potential or physical flow rate. The membrane detail and apparatus have different length scales. The funnel uses a generic semipermeable material. The plant detail summarizes protoplast water permeability without separately resolving the plasma membrane and tonoplast.

The funnel conserves total water volume and each side's retained solute. Its internal and external levels follow their respective cross-sectional areas; their difference determines pressure. The gauze control depicts free exchange after mixing and omits the initial transient.

The cell model conserves impermeant solute while its concentration changes inversely with volume. The plant wall stretches slightly and develops pressure after contact. Real wall anisotropy, viscoelasticity and growth are omitted. The red-cell rupture threshold is illustrative, not a physiological measurement. Damaged plant cells may fail to recover.

<details>
<summary>Why a water channel is not an active pump</summary>

A channel reduces resistance to water passage and can accelerate uptake or loss. The conditions on the two sides determine the direction. A carrier can also mediate passive transport, so the presence of a protein does not establish active transport. Active transport couples energy release to uphill movement. The idealized ATP-powered pump and its no-ATP control demonstrate that distinction; not every form of active transport consumes ATP directly.

At the end of the passive comparison, a tracked pair continues exchanging while average concentrations remain stable. Protein shapes, exchange paths and the ATP symbol explain principles rather than the molecular conformation of a specific transporter. Membrane voltage, leakage and regulation are omitted, so this is not a quantitative model of an actual ion's electrochemical equilibrium.

</details>
