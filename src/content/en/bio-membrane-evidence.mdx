<h2 id="understand">Evidence requires an inferential step</h2>

The association between lipid solubility and permeability supported a lipid component. Extracted red-cell lipids spreading over roughly twice the membrane area supported a bilayer, subject to reliable extraction and area measurement. Interfacial-tension comparisons suggested a contribution from proteins.

Dark–light–dark electron contrast is not direct chemical identification of a protein–lipid–protein sandwich. Later redistribution of cell-surface markers challenged a static uniform coat. The fluid mosaic model connects a bilayer, embedded proteins and lateral mobility.

<h2 id="try">Ask each observation a specific question</h2>

- Track the brown head during lipid spreading and confirm molecular number is conserved.
- Immobilize membrane proteins in the fusion control and compare label redistribution.
- Broaden the electron-image response and examine band separation without treating contrast as a molecular label.
- Check extracellular carbohydrates, water-facing heads, inward tails and spanning proteins in the structural model.

<summary>Which numbers are historical?</summary>

Frye and Edidin observed antigen redistribution at 37 °C; more than 90% of heterokaryons showed extensive mixing by 40 minutes. This animation uses reproducible angular diffusion to preserve marker identity and illustrate lateral motion. Its diffusivity is not fitted to original trajectories, and a circumference simplifies a membrane surface.

Permeability rates, relative interfacial tensions and contrast profiles are explanatory models. Droplet pressure follows the Laplace relation as tension changes; this is not the historical apparatus. Ideal lipid spreading assigns equal footprints to identical molecules. Extraction, compression and cell-area estimation introduce real experimental uncertainties. Molecular drawings are not atomic-scale measurements.
