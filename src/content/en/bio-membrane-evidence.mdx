<h2 id="understand">An unseen structure can still face a test</h2>

A bilayer is not established by unfolding a drawing that already contains two layers. First estimate the total red-cell surface area A, then measure the area B covered by extracted lipids spread into a monolayer. With equal molecular footprints and complete extraction, one original layer predicts B approximately equal to A; two layers predict approximately 2A. Comparing those predictions with independent observations supplies the argument.

Different evidence answers different questions. An association between lipid solubility and passive permeability suggests a lipid component. Area constrains layer count; water affinity constrains molecular orientation. Low interfacial tension can suggest a protein contribution without uniquely establishing a continuous coat. Dark–light–dark electron contrast does not directly name its chemical components.

Marker mixing adds a dynamic test. Fixed positions predict continued separation, yet later samples show mixing. The fluid mosaic combines such evidence with protein properties and localization studies. Some components can move laterally while membrane sidedness remains meaningful; this does not imply unrestricted motion of every protein.

<h2 id="try">Change one assumption and make a prediction</h2>

- **Area:** losing extracted lipid leaves A unchanged but lowers B/A. Why does this prevent an unconditional inference from one ratio?
- **Permeability:** with diffusivity, thickness and starting concentrations fixed, increasing partition coefficient K increases initial net flux. Charged solutes and carrier-mediated transport need different models.
- **Imaging:** broaden the imaging response and contrast peaks widen. Their underlying source remains unchanged, and its chemical identity is still not established.
- **Motion and materials:** anchor one marker; the others can still move. Rigid wire can represent bilayer geometry but not changing neighbors. A sliding connection also does not reproduce real membrane dynamics.

<details>
<summary>Why are the original areas not exactly doubled?</summary>

In Table I of Gorter and Grendel's 1925 paper, human samples 18 and 19 both have an estimated total cell area of 0.47 m². Their spread lipid areas are 0.92 and 0.89 m², giving ratios of approximately 1.96 and 1.89. A includes the whole outer surface of all sampled cells, not one projected face. The factor of two does not mean the front and back of a red cell.

The film preserves the independent observations before introducing hypotheses. Its later 40 equal-footprint lipids are a geometric illustration, not a historical molecular count. Extraction, compression and cell-area estimation affect the inference. An ideal bilayer with only half its lipid recovered can also produce a ratio of one.

<details>
<summary>The fusion study did not record these continuous molecular paths</summary>

Frye and Edidin used indirect fluorescent antibodies to examine fused cells sampled at different times. At 37 °C, source markers were largely separate at 5 min; by 40 min, more than 90% of heterokaryons showed extensive mixing. The film's two historical panels are representative reconstructions of distinct samples, without shared molecular identities or a continuous morph.

The original study also tested explanations using inhibitors and temperature conditions. The continuously moving ringed marker appears later in an explicitly labeled explanatory model. Random angular diffusion on a one-dimensional closed path preserves source identity. Angular D has units of radians squared per teaching-time unit; radius changes only the display. This is not a fitted physical diffusivity on a two-dimensional membrane surface, and it does not claim to exclude every alternative pathway.

<details>
<summary>What do the independent calculations represent?</summary>

The passive solubility–diffusion model uses P = DK/h and computes forward, reverse and net flux between finite aqueous pools of equal volume. It neglects substantial membrane storage, carriers, electric fields and metabolism. Both directional fluxes can remain nonzero at equilibrium while net flux vanishes.

The interface model uses ΔP = 2γ/R for an ideal spherical oil droplet. The coverage–tension relation is a qualitative interpolation, not historical data or a mechanical model of a living cell with a cytoskeleton. Image pixels and their profile share a normalized Gaussian-blur model; it explains imaging response, not chemical identity. The protein's water-facing ends, nonpolar waist and extracellular carbohydrate establish spatial constraints, not an atomic calculation.
