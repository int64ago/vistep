<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

This film covers metallic bonding and electron-gas theory on pages 86–87, and graphite as a mixed crystal on page 90 of PEP Selective Compulsory 2. These are consecutive mechanism demonstrations. Positive ion cores and a shared electronic region distinguish delocalized metallic valence electrons from localized bonding pairs. See the [University of Texas chemistry course](https://ch301.cm.utexas.edu/imfs/solids/metallic-solids.html). The colored region is neither liquid nor measured charge density. The whole metal is not net positively charged.

Layer slip uses a two-dimensional triangular close-packed section. Three rows shift by one spacing while the minimum necessary lift prevents cross-boundary spheres from overlapping. The shared region remains connected. Real plasticity commonly involves dislocations and other microstructural processes, as outlined by [Cambridge DoITPoMS](https://www.doitpoms.ac.uk/tlplib/miller_indices/uses.php). Stress, hardening and dislocation fields are not solved, and synchronized layer motion is not real plasticity dynamics.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

Conduction is shown through distributions and ensemble response. The normalized Drude equation dv/dt=−E−v/τ gives v(t)=−Eτ(1−exp(−t/τ)) for zero initial drift, with conventional current j=−v. Charge magnitude, mass and number density are normalized to 1; j₀ is the current scale at reference field and relaxation time. Reversing the field reverses current and drift. Zero field produces zero drift. See the [UCSB solid-state lecture notes](https://web.physics.ucsb.edu/~phys123B/w2015/lecture1.pdf).

The Gaussian distribution is a classical teaching illustration, with width 1.3√(T/T₀) and conserved unit area. It is not the true Fermi velocity distribution of a metal. The scattering comparison assumes τ/τ₀=T₀/T at fixed field and density, an approximate high-temperature trend. Residual resistivity, impurities, band changes and superconductivity are excluded. A broader random-velocity distribution can coexist with smaller mean drift; faster thermal motion does not necessarily mean more current.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

Graphite uses honeycomb coordinates with the textbook’s 142 pm in-plane distance, 335 pm interlayer spacing and AB stacking. Translating one sheet by a lattice vector preserves atom identities and intralayer bond lengths. The finite boundary is a viewing cut, not the bulk coordination environment. Interlayer energy, friction and real time are not simulated.

The orbital close-up selects a genuine six-membered cycle from that same lattice. Meridional p_z sections follow qualitative |z|exp(−r/a)=constant contours. Drawing scale and threshold are illustrative. Colors denote wavefunction phase, not charge. Superimposed atomic-orbital sections are not a computed graphite band structure or total charge density.

A normalized tensor σ=diag(1,1,0.02) acts on E=(cosθ,0,sinθ), giving J=σE and longitudinal conductivity cos²θ+0.02sin²θ. **The ratio 0.02 is a teaching parameter, not a measured graphite sample.** Arrows show direction only; read magnitude from the numerical evidence. Actual anisotropy depends on structure, defects and temperature. Both [early cross-plane measurements](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.103.544) and [modern stacking-fault transport research](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms6837) establish finite cross-plane conduction. The textbook’s absolute wording is a simplification of strong anisotropy, not evidence that cross-plane conductivity is exactly zero.
