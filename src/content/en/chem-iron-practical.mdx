<section id="understand">
## Seven experiments with distinct observations

This film preserves the order of Practical Activity 2: iron displacing copper from CuSO₄; ferric chloride with copper; ferric ions oxidizing KI followed by starch; ferrous salt with zinc; acidified permanganate reduced by ferrous ions followed by KSCN; a ferric/thiocyanate positive comparison; and ferric ions treated with iron before testing.

Fe⁰, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ interconvert with different partners. Copper deposits, dissolving copper, blue starch–iodine and fading permanganate each report a particular material. Thiocyanate colour is a coordination change, so colour alone does not establish redox.

<section id="try">
## Limiting reactants determine what remains

Change extent and co-reactant supply within one experiment while checking the iron-state account. Fe³⁺/Cu is 2:1, Fe³⁺/I⁻ is 1:1, and Fe²⁺/MnO₄⁻ is 5:1. Insufficient iron powder leaves ferric ions available for a red response.

If permanganate remains, the model does not enter the textbook’s subsequent KSCN step, which follows disappearance of purple. Starch blue requires iodine first. Each chapter represents a new independent tube, without silently carrying material from a preceding experiment.

<summary>Stoichiometry, coordination and real conditions</summary>

The model independently conserves Fe, Cu, Zn, I and Mn and assumes sufficient acid where required. In particular, 2Fe³⁺ + Fe → 3Fe²⁺ includes iron entering from the metal; total dissolved iron cannot be held constant. Competing reactions, passivation, mass transfer and rates are omitted, and colours are qualitative.

Colour development reuses the independent two-complex equilibrium model with illustrative constants at 25°C and zero ionic strength from [original research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ica.2017.06.017). The displayed equation gives only the first coordination step; further species can exist, and colour strength is not measured absorbance. [RSC’s iron experiment](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/oxidation-states-of-iron/517.article) supports starch–iodine, permanganate and thiocyanate observations. [Original copper-etching research](https://research.me.udel.edu/~prasad/papers/2006_JMM_Zhang.pdf) supports ferric oxidation of copper.

Textbook: Required Book 1, page 90, PDF page 95. All seven sub-experiments and their order were checked in the body text. Illustrative amounts explain evidence and mechanisms rather than prescribe a laboratory procedure.
