## Build an oxidation-state map from distinct evidence

Copper grows on an iron wire while iron enters the water as Fe²⁺. Change the reactants to copper metal and Fe³⁺, and copper now dissolves: copper supplies the electrons and iron three accepts them. The element names alone do not distinguish these reactions. The reacting species and their partners matter.

The film preserves all seven sub-experiments in Practical Activity 2, in textbook order. Each uses a fresh sample. Follow the material first, then record its route between Fe⁰, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺. The node positions mean formal oxidation states, not energy. The map records these reactions; it is not a universal reaction-feasibility solver.

The key comparison concerns iron two. With zinc, Fe²⁺ accepts two electrons and becomes metal. With acidified permanganate, each Fe²⁺ gives up one electron and becomes Fe³⁺. **A redox role belongs to a particular reaction, rather than being a permanent label for iron two.**

<section id="try">

## Attribute the colour, then account for what remains

In the iodide reaction, the blue response after starch belongs to the iodine-containing system. The tracked iron remains in the water. Blue is not a specific test for iron two. In the SCN⁻ control, coordination changes the colour while the central iron stays formally plus three, so the record remains at the same oxidation-state node.

Use the explicit material presets to test a new prediction: can one metal iron atom facing five Fe³⁺ still leave a red response? The metal supplies only two electron credits. Two Fe³⁺ become Fe²⁺, and the donor also enters solution. The result is three Fe²⁺ plus three remaining Fe³⁺. **Partial reduction and a subsequent red response are compatible.** These integer units are teaching amounts, not the textbook reagent doses.

The permanganate view preserves the textbook order: the SCN⁻ test follows disappearance of purple. Excess permanganate leaves the test pending, rather than treating the more complicated coexistence of reagents as the same diagnostic. Watch and explore keep separate states.

<summary>Atom and electron accounts, with their limits</summary>

The acidic net reaction is:

5Fe²⁺ + MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ → 5Fe³⁺ + Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O.

Each of five iron two ions supplies one electron. The accepting account includes the oxygen-containing permanganate and acid. A separate close view preserves one manganese, four oxygen and eight hydrogen identities. This is a conservative **net-composition allocation**, not an elementary mechanism, isotope-provenance result or measured rate. Hydration and bulk solvent water are omitted. Other manganese products outside the stated acidic branch are not predicted.

The marked credits travel on an accounting rail. They describe electron allocation, not free electrons flying through water. Credits in transit remain in the charge account. A metal atom keeps its identity when it leaves the solid; the dissolved product is not a duplicated atom.

Colours are qualitative. The iodine view represents an iodine/iodide/starch host system, without claiming a unique I₃⁻ structure or measured absorbance. Fe²⁺ coordination and complete thiocyanate equilibria are omitted; this does not imply that Fe²⁺ can never bind SCN⁻. Concentration, acidity, chloride coordination, competing reactions, passivation and transport can affect real observations. Conservation alone cannot establish feasibility or mechanism.
