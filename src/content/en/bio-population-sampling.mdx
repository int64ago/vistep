<h2 id="understand">Catches and records are not population totals</h2>

A light trap detects selected insects through their responses to light. Changing activity conditions changes catches while retaining the same simulated population. Insects follow the baffle, funnel and collection chamber, folding their wings through the narrow neck. Pale and darker insects represent stronger and weaker attraction. Weather, effort, light source and the target organism affect detection; catch is not total abundance.

Mark–recapture preserves identity. Sixteen animals are initially captured, marked and released before a second sample of twelve. The same 48 simulated individuals remain present in habitat or holding tray throughout. Recognized marks determine the ratio estimate. Sampling variation or mark loss can change that estimate; a single sample need not recover the known simulation total exactly.

<h2 id="try">Sample actual spatial objects</h2>

The 10×10 m simulated meadow contains 80 target plants plus background herbs. Each quadrat covers 1 m². Coordinates are selected before plants are counted. Resampling changes the selected plots while retaining plant identities and locations. More observations can add information, but do not repair systematically selecting only dense patches.

A close-up uses one fixed quadrat in the same meadow to explain boundaries. Count by the stem base: **include top and left edges, exclude bottom and right edges**. Adjacent plots then avoid double-counting shared boundaries. This deliberately selected teaching close-up is not described as a random draw.

“Enter field counts” replaces the simulated field with blank records for actual observations. Missing values do not become zero counts. Density is the total count divided by the corresponding recorded area. A real report should also preserve coordinates, species, date, plot area, selection method and incomplete records.

Camera, scat-DNA and acoustic views compare evidence about individuals. Repeated images of matching same-flank patterns need not represent different animals. Multiple microsatellite allele combinations offer another basis for matching. Stable vocal features can support suitable individual-monitoring studies, but not every sound identifies an individual reliably. Six illustrative records contain three confirmed identities and one unresolved record. A confirmed minimum is not the full population total.

<h2 id="deeper">Use an estimator with its assumptions</h2>

The textbook example uses M=39 marked animals, n=34 recaptures and r=15 recognized marks, producing N≈M×n/r=88.4. Over a 1 ha study area, the corresponding density is 88.4 animals/ha. Tests use this independent benchmark. Zero recognized marks do not produce a finite ratio estimate or a population of zero. Approximate closure, mark retention, mixing and capture opportunities need assessment; real studies may require more appropriate estimators.

Quadrat counts come directly from plant coordinates. A census of all 100 grid cells recovers 80 plants and 0.8 plants/m², independently checking area and boundary accounting. Smaller random samples have sampling error; no particular draw is guaranteed to outperform another estimate.

All habitats, patterns, allele values and waveforms are original teaching examples, **not field photographs, real genotypes or recordings**. Feature comparison illustrates identity logic rather than a validated recognition system. Real monitoring must address genotyping error, degradation, noise, mistaken identity and incomplete detection.
