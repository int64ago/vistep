## A hole is not squeezed empty space

For free, uniform heating of an isotropic plate, every length changes by the same fraction. Both outer and inner rims move outward. Imagine replacing the removed disk: it would expand with the plate, so the hole must accommodate the larger disk.

Local heating, temperature gradients and restraints can produce different deformations. Hole growth here depends on the stated uniform, free conditions. Material markers track positions; they do not depict individual atoms growing in radius.

## Calculate both parts separately

Heating the plate while keeping the plug cold increases radial clearance. Subsequently warming a plug of the same material removes most of that extra clearance. Initial radii are 20 mm for the hole and 19.98 mm for the plug. With the aluminium parameters and a 100 K rise, plate-only heating gives 70 μm clearance; equal heating gives 20.05 μm.

Exploration switches among plate, clearance detail and rod, with temperature, material and rod-length inputs. Plate and rod dimensional changes are enlarged 100 times. The clearance detail has a fixed micrometre scale. Readouts retain physical dimensions.

## Strain meets a boundary condition

<div className="formula">
  ε_thermal = αΔT, ΔL_free = L₀αΔT
  <br />
  ε_total = ε_elastic + ε_thermal
  <br />σ = E(ε_total − αΔT)
</div>

A free rod has total strain αΔT and zero axial stress. Ideal rigid restraints impose zero total strain and stress −EαΔT. Heating produces compression; cooling produces tension. A temperature difference has the same numerical value in kelvins and Celsius degrees; this is not a law in absolute temperature.

Representative aluminium values are α=25×10⁻⁶/K and E=70 GPa; steel uses α=12×10⁻⁶/K and E=200 GPa. Real alloys depend on composition and temperature. Three-dimensional uniform scaling gives volume ratio (1+αΔT)³ and reciprocal density ratio. The familiar 3αΔT is only its first-order expansion.

Atomic potentials, transient heat conduction, contact interference, yielding, buckling and flexible supports are omitted. The rod is a one-dimensional linear thermoelastic example, not a structural safety calculation.
