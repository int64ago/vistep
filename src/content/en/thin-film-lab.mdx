<h2 id="understand">Two reflections create one set of fringes</h2>

Both soap films and air wedges combine reflections from their boundaries. Propagation and reflection phase must be counted together. The textbook first observes a soap film in salt-yellowed flame light, then compares colors.

<h2 id="try">Change thickness and compare one sheet with two</h2>

Follow monochromatic fringes as thickness varies, then switch to a three-color approximation. Compare the paper supports and fringe spacing within the first 4 mm of the wedge.

<h2 id="deeper">Calculation assumptions</h2>

<details>
<summary>Normal incidence in a symmetric lossless film</summary>

<div className="formula">
  δ = 4πnd/λ; R = 4r²sin²(δ/2) / [(1−r²)²+4r²sin²(δ/2)]; wedge Δx = λ/(2α)
</div>

Here r is the amplitude reflection coefficient from the outer medium to the film. Soap index is 1.333 between air; the air wedge has index 1 between glass of index 1.5. R sums multiple reflections analytically. The two drawn waves compare only the first reflected phases, not the full field amplitude. Incidence is near normal; dispersion and absorption are omitted. Color samples 650, 530 and 460 nm and is not calibrated colorimetry.

Soap thickness is d=(30+1250y)D nm, with normalized downward position y and adjustable teaching parameter D. It is not a fluid-dynamics solution. Sheets are 40 μm thick and plates 80 mm long; gap d=x·40N/80 μm. The side-view angle is enlarged and the fringe inset covers only the first 4 mm. Spacer removal interpolates static geometries rather than modeling mechanics.

</details>
