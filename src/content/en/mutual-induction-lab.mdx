<h2 id="understand">A shared magnetic path, separate electrical circuits</h2>

Two windings sit on the iron ring. One connects to a source and switch; the other connects to a sensitive meter. Their wires never join. Closing the switch builds the primary current and changes the flux linking the secondary. Once direct current settles, the magnetic field remains but the induced emf falls to zero. Opening the switch reduces the flux and reverses the meter response.

The nested-coil experiment repeats induction continuously. An audio-frequency source drives the inner winding, while the outer winding feeds a receiver. The alternating current produces an alternating secondary emf. Withdraw the inner coil and let it stop: weaker coupling produces a weaker received signal.

<h2 id="try">Separate current from its rate of change</h2>

- Compare the iron ring immediately after closing, at steady direct current, and after opening.
- Withdraw the audio coil while preserving the drive frequency and amplitude.
- At fixed insertion, raise the frequency and compare the induced voltage amplitude.
