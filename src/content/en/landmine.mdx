<h2 id="understand">Why can danger remain after a war ends?</h2>

Grass, leaves and soil can conceal what lies beneath ordinary ground. When a teaching section opens, a complete landmine casing becomes visible. **The mine has not moved; our view has changed.** This is not an excavation, detection or disposal demonstration.

The original procedural reconstruction takes its exterior reference from the [Smithsonian National Museum of American History's Model 43 Tellermine collection](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/nmah_407167). Its circular casing, top cover, rolled edges and side handle remain connected, while the interior stays closed. The reference is a type of anti-tank mine. Landmines differ in shape and activation, and this exterior cannot identify another object's type or condition.

A landmine connects certain external events to the rapid release of stored energy. **Triggering is an event, not the source of all the released energy**: the explosive energy was stored chemically beforehand. The film neither presses the object nor shows internal activation construction or blast propagation.

The [ICRC's explanation of enduring hazards](https://www.icrc.org/en/article/landmines-and-explosive-remnants-enduring-human-cost-measured-decades) describes their lasting effects on communities. An ended conflict, returning vegetation or an aged exterior does not replace professional assessment.

<h2 id="try">Observe the same casing; never test a real object</h2>

- Choose “Exterior” to inspect the complete silhouette, coating and attached handle. The camera can rotate; the object stays in place and remains closed.
- In “Soil section,” switch between the surface and teaching views. Uncut earth stays opaque, and a supporting soil layer remains in contact with the casing beneath it. The section changes only the lesson's visibility.
- Revisit “Vegetation.” The same deterministic grass and leaf positions persist while the visible cover changes; the object is never placed again.
- Move “Time passing” to its end. The state remains unknown. The timeline has no automatic safe moment.

These controls do not teach identification, activation, detection or disposal. If you encounter a suspicious object, **do not approach or touch it; contact local authorities or a professional mine-action organization**. Follow local official risk guidance. [UNMAS risk education](https://unmas.org/en/news/unmas-south-sudan-provides-life-saving-risk-education-to-sudanese-refugees) emphasizes these basic behaviors.

<h2 id="deeper">What can a realistic exterior tell us?</h2>

<details>
<summary>Why does visible wear not establish safety?</summary>

Coating, wear, corrosion and attached soil are exterior observations. They cannot reliably show that an interior is inert. The film's textures are fixed visual details, not a calculation of danger with age. Old does not mean safe; intact does not mean touchable.

Landmines and other explosive remnants can remain dangerous for years or decades. The [ICRC's account of lasting impacts](https://www.icrc.org/en/article/landmines-impact-communities) explains why recovering surroundings do not establish that hazards have gone away.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How far does the energy explanation go?</summary>

The film connects an external event, triggering and stored energy release. Landmines do not all activate in the same way. This explanation includes no specific conditions, thresholds, reactive materials or real timescale. It does not model blast dynamics, fragments or injury ranges.

A still, complete casing does not reveal its internal energy state. A realistic rendering helps viewers understand space, concealment and contact, but cannot substitute for a professional assessment.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do the camera, soil and exterior stay consistent?</summary>

The mine's position remains fixed. Earth, surface, grass and leaves share one observation cut. The supporting soil height derives from the casing's lowest point. Close views preserve the complete closed exterior and side handle instead of dismantling the object. The 3D view, 2D exterior view and catalog cover share the same casing profiles.

Surface changes and camera framing reconstruct deterministically from lesson time. Pausing or seeking never randomizes object positions. This is not a real-site reconstruction and contains no scale or coordinates for field assessment, or any assembly, installation or removal procedure. Real assessment and treatment belong with the appropriate local authorities and professionals.

</details>
