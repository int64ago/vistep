---
title: What changes before equilibrium shifts?
description: Separate an immediate concentration change, subsequent chemical relaxation and the color of a mixture.
---

<h2 id="understand">Zero net change does not mean zero reaction</h2>

With equal temperature, volume and atom totals, nitrogen plus hydrogen and an equivalent amount of ammonia can approach the same equilibrium composition. Equal forward and reverse rates need not be zero, and different species need not have equal concentrations.

Changing conditions introduces another distinction. Expanding a sealed syringe first dilutes nitrogen dioxide, lightening it; subsequent dimer dissociation partly darkens it. Compression first darkens it, then chemical relaxation partly lightens it. The second stage cannot replace the entire sequence or prove that the final sample is lighter than before compression. The side-view optical path is fixed.

<h2 id="try">Retain initial conditions, references and identities</h2>

1. Change the initial ammonia-system composition and catalytic acceleration. Check conserved atoms, two nonzero equilibrium rates and the shared endpoint.
2. Seek to the moment when the syringe piston stops, then compare the later relaxed state.
3. Compare the 400 °C and 500 °C ammonia time curves, then inspect textbook pressure entries at different temperatures. Catalysis speeds the illustrative approach only.
4. Follow every activity: iron-salt additions, subsequent iron powder, base then acid in the same chromium sample, heating/cooling copper solution, and exchange of two identified gas flasks.
5. Change chromium drop counts. Check cumulative volume and whether the addition reverses the earlier acid–base conditions. Your own observation record starts blank.

<h2 id="deeper">Five textbook positions, distinct evidence</h2>

Coverage includes elective 1 pp32–34 on dynamic equilibrium, experiment 2-2 on pressure, experiment 2-3 on temperature, pp46–48 on ammonia conditions and every system in activity 1, pp55–56. Dynamic models and industrial data analysis are identified separately from student experiments.

The dynamic ammonia model fixes volume at 1 L, starting with 1 mol N₂ plus 3 mol H₂, or 2 mol NH₃. Formal rates are `vforward=0.01[N₂][H₂]³` and `vreverse=0.04[NH₃]²`, with corresponding coefficient units and illustrative values. This is not a claim that industrial ammonia synthesis is one four-molecule elementary event. Integration conserves both elements; the catalyst multiplies both rate coefficients equally.

For `2NO₂⇌N₂O₄`, the model uses the textbook dimerization enthalpy of −56.9 kJ/mol. [Purdue's temperature demonstration](https://chemed.chem.purdue.edu/demos/demosheets/16.3.html) supports the color trend and thermodynamic reasoning. The 25 °C dissociation value `Kc=0.01 mol/L` is an illustrative calibration, adjusted through a constant-enthalpy temperature approximation and gas-concentration conversion. It is not a reported textbook measurement. Each sample contains 0.4 mmol of NO₂ equivalents, preserving `n(NO₂)+2n(N₂O₄)`.

The syringe view chooses 40 mL as its expanded volume; the textbook specifies only the 20 mL starting position. Piston movement approximates a rapid isothermal volume change with frozen chemistry, followed by reversible mass-action relaxation. Opacity depends on NO₂ concentration and a fixed optical path. Real rapid compression can also change temperature. [Purdue's syringe demonstration](https://chemed.chem.purdue.edu/demos/demosheets/16.2.html) distinguishes immediate concentration from subsequent reaction; color comparisons also depend on viewing direction. This film specifically uses a fixed side-view path.

The baths approximate rapid thermal equilibration, omitting heat-transfer dynamics. The relatively dilute nitrogen-oxide loading is used for a gas-phase illustration, not extrapolated into condensation or freezing. The connected flasks in experiment 2-3 are clamped before separate baths; separately sealed drawings represent that state. The activity preserves each flask's amount, identity and stopper throughout exchange. Toxic NO₂ is shown as a closed laboratory demonstration, without a home preparation procedure.

Ammonia equilibrium percentages come directly from textbook table 2-2, for an initial nitrogen-to-hydrogen volume ratio of 1:3. At 20 MPa, for example, 400 °C gives 38.2% and 500 °C gives 19.1%. The separate curves use `y(t)=yequilibrium(1−exp(−kt))` with illustrative temperature and catalyst parameters. Discrete data are not extrapolated into an arbitrary high-pressure prediction. Equilibrium mixture volume fraction is not overall factory yield. [BASF's production account](https://www.basf.com/gb/en/media/science-around-us/fertilizer-out-of-thin-air) describes real catalyst-bed and pressure-vessel demands; this film is not equipment design or economic optimization.

The iron mixture starts with 10 mL water and five drops each of 0.05 M FeCl₃ and 0.15 M KSCN, split equally three ways. A drop is assumed to be 0.05 mL. Additions update both volume and material balance. We approximate `Fe³⁺+SCN⁻⇌FeSCN²⁺` with illustrative `K=200 L/mol`; the textbook uses a simplified Fe(SCN)₃ expression. [Purdue's account](https://chemed.chem.purdue.edu/demos/demosheets/18.5.html) describes multiple red complexes, so color does not identify one unique formula. Added iron metal consumes iron(III) through `2Fe³⁺+Fe→3Fe²⁺`, conserving iron atoms including the metal added.

The chromium view retains 2 mL of 0.1 M dichromate, first 6 M NaOH and then 6 M H₂SO₄, defaulting to eight drops each. A two-species equilibrium plus total chromium and nominal acid–base equivalents computes the color mixture. [RSC's experiment](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/a-chromate-dichromate-equilibrium/1710.article) emphasizes coexistence of both forms. Our simplified model omits HCrO₄⁻, activities and real concentrated-sulfuric-acid dissociation; its internal acidity is not presented as measured pH. Chromium(VI) materials require laboratory disposal procedures.

The copper view uses the textbook's 2 mL of 0.5 M CuCl₂, with one heated/cooled tube and one room-temperature reference. A two-form aqua/chloro model conserves total copper and chloride and calculates a mixture color. Remaining aqua species do not vanish. The [University of Sydney demonstration collection](https://scilearn.sydney.edu.au/fychemistry/demonstrations/files/fullset.pdf) describes the green color and mixed aqua/chloro species. The textbook's `[Cu(H₂O)₄]²⁺` drawing is not treated as the sole aqueous coordination structure, and a green solution is not labelled pure yellow `[CuCl₄]²⁻`.

Pages 48–49 also call for cooling, ammonia separation and recycling unreacted feed. The final industrial view uses a finite batch starting with 1 mol N₂ and 3 mol H₂. Each pass applies the 400 °C, 20 MPa equilibrium volume fraction, `y=0.382`, corresponding to nitrogen conversion `x=2y/(1+y)`, followed by ideal complete separation of newly formed ammonia. Collected liquid and remaining gases conserve both elements across three passes. Real condensation efficiency, impurity purges, makeup feed, heat exchange and flow distribution are omitted; this is not a whole-plant simulation.
