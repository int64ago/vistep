<h2 id="understand">Three forms of evidence</h2>

Conditioning begins by separating tests. Food already evokes salivation; an untrained sound does not have the same effect. Repeatedly pairing a predictive sound with food changes the sound-alone response. Presenting that sound without reinforcement subsequently reduces the response. The collection level is a relative illustration, not recorded salivary volume. Training and testing must remain distinct.

Motor-cortex research compares stimulation locations. Different regions of the precentral gyrus relate to different body parts, generally on the opposite side. The leg representation lies above the face representation. A large cortical representation reflects control demands rather than physical body-part size. The drawing is schematic, not three sharply bounded cortical buttons.

The language examples are observational cases. The textbook's S profile preserves comprehension but impairs fluent expression; its H profile preserves speech production while impairing comprehension. Hearing and vocal organs remain intact in those reported comparisons. Functional dissociation suggests different processing contributions, not two isolated switches or experimental injury to people.

<h2 id="try">Preserve the comparison</h2>

Change training trials, unreinforced trials and whether the sound predicts food. Then compare sound-alone and food-alone tests. An unpaired sound does not acquire association in this model, while the basic food response remains.

Switch cortical regions and stimulated sides, checking that the body marker follows the same mapping. In the language cases, identify preserved as well as impaired functions. An individual case does not establish a complete universal network map.

<h2 id="deeper">Declared rules and limits</h2>

Learning follows Vnext = V + α(λ − V), with α=0.25. Predictive pairing uses λ=1; unreinforced or unpaired trials use zero. The independent solution after n training trials is V=1−(1−α)ⁿ. After m unreinforced trials it becomes V(1−α)ᵐ. This selected rule illustrates acquisition and extinction, without modeling recovery, generalization or erasure of all memory.

The motor model preserves position and opposite-side mapping. The language model preserves the textbook's reported functional combinations. Coordinates are not brain-image registration, and colored regions are not surgical localization. Real language depends on connected, individually variable networks.
