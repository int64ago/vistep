<h2 id="understand">The coil does not supply the load current</h2>

This scene chooses a normally-open, monostable DC plunger relay. Its coil builds a magnetic field; the plunger overcomes a return spring and moves an insulating linkage. A copper bridge then joins two fixed load contacts. After de-energization and release, the spring returns the mechanism and opens the contacts. Normally-closed, changeover and latching types are different configurations.

The control coil and switched load have no conductive connection. An independent source supplies the load. Changing that source from 5 V to 12 V alters load power without changing coil current or motion in this model. The relay controls another circuit rather than creating load energy from a small coil current.

<h2 id="try">Why can the same 2 V fail to pull in yet maintain closure?</h2>

Starting from rest at 2 V produces coil current, but magnetic force across the open gap is below spring preload. The plunger stays put and the load stays disconnected. Apply 5 V first, then reduce it to 2 V: the gap is already smaller, so the same holding current produces stronger attraction and maintains closure. Pickup and holding start from different geometries.

The model gap shrinks from 1.00 mm to 0.40 mm across a 0.60 mm stroke. The same computed displacement moves the plunger, insulation and copper bridge. Both moving contact circles meet their fixed partners tangentially at closure. Winding texture is illustrative rather than a turn-by-turn manufacturing drawing; geometry is enlarged to expose gaps and contacts.

Defaults are 800 turns, 125 Ω coil resistance, 30 mm² pole area, 1 g moving mass, 6 mN preload, 15 N/m stiffness and 0.04 N·s/m damping. They construct a teaching example rather than fit a product. High-permeability iron is assumed; saturation, eddy currents, remanence, contact bounce, elastic overtravel, wear and arcs are omitted.

De-energization includes an ideal 0.7 V flyback diode. Coil current first recirculates locally and decays; the spring returns as attraction weakens. Unclamped voltage spikes, arcs and driver ratings are outside the model. The last chapter mechanically blocks the plunger: coil current and attraction remain, yet the contacts do not close. An energized coil alone does not prove load continuity.
