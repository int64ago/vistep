<h2 id="understand">Separate infection, regulatory effects and observations</h2>

Cell-free fungal culture filtrate can reproduce abnormal rice elongation, distinguishing infection from the action of soluble growth-promoting material. In the original reconstruction, hyphae remain at the filter while representative solutes follow the funnel neck into the collector. Matched rice seedlings then provide the comparison. This is historical evidence, not an operational pathogen-culture protocol or a guarantee that an arbitrary real filtrate is sterile.

The cutting and fruit activities ask students to design comparisons and collect observations. Every default root count, length, ethylene amount and firmness value here is a **declared teaching simulation**, not fabricated field data. Actual root observations can replace the examples.

<h2 id="try">Observe individuals before comparing groups</h2>

Begin with broad concentration spacing, then narrow the tested range. Each group contains three cuttings with attached roots. The record table preserves individual counts before calculating means. The best observed group suggests a range for further investigation; it does not establish a universal exact optimum.

“Enter observations” starts with blank entries. Missing values stay missing rather than becoming zeros. Entered counts drive both roots and statistics. Match species, cutting maturity, application method, exposure duration, culture conditions and observation date. A concentration comparison must not simultaneously change exposure duration. All displayed concentrations use arbitrary units, not application rates for NAA, IBA or 2,4-D.

The fruit sequence first contrasts pH-dependent ethephon decomposition, then compares treatment with control at matched initial maturity. Relative firmness accompanies color. The last comparison deliberately changes starting maturity: a softer final fruit cannot automatically be attributed to treatment. Real investigations need replicates, consistent observation times and suitable indicators for the chosen fruit; responses are not universal across fruit types.

<h2 id="deeper">Calculations and limits</h2>

A declared single-peaked function and fixed individual differences generate the default cutting specimens. Statistics use their discrete counts; a theoretical smooth curve does not replace observations. Entered data supersede those defaults. Tests independently check mean and sample standard deviation using 2, 4 and 6, and verify missing-value handling.

The fruit model conserves remaining ethephon precursor plus released amount. A smooth pH-dependent rate illustrates greater stability under acidic conditions. Normalized kinetics then connect external ethylene, endogenous production and maturity. These are not measured product parameters, and pH 3.5 is not implemented as an exact switch. Neither color nor firmness alone establishes complete food quality. No edible-fruit treatment recipe is provided.
