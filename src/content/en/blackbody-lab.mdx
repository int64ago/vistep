<h2 id="understand">Why does radiation turn downward at short wavelengths?</h2>

A small opening in a heated cavity approximates a blackbody. Spectroscopy reveals two changes during heating: emission increases and the peak moves toward shorter wavelengths. Beyond that peak, however, emission falls toward the short-wave end. A theory must explain that turn.

<h2 id="deeper">What changes is the mean energy of each mode</h2>

Classical equipartition assigns each electromagnetic mode mean energy kT. Together with the increasing number of short-wave modes, it gives the Rayleigh–Jeans spectrum. This works in the long-wave limit but diverges toward short wavelengths.

Planck introduced discrete energy units. Higher frequency means larger steps, ε = hν = hc/λ. At the same temperature, high-energy excitations have lower statistical weights, so each mode’s mean energy no longer stays at kT. The energy bars, discrete ticks and mean-energy readout all depend on the same wavelength input. kT is a thermal scale, **not a fixed energy allowance for every oscillator**; quantum suppression has no abrupt wavelength cutoff.
