<h2 id="understand">Fix the current, field and probe directions first</h2>

Conventional current to the right does not imply electrons moving right. In an electron-conducting sample, average drift is leftward. Magnetic dots point out of the screen; crosses point into it.

Magnetic force is `q(v × B)`. For negative electrons drifting left in an outward magnetic field, it points toward the lower edge. Negative charge accumulates there, leaving the upper edge relatively positive and creating a transverse electric field. In steady state, electric force FE balances magnetic force FB, so carriers do not continue accelerating sideways.

**The red probe stays at the lower edge and the black probe at the upper edge.** The reading is `Vlower − Vupper`. It is negative for electron carriers with rightward current and outward field. Swapping the probes would also reverse the measured sign.

<h2 id="try">Change one sign while holding the others fixed</h2>

- Set magnetic field to zero. Longitudinal current remains while transverse voltage vanishes.
- Reverse only the field and voltage reverses. Reverse current as well and the original voltage sign returns.
- Keep conventional current and field fixed, but use positive carriers. Drift turns rightward and magnetic force still points downward. Positive charge now accumulates at the lower edge, reversing voltage polarity.
- Reduce carrier density at fixed current and field. The same current requires faster drift, increasing Hall-voltage magnitude.
- Inspect the transverse forces. Direction arrows are normalized; FE and FB have equal magnitudes and opposite directions.

<h2 id="deeper">One relation determines both sign and scale</h2>

<details>
<summary>From force balance to transverse voltage</summary>

Take x rightward, y upward and B along z out of the screen. With sample width w, thickness d and signed carrier charge q:

<div className="formula">vₓ = I/(nqwd)</div>
<div className="formula">Fmagnetic,y = −qvₓB; E_y = vₓB</div>
<div className="formula">Vlower − Vupper = E_y w = IB/(nqd)</div>

Width cancels from the voltage formula. Thickness, density, current, field and carrier sign still matter. At 10 mA, 0.2 T, n = 10²² m⁻³ and d = 0.5 mm, electron conduction gives approximately −2.50 mV.

Magnetic force is perpendicular to velocity and does no direct work. The longitudinal source maintains current, with Joule heating in a real conductor. Transverse voltage does not mean the magnetic field continuously supplies energy to electrons.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Limits of the single-carrier model</summary>

The uniform sample is 20 mm long, 8 mm wide and 0.5 mm thick, with one dominant carrier type. Mobility is 0.1 m²/(V·s), providing a consistent longitudinal Ohmic response. Default drift speed is about 1.56 m/s. Each film chapter expands only 20 ms of drift, making motion much slower on screen.

Dots represent selected tracers, not every carrier; density is given numerically. Changes of field, density and carrier sign compare steady configurations. Edge-charge buildup and thermal motion are not simulated.

Multiple bands, simultaneous electron and hole transport, Hall scattering factors, contact errors, anomalous Hall and quantum Hall effects are omitted. Inferring density from a measurement therefore requires checking the single-carrier approximation and sample thickness, among other conditions.

</details>
