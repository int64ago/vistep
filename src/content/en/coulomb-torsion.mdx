<h2 id="understand">From visible deflection to a controlled force comparison</h2>

Like-charged spheres repel. A suspended sphere settles where electric force, gravity and the string constraint balance. Bringing the other sphere nearer generally increases deflection, but the suspended sphere also moves. The support displacement is therefore not the final centre-to-centre separation.

A torsion balance turns a tiny electric force into a measurable twist. Following the textbook's null procedure, rotate the upper end of the suspension until sphere A returns to its reference line. This restores the prescribed separation and lever arm. At null, κφ = LF, so twist is proportional to electric force.

With both charges fixed, halving separation gives four times the force. With separation fixed, touching A with an identical neutral conducting sphere and separating them approximates a charge of Q/2 on A; the force becomes half as large. These controlled comparisons establish F ∝ q₁q₂/r².

<h2 id="try">Make returning to the reference position a necessary step</h2>

- Change the support separation in the suspended-sphere apparatus. Follow both the actual separation and the deflection.
- In the torsion apparatus, lower the null adjustment to zero. A leaves the reference line, changing both separation and lever arm. Restore the adjustment to 100% before comparing twists.
- Drag the charge-sharing sequence. Sphere D gains charge only after contact with A, and A retains half after separation.

This group follows pages 6–8 of chapter 9, section 2 in PEP Required Book 3. The glass enclosure, insulating arm and adjustable suspension preserve the apparatus relationships. Spheres are enlarged for phone readability.

<h2 id="deeper">Conditions behind the model</h2>

<details>
<summary>Point charges, the null method and charge sharing</summary>

The model treats spheres as point charges, calculates their actual centre geometry and solves electric and suspension torques together. Before null, the display reports the actual force magnitude; the upper twist alone is not a force measurement. At null, the arm is perpendicular to the line between charges, giving κφ = LF.

Equal charge sharing assumes identical isolated conductors sufficiently far from external influence. D approaches along the arm and charge exchange is modeled separately; detailed surface redistribution caused by the third sphere is omitted. The separated equilibrium is the comparison state. The textbook notes that Coulomb originally used elder-pith balls. This follows its modern schematic rather than claiming a historical materials reconstruction.

Motion shows a sequence of quasi-static equilibria, not actual oscillation or adjustment speed. Leakage, breakdown and damping are omitted. Charges and suspension stiffness are teaching parameters, not an instrument calibration.

</details>
