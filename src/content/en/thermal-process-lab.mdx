<h2 id="understand">Different thermal processes leave different evidence</h2>

Joule heating follows I²Rt. Two identical calorimeters make controlled comparisons visible: doubling current at fixed resistance gives four times the input energy; doubling resistance at fixed current gives twice the energy. This modern teaching apparatus explains the historical result cited on PEP Required Book3 page80. It is not a reconstruction of Joule’s original instrument.

A hot egg cools in cold water while the water warms. The ideal insulated two-body system conserves energy, and heat transfer follows the temperature difference. Starting at equal temperatures does not spontaneously separate them into hot and cold again. This follows the directionality discussion on page93.

Iron emits thermal radiation even at room temperature. Heating strengthens its visible emission until it glows. No visible glow does not mean no radiation. The solid-iron model stops at1750 K; its spectral peak remains infrared throughout. Page124 uses glowing iron to introduce thermal radiation and energy quantization, not to assert that the spectral peak has already entered visible wavelengths.

<h2 id="try">Control one variable at a time</h2>

The left calorimeter stays at2 A and4 Ω. Change the right current or resistance while keeping heating duration equal. Both thermometers use the same heat capacity and environmental loss model; input energy differs from energy retained in the water.

For egg cooling, change water mass and contact time, then begin at equal temperatures. For iron, change absolute temperature and compare peak wavelength and visible radiation fraction. Spectra are individually normalized to their peaks to compare shapes; their plotted heights do not compare total emitted power.

<details>
<summary>Why these values are not experimental measurements</summary>

Each calorimeter has600 J/K heat capacity,20 °C surroundings and0.25 W/K thermal conductance. It obeys C dT/dt = I²R − G(T−Tambient), so input energy equals stored-energy increase plus environmental loss. The source maintains selected current, resistance is constant, and boiling/evaporation are excluded. Real measurements require calibration of heat capacity, losses and resistance variation.

Egg heat capacity is200 J/K; water specific heat is4200 J/(kg·K); inter-body conductance is2 W/K. Both bodies are treated as uniform-temperature lumps, neglecting the cup and environment. Real eggs have internal gradients. Equilibrium temperature is heat-capacity weighted, not generally the arithmetic mean.

Iron uses a greybody Planck spectrum with emissivity0.8 and integrates380–780 nm for visible fraction. Display color uses three spectral samples at fixed illustrative exposure; display saturation is not a temperature/color calibration. Temperature is prescribed rather than obtained from furnace heating dynamics, and the upper bound stays below iron’s melting point.
