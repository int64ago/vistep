<section id="understand">

## Temperature changes the solvent’s capacity

The textbook investigation dissolves crude benzoic acid with heating, filters hot, cools to crystallize, then filters, washes, dries and weighs the product. Sand is removed during hot filtration, salt mainly remains in mother liquor, and temperature-dependent acid solubility enables crystal formation.

Textbook solubilities at 25, 50 and 75°C are 0.34, 0.85 and 2.2 g per 100 g water. Cold solubility is not zero, so some acid remains dissolved. Neither crystal appearance nor mass recovery alone establishes purity.

</section>

<section id="try">

## Locate each loss

Change water quantity and filtration temperature. Too little water prevents full dissolution; premature cooling can retain acid on the first filter. More water leaves more acid dissolved after cooling.

Increase cold wash quantity and compare retained salt with acid lost to washing. The chloride view explains silver-chloride evidence without a calibrated visual detection limit. Parameters are teaching comparisons, not food-additive preparation or purity certification.

</section>

<section id="deeper">

<details>
<summary>Textbook data, material balance and detection limits</summary>

The textbook specifies 1.0 g crude material and 50 mL water. The model chooses 0.90 g acid, 0.07 g NaCl and 0.03 g sand; impurity proportions were not measured by the textbook. Water density is approximated as 1 g/mL. Piecewise linear interpolation stays between the 25–75°C solubility anchors.

Dissolved acid cannot exceed the selected temperature’s capacity. Undissolved or prematurely crystallized acid stays with sand during hot filtration. Cooling crystals equal acid in filtrate minus acid retained in mother liquor. The small salt amount is assumed fully soluble and excluded from an ideal acid crystal lattice. Coprecipitation, adsorption, supersaturation and nucleation delays are omitted. Needle shapes and marker counts are not quantitative particle predictions.

A crystal cake retains a chosen 2 mL mother liquor. Washing re-equilibrates acid at the 25°C capacity, allowing warmer retained liquor to recrystallize before accounting for additional crystal dissolution, followed by one perfectly mixed wash and the same retained-liquid amount. Drying leaves retained nonvolatile solutes in the product and transfers water to an evaporation account. Acid, salt and water are separately conserved across hot filter cake, mother liquor, wash, drying and product. With no crystals, no fictitious retained cake liquor is added.

Textbook mass recovery is dry solid divided by crude sample mass. Model purity is product acid divided by dry solid. Recovery of the original acid is a third measure. Values arise from the stated assumptions rather than experimental measurements.

A dissolved sample can be tested under dilute-nitric-acid conditions using Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl↓. Residual NaCl gives a theoretical AgCl upper amount. Background, reagent errors and interferences are omitted. Turbidity is schematic, with no visual detection-limit calibration. No visible solid does not imply exactly zero chloride, and this test alone does not establish overall benzoic-acid purity.

</details>
