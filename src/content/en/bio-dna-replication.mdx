<h2 id="understand">Compare distinguishable predictions first</h2>

Nitrogen-15 and nitrogen-14 are stable isotopes. E. coli is first grown under heavy labeling, then transferred to light medium. DNA is extracted across generations. Equilibrium centrifugation in cesium chloride produces bands at matching buoyant densities; it does not simply pellet the bacteria.

A single intermediate band after one generation differs from conservative copying’s heavy-plus-light prediction. Intermediate and light bands after two generations agree with semiconservative copying: each daughter contains one old and one newly synthesized strand. The images reconstruct qualitative textbook observations and ideal predictions, not original scans or molecular-weight measurements from geometric tube lengths.

- Pause the fork and check that both synthesis arrows follow 5′ → 3′. The lagging side forms fragments before joining them.

<h2 id="deeper">Model boundaries</h2>

<details>
<summary>What has been idealized?</summary>

The generational model assumes synchronized complete copying, fully heavy parental DNA and exclusively light new material after transfer, without isotope recycling, degradation or repair. From generation n = 1 onward, semiconservative copying leaves two hybrid duplexes among 2ⁿ total, for a hybrid fraction of 2¹⁻ⁿ. Original heavy material is conserved. Band positions use normalized heavy fraction rather than measured absolute densities. Display brightness aids recognition and is not a substitute for measured peak areas.

The simple dispersive model distributes old material uniformly among mixed segments in every duplex. It predicts one intermediate band followed by one lighter band. This tests a specific alternative, not every conceivable recombination or mixing process.

The fork is a very short internal window, with flanking DNA omitted. Prime labels indicate direction rather than actual chromosome termini. Primer and Okazaki-fragment lengths are strongly compressed. Purple RNA markers leave during replacement; RNA is not chemically transformed into DNA. Upstream extension needed for primer replacement lies outside the window, and telomere replication is not simulated. Enzyme shapes, speed and timing are explanatory states, not measured kinetics. Energy, substrates and other necessary components are assumed available. Real copying also requires accessory proteins, proofreading and repair, and is not absolutely error-free.
