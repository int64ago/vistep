<h2 id="understand">Interpret a conclusion with its experimental conditions</h2>

Carrot phloem cells can regenerate complete plants with appropriate nutrients and hormones. This supports retention of developmental genetic information after differentiation. Regeneration remains dependent on cell state and culture conditions.

Textbook p. 125 gives approximately 50 proliferative generations for fetal lung fibroblasts, 20 for middle-aged sources and 2–4 for older sources. The original range is preserved. These numbers do not predict human lifespan or describe all cell types.

<h2 id="try">Follow distinct lines of evidence</h2>

- Remove suitable culture conditions and observe incomplete regeneration.
- Set generations already used and compare the remaining ranges. This is arithmetic from textbook values, not a biological prediction.
- Compare the textbook nuclear-exchange account with the original research limitations.

<h2 id="deeper">A young nucleus does not automatically rejuvenate a cell</h2>

<details>
<summary>Why separate the textbook comparison from original studies?</summary>

The textbook describes vigorous proliferation after young nuclei are combined with old cytoplasm. In a 1976 study, young nuclei with cytoplasts from older cultures mostly showed limited division. Culture passage age is also different from donor age. A separate 1983 experiment found at least 40% inhibition of DNA synthesis after fusion with senescent cytoplasts. The bar illustrates this lower bound, not a full original dataset. Different experimental conditions must not be pooled. Both compartments can influence proliferation.

The canonical hermaphrodite somatic lineage generates 1090 cells; 131 die and 959 remain. Germ cells are excluded. The small tree illustrates branch structure, not the actual complete lineage or death fraction. Progress tallies fates rather than placing all cells simultaneously in one animal. CED-4 → CED-3 preserves a simplified execution relationship, not the whole regulatory network.
