<h2 id="understand">Observation needs comparisons and calibration</h2>

Redshifts require laboratory wavelengths and independent distance estimates. An antenna excess requires checks of instrumental and environmental backgrounds. A sky map’s colors need an explicit temperature scale.

The low-redshift example spans 5–200 Mpc and H₀=50–90 (km/s)/Mpc, default 70. Illustrative hydrogen lines at 434.05, 486.13 and 656.28 nm share one redshift factor. Line widths, strengths and peculiar velocities are omitted. Plotted points are model samples without historical error bars, not estimates of current cosmological parameters.

Antenna temperature is Treceiver+Tatmosphere+Tsky, using 3 K, 1 K/sin(elevation), and 2.725 K respectively. This additive radiometer-temperature approximation omits frequency response, ground spillover, gain drift and calibration-load details. Subtraction holds elevation at 60 degrees. The raw receiver reading remains visible while the upper readout reports the corrected result.

The Mollweide equal-area map combines synthetic angular sine, cosine and second-order Legendre modes around 2.725 K, with deviations of tens of microkelvin. It is not observed Planck data, a random ΛCDM realization or a fitted angular power spectrum. See the sources for real maps and their context.
