---
name: Ball collisions at a ramp outlet
title: How can landing distances test momentum conservation?
---

<section id="understand">

Elective Book 1, Chapter 1 §4 (pages 19–20), collides equal-size balls at a horizontal ramp outlet. The incident ball is heavier than the target. A plumb line projects the outlet onto the floor to establish the common distance origin O.

Remove the target and release the incident ball from a marked height to record P. Replace the target and release from exactly the same point to obtain post-collision landings M and N. Equal drops and zero initial vertical velocities give equal flight times, so horizontal distances can replace speeds in a ratio comparison.

</section>
<section id="try">

Compare m₁·OP with m₁·OM + m₂·ON. These have units of mass times distance, not momentum; dividing by the common flight time converts them to momentum. A real experiment repeats releases, identifies representative landing locations and estimates uncertainty.

Changing restitution changes speed sharing and kinetic-energy loss while preserving horizontal momentum. Lowering the collision release but retaining the old P creates an apparent inequality. Momentum conservation still holds; the old P no longer represents the actual incident speed.

</section>
<section id="deeper">

<details>

<summary>Finite ball diameter and a common outlet</summary>

Both radii are 18 mm. Incident mass is 40 g and the adjustable target remains lighter. At impact their centers are x = −2R and x = 0, touching without overlap. The target leaves immediately; the incident ball crosses a short horizontal lip before also launching at x = 0. Launch times differ slightly, but each flight from outlet to floor takes the same duration. Landing occurs when the ball bottom first contacts the paper.

The ramp surface follows a normal offset of the center trajectory, preserving contact. Approach motion is ideal solid-sphere rolling; the short lip is frictionless, so post-collision translation and spin are not forcibly reset to rolling. Air drag, external horizontal impulse during impact and tangential ball-contact impulses are omitted.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Collision model and measurement status</summary>

For a stationary target, restitution e and horizontal momentum give v₁′ = (m₁−em₂)u/(m₁+m₂) and v₂′ = (1+e)m₁u/(m₁+m₂). Incident speed comes from an independent rolling model. The physical experiment instead determines it through P without assuming a known release-height law.

The displayed records are deterministic teaching simulations, without random marks masquerading as measured scatter. A heavier incident ball keeps both post-impact velocities forward in this setup. A tilted outlet, unequal drops, altered release point or lip friction violates conditions behind the common-flight-time comparison.

</details>

</section>
