<h2 id="understand">Balance absorbed and escaping energy first</h2>

Global mean absorbed sunlight is `I=S(1−α)/4`, already excluding reflection. S is the solar constant and α the planetary albedo. Without gray-layer longwave coupling, a black surface equilibrates at `σTs⁴=I`. For the idealized S = 1361 W/m² and α = 0.3 example, I is about 238.2 W/m² and surface temperature about 254.6 K.

Add a gray layer transparent to sunlight but partially absorbing thermal radiation. Longwave absorptivity and emissivity are both ε. Surface emission `(1−ε)σTs⁴` escapes directly; the layer absorbs `εσTs⁴` and emits `εσTa⁴` upward and downward. Downward emission creates no extra energy: it belongs in the layer's own budget.

<h2 id="try">A warmer surface can retain the same planetary balance</h2>

At ideal equilibrium with positive ε, `Ta⁴=Ts⁴/2` and `Ts⁴=I/[σ(1−ε/2)]`. At ε = 0.8, surface temperature is about 289.3 K. Surface emission about 397.0 balances absorbed sunlight 238.2 plus downward emission 158.8 W/m². Direct escape 79.4 plus atmospheric upward emission 158.8 still totals about 238.2.

The film also steps ε from an initial 0.4 equilibrium to 0.8. Temperatures stay continuous, so outgoing energy initially falls and net storage starts near 59.5 W/m². Positive heat-capacity equations then evolve both temperatures and restore outgoing flux. The five-year window does not end at exact equilibrium; a separate chapter displays the analytic equilibrium instead of treating the final transient frame as a mathematical limit.

<h2 id="deeper">This is not a complete climate model</h2>

<details>
<summary>Heat capacities, albedo and limiting cases</summary>

The transient equations are `Cs dTs/dt=I+εσTa⁴−σTs⁴` and `Ca dTa/dt=εσTs⁴−2εσTa⁴`. Illustrative capacities are Cs = 8×10⁷ and Ca = 10⁷ J/(m²·K). Adding their budgets gives net planetary input, allowing stored heat to be independently checked against its time integral. These values do not fit real ocean or climate response times.

At ε = 0, the layer is radiatively inactive and its undefined equilibrium temperature is not absolute zero. At ε = 1, all surface longwave is absorbed, yet the atmosphere still emits I to space. Neither permanent energy imprisonment nor infinite surface warming follows. Albedo changes absorbed sunlight, unlike changing longwave ε.

Explore chooses analytic equilibrium or a transient starting at ε = 0.4 equilibrium. Time controls appear only for the transient. Gray emissivity is not CO₂ concentration or a linear proxy for it. Spectral lines, convection, latent heat, clouds, multiple layers, geography and feedbacks are omitted. No emissions scenario, real climate sensitivity or future temperature is predicted.

</details>
