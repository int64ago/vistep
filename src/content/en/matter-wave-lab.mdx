<h2 id="understand">Why does a crystal enhance selected directions?</h2>

The electron-diffraction photograph and X-ray spots in the textbook are experimental evidence. This film uses explicitly identified calculations to explain those phenomena. Its curves, rings and dots are not digitized observations from those photographs.

Follow two scattering paths. O and A lie on one incoming wavefront; O and C lie on one outgoing wavefront. The lower route therefore travels the two extra segments AB and BC. Each is d sinθ, giving a total of 2d sinθ. Changing plane spacing or grazing angle changes both the measured path difference and the phases of the lower traces. There is no independent phase slider disconnected from the geometry.

When the difference contains an integer number of wavelengths, plane contributions reinforce one another. Eight-plane addition explains the angular peak. Electron wavelength depends on momentum: greater accelerating voltage shortens it, contracting the rings for the same foil and screen distance.

The local diagram uses a kinematic approximation for coherent contributions. It omits absorption, multiple scattering and real atomic form factors. The electron-reflection example uses 100 V, 213 pm spacing and eight planes. It does not reconstruct the nickel apparatus, peak positions or measured intensities of Davisson and Germer.

The Laue view retains a three-dimensional calculation: a simple cubic lattice with a 400 pm cell, illuminated along z. For each reciprocal vector G it solves s_out=s_in+λG together with |s_out|=1. At zero rotation, (2,1,−1) has d=400/√6 pm, λ=400/3 pm and θ≈24.095°, so the local path difference is one wavelength. Varying the close-up parameters tests phase addition; that alone does not replace the full three-dimensional diffraction condition. The section uses a uniform magnification adjusted to keep its paths in view; pixels from different control states are not a common picometer ruler. Allowed solutions at the same detector position are merged for display. The wavelength label identifies the selected family, not a spectral measurement. Spot size and brightness identify positions and cannot support structure refinement.
