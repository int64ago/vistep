<h2 id="understand">Related wave physics, different experiments</h2>

Single-crystal scattering selects directions; many grain orientations turn scattering cones into rings; electron interference accumulates localized detections into fringes. These are different apparatus and observables. The textbook’s broad electron-diffraction entry is not relabeled as a specific single-electron double-slit experiment.

The X-ray scene uses polychromatic Laue geometry with positions calculated from a three-dimensional lattice. A separate plane view explains path difference. Optical and solid-state textbook entries preserve their respective learning positions.

<h2 id="try">Change angle, voltage and orientation</h2>

Scan grazing angle to find a coherent peak, raise accelerating voltage to contract rings, or rotate the X-ray crystal to follow its spots. The local phase view compares relative phase over one cycle as an explicit teaching-state control.
