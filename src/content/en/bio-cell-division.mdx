<h2 id="understand">Distinguish chromosome number from DNA replication</h2>

After DNA replication, connected sisters are still counted as one chromosome. At anaphase, separated sisters become daughter chromosomes: the undivided cell temporarily has twice as many chromosomes while each pole receives one set. The mechanism uses 2n = 4; actual onion cells have 2n = 16.

Plants build a cell plate and new wall; animals form a cleavage furrow. Both daughters retain the parental chromosome number. The model preserves each sister’s identity and continuous position.

- Switch plant and animal cytokinesis.
- Compare 2, 4 and 8 mm cubes at equal inward penetration depth.
- Move the fixed-field observation index or change fields; verify that previous cells have not progressed through division.

<h2 id="deeper">Fixed observations are not live tracking</h2>

<details>
<summary>Can counts estimate stage durations?</summary>

The textbook counts two fields of about 50 cells each. The model uses an explicitly synthetic field, not micrographs or measured samples. Stage fractions approximate relative durations only under assumptions such as asynchronous division, continued proliferation and representative sampling. One field does not directly time an individual cell.

For a cube, S = 6L² and V = L³. The fraction reached to inward depth d is 1 − max(L − 2d, 0)³/L³. At d = 0.5 mm, sides of 2, 4 and 8 mm give approximately 87.5%, 57.8% and 33.0%. This is the textbook’s mathematical model, not an invented agar experiment. Real cell size also depends on organelles, metabolism and transport.
