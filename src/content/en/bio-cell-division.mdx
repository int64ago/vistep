<h2 id="understand">Separate replication, sister separation and cytokinesis</h2>

Replicated sisters remain associated at their centromeric region, so chromosome number does not immediately double. At anaphase, separated sisters become chromosomes. The still-undivided model cell then contains 8 chromosomes, with 4 at each pole. Completed cytokinesis gives each daughter the original 4. Each colored sister retains its identity; anaphase does not generate another copy of the DNA.

The mechanism uses 2n = 4; an onion root-tip cell has 2n = 16. The DNA display is a relative amount: 4 becomes 8 during replication. After division, the displayed 8 refers to both daughters together, not 8 in each daughter. A plant cell plate expands toward the existing boundary; an animal cleavage furrow contracts inward.

<h2 id="deeper">What can each kind of evidence establish?</h2>

<details>
<summary>Why do fixed shapes need independent evidence of time order?</summary>

Dissociation, rinsing, staining and squashing improve visibility, but prepared cells no longer proceed through normal division. Condensation, central alignment, separated groups and two nuclei in different cells suggest a candidate order. Arranging those cells in a row is not a recording of one cell progressing through all stages.

A separate view draws on [Figure 6c in Pietra et al.’s original 2013 study](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms3779.pdf): one wild-type Arabidopsis cell at 0, 20, 33, 38 and 69 min. The original DNA and microtubule arrangements are schematically redrawn at these five published times. No intermediate recorded frames are invented. The study used H2B-YFP to label chromatin and mCherry-TUA5 to label microtubules; diagram colors aid tracking rather than depict natural colors.

These are not onion data. The 69 min segment is neither a complete cell cycle nor a mean stage duration. Diagram distances are not a scaled reproduction of the micrograph and cannot establish chromosome speed. A [2019 long-term root imaging study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6446972/) separately identifies intervals bounded by two observed division events and boundary records containing only one event. Tracking duration and acquisition frequency limit the conclusions that can be drawn.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Does a larger metaphase fraction prove a longer metaphase?</summary>

No. The 50-cell fields, phase boundaries and starting ages here are explicitly synthetic, not micrographs or measured textbook samples. The two populations have identical cycle settings. Starting the second population synchronously in metaphase changes its fraction from 4/50 to 50/50 without changing the programmed stage duration. A snapshot fraction therefore does not uniquely determine duration.

Using stage fractions to discuss duration requires assumptions about age distribution, proliferation, synchrony and sampling. Even asynchronous growth with representative sampling does not guarantee a uniform age distribution, so raw fractions are not automatically duration fractions. The textbook’s approximate 12 h onion cycle must not be used to convert this synthetic field into measured hours.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What can the size model calculate exactly?</summary>

For a cube, S = 6L² and V = L³. The fraction reached to inward depth d is 1 − max(L − 2d, 0)³/L³. At d = 0.5 mm, sides of 2, 4 and 8 mm give 87.5%, approximately 57.8% and approximately 33.0%. Equal shell thickness covers a larger fraction of the smaller cube.

This is the textbook’s mathematical comparison, not an added agar experiment or a claim about millimeter-sized cells. Real cell size also depends on internal transport, organelles and metabolism; this geometry alone does not imply that smaller is always better.

</details>
