<h2 id="understand">Turn theory into comparable observations</h2>

Interferometers compare phases, muon experiments compare survival, and atomic clocks compare readings after reunion. The train is a thought experiment requiring time, position and a reference frame for each event.

<h2 id="try">Rotate, change speed, change altitude</h2>

Rotate the interferometer to compare an ether-wind prediction with an ideal null result. Change muon speed to update proper time and survival. Reverse flight direction or change altitude to update all three clock contributions.

<summary>Calculating the relativistic models</summary>

<div className="formula">γ = 1/√(1−v²/c²); t′ = γ(t−vx/c²); N/N₀ = exp[−t/(γτ₀)]</div>

The ether hypothesis uses Galilean velocity addition without Lorentz contraction, effective single-arm L=11 m, λ=500 nm and a 30 km/s ether wind. A 90-degree rotation predicts about 0.44 fringes using exact classical round-trip times. The lower ideal null strip does not recreate historical precision. Arms, source and detector rotate together.

The train uses L=c=1, ground events (t,x)=(0,−0.5),(0,+0.5), and β=0.6. Reception times are 0.3125 and 1.25; transformed emission times are −0.375 and +0.375. These two pairs must not be confused. The drawing uses ground coordinates, not a train-frame simultaneity slice.

Muons use the mean proper lifetime 2.1969811 μs, not a half-life; default speed is 0.995c over 10 km. Dots round an expected fraction without detector efficiency, energy loss or random histories.

<div className="formula">Δτ ≈ [gh − (vaircraft²−vground²)/2]·t/c²</div>

Flying clocks use a weak-field, low-speed equatorial approximation: ground rotation 465.1 m/s, aircraft speed ±250 m/s relative to ground, default altitude 10 km and time 40 h. Latitude changes, oblateness and real flight paths are omitted. The aircraft loop shows direction and progress, not actual circumnavigation timing. All nanosecond readings are model predictions, not 1971 measurements.
