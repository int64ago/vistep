<section id="understand">
## The resist determines which copper remains

Copper foil is bonded to an insulating substrate. The textbook inquiry protects a pattern with a water-resistant material; exposed copper reacts with ferric chloride. The net ionic reaction is Cu + 2Fe³⁺ → Cu²⁺ + 2Fe²⁺. Removing the resist after etching reveals the retained copper pattern.

The same pattern and cross section remain identifiable throughout. Dark resist, copper-coloured metal and pale substrate have distinct roles. Copper foil is opaque: the top view remains copper until exposed areas are etched through, while the cross section reveals intermediate thinning.

</section>
<section id="try">
## Change protection and oxidant independently

Without resist, all copper is exposed. With the pattern protected, only uncovered area participates in the ideal etch. Initial total copper is one relative amount, and each two ferric amounts consumed allow at most one copper amount to enter solution. Limited ferric ions leave exposed copper behind.

Extent is conversion rather than elapsed minutes. Compare the cross section, the masked top view and the top view after resist removal to follow the same copper.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Independent model and manufacturing limits</summary>

The protection boundary comes from shared line segments, rounded distances and terminal discs. A 40-by-24 equal-area sampling grid estimates protected area; copper removal is limited by both exposed area and half the ferric amount. The grid is not a manufacturing resolution. Copper, iron and transferred electron amounts are conserved.

The model uses uniform vertical etching and omits mass transfer, rates, undercut, chloride complexes and etchant regeneration. [Original University of Delaware research](https://research.me.udel.edu/~prasad/papers/2006_JMM_Zhang.pdf) examines masks, undercut and complex formation in real foil etching. [Georgia Tech’s research archive](https://hdl.handle.net/1853/43026) provides kinetics context. These engineering conditions are not converted into home fabrication instructions.

Textbook: Required Book 1, pages 74–75, PDF pages 79–80, inquiry on making patterns on copper-clad board.

</details>
