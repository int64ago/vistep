name: Position and velocity with sensors
title: How does a sensor turn a sound pulse into velocity?
---

<section id="understand">

PEP Required Book 1 §1.3 (page25) introduces two arrangements. In the first, a moving object emits infrared and ultrasound together. A fixed receiver starts timing when infrared arrives and stops when sound arrives. Neglecting the tiny infrared travel time gives x ≈ cΔt.

The second uses one fixed ultrasonic transducer. Sound reaches the object and returns, so x = cΔt/2. The paths differ; the same factor cannot be applied to both.

</section>
<section id="try">

Pause a pulse halfway and compare it with the cart. Once emitted, sound is not dragged by its moving source. In reflection mode, pulse and target must meet at the reflection instant.

Repeated measurements produce positions over time. Position difference divided by the matching time difference gives average velocity. The textbook uses short intervals around0.02 s to approximate instantaneous velocity. Here the cart is uniform, so both coincide. Exploration changes signed velocity and pulse progress.

</section>
<section id="deeper">

<details>

<summary>Motion during propagation</summary>

The model uses343 m/s sound in stationary air and vacuum light speed for the infrared approximation. One-way timing retains infrared’s tiny travel time while the interface shows the textbook approximation. Reflected timing solves the interception of a uniformly moving target, then the return flight. Thus cΔt/2 belongs to the reflection instant. Successive reflection times are used when reconstructing velocity.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Real-instrument limits</summary>

Air temperature changes sound speed. Transducers have a brief post-transmission blind period; small or tilted targets may return weak echoes, and multiple reflections can confuse range. Only a clean single path is shown. Pulse propagation is greatly slowed, but millisecond readings retain physical model time. Green strokes visualize sound-pulse position, not visible light.

</details>

</section>
