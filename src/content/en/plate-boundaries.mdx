<h2 id="understand">Boundaries depend on relative motion</h2>

Two moving plates need not separate or compress their boundary. If both have velocity `(1,0)`, relative velocity is zero. The diagram uses right plate B as its reference. The normal points left to right, the tangent points upward, and relative velocity is right minus left. Length and time are normalized.

Positive normal relative velocity separates the plates; negative normal velocity converges them. Zero normal but nonzero tangential velocity gives pure transform motion. Arrows are velocities rather than forces. Adding the same reference-frame velocity preserves the classification.

<h2 id="try">Opening, subduction and sliding</h2>

During divergence, the new central strip has width equal to normal relative speed times time. This accounts for kinematic space generation without calculating magma flow or heat sources.

The convergent section separately assumes that the left plate can subduct. Material moves along a continuous unit-arclength path with fixed thickness, approaching horizontally and then descending. The bend does not arbitrarily change streamline speed or break plate thickness. Curvature and dip are specified teaching geometry, rather than a prediction derived from plate speed.

Pure tangential motion offsets the paired reference lines without generating or consuming normal width. Oblique motion can combine convergence and sliding; real boundary motion need not fit three mutually exclusive presets.

<h2 id="deeper">Classification is not full geodynamics</h2>

<details>
<summary>The same convergence does not decide which plate subducts</summary>

Keep the convergent velocity but remove the assumed subductability and show continental contact. Rigid geometry stops at contact, without interpenetration or quantitatively invented mountain building. Real deformation, thickening and subduction need density, temperature, rheology, forces and history. This does not claim that continental material can never descend under any conditions.

Explore changes both plates' normal and tangential velocities, elapsed time and, during convergence, the subduction premise. Relative displacements, arrow components, classification and material markers respond. Global spherical plate motion additionally needs Euler-pole rotations; local straight-boundary translation is not a global solution.

Earthquake stress accumulation, locking, ruptures and hazards are not modeled. Boundary type does not predict an event's time or magnitude. Plan and section have separate display scales and remain local kinematic explanations.

</details>
