<h2 id="understand">Each pattern comes from a different operation</h2>

Start with floating paper in a basin: tap once, then tap the same point periodically. Ripples travel outward while the paper moves locally. The magnified side view retains small horizontal motion as well as vertical displacement; the paper does not keep travelling with a crest.

The ripple-tank trials use a reflecting barrier, a submerged shallow-water plate, a gap between two barriers, and dipping rods. These change boundary conditions, propagation speed or source phase relationships.

<h2 id="try">Six textbook investigations</h2>

- Compare single and periodic tapping while tracking the same piece of paper.
- Change incidence angle and measure both reflection angles from the normal.
- Change the shallow-side depth and compare speed, wavelength and direction at fixed source frequency.
- Vary gap width at fixed wavelength, then wavelength at fixed gap width.
- Drive two rods from one vibrating strip, then switch to independent drivers to vary phase and frequency.
- Compare stationary and moving sources while tracking each crest’s original emission centre.

<h2 id="deeper">Models and limits</h2>

<div className="formula">
  Long-wave speed v≈√gh; sin i/v₁ = sin r/v₂; at fixed frequency, λ₂/λ₁ = v₂/v₁
</div>

The reference depth is 8 mm. Diffraction uses a separate 1 mm shallow-water setup to retain the long-wave approximation even at a 3 cm wavelength. Surface tension, viscosity and tank-edge echoes are omitted. Parameter changes compare established linear fields rather than modelling every transient. Reflection satisfies zero normal surface slope at a rigid barrier. A depth step matches elevation and depth-weighted flux, including a reflected wave.

Diffraction uses an aperture-wavelet integral to represent spreading, without precise near-gap or absolute-amplitude predictions. Interference adds two cylindrical waves with their propagation phases. Unequal amplitudes generally prevent perfect cancellation; frequency detuning makes the resultant motion at a fixed probe vary with time.

Each crest retains its emission centre while its radius grows at wave speed. Circles are not water-particle paths. Colour represents instantaneous elevation, not flow direction or energy. Paper motion uses a local small-amplitude long-wave approximation and magnified side-view height.
