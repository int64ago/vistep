<section id="understand">

## What does red establish?

Under matched test conditions, thiocyanate forms coloured complexes with iron(III); the iron(II) control lacks that response. A mixture of both can still turn red. The test indicates available iron(III), not the absence of iron(II). This is colour within a solution, not a red precipitate.

Follow one metal atom. Its two donated electron credits go to two iron(III) ions. The metal atom also enters solution as iron(II), giving **2Fe³⁺ + Fe → 3Fe²⁺**. There are now three dissolved iron units, not two. The textbook lets the powder react before adding the test reagent.

Moving clear liquid into another tube does not change the ions’ oxidation state. Leaving unused powder behind removes a possible electron donor. Chlorine can then accept electron credits from iron(II): **2Fe²⁺ + Cl₂ → 2Fe³⁺ + 2Cl⁻**. Thiocyanate already in the water binds again. If metal is carried over, the same limited oxidant dose can have a different net outcome.

## The ligand was not consumed

Keep sample a unchanged. In b, iron powder first reduces some free iron(III). The original complexes initially remain, so the first-step formation quotient Q₁ rises. As the mixture redistributes, complexes can release their existing thiocyanate and replenish free iron(III). The ligand remains in the water.

Adding thiocyanate to c first increases free ligand and lowers Q₁. Binding then shifts more iron into coloured species. This does not add any iron. Colour depends on ligand concentration, acidity and other conditions; deeper red is not automatically a scale for total iron.

</section>
<section id="try">

## Predict, then inspect the accounts

- Does a red mixed sample rule out iron(II)? Follow the ion that remains in the water.
- Why can the same limited oxidant dose give different outcomes with or without metal? Compare electron donors and total iron.
- Does adding ligand increase total iron? Compare free and bound ligand before revealing the conserved iron account.
- When a negative ligand lowers the complex charge from +3 to +2, has the central iron been reduced? The purple pair represents shared bonding electrons, distinct from the gold redox credits.

<section id="deeper">

<details>
<summary>Oxidation state, complex charge and hydration</summary>

The aqueous iron centre already has a coordination environment. Thiocyanate binds through nitrogen as one water ligand leaves; a second incoming thiocyanate displaces another water. The remaining hydration environment is schematic. Mono- and bis-thiocyanate formulas do not mean total coordination numbers of one and two.

Fe(SCN)²⁺ has total charge +2 and Fe(SCN)₂⁺ has +1. Iron remains formally +3 in both. A shared bonding pair is not the net electron transfer that reduces iron(III). [Elijošiūtė’s original dissertation](https://epubl.ktu.edu/object/elaba%3A2192910/2192910.pdf) examines Fe–N connectivity, spectra and computed structures. Only connectivity informs this view; optimized structures in a sulfate-containing system are not presented as measured coordinates for the textbook solution.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What the equilibrium numbers can establish</summary>

The textbook uses Fe³⁺ + 3SCN⁻ ⇌ Fe(SCN)₃ as a simplified equilibrium expression. The more detailed illustrative model here forms Fe(SCN)²⁺ and Fe(SCN)₂⁺ stepwise. Displayed Q₁ refers specifically to **Fe³⁺ + SCN⁻ ⇌ Fe(SCN)²⁺**, not the three-ligand expression.

[The original study by de Berg, Maeder and Clifford (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ica.2017.06.017) reports zero-ionic-strength extrapolations of log K₁ = 2.85 and log K₂ = 1.51 at 25 °C. The accessible [author-institution abstract](https://research.avondale.edu.au/items/d2ccd1cc-c10e-481e-8513-5bc2a400bd6b) describes initial spectra and the extrapolation method; this does not imply access to all raw spectra. Species distributions satisfy both iron and thiocyanate balances.

The illustrative starting concentrations are 2.5 mmol/L Fe(III) and 1 mmol/L SCN⁻. They differ from the textbook recipe, and the addition is not inferred from four drops of unspecified volume. A finite perturbation is followed by a conservative compositional path to equilibrium, not a kinetic solution. The total coloured population may decrease while one subtype increases slightly.

Colour is qualitative, not fitted absorbance. Volume change, activity corrections, hydrolysis, higher complexes and long-term side reactions are omitted. [Delaware’s laboratory guide](https://udel.edu/~dougr/Exp5.pdf) distinguishes activities from concentration quotients. Fe(II) complexation is also omitted: assigning released ligand to the free pool is an approximation, not a claim that iron(II) can never bind thiocyanate.

</details>
