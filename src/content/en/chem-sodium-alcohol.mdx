<section id="understand">

Experiment 7-4 on pages 77–78 of Compulsory Book 2 goes beyond observing bubbles. The textbook checks gas purity, observes water droplets after combustion, then turns the collector upright and adds limewater. Water formation and absence of visible limewater cloudiness must be interpreted with the reactants; no single observation proves absolute gas purity.

The net reaction is 2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂. Hydroxyl hydrogen forms the gas while ethyl C—H connections remain. Ethoxide anions and Na⁺ balance charge, with one H₂ per two Na. The Royal Society of Chemistry includes the sodium reaction in its [alcohol-properties teaching resource](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/the-properties-of-alcohols/463.article).

Experiment 1-1 on page 7 of Selective Compulsory 3 compares equal sodium pieces in distilled water and anhydrous ethanol. Water reacts more vigorously. Sodium’s density of approximately 0.97 g/cm³ is documented by [RSC](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/11/sodium), while the textbook gives approximately 0.789 g/cm³ for ethanol. Floating or sinking and reaction intensity are distinct observations.

</section>

<section id="deeper">

<details>
<summary>Rate, acidity and structural interpretation</summary>

The textbook uses group interactions and the O—H environment to explain contrasting behaviour. This classroom comparison does not quantitatively measure acidity or bond energy. Acidity concerns equilibrium in specified conditions; surface reaction rates also depend on heat transfer, mass transfer and surface state. [The author’s OpenStax textbook](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/17-2-properties-of-alcohols-and-phenols) discusses alcohol acid–base properties, solvation and substituent effects.

Two selected smooth timelines illustrate faster water and slower ethanol reactions, without claiming a measured rate ratio. Available sodium and O—H donors limit reaction extent. Na, C, H, O and ionic charge are conserved. Model units do not represent actual vessel millilitres, and bubble count is not a gas-volume scale.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Tests and apparatus limits</summary>

The apparatus retains sodium in liquid, tube, stopper, nozzle and support. The combustion collector is shown upright in the subsequent limewater view. Water droplets and clear limewater are textbook observations. The model balances complete hydrogen combustion to water, without predicting gas impurities, ethanol-vapour carryover, detection limits or condensation heat transfer.

Density determines the illustrated floating/sinking relationship, not the reaction mechanism. Metal shape and motion are qualitative; surface tension, melting and thermal effects are not computed. The structural view is a net atom-and-charge account, not a simultaneous elementary process.

</details>
