<h2 id="understand">From voltage polarity to electric and magnetic energy</h2>

Connect the oscilloscope first to a battery and then to an AC teaching supply. Direct voltage keeps one polarity; sinusoidal alternating voltage crosses zero. Two antiparallel LEDs connected to a teaching generator reveal opposite current directions. Slip rings maintain contact without rectifying the output.

With a coil in series, the lamp is dimmer on AC than DC at equal source RMS voltage because inductive reactance further limits current. A series capacitor passes a charging transient on DC, then blocks steady current. AC repeatedly charges and discharges it; electrons do not cross the dielectric.

Switch a charged capacitor onto a coil. Electric energy becomes magnetic energy and then returns as opposite capacitor charge. Real circuit resistance makes the measured oscillation decay.

<h2 id="try">Preserve the comparison conditions</h2>

- Compare a series coil on DC and AC using the same three-volt RMS supply.
- Hold capacitance fixed and raise frequency, then hold frequency fixed and increase capacitance.
- Pause as the LEDs alternate and inspect the current sign.
- Set LC resistance to zero to preserve total electromagnetic energy, then add loss.
