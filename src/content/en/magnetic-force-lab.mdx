<h2 id="understand">Currents create fields and experience magnetic forces</h2>

The suspended wire’s current, external field and force are perpendicular. Reverse only the current, then only the field: each reversal changes the force direction. Reverse both and the original force returns. Force and displacement share one damped elastic-support model, with displacement enlarged for observation.

A copper solenoid produces an axial field. Soft iron magnetizes with that field, so reversing the current swaps the coil poles but does not turn attraction into repulsion. This differs from the response of a permanent magnet.

Parallel currents attract when they flow in the same direction and repel when their directions oppose. The historical spring apparatus turns this effect into a contact cycle: attracting turns shorten the spring, the contact opens, current stops and the spring returns. The mercury contact is a virtual historical apparatus, not a practical home activity.

<h2 id="try">Change one condition while keeping the apparatus</h2>

- Set wire current to zero, then reverse current and magnetic field separately.
- Compare positive and negative solenoid currents: the iron nail remains attracted.
- Reverse only the second parallel-wire current.
- Seek the spring to contact opening or closing. Zero current removes the cycle.

The wire length is 0.12m, effective support stiffness 0.8N/m and mass 0.025kg. Its motion uses the damped small-oscillation solution. Parallel wires use the long-wire approximation; exaggerated shape changes show direction rather than measured deformation.

The solenoid uses the axial field of a finite cylindrical winding. Nail force is proportional to the spatial gradient of field squared under a linear induced-dipole approximation. Saturation, hysteresis and feedback on the field are omitted. Nail displacement indicates attraction direction and is not a measured force response.

The spring is a mass–spring–damper model with a hysteretic electrical contact and an illustrative magnetic-force coefficient. Fixed time steps reconstruct its history deterministically. Arcing, self-induction and mercury fluid dynamics are not simulated.
