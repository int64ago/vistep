<h2 id="understand">Follow the working liquid</h2>

External water wets the bird’s felt without entering its sealed body. Evaporation cools the head and lowers its internal vapour pressure. The warmer lower bulb pushes working liquid up the neck, changing the mass distribution until the bird tips.

Tipping exposes the bottom of the tube above the liquid surface. Vapour connects the bulbs, pressure equalizes and liquid drains down. Gravity restores the bird, while dipping replenishes the water on its felt.

<h2 id="try">Change the evaporation conditions</h2>

- Compare a wet and dry head, starting at the same temperature.
- Increase relative humidity and observe the delayed first dip. Saturated air prevents net evaporative cooling in this model.
- Scrub time to the instant when the tube opening emerges, then follow drainage and restoration.

<h2 id="deeper">What this heat-engine model covers</h2>

<div className="formula">
  Pressure difference → rising liquid → changed torque → tipping and venting → drainage
</div>

This reduced model couples representative vapour pressure, liquid-volume conservation, finite flow response and gravitational torque. It is not a calibrated model of a particular toy’s working fluid, size, period or efficiency, nor a complete thermodynamic energy budget.

Wet-head mode assumes that external water is replenished. Heat comes from the environment while evaporation maintains the cooler head: this is not perpetual motion. Changing humidity starts an independent trial from the same initial state.
