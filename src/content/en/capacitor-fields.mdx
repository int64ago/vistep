<h2 id="understand">First ask what is fixed: charge or voltage?</h2>

Two parallel conductors carry equal, opposite free charges, producing an electric field from the positive plate toward the negative plate. Potential decreases in that direction. A steeper potential drop per distance means a stronger field.

Isolate the capacitor to fix its plate charge. Increasing the air gap leaves the central field approximately unchanged, but voltage rises because the field extends across a greater distance. Gap, field and voltage describe different quantities.

Compare materials that completely fill the gap. **At fixed free charge**, polarization creates opposing bound surface charges, reducing the net field and voltage. Raising relative permittivity from one to four lowers the voltage from 12 V to 3 V.

**At fixed voltage**, the source supplies more free charge. With complete filling and unchanged gap, the net field remains `V/d`, while free charge becomes four times larger. A claim that dielectric reduces field therefore needs a stated comparison condition.

<h2 id="try">Read the potential profile</h2>

- With air and fixed voltage, raise source voltage. Field, potential difference and free charge increase together.
- With air and fixed charge, widen the gap. Field stays constant while voltage rises.
- Fill the gap with relative permittivity four. Compare fixed charge and fixed voltage using the V and Q readings.
- Make only the central half of the gap dielectric. The profile changes slope at both interfaces, with steeper air sections.
- Vary permittivity. The same normal electric displacement corresponds to different local fields, while potential stays continuous.

<h2 id="deeper">Layered gaps add potential drops in series</h2>

<details>
<summary>Free charge, polarization and field</summary>

For area A, free charge Q and relative permittivity κ, normal electric displacement D is continuous across interfaces without additional free surface charge:

<div className="formula">D = Q/A; Eair = D/ε₀; Edielectric = D/(κε₀)</div>

Polarization is `P = D(1 − 1/κ)`. The dielectric surface near the positive plate carries negative bound charge, with positive bound charge on the opposite surface. The dielectric remains neutral overall. Bound charges differ from free charges supplied to the conductors by the source.

</details>
