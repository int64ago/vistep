<h2 id="understand">Read a curved track together with its direction of travel</h2>

The cathode-ray apparatus first accelerates electrons. With no magnetic field, the model beam travels straight. A transverse field bends it; reversing the field reverses the bend. Electrons are negative, so the positive-charge force rule must be reversed.

In a uniform field, perpendicular velocity gives circular motion. Stronger field reduces the radius; faster electrons give a larger radius. A velocity component along the field remains unchanged, producing a helix. Magnetic force is perpendicular to velocity and cannot by itself change speed.

The mass-spectrometer view accelerates singly charged neon-20 and neon-22 ions through the same voltage. Their speeds are different. The heavier isotope has a larger radius, proportional to the square root of mass in this setup. Do not confuse this with a selector that gives both isotopes equal speed.

The positron view joins two curved segments at a lead plate with a common tangent. Momentum loss tightens the curvature after passage, revealing the travel direction. Field direction then determines charge sign. Ionization and penetration evidence are also needed to identify a positron; curvature alone does not identify every positive particle.

<h2 id="try">Change one input into a counterfactual</h2>

- Reverse the magnetic field and compare the bend; zero field restores a straight path.
- Raise accelerating voltage at fixed field and read the radius.
- Launch obliquely to preserve motion along the field axis.
- Switch the positron view’s charge hypothesis while keeping travel direction fixed.

<h2 id="deeper">Equations, detector limits and historical images</h2>

<div className="formula">v = √(2|q|U/m); r = mv⊥/(|q|B); T = 2πm/(|q|B)</div>

Low-energy electron and ion paths use the analytical nonrelativistic Lorentz solution. Circular tracks stop at the displayed tube wall; subsequent scattering is not modelled. The helical view scales axial and transverse distances separately, so its pitch is not measurable from the picture. Motion is greatly slowed and luminous points are not direct images of individual electrons.

The positron uses relativistic momentum: p²c² = K(K + 2mc²), followed by r = p/(|q|B). The 6MeV and3MeV examples illustrate energy loss and are not fits to Anderson’s photograph. Plate energy loss is prescribed; stochastic scattering and loss distributions are omitted.
