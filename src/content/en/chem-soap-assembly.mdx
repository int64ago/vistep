<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

The soap research task on PEP Selective Compulsory 3, printed page 82, covers saponification, salting out, washing, shaping, cleaning and surfactant classes. This visualization links the investigations without turning an open research task into a universally successful recipe.

One triglyceride and three hydroxide equivalents yield one glycerol and three fatty-acid salts. Original glycerol-side ester oxygens remain with glycerol; the three R chains may differ. Stable backbone positions show the net result, not all tetrahedral intermediates, proton transfers or reaction rates.

NaCl reduces soap solubility and separates a soap-rich phase from mother liquor containing glycerol and salts. Suitable separation and washing precede shaping. Feedstock and process affect residual alkali and purity; the animation does not certify a sample for skin use.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

The illustrative salting capacity is S = 3 / (1 + 4s), where s is dimensionless salt addition. Dissolved plus separated soap remains 3. The coefficient is not fitted to data and predicts no practical yield. Retained-liquor impurity follows its initial amount divided by 1 plus the wash-volume ratio. This explains dilution, omitting soap losses, repeated filtration and transport details.

Hydrophilic heads lie outside the oil droplet while tail ends enter it. Oil, heads and tails share one geometry. Agitation and available soap control a teaching detachment sequence, not a quantitative surface-tension or cleaning-rate solution. The thin contact neck is a deformation sketch without a three-dimensional volume solver.

The hard-water control uses Ca²⁺ + 2 RCOO⁻ → Ca(RCOO)₂↓. Less soap remains available for coverage and detachment. Magnesium can have similar effects; this does not imply every anionic synthetic detergent precipitates equally.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

An adsorbed layer on an oil droplet, an emulsion and a small micelle are not identical objects or scales. Micellization depends on concentration, temperature, salts and molecular structure. No critical micelle concentration is predicted here. Interfacial and mechanical cleaning does not require chemical destruction of the oil.
