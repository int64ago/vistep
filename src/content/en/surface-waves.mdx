## A travelling shape and local parcel motion

A crest advances while a first-order water parcel oscillates around its mean position. The film separates the travelling surface profile from an enlarged local orbit. Deep-water orbits are nearly circular and decay exponentially with depth. In shallower water the impermeable bed flattens them into ellipses; at the bed only horizontal oscillation remains.

Closed orbits are a first-order result. Real waves can involve higher-order Stokes drift, mean currents, breaking and viscosity. This picture does not imply that waves can never transport water.

## Keep three motions separate

At fixed wavelength, move the observation level to the bed and check that the vertical semiaxis becomes exactly zero. In deep water, increase wavelength and compare phase speed. Then combine nearby wavelengths and follow envelope and carrier markers separately. Under shallow-water, long-wave conditions, their speeds converge.

Zero amplitude stops parcel motion while the dispersion relation still specifies the speed of an allowed mode. That number does not imply visible wave energy at zero amplitude.

## One finite-depth wave solution

<div className="formula">
  ω² = gk tanh(kh), k = 2π/λ
  <br />
  Phase speed c = ω/k
  <br />
  Group speed cg = c[1 + 2kh/sinh(2kh)] / 2
</div>

For mean parcel position (x₀,z₀), the mean surface is z = 0 and the bed is z = −h. First-order displacements are:

<div className="formula">
  ξ = −A cosh[k(z₀+h)] / sinh(kh) · sin(kx₀−ωt)
  <br />ζ = A sinh[k(z₀+h)] / sinh(kh) · cos(kx₀−ωt)
</div>

The model assumes small-amplitude gravity waves in incompressible, inviscid, irrotational water of constant depth without a background current. Surface tension is excluded. In deep water c approaches √(g/k) and cg approaches c/2; in the shallow-water, long-wave limit both approach √(gh). The two-mode drawing uses its exact finite-difference envelope speed Δω/Δk. Only the narrow-band limit equals dω/dk.

The depth control compares different uniform water bodies; it does not follow one wave toward a beach or calculate shoaling amplification at conserved frequency. Motion is slowed. Surface height and local displacements are separately enlarged; the illustration does not encode depth proportions.
