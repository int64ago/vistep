## Peak direction and peak width differ

Neighbouring slits have path difference d sinθ. Whole-wavelength differences align their centre contributions into principal maxima. Adding slits without changing centre spacing leaves those directions fixed, but brings adjacent zeros closer and narrows the peaks.

The film compares two and many slits, then sums the field at one selected direction as a head-to-tail phasor chain. At a maximum the vectors align; slightly off it they turn and can close. Reinforcement comes from superposition, rather than a grating passing only a few geometric rays.

## Vary count, spacing and width separately

Change countN, centre spacingd, slit widtha and observation sine. The grating drawing uses a fixed scale so more slits occupy more total width. Each curve is normalized to its own central peak of1. Equal plotted height does not mean equal fixed-exposure intensity: with unchanged illumination per slit, the raw central intensity scales asN².

## The single-slit envelope multiplies the multi-slit factor

<div className="formula">
  Principal orders: d sinθ = mλ
  <br />I / Icentral = [sinβ / β]² · |Σ exp(ijφ) / N|²
  <br />β = πa sinθ/λ; φ = 2πd sinθ/λ
</div>

The model uses coherent equal-amplitude, uniformly illuminated rectangular slits in scalar Fraunhofer diffraction. Internal lengths are nanometres; larger spacing and widths are displayed in micrometres. The horizontal axis is sine of angle, not equal spacing on a flat screen. Colours identify sampled wavelengths rather than calibrated perception.

Only orders with|mλ/d| no greater than1 have propagating directions. Even these need not be bright. Witha=d/2, the second principal order coincides with a single-slit envelope zero and is missing. Oblique incidence, blaze profiles, polarization efficiency and near-field propagation are omitted; this is not a complete industrial-grating efficiency prediction.
