<section id="understand">

Required Book 2, Chapter 6 §2 (pages 27–30), connects string release, a rotating sandbag, a two-ball demonstrator and a ball inside a funnel. “Centripetal” describes the inward effect of a resultant; it is not an extra force added to gravity, tension or support.

On a smooth horizontal table, a released ball follows the tangent at release. A bag circling horizontally hangs on an inclined string: part of tension supports its weight, while its horizontal component bends the path. Inside a funnel, oblique support and weight likewise combine into a horizontal inward resultant.

</section>
<section id="try">

Follow the hand crank, closed belt and stepped pulleys to the rotating channels. Each ball pushes a stop, which operates a lever and spring sleeve. Begin with equal masses, radii and angular speeds, then change just one condition.

Doubling mass at fixed radius and angular speed doubles the radial force. Doubling radius at fixed mass and angular speed also doubles it. Doubling angular speed at fixed mass and radius multiplies force by four. These comparisons use F = mω²r; holding angular speed fixed is different from holding linear speed fixed.

Exploration switches between actual apparatus. Inspect tangential release, change the bag’s angular speed, or change the funnel orbit radius. A funnel’s required angular speed follows from its radius and slope, rather than being an independent control.

</section>
<section id="deeper">

<details>

<summary>Force transmission and measurement</summary>

The demonstrator is a reconstruction of the textbook mechanism, not a dimensionally exact commercial instrument. An adjustable pushrod accommodates each selected ball radius. Both arms of the massless lever retain their lengths as the lever rotates about a fixed pivot. Spring extension is solved from torque balance. The sleeve translates vertically; a horizontal sliding joint accommodates the sideways motion of the lever endpoint. Readouts show calibrated radial force on the ball, not uncorrected spring tension.

A single closed, nonslipping belt connects pulleys at matching heights. Equal pulley radii give equal angular speeds; doubling the driver radius relative to the follower doubles the follower speed. Bearing resistance, belt elasticity, lever inertia and spring oscillations are omitted. Readings represent steady rotation after each configuration has been set.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Constraints for the bag and funnel</summary>

For a stable conical pendulum of length L, cosθ = g/(ω²L), Tcosθ = mg and Tsinθ = mω²r. At insufficient speed the model returns to vertical suspension instead of inventing an impossible horizontal orbit.

For a smooth funnel wall inclined by β above horizontal, Ncosβ = mg and Nsinβ = mω²r, giving ω² = gtanβ/r. The ball center lies one ball radius along the cone’s surface normal, preserving real contact with the inner wall. Air resistance, friction and spin are omitted. The textbook funnel is a force-analysis application; the scene depicts a consistent ideal steady orbit, not an experimental recording.

</details>
