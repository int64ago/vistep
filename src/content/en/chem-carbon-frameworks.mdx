<section id="understand">

A structural formula records connectivity; a ball-and-stick model places it in space. Methane’s four directions are not a flat cross, ethene is planar, and ethyne is linear. Multiple sticks encode bond order, so every stick must not be counted as a separate sigma bond.

Textbook Experimental Activity 8 remains a **model-construction activity**. No chemical reaction is performed, and switching the selected molecule does not mean one substance spontaneously turns into another.

</section>
<section id="try">

Rotate methane, ethene and ethyne to expose their spatial arrangements. Ethane, ethene and ethyne contain 7 sigma, 5 sigma plus 1 pi, and 3 sigma plus 2 pi bonds respectively; connectivity generates each count.

Dichloromethane offers six chlorine-vertex choices and continuous rigid alignment. Butane and isobutane share a formula but have different carbon adjacency. The four-carbon graphs derive hydrogen counts from each carbon’s remaining valence.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Geometry, rotations and counts</summary>

The tetrahedron uses normalized directions (1,1,1),(1,−1,−1),(−1,1,−1) and (−1,−1,1). Every pair has dot product −1/3, giving 109.4712°. Ethene uses an ideal coplanar 120° geometry and ethyne a 180° line. Terminal alkane hydrogens and the two hydrogens at internal chain carbons derive from the same tetrahedral constraints.

Every choice of two chlorine vertices maps to the same reference through one of the tetrahedron’s 12 proper rotations, without reflection or reconnecting bonds. Four-carbon graph hydrogen counts are four minus the adjacent bond-order sum.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Precision and pi guides</summary>

One model unit represents 100 pm. Approximate teaching lengths are C–H 109 pm, acetylenic C–H 106 pm, C–C 154 pm, C=C 134 pm, C≡C 120 pm and C–Cl 177 pm. Ideal angles differ from fine experimental geometry. Thermal motion, energy minimization and electron density are not calculated.

Translucent regions mark pi directions; they are not computed isodensity surfaces. Two regions of one colour form one pi guide. The 3D and flat fallback share atomic coordinates, connectivity, rotations and pi-guide geometry. Sphere radii and stick thickness are illustrative. The C4 examples are not an exhaustive hydrocarbon enumeration.
