<section id="understand">

The two textbook experiments form one evidence chain. Sulfur dioxide dissolves, less gas remains in the inverted tube, and water rises. The resulting solution is acidic. Fuchsin added to it fades, then regains colour on heating. Sulfur dioxide is colourless; dots only track sulfur-containing material.

Experiments 5-1 and 5-2 keep separate learning entries. The final unheated control is an added teaching comparison, not a claim that the textbook lists another experiment.

</section>
<section id="try">

Compare a sealed tube with one opened underwater. The sealed boundary prevents contact with water, so changing progress cannot move water through the stopper. Once open, gas decreases, water rises and dots move from the gas inventory to the aqueous inventory.

In the fuchsin view, adjust bleaching and colour recovery separately. These are qualitative observation states, not seconds, concentrations or heater temperatures. The paired samples show different outcomes from an equally bleached starting point.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Why the gas space shrinks</summary>

The approximately uniform tube is 0.18 m high with its mouth 0.03 m underwater, at an ambient pressure of 101325 Pa. When water rises by r from the mouth, gas pressure is P₀−ρgr and its volume fraction is (H−r)/H. Their product determines the remaining ideal-gas amount; the model solves for r. Temperature is fixed and the rounded cap volume is neglected.

Dissolution extent is an input. Animation speed is not a diffusion model or a fitted Henry-law equilibrium. The textbook’s approximate solubility does not by itself establish a liquid-rise time curve. Aqueous dots include the connected reservoir and schematically show transfer and dispersion, not molecular trajectories. With no gas phase remaining, the model assigns no gas pressure.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Acidity and colour recovery</summary>

The textbook writes SO₂+H₂O⇌H₂SO₃ to introduce the reversible interaction of an acidic oxide with water. The solution is not represented as containing only discrete H₂SO₃ molecules; dissolution and acid-dissociation equilibria matter. This film shows an acidic result without inventing a numerical pH unsupported by concentration data. The paper colour is qualitative, not a calibrated chart.

Fuchsin is represented by conserved coloured and colourless state fractions. Heating restores part of the bleached fraction. This is not a molecular-structure or kinetic model. The textbook refers to unstable colourless products with certain dyes; recovery cannot be generalized to all dyes or conditions, or confused with chlorine-water oxidative bleaching.

Textbook: required 2, pages 3–4 (PDF 8–9). The [University of Wisconsin sulfur-dioxide demonstration](https://www2.chem.wisc.edu/deptfiles/genchem/demonstrations/Gen_Chem_Pages/1819group1-8page/preparation_sulfurdioxide.htm) independently shows indicator change as the gas dissolves. These visuals support classroom understanding; real work involves an irritating toxic gas and ventilation controls.

</details>
