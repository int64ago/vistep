<h2 id="understand">Compare the changing process, not merely the presence of a field</h2>

A magnet held beside a coil can produce substantial flux while induced emf remains zero. Moving it closer or farther changes flux and deflects the galvanometer. Reverse either the pole or the motion while holding the other fixed: the emf reverses.

The aluminium ring makes the electrical response mechanical. Current in a closed ring opposes relative motion. A split interrupts that circulating current: an emf may still exist, but the sustained loop current and its force disappear. Ring motion is enlarged and is not a measured displacement.

A high-impedance voltage sensor records the falling-magnet experiment. At the same turn count, a higher release changes speed and the voltage pulse. Keeping the trajectory fixed while changing turns isolates the proportional dependence on turn count. The pale80-turn trace preserves that comparison.

The disc generator uses a different apparatus. Rotation gives each radius a different conductor speed; magnetic force separates charge radially. Axle and rim brushes complete the external circuit. A stationary disc does not generate merely because it is in a magnetic field.

<h2 id="try">Keep the control variables fixed</h2>

- At a fixed position, set magnet speed to zero; then reverse speed and pole separately.
- Open and close the ring under the same magnet motion.
- Compare release heights at fixed turns, then compare turns at fixed height.
- Stop or reverse the disc and check emf polarity.

<h2 id="deeper">Flux, force and energy come from the same function</h2>

<div className="formula">ε = −N dΦ/dt; I = ε/R; Fmagnet = NI dΦ/dz</div>

The bar magnet is approximated as an axial point dipole. A circular loop of radius0.025m has flux μ₀ma²/[2(a²+z²)^(3/2)], where m is magnetic moment. The energy balance is−Fv = I²R. Self-inductance, finite magnet shape and feedback from ring motion are omitted.

The falling-magnet sensor draws negligible current, so magnetic braking is neglected. The magnet follows gravitational motion and stops at the bottom. The drawing shows representative windings; calculations use the labelled total turn count. The disc uses ε = Bωr²/2 with ideal brushes, omitting contact resistance and fringe fields. The axle-to-rim polarity convention remains fixed.
