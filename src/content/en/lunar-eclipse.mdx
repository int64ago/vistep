<h2 id="understand">A full Moon also needs to be near a node</h2>

Lunar eclipses occur near full Moon, without occurring at every full Moon. The lunar orbit is inclined about 5.145° to the ecliptic, so most full Moons miss Earth's shadow above or below. Nodes mark where the two orbital planes cross. A full Moon near a node can enter the shadow.

The model fixes full-Moon geometry and changes inclination i, orbital angle F from the node and lunar distance D. Latitude obeys `β=asin(sin i·sin F)`, giving a shadow-axis offset `D sin β`. The film's F scan compares different full-Moon geometries rather than minutes in a real eclipse.

<h2 id="try">Penumbra, umbra and projected area</h2>

The finite Sun produces an umbral and penumbral cone behind Earth. In the penumbra only part of the direct Sun is blocked; in the umbra direct sunlight is entirely blocked. Calculated cross-section radii determine both shadow disks, and the enlarged lunar close view uses the same intersection geometry.

Touching only the penumbra gives a penumbral eclipse. Partial overlap of the lunar projection with the umbra gives a partial eclipse; complete containment gives totality. “Projected disk in umbra” reports overlapping circle area, not a percentage brightness decrease or a test of the Moon's center alone.

<h2 id="deeper">Geometry does not directly predict color</h2>

<details>
<summary>Atmospheric light, distance and approximations</summary>

Earth's atmosphere can scatter and refract remaining sunlight into the umbra, giving totality a copper-red appearance. The optional tint qualitatively illustrates this light. Neither gray shading nor red tint comes from radiative transfer or predicts an event's brightness. Direct sunlight being blocked does not imply zero light of every kind.

Changing lunar distance changes both axial offset and shadow radii. Paraxial similar triangles give `R_umbra=R_E−x(R_S−R_E)/S` and `R_penumbra=R_E+x(R_S+R_E)/S`, where x is axial lunar distance and S is Sun-Earth distance. Spherical mean radii omit atmospheric shadow enlargement, terrain, changing solar distance and real ephemerides. This does not predict event dates, contacts or durations.

Explore changes F, i, D and the qualitative light tint. Zero inclination is a counterfactual: under this fixed full-Moon alignment, the Moon passes centrally through the umbra. Wide and close cross-sections each retain a fixed scale throughout a chapter. Exploration uses the fixed wide view; the separate fixed-size lunar close view stays legible on phones.
