<h2 id="understand">A printed image first appears somewhere else</h2>

A laser printer does not use a tiny pen to draw on paper. Follow the gold pixel through six stages and watch its information change carriers.

**The first canvas is the drum's electrical potential.** A charging roller prepares a uniform surface. The laser then changes the potential at image positions. There is no visible ink yet, only a latent electrical image.

In the negatively charged toner model used here, exposure makes the drum less negative. The field between developer and drum moves toner to those exposed areas. The image becomes visible as powder, but remains on the drum.

A transfer field moves the powder to passing paper. Finally, heat and pressure soften the toner resin and bond it to the sheet. **The laser writes the electrical pattern; toner makes it visible; the fuser makes it last.**

<h2 id="try">What would you see if you stopped here?</h2>

Open Explore to select a stage or scrub through the cycle. Follow each stage's output rather than only the final page.

- **Exposure:** the information exists as a potential difference, but there is no visible toner image.
- **Development:** the image is visible on the drum, not yet on the paper.
- **Transfer:** toner reaches the sheet but is not firmly attached.
- **Fusing:** loose powder becomes a durable mark.

Choose another pattern and another pixel. Every image cell follows the same causal chain, coordinated with the movement of the drum and paper.

> Why is a freshly printed page often warm? The fusing stage gives you the answer.

<h2 id="deeper">Why does negative toner move toward a still-negative drum?</h2>

<details>
<summary>Potential differences matter, not just plus and minus signs</summary>

Electric fields depend on differences in potential. Saying that like charges repel does not explain the complete machine. An exposed drum region may still be negative, but its potential can be higher than the developer's, creating a field that moves negative toner toward it. The unexposed region has a different field relationship.

The experiment distinguishes relative charge states; drawn symbols are not measured voltages. Polarity, development method, transfer structure and actual potentials vary between printers.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why is cleaning part of printing?</summary>

Not all toner transfers to paper. Leftover powder must be removed and the drum prepared for charging again, or the previous image could affect the next page. Finishing one cycle establishes the starting conditions for the next.

</details>

This is a monochrome electrophotographic printer, not an inkjet. Color laser printers coordinate multiple image layers and registration, but each layer follows a similar basic process. Proportions and timings are simplified; shafts, gears, belt and paper share a continuous kinematic model.
