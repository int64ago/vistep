<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

This film follows PEP Selective Compulsory 2, Chapter 3, Section 4: Experiment 3-2 on page 95, Experiment 3-3 on page 96, Experiment 3-5 on page 97, and the page 104 laboratory activity “Formation of simple coordination compounds.” The final FeCl₃/K₃[Fe(CN)₆] comparison belongs to that activity; Experiment 3-4 is not counted again as a new position here.

The six salts retain textbook order: anhydrous CuSO₄, CuCl₂·2H₂O, CuBr₂, NaCl, K₂SO₄ and KBr. The first three solids begin white, green and dark brown, but become blue in ample water. The other salts serve as controls. Concentration, hydration and competing ligands matter; this dilute comparison does not describe every copper-halide solution at every concentration.

A little ammonia precipitates Cu(OH)₂; further ammonia and mixing dissolve it into a deep-blue solution. See the [RSC equilibrium experiment](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/an-equilibrium-using-copperii-and-ammonia/1711.article). Ethanol and scratching promote deep-blue [Cu(NH₃)₄]SO₄·H₂O crystallization, distinct from the original hydroxide. A related preparation appears in the [university laboratory manual](https://www.pssou.ac.in/naac/1.3.3/2.%20MANUAL%20FOR%20FIELD%20WORK%2C%20PROJECTS%2C%20INTERNSHIP/BSC_CHEMISTRY.pdf).

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

Copper and iron progress represents qualitative observed stages, not drops, time or predicted yield. Copper is allocated among hydrated, hydroxide, dissolved-complex and crystalline pools summing to 1. The final crystal fraction 0.8 is a drawing parameter, **not an 80% experimental yield**. Color interpolation is not spectroscopy. Water controls retain the principal solid without modeling every trace-solubility or dilution effect.

The close-up preserves the copper center and ligand identities. Idealized water uses O—H distance 0.96 Å and angle 104.5°; ammonia uses N—H distance 1.01 Å and angle 107°. Four final equatorial Cu—N distances of 2.03 Å and two axial Cu—O distances of 2.33 Å follow [an aqueous X-ray scattering study](https://academic.oup.com/bcsj/article-abstract/52/2/415/7358133) under its specific conditions. Hydrated copper and higher-ammonia solutions need not retain that exact coordination. Pale axial waters distinguish the textbook shorthand from additional solvation. The replacement paths are pedagogical, not transition states or kinetics.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

The silver calculation uses ideal concentrations, Ksp(AgCl)=1.8×10⁻¹⁰ and β₂([Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺)=1.7×10⁷, as tabulated in [Truman University’s analytical chemistry material](https://chemlab.truman.edu/chemical-principles/inorganic-quantitative-analysis/). At specified free ammonia L, define α=1+β₂L². Dissolved total silver x and background chloride c obey x(c+x)=Kspα. The positive root gives saturation solubility, capped by initial silver. Free silver is x/α; complexed silver is x−x/α. Silver and released chloride are conserved. See also [Indiana University’s demonstration](https://www.chem.indiana.edu/faculty-research/faculty-resources/chemistry-demos/demo/solubility-of-silver-chloride/).

Default initial silver 0.003 mol/L and background chloride 0.05 mol/L are illustrative model inputs, **not reconstructed concentrations from the textbook’s drops**. Volume is fixed and free ammonia is externally prescribed, not confused with total ammonia added. Activities, ammonia protonation and other complexes are omitted. Higher chloride or insufficient free ammonia can leave precipitate; complexation does not dissolve unlimited solid unconditionally.

The ferric chloride sample develops red thiocyanate-complex color. FeSCN²⁺ is a simple representative, with actual speciation dependent on conditions; see [Buffalo State’s equilibrium experiment](https://staff.buffalostate.edu/nazareay/che112/ex6.htm). Ferricyanide predominantly retains its coordinated iron and shows no comparable obvious red under the textbook’s brief aqueous conditions. This does not claim stability under every light, acid or time condition. Real classroom work still follows the school’s quantities and waste-handling procedure.
