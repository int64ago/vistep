<h2 id="understand">Identify the fixed quantity before interpreting a reading</h2>

Capacitance describes how much charge a capacitor holds for a given potential difference. First charge the plates, then disconnect the source. Neglecting leakage, charge Q remains fixed. The electrometer observes voltage U, allowing changes in capacitance to be inferred from C = Q/U.

With overlap and material fixed, reducing separation increases capacitance and lowers voltage. With separation fixed, reducing overlap decreases capacitance and raises voltage. Inserting a dielectric increases capacitance and lowers voltage. Each comparison changes one condition.

If an ideal source remains connected, voltage stays fixed and charge changes instead. That is a different experiment from the isolated-capacitor comparison.

<h2 id="try">Use charge and voltage to check controlled variables</h2>

- Keep the source isolated and separately change separation, overlap and dielectric. Check that Q stays constant.
- Changing overlap physically offsets one plate. The total area of each plate does not shrink.
- Reconnect the 100 V source and change geometry: voltage stays fixed while charge changes. Disconnecting preserves the charge present at that moment rather than resetting a default.

This follows the capacitance-factor experiment on pages 41–42 of chapter 10, section 4 in PEP Required Book 3.

<h2 id="deeper">Approximating plates and the electrometer</h2>

<details>
<summary>How are capacitance, dielectric and pointer related?</summary>

The central parallel-plate approximation gives C = ε₀εᵣS/d. Partial dielectric insertion divides the overlap into parallel areas with and without dielectric. Fringing at both plate and material boundaries is omitted. Field, energy and voltage use the same capacitance and charge state.

The electrometer is approximated as having high input impedance and negligible added capacitance. Its pointer balances an electric torque proportional to voltage squared against a restoring torque proportional to angle. Numeric voltage is a model reading, not a calibration for physical hardware. Real electrometers add capacitance and leakage slowly reduces charge. Plate separation is visually enlarged; numerical values use model dimensions.

</details>
