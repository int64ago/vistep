<section id="understand">

Two egg-white samples turn cloudy. Water clears one but leaves aggregates in the other. Why? And what does yellow colour after nitric acid tell us? Follow the same protein population while distinguishing **solubility, conformation and chemical composition**.

## Cloudiness starts the question; water provides a comparison

Elective experiment 4-3 uses ammonium sulfate to salt out protein. The film retains eight chain identities as they cluster and disperse. Water does not remove salt: increasing volume lowers its concentration and changes solubility conditions. Low and high salt have different effects, and proteins need not precipitate at the same concentration.

Experiment 4-4 compares heat, silver nitrate and ethanol in separate samples. Heat can disturb interactions stabilizing a fold; metal ions interact with protein groups; ethanol changes the solvent environment. Different causes can produce similar aggregation. Water leaves these treated samples aggregated. **Dilution is not redissolution, and redissolution alone does not establish recovered activity.** Denaturation changes conformation and function without necessarily hydrolysing the peptide chain.

## Three required-book observations address different levels

The first part of required experiment 7-9 uses lead acetate. Follow unfolding and aggregation, then predict the effect of water: dilution does not erase these aggregates. Continuous chain symbols distinguish this conformation change from chain hydrolysis. They do not locate actual lead-binding sites.

The second part uses concentrated nitric acid and heat. White precipitation precedes yellow colour. Enlarge a **tyrosine side chain** and locate its phenolic hydroxyl, aromatic ring and methylene connection to the peptide chain. A hydrogen next to the hydroxyl is replaced by a nitro group. The ring and chain connection remain; nitrated aromatic sites contribute to the colour. This is a net structural correspondence, not an elementary reaction pathway.

Equal protein amounts need not contain equal numbers of susceptible sites. Tryptophan can also contribute to the test; phenylalanine has an aromatic ring but lacks the phenolic hydroxyl and responds weakly under usual test conditions. An aromatic ring therefore does not guarantee equally strong yellow colour. A weak result cannot establish absence of protein. Actual responses also depend on concentration, site environment, treatment conditions and other reactions.

The third part burns hair and silk. Fibres curl and darken as thermal decomposition changes composition. The enlarged material account separates into altered residue and volatile products; it is no longer merely a different fold of the original material. The textbook's burnt-feather odour is a separate observation that the screen neither reproduces nor verifies.

</section>
<section id="try">

## Predict before changing one condition

Which sample should disperse after adding water: salted-out protein or the represented denatured aggregates? At equal protein amount, should a sample with fewer reactive sites still be equally yellow? Can the same prediction be transferred from tyrosine to phenylalanine simply because both have aromatic rings?

Exploration captures the first visible film state and retains later edits. Change progress, water, site count, side-chain identity, acid or warming conditions. Watching or replaying the film does not overwrite your experiment. Without nitric acid, the model forms no nitrated product. An unheated model does not predict the actual rate of slow reactions.

<details>
<summary>Model, net reaction and limits</summary>

The eight curves are enlarged chain symbols, not a real atomic structure or folding pathway. Soluble/aggregated and native/denatured are two partitions of the same inventory. The illustrative relation S=1.4exp(0.8I−3I²) gives a solubility trend, with I a model salt concentration. Aggregation fractions of 0.80–0.92 are selected teaching parameters, not temperature, time or dose measurements. Neither universally harmless salting out nor universally irreversible denaturation is assumed.

The colour account represents one formal mononitration: Ar–H + HNO₃ → Ar–NO₂ + H₂O, with Ar the tracked aromatic side chain. Nitro substitution does not break the peptide chain. The drawing does not resolve the full mechanism in strong acid, all side reactions or tryptophan products. A, B and C are equal amounts of illustrative proteins with 3, 1 and 0 susceptible sites; they are not real sequences. Colour is neither absorbance nor a detection-limit calculation. The weak phenylalanine comparison concerns the usual test and does not mean its ring can never be nitrated.

Burning divides illustrative material between residue and volatile products without identifying individual molecules, real yields or odour compounds. It does not depict hydrolysis into amino acids.

Protein structural levels and tofu preparation have separate explanations. The apparatus belongs to the textbook laboratory context; this film focuses on evidence and explanation.
