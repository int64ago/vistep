<section id="understand">

Cloudiness alone does not distinguish salting out from denaturation and aggregation. Continue with water addition while tracking the same protein population.

<ChemProteinStates client:visible />

## Water addition provides the comparison

Elective experiment 4-3 precipitates egg-white protein with ammonium sulfate, then observes redissolution after water addition. The same protein identities remain in the picture; neither protein nor salt simply vanishes. Dilution changes concentration and solubility conditions.

Experiment 4-4 separately uses heat, silver nitrate and ethanol. Under the textbook conditions, their aggregates do not simply recover on adding water. Salting out exploits solubility changes; denaturation alters conformation and function and can promote aggregation, but does not mean the peptide chain has already been hydrolysed. Precipitation, redissolution and functional recovery are distinct observations.

## Preserve reagent identity and evidence type

Required experiment 7-9 uses lead acetate, whereas the elective comparison uses silver nitrate and ethanol. The film retains these different curricular identities.

Concentrated nitric acid first produces white precipitation; warming can then produce yellow colour associated with susceptible aromatic residues. Equal colour strength cannot be assumed for all proteins. Burning hair or silk involves thermal decomposition and composition changes. The textbook’s odour description is separate textual evidence; the screen does not simulate smell.

<section id="try">

## Compare dilution with redissolution

Raise treatment progress in the salting view, then add model water. Aggregation decreases. Repeat with heat, silver salt, ethanol or lead salt: concentration falls, but the water control does not erase the aggregates already represented by that treatment.

In the nitric-acid view, change the illustrative fraction of reactive aromatic sites. Precipitation and yellow colour are independent variables. Fewer reactive sites weaken the colour without making total protein disappear. Exploration settings do not affect the film.

<details>
<summary>Teaching model and limits</summary>

The eight curves are enlarged chain symbols, not atomic structures of egg proteins. Salting retains their folded shapes; denaturation spreads and aggregates them. Neither view computes a real energy landscape or bond mechanism. Soluble/aggregated and native/denatured are two separate partitions of the same protein inventory.

The selected solubility relation S=1.4exp(0.8I−3I²) illustrates low- and high-salt trends. I is a model concentration, not a fitted experiment. Added water changes concentration and capacity. Aggregation fractions 0.80–0.92 for the denaturing treatments are illustrative parameters, not dose or temperature predictions. The model retains a treatment state; it does not model refolding kinetics. Liquid levels and floc shapes are qualitative, and the percentages are not measurements. General salting out is not assumed perfectly harmless, nor all denaturation universally irreversible.

The colour account tracks reactive aromatic sites and separates white precipitation from later yellow development. It does not predict absorbance or treat all aromatic residues as equally reactive. The textbook metal salts, concentrated acid and burning observations belong in a school laboratory; the film does not reproduce direct smelling or handling procedures.
