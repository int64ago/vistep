<h2 id="understand">The tip senses light; the region below bends</h2>

An intact coleoptile bends toward unilateral light. Removing its tip removes the selected response. Shading the tip prevents directional bending, whereas shading the lower region does not. Together these comparisons separate stimulus perception from the region of differential growth. They do not mean that light is required to produce auxin: a shaded intact tip can still support elongation.

The Darwin, Boysen-Jensen, Paal and Went sequences are **original reconstructions of historical experiments**. Brown markers visualize a proposed influence. They do not claim that those investigators directly observed IAA molecules. Evidence for a transferable growth-promoting substance and subsequent chemical identification of indole-3-acetic acid are distinct steps.

<h2 id="try">Choose comparisons that distinguish explanations</h2>

An agar gap allows the tip's influence to reach the growing region; an added barrier tests passage. Paal's off-center tip produces asymmetric growth even in darkness. Went's comparison first conditions agar by contact with tips, then places it on one side of a decapitated coleoptile. Blank agar provides the corresponding control. Persistent representative markers cross the actual tip–agar contact before the growth comparison.

Move the concentration cursor across the root, bud and stem responses. Zero on the vertical axis means **no growth change relative to an untreated reference**, not no growth. The same concentration can lie in different response ranges for different organs. These declared illustrative functions are not concentration recommendations for cultivation.

The transport view compares two hypotheses: baseward-only transport and transport in both directions. A positive result with an apical donor and basal receiver is compatible with either. An inverted segment and a no-donor control provide discriminating comparisons. The inverted segment has its morphological base at the top of the screen: polarity is anatomical rather than gravitational. Displayed amounts are **conditional predictions**, not invented measurements. Real roots have distinct tissue-specific circulation routes; the simplified assay does not establish universal one-way transport throughout a plant.

<h2 id="deeper">Growth lengths also determine the visible geometry</h2>

Both sides begin at 105 normalized length units and gain 20 times local signal times progress. Their length difference across a width of 24 determines curvature. The sheath, tip, agar and cap share this geometry. Numerical integration independently verifies both edge lengths, while mirrored illumination and removed-tip controls test boundaries.

A first-order loading model leaves exp(−3p) in the tip and transfers the remainder into agar. Sixteen persistent representative tracers cross the contact at quantiles of that loading distribution. Numerical amount is exactly conserved; finite markers are a discrete illustration. The transport assay uses the analytic solution for two consecutive transfers among donor, tissue and receiver. Organ-response functions combine declared promoting and inhibiting terms rather than fit measurements from a particular plant.
