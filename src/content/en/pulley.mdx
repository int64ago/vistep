<h2 id="understand">Count the supporting strands, not the wheels</h2>

A single fixed pulley redirects the effort. It still takes 120 N to lift the **120 N load** because only one strand supports that load.

When a pulley moves with the load, two strands pull upward on the moving assembly. At 60 N each, they supply the required 120 N together. With four supporting strands, each carries 30 N. Count the strands that directly support the moving assembly, rather than every wheel in the picture.

As the load rises, all supporting strands become shorter. Since the rope keeps the same total length, that combined shortening appears at the free end. Two strands require twice the pulling distance; four require four times the distance.

The final chapter adds 40 N of weight to the moving block itself. The rope must now support 160 N, so each of four strands carries 40 N. Lifting the apparatus takes work as well as lifting the blue load.

<h2 id="try">Balance the force account and the length account</h2>

- In exploration, switch between one, two and four supporting strands. Follow the continuous copper rope, then compare the required pull.
- Keep the arrangement fixed and scrub the lift distance. The free end and both travel scales follow the same rope-length constraint.
- Pause in the final chapter. Compare input work with the work of lifting the load and the moving block together.

Exploration uses a weightless moving block. The final film chapter isolates its weight as a correction. All positions come from the same deterministic calculation, including after seeking.

<h2 id="deeper">Two constraints describe one machine</h2>

<details>
<summary>Support and rope length</summary>

Let n be the number of supporting strands, W the load weight and B the moving block weight. In quasi-static motion:

<div className="formula">n T = W + B; pulling distance = n × lift</div>

<div className="formula">input work = T × pulling distance = (W + B) × lift</div>

The rope is massless and inextensible. The model omits bearing friction and rotational inertia. Straight strands meet circular grooves tangentially. Arc lengths stay fixed, so the position of the free end follows from total length conservation rather than being snapped to a prescribed endpoint.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Limits of the integer ratio</summary>

Angled strands support the load through only the vertical components of their tensions. Friction and acceleration also change the force relationships. Real lifting equipment requires rated ropes, sheaves, anchors and attachments; this scene is not a rigging or equipment-selection guide.

</details>
