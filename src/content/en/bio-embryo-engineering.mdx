<h2 id="understand">Understand it</h2>

Elective volume 3, pages 56–63, connects fertilization and early development to IVF, embryo transfer and splitting. This film uses livestock examples. Oocyte maturation and sperm capacitation are separate preparations. The sperm traverses surrounding structures before membrane contact and fusion. Male and female pronuclei retain their distinct origins; later nuclear-envelope breakdown is not represented as two bubbles simply merging.

During early cleavage, cell number increases while total volume remains approximately constant. Dividers show sectional partitions, not exact three-dimensional blastomere geometry. Compressed animation time cannot establish development times for cattle, sheep, mice or other species.

Transfer requires embryo evaluation and a physiologically suitable same-species recipient. Six samples are synthetic examples, not an observed collection success rate. E1 follows the catheter through the cervix into the uterine horn on the corpus-luteum side. Embryo and instrument dimensions are enlarged to explain the route; this is not procedural training. Transfer still requires subsequent pregnancy checks and evaluation.

Distinguish the blastocyst's inner cell mass, trophectoderm and cavity. Splitting into two pieces is insufficient: the inner cell mass needs appropriate allocation. Forty origin markers track identity rather than real cell counts. A balanced cut distributes the eight inner-cell-mass markers four and four; an offset cut produces eight and zero.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

- Remove oocyte maturity or sperm capacitation independently. One cannot substitute for the other, and motility alone does not prove capacitation.
- Pause before and after membrane contact. Follow the sperm origin, membrane passage and separate pronuclei. Extracellular layers and biochemical details are simplified; the opening does not represent a physical piercing instrument.
- Change the recipient-cycle offset. The interface compares timing without inventing pregnancy probabilities.
- Follow E1 inside the catheter until release into the uterine lumen. CL identifies the corpus luteum and the corresponding side.
- Compare cuts through and away from the inner cell mass. Keep actual observations separate from synthetic marker counts.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

**Synchrony is not a universal threshold.** The FAO cattle-transfer manual discusses how tolerance of donor–recipient asynchrony varies with embryo quality, storage and stage. The scene therefore displays a day difference without declaring that only exact alignment succeeds. A recipient's gestational environment does not replace the embryo's nuclear ancestry.

**One geometric route.** The uterine lumen, catheter and E1 trajectory use the same piecewise curves. The embryo travels within the instrument before moving beyond its tip. This schematic explains connectivity rather than reproducing an individual's anatomical dimensions or teaching a clinical procedure.

**Splitting limits.** A radius-55 sectional outline contains persistent origin markers. The blade crosses before opposite translations separate the portions; markers neither appear nor disappear. The cut changes geometric allocation, while injury, cell death, regulation, culture and recipient differences remain outside the model. No survival probability is calculated. Real splitting can use suitable morulae or blastocysts; this film selects a blastocyst to explain inner-cell-mass allocation, without implying that morulae contain a blastocoel.

Shared embryonic origin does not guarantee identical phenotypes. The textbook notes limitations involving birth weight, coat patterns and increasing numbers of splits. Research on demi-embryo morphology and expression similarly separates producing two portions from establishing equivalent subsequent development.
