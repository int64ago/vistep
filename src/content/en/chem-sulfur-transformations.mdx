<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Sulfur and electron inventories</summary>

The copper equation is Cu+2H₂SO₄(concentrated)→CuSO₄+SO₂+2H₂O. One sulfur enters SO₂ while another stays in sulfate; not all consumed sulfuric acid is reduced.

In the sulfide/sulfur-four comproportionation path, two sulfurs at −2 each lose two electrons to reach zero. One sulfur at +4 gains four electrons to reach zero. Sulfur and electrons balance; aqueous speciation and acid–base reactions are not compressed into a single claimed molecular mechanism.

Acidic permanganate accepting five electrons per ion forms Mn²⁺. Sulfur moving from −2 to zero supplies two electrons per sulfur, whereas reaching +6 supplies eight. These are separate admissible balances, not evidence that fading uniquely identifies either endpoint. Insufficient acidity can also change the manganese product; that branch is not simulated.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Iron–sulfur heat propagation and limits</summary>

Thirty-six adjacent cells store temperature and reacted fraction. A local heat pulse acts for only 0.7 model time units, followed by neighbour diffusion, threshold-triggered exothermic reaction and environmental heat loss. The explicit time step is 0.01, with diffusivity 4, heat-loss coefficient 0.15, reaction coefficient 5 and heat-release coefficient 1.4. All are dimensionless teaching parameters, not measured iron–sulfur kinetics. The discrete model explains ignition, propagation and cooling.

Reaction in each cell is limited by the available iron or sulfur. The default Fe/S amount ratio of about 1.15 approximates conversion of the textbook activity’s mass ratio, leaving a small iron excess in the ideal main pathway. Pure product is not claimed. Real work in air can include iron oxidation and sulfur combustion; the heat-front model computes only Fe+S→FeS.
