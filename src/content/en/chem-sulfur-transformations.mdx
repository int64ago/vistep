<section id="understand">

A yellow dispersion, fading fuchsin and a moving glow are different kinds of evidence. Begin with those observations, then follow the atoms, identify the electron donor and ask what sustains the change. Colour alone does not establish purity, concentration or a complete reaction pathway.

The film preserves three textbook positions: Required Book 2, P5 experiment 5-3, the P8 inquiry and P30 activity 5. The numbered experiment uses a liftable copper coil; the activity uses a copper piece. When the activity entry borrows the earlier cooled-solution observation, the scene identifies it as evidence from P5.

</section>
<section id="try">

**Predict before revealing the account.** Two permanganate units can accept ten electron units if both reach the selected Mn²⁺ endpoint in acid. Sulfur starting at −2 provides two electrons when it reaches zero, but eight when it reaches +6. Compare those two assumed endpoints, then change the available oxidant and sulfide amounts.

The two sulfur atoms in the copper reaction have different destinations. Follow the same 15 atoms: one sulfur stays at +6 in sulfate, the other reaches +4 in SO₂, and the remaining oxygen and hydrogen form two water units. With only one sulfuric-acid unit and one copper unit, how much copper can this selected net reaction consume?

On the iron–sulfur strip, remove ignition and then remove reaction heat feedback separately. Finally, vary the initial masses and compare the dark appearance with the predicted material left over.

</section>
<section id="deeper">
<details>
<summary>Electron packets represent an account, not a flight through water</summary>

In the sulfur-forming net pathway, two sulfur centres starting at −2 each provide two electron units. One centre starting at +4 accepts four, bringing all three to zero. The diagram keeps their identities. “In transit” retains the unassigned part of the formal account; it does not assert that solvated electrons follow these paths or that every displayed account state is an isolable intermediate.

For copper, the net equation is Cu + 2H₂SO₄(concentrated) → CuSO₄ + SO₂ + 2H₂O. Only one of its two sulfur atoms is reduced; copper changes from zero to +2. Composition frames group net material, rather than depict isolated salt molecules or a bond-by-bond mechanism. Ending contact does not erase products already formed. For the copper-piece apparatus, the cooling view represents a selected post-reaction state: it does not predict the cooling rate or claim that switching off heat instantly stops every reaction.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Why fading permanganate does not identify the sulfur endpoint</summary>

The film compares two separately assumed endpoints. Purple fading does not automatically choose between them. With excess protons and the selected Mn²⁺ endpoint, each Mn accepts five electron units. Two permanganate units and five initial sulfide units therefore permit at most five sulfur units to reach S, or 1.25 to reach sulfate.

A full acidic net equation also needs hydrogen and oxygen. Written in the H₂S acid form, the two selected accounts are 5H₂S + 2MnO₄⁻ + 6H⁺ → 5S + 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O, and 5H₂S + 8MnO₄⁻ + 14H⁺ → 5SO₄²⁻ + 8Mn²⁺ + 12H₂O. These are stoichiometric bounds, not a complete speciation, kinetic or yield model of the textbook sodium-sulfide tube. Insufficient acidity may also change the manganese product and invalidate the same five-electron budget.

The sulfide/sulfur(IV) scene traces only sulfur and its electron subaccount. The earlier textbook example SO₂ + 2H₂S → 3S + 2H₂O supports the sulfur-forming relation, without supplying every ion or acid–base equilibrium in the activity solution.

</details>
<details>
<summary>A moving glow and a dark product answer different questions</summary>

The heat model stores temperature variables and reaction fractions in 36 neighbouring cells. A local source acts for 0.7 model-time units. Heat diffusion, reaction heat and environmental loss then determine propagation. The time step is 0.01; diffusion, cooling, reaction-rate and default heat-release coefficients are 4, 0.15, 5 and 1.4. All are dimensionless teaching parameters, not measured iron–sulfur temperatures or rates.

P30 supplies 1.0 g iron and 0.5 g sulfur. If only the ideal main reaction Fe + S → FeS goes to completion, sulfur limits the reaction: about 1.371 g FeS forms and 0.129 g iron remains. A dark appearance does not prove pure FeS. Oxidation of iron and combustion of sulfur in air lie outside that main-reaction calculation, so product analysis remains necessary to test the prediction.

</details>
