<section id="understand">

Near the two glass-rod tips in textbook Figure 5-12, ammonia reacts with hydrogen chloride: NH₃(g)+HCl(g)→NH₄Cl(s). Both reactant gases are colourless. The visible white cloud comes from newly formed fine suspended solid particles.

Smoke and mist can both look white, but their dispersed phases differ. Here smoke means small solid particles in a gas; water mist consists mainly of liquid droplets. The enlarged shot explains particle nature without presenting the polygons as measured microscopic shapes.

</section>
<section id="try">

Change the relative rod separation and compare product formation at equal model progress. Wider separation delays substantial overlap. Choose NH₃ only: without the other reactant, ammonium chloride does not form in this model.

Colourless gases cannot be assigned measured concentrations from their visible colour. Hollow marks only help tracking at the opening. Cloud brightness follows the computed product field qualitatively; it is not a calibrated experimental yield scale.

</section>

<details>
<summary>Conserving material in the reaction field</summary>

A 40×32 grid stores nonnegative NH₃, HCl and product amounts. Neighbouring cells exchange material with no loss through the edges. The two sources supply equal teaching amounts during the first half of the simulated interval. Each local reaction step consumes equal reactant amounts, bounded by their available inventories, and adds the same amount of NH₄Cl. Nitrogen, chlorine and hydrogen therefore match the input ledger.

Time, diffusion and reaction constants are dimensionless teaching parameters. The two gases use the same effective diffusion coefficient to isolate contact-dependent reaction. The model does not predict their real diffusion-rate ratio or turn the rod demonstration into a long-tube Graham-law measurement.

Nucleation, coagulation, humidity, settling, airflow and surface deposition are not quantitatively resolved. Real aerosol formation has multiple stages, lumped here into local product formation. The observation window is a conservative teaching region, not a claim that the textbook rods are enclosed in a sealed box. Progress is not measured experimental seconds.

</details>

<summary>Textbook evidence and research scope</summary>

Textbook: required2, printed page14 / PDF19, Figure5-12 and adjacent text. The two-rod photograph and description of ammonium chloride crystals were checked. This is a body phenomenon, not a newly invented numbered experiment.

The [2019 JCP abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31005123/) identifies gas reaction, nucleation, growth and the role of water. It supports separating contact reaction from visible-particle formation rather than treating each colliding gas pair as one visible grain. Only the abstract was read; no paper rate constants or particle-size data were copied into this model. The [University of Wisconsin demonstration](https://demolab.chem.wisc.edu/physical-chemistry-demonstrations/kinetics-rate-of-diffusion-the-ammonium-chloride-ring-2/) uses a different long-tube apparatus and supplies corroborating evidence for white ammonium chloride at gas contact. It does not replace the textbook’s two-rod composition.
