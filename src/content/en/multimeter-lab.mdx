<h2 id="understand">Connections, ranges and scales change together</h2>

One pointer can serve several measurements only when the **connection, range and scale agree**. Follow the circuit path before reading the pointer.

- Voltage: connect across the target and convert the linear scale using the selected range.
- Current: insert the meter in series; its internal resistance also changes current.
- Resistance: isolate the specimen from external power. Short and zero the probes before measuring, and check again after changing the multiplier. The ohm scale is nonlinear, with zero on the right.

The sequence covers a fixed resistor, cold filament, hand contact and diode. Diode readings depend on direction and instrument conditions; they are not a fixed material resistance. This example follows the cited HIOKI analog meter: its black probe is internally positive on the resistance function. Other instruments require their own manual.

<h2 id="try">Follow the probes to diagnose faults</h2>

The fault sequence starts with an unlit lamp and checks the battery, switch, wire and outer socket terminals. **Voltage across a section does not guarantee normal operating current through it.** Diagnosis is restricted to four specified single faults. Other real possibilities, including a broken filament, remain outside this set.

<h2 id="deeper">Model and measurement limits</h2>

<details>
<summary>Model, readings and limits</summary>

The voltmeter has finite input resistance at 20 kΩ/V; the ammeter adds 0.5 Ω. Resistance mode combines a 1.5 V internal supply, adjustable internal resistance and current movement:

f = I / Iₘ; R = Rₘ (1/f − 1).

After calibration, Rₘ = 20m Ω, where m is the multiplier. A change in internal resistance represents zero error. The diode solution intersects an exponential junction curve with the instrument load line; reverse leakage is approximated without breakdown. Hands use an equivalent contact resistance. The DC lamp uses fixed resistance rather than a complete thermal model. Faulty contacts use very high finite resistance; the whole network is recalculated when the voltmeter is connected.

Images and data are teaching-model outputs, not physical measurements.

</details>
