<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

This scene covers the liquid-crystal discovery on PEP Selective Compulsory 2 page 69. Cholesteryl benzoate first becomes a cloudy fluid and then clears. T₁ marks melting and T₂ clearing. The relative temperature axis anchors those learning points without fitting numerical temperatures absent from this textbook passage.

The tilted small cell illustrates fluidity; it does not reconstruct Reinitzer's apparatus. A solid retains its shape with the cell, while liquid levels under gravity. Equal cross-sectional area is retained, neglecting expansion, menisci and viscosity. Background-grid visibility depicts cloudiness qualitatively, without calculating scattering. Cloudiness does not define every liquid crystal. See [Kent State's liquid-crystal history](https://www.kent.edu/amlci/our-legacy).

Historical cholesteryl benzoate has a cholesteric phase with spatially twisting average orientation. The later scene explicitly switches to a planar nematic teaching model, separating long-axis alignment from crystal-like long-range positional periodicity. It is not the historical material's full structure. Smectic, discotic and other phases are not all described by this nematic example.

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

Temperature and tilt expose different observations. Short visual crossfades near the transitions do not calculate coexistence ranges, melting rates or hysteresis.

All 24 rod identities persist, with a ring following one. Motion progression changes centres while disorder changes long-axis angles. Compare mobile aligned units with mobile disordered units. Reflecting boundaries and hard-centre separation prevent visual intersections; they are not molecular dynamics with a realistic interaction potential, and progression units are not physical seconds.

The original-lattice indicator averages finite-sample Fourier amplitudes at the initial x and y spacings. It is not a universal crystallinity test: random finite samples retain residual values, and rotation or different wavevectors change the result. Planar orientational alignment is Q=|mean exp(2iθ)|, so θ and θ+π represent the same headless axis. This differs from the usual three-dimensional Legendre order parameter. Both indicators describe this constructed comparison, not experimental data.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

The electrical extension models a separate ideal nematic layer with strong planar anchoring, positive dielectric anisotropy, one Frank elastic constant, uniform field, fixed temperature and quasistatic equilibrium. Short segments show local average directions, not scaled individual molecules or bonded chains. See [Andrienko's Max Planck liquid-crystal notes](https://pages-andrienko-10bcaa2045685d6c9509367c68ef5f030a701b968e1f8e8.pages.mpip-mainz.mpg.de/teaching/liquid_crystals/liquid_crystals.pdf).

With u=z/d, tilt α from the plane and v=V/Vₜₕ, the assumed problem is α″+(πv)²sinαcosα=0, with α(0)=α(1)=0. Below threshold the profile is untilted. Above threshold, the model solves 2K(k)/π=v using the complete elliptic integral and inverts the first integral for a symmetric profile, whose maximum is arcsin k. Boundary directions do not rotate with the interior. Uniform field is an approximation; real devices require electrostatics, elastic anisotropy, pretilt, surface conditions and temporal response.

The optical example uses nₒ=1.5, nₑ=1.7, thickness 1.375 μm and wavelength 0.55 μm. These teaching parameters are not historical measurements. It integrates n-effective−nₒ through the thickness using 1/n-effective²=cos²α/nₑ²+sin²α/nₒ². Retardation is δ=2π/λ times that optical-path integral. With the in-plane axis at 45° to crossed polarizers, ideal transmission is sin²(δ/2). Reflection, absorption, multiple interference, scattering and spectral bandwidth are omitted.

The chosen no-field cell gives half-wave retardation and therefore a bright output at this wavelength. Increasing field changes orientation and retardation, darkening it; removal restores the starting state. Other display modes can switch differently. This is not a full twisted-nematic pixel, colour-filter array or driven screen, and modern room-temperature materials are not identified with the historical heated sample.
