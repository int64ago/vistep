<h2 id="understand">Understand the principle</h2>

This film covers Experiment 3-1 on page 71 and “Preparation of alum crystals” on page 93 of PEP Selective Compulsory 2. The three routes use cooled molten sulfur, iodine vapor depositing on a cold surface, and concentrated hydrochloric acid added to saturated sodium chloride solution. A nearby copper-sulfate photograph does not replace the third numbered experiment. Redissolution with water is a sourced extension from the [University of Wisconsin demonstration](https://demolab.chem.wisc.edu/analytical-chemistry-demonstrations/dissociation-and-solubility-precipitating-sodium-chloride-from-its-solution-2/).

Sulfur retains its material identity across powder, melt and crystallization. The needle-like texture does not predict its allotrope, nuclei or cooling curve. The iodine apparatus places cold water above the watch glass and deposits underneath. One of 48 persistent parcels is ringed; source, vapor and deposit counts always sum to 48. They are not molecules, and their smooth paths are not molecular dynamics or measured rates. The retained-parcel model neglects leakage and does not describe the covered beaker as a pressure-tight vessel. Removing the cold boundary is an ideal contrast, not a claim that warm real containers can never collect crystals. See the [University of Colorado sublimation demonstration](https://www.colorado.edu/lab/lecture-demo-manual/t560-prep-notes).

<h2 id="try">Try it yourself</h2>

The salt comparison separates acid addition from dilution. Its explicitly idealized 1:1 salt starts with normalized sodium and chloride amounts of 1 and volume 1. Acid volume a supplies chloride amount 4a; water adds volume w. Precipitate x solves (1−x)(1+4a−x)=(1+a+w)², constrained to x≥0. Sodium in solid and solution sums to 1. Undersaturation does not create forced precipitation.

The values 4 and equilibrium constant 1 are teaching parameters, **not NaCl/HCl concentrations or a measured solubility product**. Concentrated solutions require activities: acid changes volume, ionic interactions and water activity simultaneously. These numbers explain allocation, not experimental masses or yields. Water shrinks the same deposit, helping distinguish crystallization from creating a different insoluble salt.

The alum sequence starts with warm saturated solution, clear-liquid transfer, covering, cooling and overnight seed formation. The film begins after transfer; solution preparation and pouring are not simulated. A selected seed moves continuously to the suspending thread. Replenished warm saturated solution must first cool close to room temperature. The textbook’s temperature ranges, overnight waiting and repeated replenishment remain part of the real supervised procedure. The film’s progression is not physical seconds.

<h2 id="deeper">Go deeper</h2>

One growth cycle conserves normalized formula-unit amount 1, including initial seed amount 0.06. For soluble capacity c, equilibrium crystal amount is max(0,1−c). An arbitrary exponential relaxation approaches that state; added crystal material is subtracted from solution. Linear size follows the cube root of crystal amount. A shared six-vertex octahedron supplies the geometry. Fixed density and relative face growth are assumptions, without real transport, nucleation barriers, adsorption or defects. Increasing soluble capacity can dissolve the seed: a seed alone does not guarantee growth.

Alum is KAl(SO₄)₂·12H₂O and incorporates crystal water. The normalized values represent formula-unit allocations, not a quantitatively complete mass balance that neglects water incorporation. See [IUCr crystal-growing guidance](https://www.iucr.org/__data/assets/pdf_file/0010/155944/CG_brochure.pdf) for seeds, supersaturation and crystal quality.

The layered sequence retains a clear core. It lifts above the liquid and vessel rims before moving between baths. Purple and clear layers only grow while immersed in the relevant solution. The enlargement is an artificial section, not a simulated optical image. Layer volumes add and determine outer radius; real interfaces can mix, diffuse, redissolve or grow unevenly. Switching between chrome-alum and alum solutions does not recolour the entire core or imply that aluminium and chromium cannot share sites. Formulae, chromium colour and octahedral growth are supported by [National Taiwan University’s alum laboratory notes](https://teaching.ch.ntu.edu.tw/gclab/eng/wp-content/uploads/5_M07_Preparation-of-alum.pdf).
