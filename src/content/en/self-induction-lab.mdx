<h2 id="understand">Inductance preserves continuity of current</h2>

First adjust two lamp branches to equal steady brightness. Reconnect the source: the lamp in series with the coil rises more slowly than the resistive reference. Self-induced emf opposes the increase of current; it does not permanently prevent current.

The switch-off experiment uses a different connection. A lamp and coil sit in parallel, with the switch in the source branch. Opening that switch removes the source but leaves a closed coil–lamp loop. Coil current continues in its original direction and decays. Lamp current reverses.

A bright flash is conditional. If winding resistance is below lamp resistance, the coil may carry more current before opening than the lamp did. That current enters the lamp after opening and can make it brighter. Raise winding resistance and the flash disappears, even though reversal and gradual decay remain.

<h2 id="try">Move from brightness to signed current</h2>

- Change inductance in the switch-on comparison and observe the approach to the same steady state.
- Select the current sensor to compare an RL series circuit with the same total resistance and no inductance.
- Compare two-ohm and ten-ohm windings after opening.
- Move time across the switching instant and check both current directions.

Lamps are fixed six-ohm resistances; displayed light follows electrical power without filament thermal delay. The switch-on circuit uses a six-volt source, one-ohm common resistor, and two-ohm winding and comparison resistances. Its analytical solution includes the common resistor. Sensor mode uses a separate series circuit with eight-ohm total resistance in both comparisons. After opening, magnetic energy dissipates in the closed winding–lamp loop.
