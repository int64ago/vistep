# Excavator review — 2026-09-06 (rebuild, revised with circuit and joystick)

[简体中文](zh-CN/qa-excavator.md) · [Production brief](examples/excavator-brief.md) · [Review index](README.md#review-records)

The excavator was rebuilt from scratch after the first version was judged unconvincing: a toy-like body, washed-out materials, floating pins, a detached textbook circuit diagram and close-ups that cropped the chassis. This record separates model checks, browser stills, full-length playback, transcription and the evidence that remains missing. It is a pre-publication review, not a deployment record.

## What changed

- New planar model of a 20-tonne-class machine: gooseneck box boom, tapered stick, H-link bucket four-bar solved from fixed link lengths, two boom cylinders, one stick and one bucket cylinder. Cylinder bores and rods, the 37.3 MPa implement relief and the pump flow follow the Komatsu PC200-8 brochure; masses and member lengths are teaching values.
- The near boom cylinder is sectioned in place with a clipping plane: barrel, piston, chrome rod, amber cap-end oil whose emissive strength follows pressure, pale rod-end oil, hoses with moving oil pellets and a relief return at the manifold. The far cylinder, stick and bucket cylinders stay whole.
- Callouts are projected from model pins into CSS pixels with leader lines; they flip and clamp inside the stage. A pressure gauge with the relief band and three typographic measures sit under the stage; on phones only the chapter's measure is shown while watching.
- A live circuit panel beside the machine: tank, engine-driven pump, main relief valve, one spool section of the main control valve with P/T/A/B ports, the boom cylinder and the right joystick. Joystick travel shifts the spool, the uncovered port width and the moving dashes follow the metered flow, and the same state drives the 3D machine. It overlays the stage on desktop (the camera slides the cylinder into the free width) and stacks above it on phones.
- Every chapter's flow and valve state derive from joystick travel (full travel = 130 L/min teaching flow); lowering uses the same speed law with the sign reversed. The pressure chapter adds a Pascal comparison of a Ø20 mm pump piston and the Ø120 mm boom piston at one pressure (area and force ratio 36).
- Eight chapters retimed to the measured 245.5-second recordings: dig, sectioned cylinder, closed circuit, pressure and Pascal, lever travel and speed, lever arm, bucket four-bar, relief and release.
- Camera framing is solved by projecting the machine's extreme points (including the full bucket sweep) rather than a nominal width, with separate phone yaw and elevation.
- 2D runtime fallback, catalog cover and social card are drawn from the same pose model; the fallback hides projected callouts.

## Evidence limits

- **Native listening** to both complete recordings has not been done in this review; transcription is the only speech check.
- **Physical-phone performance** was not measured. Headless viewport emulation is not a device test.
- **Reduced motion, offscreen pause, blocked autoplay and failed audio** rely on the shared player and Studio behaviour that this rebuild did not change; they were not re-exercised for this scene beyond the autoplay prompt appearing in headless runs.
- The Claude-in-Chrome window was hidden during this session, so the interactive browser could not render WebGL; headless Chrome stood in for still and playback capture.
