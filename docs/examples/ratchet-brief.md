# Ratchet — one-way constraint does not mean zero lost motion

Owner: `/root/hundred_mechanisms`. Independent original cutaway of a radial sliding pawl in a fixed guide, biased down by a visible compression spring. This explicitly labeled teaching variant makes its support and contact direction inspectable; it is not a claim to reproduce a specific wrench. The wheel has actual asymmetric polygonal teeth, not triangular decoration on an independently rotating disc.

Misconception: a ratchet adds forward power or prevents every fraction of backward travel. Track the same tooth and the pawl's working face. Prerequisites: forward/reverse direction. Adds unilateral motion, finite tooth-pitch backlash and release beyond the Geneva's reversible intermittent transmission.

Sources: [NPTEL, ratchet and pawl indexing](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/storage2/courses/112103174/module4/lec5/1.html) supports directional contact, biasing and slipping over teeth in one direction. [US7131353B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7131353B2/en), primary patent background, confirms sliding-pawl variants engaging externally toothed wheels. Our single-tooth radial guide is an independently derived ideal demonstrator, not a reproduction of that patented multi-tooth reversible construction.

Six shots: forward rotation; close view of ramp and spring return; rollback within the last tooth interval; blocked reverse request after the working faces meet; lift the pawl and reverse freely; compare 12 and 24 teeth at the same 22° prior advance. Phone 340×285 mechanism with a dedicated close view where contact matters; main wheel, guide, spring and pawl remain connected. Readouts and optional controls are HTML at least 16px.

Geometry: root radius .82 and outer radius 1. Each pitch has a straight ramp from its first tip to the next radial root, then a radial locking face. Counterclockwise forward rotation moves that face away from the pawl. Pawl occupies x∈[0,.075], slides vertically and has a flat end. Its seated height is the exact maximum of the wheel polygon within the open interior of this footprint, calculated by segment clipping. At each locked angle a radial tooth face at x=0 contacts the pawl's vertical working face; sideways load is carried by its guide, with no ideal upward release component. The top view has deliberate axial cutaway to reveal the spring and guide.

State: first advance A, then attempt rollback B. Previous stop is floor(A/pitch)×pitch, so output=max(A−B,previousStop) while engaged. Actual lost angle is A mod pitch and is less than one pitch; changing tooth count changes this interval. Lifting the pawl beyond the tooth-tip envelope releases the stop. Controls reconstruct that two-stage history, so seeking never invents hidden state.

Limits: quasistatic, rigid, frictionless contact, massless pawl/spring, immediate spring return when a tooth clears. No impact audio, stiffness/strength rating or safe lifting-device claim. The pawl is a holding pawl; an external drive supplies forward work. No claim that all ratchets have zero clearance or the same tooth shape.
