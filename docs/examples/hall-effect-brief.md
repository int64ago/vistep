# Hall effect: fix the probe convention before interpreting the sign

Owner `/root/hundred_mechanisms`. Misconceptions: electron drift follows conventional current; magnetic force does work to produce the Hall voltage; changing carrier sign reverses magnetic force at fixed conventional current; Hall voltage measures field independently of density and thickness. Prerequisites: charge sign, conventional current, force qv×B. Distinct from previous circuitry: transverse measurement and carrier-sign inference in one homogeneous sample.

Primary claim/source: OpenStax University Physics2 Hall effect for Lorentz-force balance and voltage; NIST Hall measurement guidance for single-carrier density interpretation and limitations. Exact verified URLs in packet. Uniform single-carrier conductor, classical low-field transport; no multiband, anomalous/quantum Hall, contacts or magnetoresistance correction.

Coordinate convention: conventional I along +x (screen right); +y screen up; +B out of screen. Signed drift vx=I/(nqwd). Magnetic force Fy=−qvxB; Hall field Ey=vxB gives electrical force qEy cancelling it. Meter RED lead at lower edge and BLACK at upper edge, so V_H=Vlower−Vupper=Ey w=IB/(nqd). For fixed +I,+B, both carrier signs deflect magnetically downward; electrons accumulate negative charge at lower edge and yield negative V_H. Changing carrier sign reverses drift, field and meter sign, but not magnetic force. No magnetic work since force is perpendicular to drift.

The carrier tracers move only along the steady longitudinal drift; edge charge/field is already in steady balance. Switching field or density compares steady configurations rather than inventing a dielectric-relaxation transient. Direction arrows have normalized lengths, not a force scale. Tracer dots are selected carriers, not a count-based density measurement. Display drift time is explicitly slowed.
