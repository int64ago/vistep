## The visible argument

A rechargeable cell stores reversible chemical work by changing the distribution of lithium between two hosts. Follow a fixed gold tracer through wet internal pores and its blue counterpart through the external conductor. At rest, voltage recovers without replacing charge. Charging reverses the original paths and requires energy from outside.

The material installation is an upright section: copper collector, layered graphite particles with conducting connections, a genuinely perforated separator, LFP particles with a conducting network, and an aluminum collector. Electrolyte occupies the porous region. Tabs physically join the collectors to a complete external load/source path. The internal ionic route and electrode reactions complete the working circuit; there is no electronic bridge through the separator. A fading front panel opens the initial cutaway without separating its working connections.

The color language is graphite blue-grey, muted LFP green, copper, ivory separator, gold Li⁺ and blue e⁻. A ring identifies tracer zero. All eighteen tracer IDs remain fixed. Paths are bounded and retraced with net charge; no modulo wrap, respawn or random particle clock is used. These are selected route tracers, not an atom-count or microscopic velocity simulation.

## Scientific scope

The specific chemistry is graphite/LFP. The average compositions are LiₓC₆ and LiᵧFePO₄, with x = 0.05 + 0.9z and y = 0.95 − 0.9z for usable SOC z. Each host has Q/(0.9F) moles of sites, Q = 3600 C and F = 96485.33212 C/mol. Electrode lithium inventory is conserved and every transferred mole corresponds to one Faraday of external charge. Electrolyte lithium inventory is held constant in the bulk approximation. Salt-anion motion and local electroneutrality are not individually rendered.

SOC endpoints bound a chosen usable window, not the presence or absence of lithium. Normal graphite storage is intercalation, not metallic lithium. Average stoichiometry does not resolve graphite staging, LFP two-phase coexistence, phase boundaries or heterogeneous particle kinetics. Visible layer thicknesses, grains and pores are exaggerated display geometry, not measured micrographs or manufacturing dimensions.

The deliberately authored LFP-like open-circuit curve is U(z) = 3.30 + 0.08w + 0.14w⁹ V, w = 2z − 1. It is **not a fit to a commercial cell**. With discharge-positive current, dz/dt = −I/Q; polarization follows dp/dt = (I − p/Rₚ)/Cₚ, and V = U − IR₀ − p. R₀ = 0.04 Ω, Rₚ = 0.03 Ω, Cₚ = 1500 F and τ = 45 s. RC is an equivalent passive state, not a literal capacitor or a resolved diffusion field.

Reversible chemical-work storage is Echem = Q∫U dz, referenced to the lower usable endpoint. Equivalent polarization storage is Cₚp²/2. Internal heat is I²R₀ + p²/Rₚ. The signed terminal power VI, both storage derivatives and internal heat satisfy an instantaneous energy identity. Exact exponential evolution and an analytic integral of p² also provide cumulative heat and terminal-energy bookkeeping. An independent RK4 integration checks this result rather than merely repeating the ledger identity.

The final film ledger is approximately 5.212 Wh initial-plus-supplied energy, 3.300 Wh retained, 1.818 Wh delivered, and 0.094 Wh internal heat. These rounded figures illustrate the model, not an appliance specification. At rest, chemical-work storage and SOC stay fixed; decreasing RC storage becomes heat. Voltage recovery does not invent charge or energy.

The controller stops current at usable capacity endpoints or illustrative 2.8/3.6 V limits, latching off for the remainder of that commanded segment. A bracketed threshold solve locates a voltage cutoff. This is not a real constant-current/constant-voltage charge protocol. The model holds temperature fixed and assigns irreversible heat to the surroundings; entropic heat, aging, side reactions, plating, spatial concentration, phase changes and calorimetry are outside scope.
