## Concept and visual direction

The question is why an ocean can rise on the far side when the Moon is on the near side. Start with the actual acceleration at several places, then subtract the center acceleration in a translating, nonrotating freely falling frame. Use the same tidal potential to construct the sea surface. There is no separate centrifugal-only far-side mechanism.

Art direction is a quiet ocean observatory: a translucent, gridded global ocean, a gold undeformed reference circle and tracked site, a compressed Moon position indicator, and a focused acceleration or latitude instrument. The surface is a mathematical global ocean without continents. Drawing plausible coastlines would imply basin calculations the model does not perform. Soft studio illumination is for reading the shape, not a Sun-illumination simulation. All geometry is code-authored; no external textures or other assets were added.

Desktop combines a spatial ocean with the relevant instrument. The 320px composition hides secondary readouts and redundant headers; solar chapters replace the latitude profile with a compact direction projection. Watch mode leaves the full caption to Showcase. The scene body is 630px in all eight checked Chinese and English phone frames. Desktop reserves a stable 500px instrument row. The self-contained 400×230 SVG cover accepts an optional color and derives its outline, graticule, near/far vectors and profile from the model.

## Independent model and limits

- Exact external point-mass acceleration is `a(r)=μ(D−r)/|D−r|³`. The relative field is the vector subtraction `Δa=a(r)−a(0)`. Only enabled external terms enter these readouts; terrestrial gravity is not being omitted from a claimed total acceleration.
- Positive tide-generating potential is `W=Σμ[1/|D−r|−1/D−(D·r)/D³]`, with `∇W=Δa`. A cancellation-resistant algebraic form retains finite-distance terms even for the Sun. The first-order equilibrium ocean has `h=W/g`.
- The leading quadrupole is `μR²(3 cos²ψ−1)/(2D³)`. The distance instrument plots this leading scaling, while the actual ocean uses the full potential. It does not claim the finite-size exact answer scales perfectly as D⁻³.
- Constants: R=6,371,000 m, g=9.80665 m/s², lunar GM=4.902800118×10¹² m³/s², solar GM=1.32712440041279419×10²⁰ m³/s², D₀=384,400,000 m and solar distance=149,597,870,700 m. GM and au are from JPL DE440 reference data. Reference lunar near/far heights are approximately 0.363/0.351 m, not coastal predictions.
- W has zero spherical mean for external masses. Small physical height changes therefore conserve ocean volume to first order. The greatly exaggerated display mesh is not a volume-conserving fluid body. The undeformed reference sphere/circle is not a seabed, and no water velocity is invented.
- Ocean displacement is magnified by up to 2,000,000. Body distances are compressed; the Moon indicator's size is not used in physics. Original-force and differential-force arrows use different display scales, explicitly marked in the inset. Original-force glyphs share a normalization within each state, including when the large solar bulk field is enabled.
- Sun azimuth is restricted to the equatorial plane. Lunar declination can differ; the instrument labels a planar direction projection and separately calculates true spatial separation. Observer rotation is a relative geometric angle, not a local day or time of high tide.
- Controls support Moon distance 0.8–1.4 D₀, source terms on/off, solar azimuth 0–180°, lunar declination −30–30°, observer latitude −80–80°, relative rotation 0–360°, and magnification 0–2 million. Distances are a thought experiment, not a lunar ephemeris; removing a source term does not mean the body disappears.
- Scope is a rigid spherical Earth and an ocean reaching equilibrium instantly. Basins, friction, Coriolis acceleration, resonant dynamics, ocean self-gravity and solid-Earth elasticity are omitted. The model predicts neither port tide times/heights nor universal two-high-tides-per-day behavior.

## Interaction, rendering and lifecycle

Four exploration views expose the surface, acceleration comparison, source alignment and observing latitude. All six ranges have explicit translated accessible names and 44px input height. Reset restores every parameter, comparison/reference frame, view, display magnification, renderer and closed disclosure. Turning both source terms off produces a zero surface and a matching explanation.

`useShowcase` owns chapter/progress, transport, reduced-motion policy and film time. There is no local animation clock, RNG, evolving simulation history, audio engine or Worker. Grid/point identities are indexed and every state is reconstructed from current inputs. The Three renderer draws each delivered state synchronously; it retains the static drawing buffer and checks the actual viewport rectangle and document visibility before drawing. Resize/intersection/visibility observers trigger a fresh state draw when needed. Unmount, manual 2D switch and context failure dispose geometries, materials, renderer, context, canvas and observers/listeners. On failure, SVG uses the same surface, vectors, camera projection and site; reset can recreate WebGL.

Perspective fitting includes a conservative ocean bound, actual deformed grid vertices in tests, Moon box corners, vector origins/tips and the marked site. It uses the actual camera basis and a margin, not only nominal object height. SVG fallback keeps the same physical state with its own responsive viewBox.
