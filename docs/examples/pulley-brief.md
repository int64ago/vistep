# Pulley — follow one unbroken rope

Owner: `/root/hundred_mechanisms`. Original SVG elevation; physical rope tangency and length are generated by `pulleyGeometry`. No textbook artwork reused.

Misconception: more wheels automatically means proportionally less work. Prerequisite: addition and length. Distinction from lever: one continuous inextensible path explains displacement multiplication. Track the same blue 120 N load and copper rope.

Source: [OpenStax College Physics 2e §9.5, pulley discussion](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/9-5-simple-machines). Claims: ideal uniform tension, supporting-strand count, force/displacement trade. Assumptions: vertical strands, quasi-static motion, no friction, no rope mass or stretch; block weight is included explicitly in final shot. Benchmark: four strands carry 120 N with 30 N tension. Boundary counterexample: a fixed pulley has one supporting strand, not two; adding 40 N moving block weight raises tension to 40 N. No rigging guidance.

Shots: 1 fixed pulley redirects; 2 moving sheave splits support; 3 load motion consumes two strand lengths; 4 four supporting strands reduce tension; 5 input and output work agree on a common travel scale; 6 the moving block's own weight adds lifting work.
