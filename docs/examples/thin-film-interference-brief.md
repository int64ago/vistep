## Question, misconception and place in the collection

A thicker transparent film need not reflect more. A vanishing propagation difference need not produce brightness. Prior knowledge: waves add and wavelength measures a cycle. This extends `wave-interference` and `double-slit` by adding the interface reflection phase rather than repeating slit geometry. It does not model scattering or rainbow refraction.

## Claims and checks

| Claim                                                                     | Technical source section                                                                                                       | Assumptions / range                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reflection toward a higher refractive index reverses electric field phase | [OpenStax §3.4, Changes in Phase](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/3-4-interference-in-thin-films) | Real positive indices, normal incidence, nonmagnetic and lossless media |

| Total reflected amplitude sums repeated internal reflections              | [University of Toronto PHY326 thin-film lab, theory](https://www.physics.utoronto.ca/~phy326/film/film.htm)                    | Coherent monochromatic illumination; no absorption or dispersion        |

Independent benchmark: a soap film of n=1.333 at 650 nm has its first bright thickness 121.90 nm and first nonzero dark thickness 243.81 nm. Separately test the zero-thickness Fresnel limit for a glass backing: 4% reflectance. Boundary counterexample: doubling quarter-wave thickness darkens an air-backed film, while changing only the backing flips the phase condition. Check boundedness and period over many thicknesses.

## Distinct direction and new evidence per chapter

The visual language is a dark optical plate, a transparent horizontal layer and three directly named wave traces. The body of the layer, its lower reflection point and the returning path share the same thickness state. Paths are continuous and separated into lanes for visibility; normal incidence actually overlaps. Film thickness is enlarged.

1. Vary thickness: the lower interface moves with its reflection point and returning path; the second wave changes phase.
2. Zero thickness: the propagation term vanishes but the return phases oppose.
3. Quarter wave: a half-wave optical round trip compensates the reflection reversal.
4. Half wave: more thickness returns the system to cancellation.
5. Same thickness, glass backing: the back reflection reverses and the result swaps.
6. Wedge: independent red/green/blue reflectance samples vary across a fixed thickness ramp.

Desktop: layer above three wave strips and two restrained optical readouts. Phone: separately sized 340-unit layer with 16 px CSS wave labels; no shrunken SVG labels. Primary controls appear only in exploration. Model qualifications live in a disclosure. Opening, turning and result are chapters 1, 5 and 6.

The equal-height wave traces isolate phase and do not claim equal Fresnel amplitudes. The reflectance value uses the complete Airy/Fresnel sum. The three-wavelength swatch is explicitly not calibrated colorimetry. No oblique-angle formula is implied by the lanes.
