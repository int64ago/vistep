## Question and misconception

Why does a hot dry road look wet? Follow the direction entering the eye through a centimetre-scale temperature layer, rather than inventing a flat reflecting sheet. Prerequisite: refraction and refractive index. Adds continuous-gradient optics to `refraction`; separates it from sharp-interface `total-internal-reflection`. The title and body explicitly narrow the result to an inferior-mirage sky-direction path, not a complete inverted scene or all atmospheric mirages.

## Independent model evidence

Uniform top air joins a layer with n²=n0²+a y continuously. Horizontal Snell invariant p=n cosθ gives an exact parabolic within-layer segment y=H+m u+a u²/(4p²). Join to uniform straight segments with continuous slopes; a true ground contact ends the ray. Independent midpoint integration of y′=tanθ, θ′=a/(2n²) checks turning position and angle. Check n cosθ at101points, dry-air n(300 K)=1.00026462, uniform-temperature straight limit, continuous exit symmetry, steep-angle ground clipping and reversed-gradient sign. No output is snapped to a preselected sky or reflection point.
