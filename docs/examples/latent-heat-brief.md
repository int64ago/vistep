# Latent heat — author brief

Status: model and key shots authored; integration-owner visual review and speech freeze pending. Author tests are numerical checks only. Continuous playback, phone performance and listening are not-run.

Misconception: energy input must always raise temperature. Prerequisites: energy, temperature and sample mass. Distinct contribution: an enthalpy ledger with changing phase mass fractions; unlike `convection`, no flow field carries the explanation, and unlike `refrigerator`, there is no thermodynamic cycle or refrigerant pressure path.

| Claim                                                                    | Source section                                                                                                                    | Assumptions / valid range                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Melting absorbs energy at nearly constant temperature during coexistence | [OpenStax University Physics2 §1.5 Phase Changes](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/1-5-phase-changes) | Pure substance, fixed near-atmospheric pressure, quasi-static, uniform temperature        |
| Fusion energy equals melted mass times latent heat                       | Same section, latent heat of fusion                                                                                               | Rounded constant334 kJ/kg near0°C                                                         |
| Real water/ice properties depend on state                                | [NIST Properties of Ice and Supercooled Water](https://www.nist.gov/publications/properties-ice-and-supercooled-water)            | The present scene uses rounded educational properties, not the full reference formulation |

Independent benchmark:100 g at−20°C needs4.2 kJ to reach0°C and another33.4 kJ to finish melting. Halfway through that latter amount, the liquid fraction is0.5 at0°C. Warming the water to20°C adds8.372 kJ. Tests independently balance every joule through both phase boundaries. Counterexamples: zero power produces no added heat; twice the power reaches an identical state in half the time; completed melting resumes temperature rise. Reverse energy removal lowers phase fraction at the same transition temperature in the equilibrium model.

Art direction: a quiet calorimetry notebook, with a persistent mass mosaic, a three-material energy ribbon and a temperature-versus-energy plot. It is not a decorative beaker whose apparent liquid volume could be mistaken for mass fraction. Liquid-state tiles soften but do not migrate. Six chapters add: warming before transition; rising phase fraction on a plateau; a return to mid-transition to compare latent energy and mass; post-transition warming; power-versus-time at identical enthalpy; reverse freezing. The mid-transition rewind is explicitly named in speech.

Desktop: wide mass mosaic and compact thermometer readout above the energy curve. Phone: an8-column mosaic beside the phase-fraction readout, with a separately sized graph and CSS16 px axes. The local heating-power/time labels stay visible during the controlled comparison. Long assumptions are in a disclosure. Opening/turning/result are warm ice, continuing power on the platform, and reversible release.

All quantities reconstruct directly from net energy, so pause and arbitrary seeking do not depend on hidden history. The model omits heat loss, container heat capacity, boiling, pressure changes, impurities and supercooling; cooling is an equilibrium reverse path, not a claim about spontaneous real nucleation. Browser framing, boundary chapters and full bilingual recordings remain for the integrator to review. No audio is produced by this author.
