# Keyboard switches — production brief

[简体中文](../zh-CN/examples/keyboard-switch-brief.md) · [Scene production](../creating-a-scene.md)

The viewer begins with color names and a vague distinction between quiet and clicky keys. The film replaces that shortcut with three visible questions: what creates force feedback, what creates sound, and what senses a press. Follow one convincing keycap, housing and moving stem through representative mechanisms. Reconstruct molded plastic, metal leaves, spring seating, guide surfaces and contacts rather than using generic blocks with changing labels. A declared front observation section reveals the internal mechanism; it is an original teaching reconstruction, not manufacturer CAD.

The planned film is **206 seconds, 3:26**, before measured speech. Nine chapters each add an observation. The sixth chapter deliberately contains two short comparisons; it does not claim that shorter travel and damping are the same feature.

After synthesis, the shared [film timeline](../../src/data/film-timeline.json) measures **213.5 seconds, 3:33.5**. The planned windows below remain the original design record; final chapter starts are 0, 22, 44, 66, 91, 113.5, 139.5, 165.5 and 189 seconds. Recorded duration is evidence of timing, not native listening.

The integrated composition keeps the large section above its compact force or state instrument on both desktop and phones. At 320/390 px, reframe the physical section for the narrower canvas and retain only the current chapter's instrument beneath it. Labels remain 16 px HTML, controls have at least 44 px targets, and no spoken cue relies on a screen side. The opening needs the complete outer identity; detail shots may intentionally emphasize the stem/leaf/jacket while a locator preserves context. The final cover shares actual scene geometry and makes the cross stem, spring and contact structure recognizable without a label collage.

## Primary facts and reference boundaries

All sources were checked on 2026-09-12. CHERRY product pages supply the representative construction and nominal anchors: [Red](https://www.cherry.de/en-gb/product/mx2a-red), [Black](https://www.cherry.de/en-gb/product/mx2a-black), [Brown](https://www.cherry.de/en-gb/product/mx2a-brown), [Blue](https://www.cherry.de/en-gb/product/mx2a-blue), [Speed Silver](https://www.cherry.de/en-gb/product/mx2a-speed-silver) and [Silent Red](https://www.cherry.de/en-gb/product/mx2a-silent-red).

| Example      | Actuation / pressure-point force | Pre-travel / total travel |
| ------------ | -------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Red          | 45 cN / no tactile peak          | 2.0 / 4.0 mm              |
| Black        | 60 cN / no tactile peak          | 2.0 / 4.0 mm              |
| Brown        | 45 / 55 cN                       | 2.0 / 4.0 mm              |
| Blue         | 50 / 60 cN                       | 2.2 / 4.0 mm              |
| Speed Silver | 45 cN / no tactile peak          | 1.2 / 3.4 mm              |
| Silent Red   | 45 cN / no tactile peak          | 1.9 / 3.7 mm              |

Brown and Blue English highlights use ambiguous force wording. Brown's Properties gives actuation 45 cN; the [French official page](https://www.cherry.de/fr-fr/produit/mx2a-brown) explicitly separates 45 cN actuation and 55 cN pressure-point force. The [Blue technical data sheet](https://www.cherry.de/fileadmin/media/Industrial/Switch/MX_BLUE/Data_sheet_MX2A_Blue.pdf) separately lists 50 and 60 cN. Its official search-index text was readable, while direct PDF opening returned an error. Do not silently report the tactile peak as actuation or turn the fitted bottom force into a published value.

The Blue page describes a two-part switching slide. Official on-page exploded-animation assets are [Blue](https://www.cherry.de/fileadmin/media/Industrial_Security/Switch/MX_BLUE/MX1A-E1NA_ExplodeANIM.mp4), [Brown](https://www.cherry.de/fileadmin/media/Industrial_Security/Switch/MX_BROWN/MX1A-G1NA_ExplodeANIM.mp4) and [Silent Red](https://www.cherry.de/fileadmin/media/Industrial_Security/Switch/MX_SILENT_RED/MX3A-L1NA_ExplodeANIM.mp4). The content worker extracted these URLs from the official HTML and passed them to the geometry author; that alone is not evidence of having watched the animations. No manufacturer image is copied into the product. [CHERRY's overview](https://www.cherry.de/it-it/lazienda/notizie/cherry-blog/article/cherry-mx-switches-at-a-glance) identifies both top-out and bottom-out damping.

[Wooting's Hall-effect explanation](https://wooting.io/post/what-are-hall-effect-keyboard-switches) supplies the magnet-on-stem and PCB-sensor relationship. [Its Rapid Trigger guide](https://help.wooting.io/article/102-how-to-set-up-rapid-trigger) supplies initial activation, movement-based reset/reactivation, ordinary versus continuous behavior and the existence of analog optical implementations. Avoid claiming that Rapid Trigger belongs exclusively to magnets. [Razer's optical example](https://www.razer.com/razer-optical-switch) explicitly allows light through the stem when pressed; other optical architectures are outside this particular section.

The independent model owns quasi-static teaching force curves, mechanical return thresholds, leaf deflection, jacket travel and internal display dimensions. Those are fitted relationships rather than measurements or precision part data. The Hall indicator uses a normalized, monotonic dipole-like distance relation and its inverse; it is not finite-magnet field simulation, sensor voltage, field strength or a Wooting calibration curve. Ordinary Rapid Trigger replays path extrema, arms after the initial threshold and leaves its active region when released above that threshold. The film uses one partial-return trajectory for both rules. There is no latency benchmark, switch sound recording or synthetic sound offered as an acoustic measurement.
