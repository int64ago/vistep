# Moon phases — production brief

[简体中文](../zh-CN/examples/moon-brief.md)

Part of the [50-scene expansion](../expansion-50.md). Begin with a large, quiet lunar portrait changing from crescent to gibbous, then pull back into the geometry that produces it. Keep the observer image connected to the orbit view with one shared solar direction. A curved terminator must result from sphere lighting, not a moving circle laid over a picture.

The visual language is an observatory: deep blue-black, warm sunlight, a detailed matte Moon and a small restrained Earth. The Moon's large portrait carries most of the information. Space views explain angles, with marked nonuniform size/distance scales and a distinct Earth-view inset. On phones, the portrait and orbital construction trade emphasis rather than squeezing two desktop panels together. The Moon uses NASA SVS’s LROC color and LOLA height maps, with their attribution preserved. The supplied color map is optimized for visual rendering, not scientific surface measurement. Earth remains an authored schematic. Do not label the 8-bit bump texture as quantitatively scaled terrain.

Eight chapters: a changing face; a sphere lit from one side; the observer's changing view; the principal phases; a full phase versus Earth's shadow; synchronous rotation; sidereal versus synodic months; and a complete visual reconstruction. The eclipse chapter rotates the orbital plane as a controlled geometric comparison, not as a prediction of its motion during one month. The month comparison explicitly uses coplanar circular motion.

The independent model uses circular inertial ecliptic orbits, parallel sunlight, a fixed orbital pole and mean periods. It computes an Earth-facing image basis, illumination, a tidally locked surface frame and geometric umbra/penumbra cones. Observer and space renderers must use these same vectors. A 5.145° inclination normally keeps a full Moon outside the shadow; a separate nodal example places it inside. Umbra overlap is an orthographic projected-area calculation, not atmospheric color or an eclipse timetable. Do not equate the far side with permanent darkness. Libration, orbital eccentricity, horizon orientation, Earthshine and real-date prediction are outside scope unless separately implemented.

Sources: [NASA Moon phases](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-phases/), [NASA's Moon questions](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/top-moon-questions/), [NASA eclipse geometry](https://science.nasa.gov/eclipses/geometry/), [NASA on orbital periods](https://starchild.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/StarChild/questions/question32.html).

Physical reference values are documented by [JPL astrodynamic parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/astro_par.html), [JPL satellite radii](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/) and [IAU nominal solar constants](https://www.iau.org/common/Uploaded%20files/IAUGA2015-Resolution-B3-recommended-nominal-conversion.pdf). Moon textures come from the [NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/); the original JPEGs are retained locally, with no recompression.

The scene is now integrated with an authored 177-second bilingual film, articles, cover, lazy import and recorded tracks. Six model tests cover principal phases, numerical illuminated area, locked surface coordinates, shadow miss/hit, sidereal versus synodic periods, and agreement between fallback polygons, shadow masks and displayed area. The portrait uses a spherical shader with fixed surface coordinates and a separate geometric shadow; the 8-bit height map only modifies subtle local shading.

Desktop key frames and all eight 320 px chapter layouts have been inspected. The phone orbit, shadow and month diagrams use different coordinates and label positions instead of scaling the desktop diagram. A fractional-width wrap in the readouts was corrected with explicit grid columns. Manual range endpoints, reset and the 2D path were exercised. Independent ASR passed all 16 recorded chapter/language pairs (minimum similarity 0.8743); this does not establish a listening review. Continuous full playback, complete failure/accessibility checks and physical-phone performance remain outstanding. See the expansion record for deployment evidence.
