# A transferable chemical signal

Owner: bio-systems. Local sources: PEP Biology elective 1; target IDs bio-elective-1-031-1, bio-elective-1-045-1.

## Question and direction

Why does a second heart slow without a connecting nerve? Track one fluid-borne signal between frog hearts and compare nerve and blood routes in the secretin experiments. Track the same material or signal through each intervention. Chinese and English narration is independently written in the handoff packet. One caption remains visible; models and controls are progressively disclosed.

## Storyboard

- heart-stimulate: A slows before any fluid reaches B.
- heart-transfer: After fluid transfer, the unconnected heart B also slows.
- heart-controls: Without release or transfer, B lacks the signal.
- secretin-route: Acid in the intestine and acid in blood give different outcomes.
- secretin-denervated: Secretion still increases after denervation.
- secretin-extract: A blood-borne mucosal extract produces a pancreatic response.
