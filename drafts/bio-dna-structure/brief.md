# DNA structure — principle-first revision

The misconception is that equal base totals or the silhouette of a double helix alone determines the local partners. The film now holds each piece of evidence fixed while changing a candidate, then follows a standard pair into the complete structure and uses a retained strand to predict its partner. This is a molecular-structure explanation, not a construction instruction sheet.

## Directed argument and preserved windows

1. `diffraction`: leave historical dimensions visible, then change only the ideal candidate and its calculated scattering. A wrong repeat faces the same reference.
2. `composition`: preserve six numbered units and total counts. Park one unit, shift neighbors into vacant places, and restore it at its new position; local matches change. The single-strand counterexample closes the inference.
3. `geometry`: expand N/O/H sites; show A–T, replace only T with C, restore T, compare G–C. H remains covalently attached to its donor. Assemble the pairs into a complete ideal duplex before making a local partner prediction.
4. `nucleotide`: distinguish a continuous 3′–O–P–O–5′ path from the hydrogen bonds across strands. No polymerization mechanism is claimed.
5. `antiparallel`: contrast direction candidates; preserve end identities and trace opposite chemical directions during a changed view. The ladder is a direction diagram, not measured molecular coordinates.
6. `helix`: retain actual 1BNA rings and 22 intrachain inter-nucleotide bridges, then keep the same six measured residue IDs while revealing their actual complementary partners. GC/AT contacts derive from the same contact model.
