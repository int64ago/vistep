## Question and causal object

What happened when a red iron solution lost and regained its colour? Track the same iron atoms, two electron credits and a marked thiocyanate ligand. A positive colour test establishes available iron(III) under the stated conditions; it does not establish a pure iron(III) sample. Reduction, coordination and simple separation must not be conflated.

## Independent model and boundaries

The distribution solves Ftot = F + C₁ + C₂ and Ltot = L + C₁ + 2C₂, with C₁ = K₁FL and C₂ = K₂C₁L. The displayed Q₁ is specifically the first-step formation quotient; it is not the quotient of the textbook three-ligand shorthand. At 25°C, the ideal zero-ionic-strength constants use logK₁=2.85 and logK₂=1.51.

The illustrative reference is FeIII2.5mmol/L and SCN1mmol/L, distinct from the textbook recipe. Removing half the free FeIII initially gives Q₁/K₁=2 while Q₂/K₂ stays1. The reduced amount d produces1.5d FeII, including0.5d metal-derived Fe. Subsequent composition paths conserve material and charge; they are not rate laws. The total coloured population decreases even though the small bis-complex population increases slightly, which is protected by a counterexample test.

Metal oxidation credits both electrons at departure; the resulting FeII is aqueous, and later motion is within solution. In-transit electron credits remain in the charge ledger. Gold markers are net electron accounting, not physical free-electron trajectories. A selected ligand route is not a fixed mole-count marker. Zero perturbation leaves the ligand in its original bound state or in the external supply. Potassium is a spectator and does not follow the ligand into the complex.

Hydration is schematic; the mono/bis formulas do not imply total coordination numbers one/two. FeII complexation, activities, hydrolysis, higher complexes and side reactions are omitted. Returning ligand to the free pool does not imply FeII can never bind it. Colour is qualitative and not fitted absorbance. The limited oxidant comparison is a possible conserved net account, not an elementary order or a prediction of the final state under excess chlorine.
