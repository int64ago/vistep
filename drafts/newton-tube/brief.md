# Newton tube — teaching-area pilot

Resolve the intuition that a feather must fall slowly because it is light. The prior lesson presents equal-mass paper in different shapes; this demonstration controls air while preserving objects, release time, zero initial speed and height. It precedes the textbook's measurement of g and later study of Newton's second law, so forces remain in the optional article.

Track the same gold feather and blue-grey iron plate across two labelled trials. The decisive shot compares the same physical instant in air and ideal vacuum. The second result is a repeat of the same apparatus, not a second uncontrolled experiment. Use two front-view cutaway tubes, soft glass highlights, manufactured caps and visible stopcocks. 3D rotation adds no causal information here. CSS labels remain 16px independently of the SVG coordinate system.

The visual objects have an identical 38-unit vertical extent, a common lower-contact reference at release, and a common floor. Translation follows the independent fall distance; contact is calculated, never cosmetically snapped. Feather and plate identities, shape, mass and drag parameters remain fixed. A pale speckled field indicates air, not resolved molecular flow. The evacuation shot fades this field but makes no claim to simulate a pump. Only the later comparison shows colored history markers, every 0.12s.

Desktop: two calm trials on one baseline, generous center separation, one shared physical timer. Phone: two narrow full-height tube columns, direct CSS condition labels above and short observations below, no side dashboard. Controls expand only in exploration. Main watch frame has no graph or force diagram. The shared player supplies pause, seeking, replay, chapter selection and narration lifecycle.

Sources: NASA Glenn, Modern Drag Equation (quadratic drag, constant illustrative Cd*A); NASA Apollo 15 Hammer-Feather Drop (independent real-world qualitative evidence under negligible atmosphere). Boundary counterexample: the feather arrives later with nonzero density; changing starting conditions would also break simultaneous arrival. Limit rho→0 is continuous. Ignore tumbling, buoyancy, tube-wall flow, rarefaction, pump mechanics and bounce.
