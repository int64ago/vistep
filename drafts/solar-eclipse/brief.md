# Solar eclipse direction

Initial intuition: every new moon should cause an eclipse. Track one observing point and one Moon. Turning point: height near an orbital node reveals the missing dimension in a top view.

Opening sketch: a blue orbital-height curve crosses the gold ecliptic line; the Moon begins at high latitude. Middle: a real-ratio angular portrait and an axially compressed shadow section connect the same observer to its sky. Result: increasing lunar distance changes totality to an annular alignment, followed by a continuous partial–total–partial crossing.

Phone composition: a single 320-unit causal instrument, with CSS-sized external labels and two compact inline readings. Desktop uses the same selected instrument with a wider coordinate field; no stacked side dashboard. The axial shadow section deliberately differs in horizontal/vertical scale and says so.

NASA geometry, eclipse types and Moon eclipse pages were opened on 2026-10-17. Independent model uses 3D Sun/Moon/observer vectors, common circle tangents and angular overlap. Sky disks use a declared small-angle circular approximation and uniform brightness. Observer moves on a transverse plane; no ephemeris, limb relief, atmospheric rendering, geographic ground track or calendar prediction.
