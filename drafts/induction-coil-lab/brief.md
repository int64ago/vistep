# Induction-coil laboratory

Four apparatus paths and nine chapters: moving/pole-reversed magnet with galvanometer; closed/split suspended aluminium ring; falling magnet with high-impedance voltage recorder; axle/rim-contact disc generator. Each textbook id maps to adjacent chapters.

Misconception: field presence is sufficient for induction, or all metals are attracted by magnets. Track the magnet, then the actual loop and meter polarity. The key evidence is a stationary-magnet zero, and the split-ring counterexample. Release-height and turn-count controls are separated; the same-trajectory eighty-turn waveform remains as a reference.

Model: circular-loop flux of an axial point dipole; emf is its analytic time derivative. Resistive current, force and power derive from the same gradient and satisfy work-to-heat balance. Ring motion is an enlarged quasistatic visual response and does not feed back. The falling voltage sensor draws negligible current. The disc uses Bωr²/2, with ideal brushes.

Tests compare the flux derivative against a numerical difference, current/pole reversals, open-ring force, equal-trajectory turn scaling, and disc power balance. Motion uses shared chapter time only.
