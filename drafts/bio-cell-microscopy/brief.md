# Cell microscopy production brief

Misconception: a larger microscope image implies a larger cell, or streaming rotates the whole cell. Track one chloroplast against an immobile wall. Prerequisite: micrometre scale and basic plant/animal cell structure.

Direction: recognizable microscope and circular specimen field; low-power off-center cell is centered before objective change. Phone stacks a small instrument above a dominant circular field, preserving readable HTML scale labels. Cork and animal epithelium compare evidence; the living leaf reveals a peripheral cytoplasmic path.

Scientific basis: FSU field-number relation and Nikon micrometry references in packet. Local curriculum: required-1 P3–4, P9–10, P50. Representative cell sizes, optical blur and constant streaming rate are explicit approximations. No photograph or calibrated velocity claim.

Independent benchmarks: FN18 / 10× = 1.8 mm; FN18 / 40× = .45 mm. Fourfold magnification preserves physical specimen dimensions. Boundary: fixed chloroplast state does not move with time; a full period returns the same chloroplast to the same point; larger power reduces the number of visible cells.

Cue evidence: cork wall chambers → nucleated animal cells → lateral stage centering → objective scaling and focus → vacuole/peripheral chloroplast distinction → persistent labeled streaming trace.
