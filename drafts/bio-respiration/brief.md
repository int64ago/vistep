## Visible causal sequence

1. **atp — What changes in the receiver?** Track glucose C6 oxygen, ATP terminal phosphorus and its three nonbridging oxygens. The original beta–gamma bridge oxygen remains in ADP; the glucose oxygen becomes bonded to transferred phosphorus. Pi alone is a controlled alternative, not a spontaneously equivalent donor.
2. **air — Where did the detected carbon dioxide come from?** Separate incoming-air scrubbing from newly formed culture gas. Gas traces follow the same connected inlet/outlet paths as the glass drawing. Bypassing the scrubber exposes source ambiguity.
3. **sealed — Does a stopper remove oxygen?** A finite initial oxygen inventory decreases before the culture is connected to the detector. Continued gas after depletion presents the question that the later mechanism answers.
4. **assay — What do the tests establish?** Preserve the qualitative A/B contrast and show residual glucose interference. Explicitly retain the high-sugar aerobic-fermentation counterexample; oxygen is not a universal ethanol-off switch.
5. **glycolysis — Six carbons, ATP, and carriers are different accounts.** Glucose carbons reach two pyruvates without CO2 release. A separate four-adenosine ledger shows two ATP investments and four productions. The carrier close-up then explicitly revisits GAP oxidation: Pi joins the aldehyde carbon as NAD+ receives a reducing equivalent. With a restricted pool left as NADH, the next fresh substrate batch is limited.
6. **aerobic — What links oxygen to ATP?** A local yeast external-NADH-entry diagram separates electron transfer, downstream proton pumping, terminal oxygen reduction to water, and proton return through ATP synthase. After stopping input, stored difference drains continuously; ATP synthesis does not drop instantaneously to zero.
7. **fermentation — How can the same carriers return?** Follow C3/C4 into CO2, the remaining two-carbon fragments through acetaldehyde reduction to ethanol, and the same N1/N2 into the next glycolytic batch. The homolactic comparison preserves all three carbons, regenerates NAD+, and yields glycolytic ATP without CO2. Thus gas is not an ATP counter.

The external yeast NADH-dehydrogenase source was read at original-abstract scope, not claimed as full-paper reading.

The ATP illustration is a **net atom map**, not a detailed transition state, free PO3 intermediate or molecular free-energy calculation. Buffer proton handling and Mg coordination are condensed. Breaking a bond alone does not release energy; the complete coupled reaction matters. The single hexokinase transfer represents only the first of two glycolytic investments.

The GAP close-up is a **net functional-group diagram**. P abbreviates a phosphate group with other oxygens omitted. Carbon positions and N1/N2 are tracked; it is not a full hydrogen/oxygen or electron-orbital map. The next substrate batch contains new molecules rather than the prior product running backwards. The aldehyde-to-acyl-phosphate state and the carrier state share the same progress intervals.

The membrane entry is specifically **cytosolic NADH entering through yeast external NADH dehydrogenase**. It is not a universal diagram of mitochondrial NADH entry; budding yeast lacks a human-style complex I. The selected oxygen/water account is a net terminal account, not the detailed oxidase mechanism. The positive reservoir uses teaching units and an explicitly bounded analytic decay; it supplies no fixed proton/ATP ratio, fitted culture rate, quantitative pH or universal ATP yield. CO2 oxygen atoms are not assigned wholesale to inhaled O2.

Apparatus observations are qualitative textbook reconstructions, independent of exploratory perturbations. The ideal aerobic route and the homolactic route are controlled examples, not universal organism-level claims. Many metabolic branches, glycerol/biomass and isotope fractionation are omitted.
