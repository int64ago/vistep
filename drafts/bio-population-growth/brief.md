## Scientific model and boundaries

The default logistic solution uses N₀=5, K=375 and r=1.8 per day as illustrative parameters. It is not Gause’s original fit. The original fitted curve has a different intercept as well as a different rate; copying its rate alone would not reproduce it.

In the shown domain N/K≤2, b=0.4+r−rN/(2K) and d=0.4+rN/(2K) are nonnegative expected per-individual daily rates. Their difference gives the same net logistic derivative. At K both are nonzero. This is a phenomenological hypothesis, not a microscopic derivation from the food allocation illustration. Exactly 24 persistent supply markers are allocated in two counterfactual populations; the declining average share motivates a hypothesis without measuring feeding kinetics.

Four persistent parent identities and their daughter identities show a selected short competing-risk realization with density/rates frozen and at most one first event per parent. It is not an unbiased stochastic simulation of the whole logistic population. Births and deaths are committed only at completion; the ledger is initial + births − deaths. Environmental change preserves N at the switch and changes later rates. With N₀=0 and no immigration, the population stays empty.

Yeast practice counts are fixed synthetic records at tenfold dilution, independent of the logistic calculation. Personal samples save `{count,dilution}` at entry and convert individually before averaging; effective repeat count, zero and missing remain distinct. The diagram’s selected single-cell field is not a full culture or a live/dead assay.

The split-sample diagram is a conditional prediction, not a result. Equal additions create a common diluted baseline; it does not model an instantaneous loss of cell number. The four selected budding events do not predict an absolute yield or lag time. Other inhibition is a real selectable counterexample. Monod’s dry-mass yield and specific growth rate are not repurposed as yeast cell-count observations or logistic carrying capacity.
