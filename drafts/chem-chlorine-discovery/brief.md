# Chlorine: evidence and explanation

The opening misconception is that identifying a product automatically identifies the source of its atoms, or that one negative reaction proves an element. The film separates a fixed historical report from a changing explanation, then uses a modern conserved atom allocation to make the alternative source visible.

The primary object is the same oxygen in PbO and newly formed water. The historical gas has no molecular tokens until an explicitly modern overlay. PbO and PbCl₂ appear as neutral composition groups without directional bonds; HCl and water retain their covalent links. The six-atom account retains one Pb, one O, two H and two Cl at every visible allocation state. Initial external water has different identifiers and is never counted as newly formed water.

## Model and limits

- `CHLORINE_HISTORY` contains frozen source records. Selecting an explanation does not regenerate evidence.
- `oxygenSourceAccount` solves the limiting-reagent net account for PbO + 2HCl → PbCl₂ + H₂O. Initial water stays separate; Pb/O/H/Cl inventories are invariant. Atom trajectories are illustrative net allocation, not historical imaging, isotope experiments or elementary kinetics.
- `chlorineGasTrain` retains the existing ideal well-mixed unit-capacity model: retained = 1 − exp(−q); outflow = q − retained. Absorption consumes two OH⁻ per Cl₂ and produces equal chloride/hypochlorite amounts.
- Nonfinite, negative, over-range and empty/degenerate tube inputs receive finite bounded results.
- Davy's residual condensation is not rewritten as measured zero water. Zero new water without any listed oxygen input is only the modern model counterexample.
- The dry-carbon negative alone does not establish an element. The lead-oxide water observation was attributed by Davy to Gay-Lussac and Thénard.
- No real rate, collection efficiency, operating amounts, temperature or home generation recipe is provided.
