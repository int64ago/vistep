## Causal sequence

1. **Steam path.** The observed black solid and combustible gas pose the weighing question. The water source and outlet are physically connected. A selected oxygen marker stops at the iron, while a separate product-hydrogen marker proceeds to the outlet. These are selected identities, not a claim that the whole outlet stream is pure hydrogen.
2. **Hot iron.** Fifteen persistent atom IDs retain three Fe, four O and eight H. Four separated oxygen paths enter a diffuse solid-composition region; the same hydrogen IDs form four pairs. A highlighted oxygen remains identifiable throughout. Successive motions are a reading order for the net account, not a proposed elementary mechanism. The endpoint formula describes bulk Fe₃O₄ composition and has no fictitious molecular Fe–O bond diagram.
3. **Solid gain.** A visibly labelled ideal calculation starts with 1 g Fe and 0.5 g water. One material batch departs the water pool, exists only in a transfer pool while moving, then joins solid and gas. The complete ledger remains 1.500 g. The final solid is about 1.382 g, containing 0.382 g oxygen supplied by water; hydrogen and remaining water stay in the total. Under partial conversion, total solid includes both unreacted iron and oxide.
4. **Gas evidence.** An explicitly conceptual air comparison shows why bubbles alone do not identify hydrogen. The textbook combustibility observation and reactant/product account add evidence. Separate FeO and Fe₃O₄ samples show why black colour alone is also insufficient.
5. **Water limit.** Two independent ideal examples contain the same 1 g Fe and 0.3 / 0.6 g water. Answers are hidden during the prediction. The first exhausts water and retains iron; the second exhausts iron and retains water. Their hydrogen yields are 16.65 / 23.88 mmol, a ratio of about 1.434 rather than two. Exploration changes the actual water input and hides the numerical answer again.

## Models and boundaries

`ironSteam` derives molar quantities, limited conversion and all mass terms from the selected net reaction. It guards nonfinite and out-of-range inputs. `ironSteamAtoms`, `ironSteamMassFrame`, and `ironSteamPrediction` provide the shared state for particles, paths, labels and ledgers. The watch director uses chapter time; exploration values do not alter the watched reference examples. Its final timing uses actual bilingual word timestamps with the 0.45-second leading cue silence included.

This film does not calculate reaction rates, a temperature threshold, phase diagrams, gas purity, steam condensation, or initial air displacement. Hot/cold is a demonstration-timescale comparison. Fe₃O₄ is the selected textbook endpoint, not the universally predicted product of iron at any temperature. The aggregate moving mass batch is bookkeeping, not measured transport dynamics. The ideal numbers are not reported textbook measurements. No explanation of molten-metal accidents is inferred from this reaction alone.
